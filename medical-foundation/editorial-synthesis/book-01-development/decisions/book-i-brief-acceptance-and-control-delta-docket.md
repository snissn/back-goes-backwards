## Authorial directions treated as settled

The following directions are no longer editorial blockers:

1. **Hard and soft name relational mechanical roles.** The primary language is comparatively shape-preserving or structurally resolving in relation to comparatively form-receiving or shape-conforming. Form reception means yielding enough to accommodate surrounding form and retain task-relevant freedom; it does not mean weakness, passivity, or absence of load.
2. **Traditional provenance appears early.** The distinction is described as **motivated by** TCM hard/soft and Yin/Yang concepts, without claiming definitional identity, a fixed tissue taxonomy, or scientific validation by correspondence.
3. **The compact authorial intuition remains visible.** “Load wants to travel through the hard body so that the soft body can remain soft” is retained and unpacked into bounded mechanical questions rather than treated as either a universal law or material to suppress.
4. **Posterior anatomy and task contribution belong in Book I.** Preferred posterior organization, posterior necessity, and signed generalized modes remain direct but non-load-bearing later hypotheses.
5. **Maintained constraint begins with the maintaining relation.** Geometry or narrowing alone is not the complete mechanism; imposed load, deformation or lost excursion, and any functional consequence remain separate links.
6. **Thoracic-outlet distinctions are optional analytical tools.** Arterial, venous, and neural material is separated only when tissue, quantity, chronology, evidence, or alternatives differ. A subtype taxonomy is not a completeness requirement or the Chapter 24 thesis.
7. **The stronger series program is preserved generously.** Preferred configuration, the three-mode model, Six-Division and channel correspondences, breath geometry, bandhas, torus models, embodiment, and practice remain visible at their proper seams without carrying Book I's foundational or medical claims.

## Escalation rule

Continue autonomously through editorial decisions, source retrieval, registry promotion, example substitution, figure design, evidence-state wording, specialist routing, and passage salvage. Escalate to the author only when one of these conditions is met:

1. two viable choices would create materially different Book I theses, reader promises, or Book I/II boundaries;
2. a proposed resolution would remove or contradict a protected authorial hypothesis rather than narrow, relabel, defer, or relocate it;
3. the decision depends on unpublished personal, embodied, clinical, or traditional knowledge that only the author can supply;
4. medical or safety review requires a material scope change rather than ordinary qualification or example replacement; or
5. the evidence leaves two incompatible narratives equally viable and the choice is primarily authorial rather than technical or pedagogical.

Do not escalate routine nomenclature, reversible example choices, source quality judgments, specialist-review scheduling, figure feasibility, null results, or narrower publication wording. Record those decisions and proceed.
