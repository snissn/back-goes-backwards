## Recovery decision

The archive makes two claims about the scale of the project that must be preserved together.

First, biomechanics can perform more medical work than a narrow rehabilitation identity usually allows. Mechanical history, distribution, cumulative exposure, compensation, internal constraint, and functional reserve may alter how a health problem is framed before a treatment decision is made. The author is not merely adding posture advice to an otherwise unchanged model of care. The longer research program asks whether a more coherent account of whole-body mechanical organization could eventually justify changes in medical education, prevention, research, and practice.

Second, the physical existence of mechanics does not make one mechanical account the primary explanation of every disease, symptom, or decision. Biological state, immune and infectious processes, genetics, development, injury, psychology, learning, work, resources, environment, care history, and meaning may enter before, after, beside, or through the mechanical relations under review. A strong mechanical program must be able to identify the question for which its account is useful and the question for which it is not.

Chapter 21 should not resolve this tension by shrinking the project to “mechanics is sometimes a modest adjunct.” Nor should it spend the archive's largest medical claims as established conclusions. It should build the reasoning interface that lets later work become more ambitious without becoming self-sealing:

> For a declared question, what specific work does a proposed mechanical relation perform, what observations distinguish it from alternatives, and where does the evidence require the explanation to stop?

That question protects both sides of the author's intent. A narrow local relation can be strongly supported even when a complete origin story is unavailable. A broad mechanical hypothesis can remain a serious research program when its missing arrows are named. A low-priority result can improve the program rather than threatening it. A later finding can also justify substantially more mechanical priority than current practice assigns.

The archive's disease lists, causal declarations, clinical promises, and medical-overhaul language are therefore recovered at different levels. Book I promotes causal-scope tools, whole-person node entry, reciprocal histories, alternatives, and stopping rules. It preserves the stronger propositions as explicit research and later-book obligations. It excludes diagnosis, triage, referral, and treatment instructions from reader prose because this volume is a foundation, not because those applications are forbidden from the series.

## Disposition legend

- **D — direct recovery:** the concept can enter Chapter 21 substantially as intended.
- **R — reconstruct:** preserve the intended question while separating variables, arrows, alternatives, or evidence states.
- **P — protect forward:** retain the concept for another chapter, book, research program, or application layer.
- **Q — quarantine:** retain the exact stronger proposition as a research obligation; do not present it in Book I as an established conclusion.
- **X — exclude from reader prose:** editorial instruction, generated certainty, protocol, or category collapse remains in provenance only.

Quarantine and exclusion are not deletion. They prevent a provisional expression from acquiring accidental publication authority while retaining the underlying idea for refinement.

## Source-coverage reconciliation

### BGB-S-1108 — mechanically led and other-primary contexts

**Preserved:** The archive needs a way to distinguish circumstances in which a mechanical history carries substantial explanatory weight from circumstances in which immune, infectious, genetic, developmental, metabolic, malignant, toxic, traumatic, social, or other processes carry more of the origin or immediate decision burden.

**Reconstruction:** These are not fixed disease bins. The same condition can contain several questions and several time-ordered relations. “Mechanically led” becomes a proposition about a named transition and endpoint. “Other-primary” becomes a scoped conclusion supported by relevant evidence, not a declaration that mechanics is absent.

### BGB-S-1125 — a larger medical role for biomechanics

**Preserved:** The intended contribution extends upstream of rehabilitation. Mechanical history and routing may change research questions, interpretation of exposure, understanding of reserve, selection of measurements, prevention hypotheses, and the relation among diagnosis, procedure, rehabilitation, work, and daily function.

**Reconstruction:** Chapter 21 does not promise that adding a mechanical account always improves care. It asks what the account distinguishes beyond ordinary medical, rehabilitation, ergonomic, behavioral, or social reasoning. A positive result can justify a larger role; a null result prevents duplication. The series' possible medical overhaul remains a future consequence of accumulated evidence, not a conclusion Chapter 21 must either announce or renounce.

### BGB-S-1225 — limits, contraindications, referral, and conventional care

**Preserved:** The archive understood that a mechanical model must coexist with diagnosis, medication, surgery, rehabilitation, specialty care, and other medically appropriate responses.

**Current allocation:** Chapter 21 promotes causal boundaries and other-primary contexts. Chapter 22 owns workflow integration. Contraindication lists, red flags, referral thresholds, personalized recommendations, and care protocols remain outside Book I and move to medically reviewed application work, principally Book V.

### BGB-S-1301 — medical framing, mechanism first, and tiered claims

**Preserved:** Start from a proposed phenomenon and chain rather than attaching a mechanical story to a list of diseases. Distinguish observation, measurement, inference, hypothesis, and application. Keep objections and limits inside the argument.

**Reader translation:** The planning scaffold disappears. Its discipline becomes one observation with several compatible graphs, exact question scope, arrow-specific evidence, alternatives, and a stopping point.

### BGB-S-1307 — causal scope and exclusions

**Preserved:** A mechanically coherent relation can be important without explaining every upstream or downstream event. Immune, infectious, genetic, developmental, metabolic, traumatic, behavioral, environmental, and social processes can lead, interact, or change the same chain.

**Reconstruction:** Exclusion is question-specific. Low mechanical priority for disease origin does not settle mechanics of present task, symptom modulation, participation, assistance, or recovery. Conversely, measurable current modulation does not identify historical origin.

### BGB-S-1308 — OA/RA boundary exemplar

**Preserved:** The comparison was meant to show that mechanical relevance and causal primacy are different.

**Reconstruction:** Osteoarthritis is not assigned to a purely mechanical box and rheumatoid arthritis is not assigned to a mechanics-free box. The evidence packet retains a matrix of initiation, current exposure, symptom/function, and response questions. The example is optional pending rheumatology review and can be replaced if it obscures more than it clarifies.

### BGB-S-1315 — skeptic handling

**Preserved:** The book should answer strong objections directly: a coherent graph is not causation; the physical existence of load does not guarantee useful mechanical explanation; a response to support does not prove etiology; whole-person context cannot be a generic closing caveat; and a routing lens must add value beyond existing fields.

**Reconstruction:** The skeptic is not a hostile character and the response is not reassurance. Each objection becomes a design requirement, comparator, alternative graph, or possible disfavoring result.

All seven assigned source obligations are represented, routed, or explicitly protected. No intended concept is silently discarded.

## Structural recovery from the archive

The archive contributes ten durable moves to Chapter 21:

1. **Mechanics can be medically upstream.** It can shape questions about history, exposure, reserve, prevention, and interpretation before a rehabilitation plan exists.
2. **Causal work is question-specific.** Origin, current exposure, symptom, function, recovery, prediction, and decision are different propositions.
3. **A mechanical chain can be partially true.** One measured link need not carry the entire disease or symptom narrative.
4. **Time permits role changes.** Mechanics can precede one transition, follow another, and participate in a reciprocal loop later.
5. **Whole-person factors belong inside the chain.** Biology, expectation, learning, work, resources, environment, care, and meaning alter named nodes and arrows.
6. **Mechanical and nonmechanical accounts can compete or coexist.** Neither is required to be a disclaimer around the other.
7. **Modulation and origin remain separate.** A support, movement, or intervention can change a current outcome without reconstructing the history that produced it.
8. **A null is informative.** Low priority, no added explanation, failed mediation, or no decision value can narrow the research program.
9. **Ambition requires discrimination.** A framework becomes more consequential when it makes predictions that can lose, not when it can redescribe every outcome.
10. **The later series remains intact.** Preferred configuration, TCM correspondence, breath geometry, and practice can later supply more specific candidate graphs, but Chapter 21 cannot validate them in advance.

## Clean conceptual recovery

### Preserve directly

- Mechanics can organize a medically relevant question beyond rehabilitation.
- Mechanical plausibility, causal priority, clinical importance, and decision relevance are distinct.
- A causal proposition needs a population, boundary, timeframe, comparator, receiver, and endpoint.
- Forward, reverse, reciprocal, common-cause, context-mediated, and incidental structures can fit the same observations.
- Mechanical roles are flexible prose descriptions, not compulsory diagnostic bins.
- Biological, psychological, social, occupational, environmental, cultural, economic, care, and meaning relations enter at named nodes.
- Pain, fear, expectation, disease state, injury, and care can change movement or loading.
- Response to a mechanical change establishes the measured response under its conditions, not the original cause.
- A structural or imaging finding can be real yet carry low priority for a selected symptom question.
- The weakest consequential arrow limits the conclusion drawn from the chain.
- Evidence from unlike methods should be compared for convergent and divergent predictions rather than pooled as interchangeable proof.
- “No added value for this question” is a successful, bounded conclusion.

### Reconstruct rather than erase

- “Disease has a mechanical origin” becomes a named exposure-to-response proposition with chronology, receiver, alternatives, and an endpoint.
- “Mechanics is the root architecture” becomes a research question about whether the proposed relation explains more than competing histories for a declared transition.
- “Minor misalignment causes systemic decline” becomes a sequence of candidate local, compensatory, physiological, and outcome arrows, each requiring its own measurement and comparator.
- “OA is mechanical and RA is immune” becomes a matrix showing how disease initiation, present joint exposure, symptom, function, and intervention response can assign different weight to mechanics.
- “This is medical, not rehabilitation” becomes a demonstration that mechanics may alter upstream questions, measurements, prevention hypotheses, and interpretations, followed by evidence of incremental value.
- “Whole-person care” becomes node-specific biological, psychological, social, environmental, occupational, care, and values relations.
- “It improved when position changed, so position caused it” becomes a present modulation result whose mediator and history remain separate.
- “The image explains the symptom” becomes a correspondence question with asymptomatic, noncorresponding, temporal, and alternative comparisons.
- “Biomechanics complements medicine” becomes an open range from duplication to integration to substantive revision, decided by evidence rather than by an editorial pledge of permanent subordination.

### Protect forward

- the hypothesis that whole-body mechanical organization is a neglected determinant of selected health trajectories;
- disease-specific mechanical-origin and mechanical-contribution claims after receiver, chronology, and alternative review;
- systemic circulation, lymphatic, neural, autonomic, inflammatory, immune, metabolic, energetic, and recovery chains;
- prospective studies of mechanical history, cumulative exposure, reserve, and delayed consequence;
- the possibility that a validated routing framework should change medical curricula, prevention, diagnosis, and treatment;
- preferred configuration and posterior necessity in Book II;
- Six-Division, channel-pair, force-line, hand–leg, and Yin/Yang correspondence in Book III;
- breath, pressure, phase, bandha, and torus-related organization in Book IV;
- assessment, self-observation, corrective practice, yoga, tai chi, rehabilitation, prevention, and clinical application in Book V; and
- condition-specific contraindication, referral, treatment, and interdisciplinary-care frameworks after appropriate medical evidence and review.

These are forward obligations, not conclusions borrowed by Book I. Rigor is added as a proposition approaches reader publication, medical consequence, or practice.

## Reader-facing sequence

1. **Open with one observation and six possible histories.** Keep observed nodes distinct from inferred arrows.
2. **Ask which question is actually being answered.** Separate origin, present exposure, symptom modulation, function, recovery, prediction, and decision.
3. **Show several ways mechanics can enter without making a taxonomy.** Use questions and time-indexed relations rather than mandatory role labels.
4. **Test a mixed disease boundary.** Use OA/RA only if prospective geometry, inflammatory-state, and experimental localization evidence can remain distinct and the nuanced matrix stays clearer than an abstract explanation after specialist review.
5. **Allow another process to lead.** Establish infection, immune disease, injury, genetics, malignancy, or another strongly supported process as a real stopping point for selected questions.
6. **Reverse the arrow experimentally.** Show pain, fear, altered recruitment, and transient inflammatory sickness changing movement or task production under bounded conditions.
7. **Build reciprocal and common-cause histories.** Let work, care, resource conditions, or biological state change both exposure and outcome.
8. **Put the whole person into the graph.** Use a node-entry matrix rather than a balance paragraph.
9. **Separate response from origin.** Compare intervention assignment, targeted mechanical or structural change, multicomponent response, active component, mediator, sensor increment, symptom or function, and history.
10. **Let structure be real but nondiscriminating.** Use asymptomatic findings to demonstrate endpoint-specific priority.
11. **Match evidence to arrows.** Compare observation, measurement, model, temporal association, experiment, mechanism, mediation, outcomes, and triangulation.
12. **End with support, narrowing, and stopping.** Return to the opening observation and show what would strengthen, limit, or disfavor the selected mechanical account.
