## Recovery decision

The archive intends more than the general observation that geometry affects mechanics. It proposes a consequential sequence: whole-body or regional geometry changes the paths available to load; internal demand is redistributed; selected tissues receive concentrated or poorly shared stress and strain; repeated exposure interacts with material state and recovery; and local response may eventually appear as a finding, loss of function, or symptom. The spine is the archive's principal image of that process. Scoliosis, sagittal-curve change, disc, endplate, facet, stenotic, and other axial contexts were meant to make global organization and local consequence visible in one structure.

That program should be preserved. It should not be replaced by a generic statement that posture is complicated, nor should a cleaner limb example quietly displace the book's axial intent. At the same time, the archive often writes the entire sequence as if one visible alignment already identifies internal loading, tissue history, clinical meaning, and correction. Those transitions are not one claim. A worked narrative earns its force by separating them.

Chapter 23 should therefore recover the original thesis through a declared mechanical case:

> For a bounded task and anatomical system, geometry can change line of action, moment arms, contact relations, active demand, and boundary conditions. These changes can redistribute local stress and strain. Whether the redistribution matters depends on the actual receiver exposure, dose history, material state, recovery, response, and clinical correspondence. Each link remains measurable, model-dependent, or unresolved in its own right.

The knee is the lead system because it provides the most inspectable path across the chapter's required levels: external contact and calculated joint moment, joint resultant and load sharing, contact area and pressure, spatially varying cartilage strain, receiver response, structural finding, symptom, and null. No single study observes that entire history. The chapter must present it as an explicit reconstruction across cadaveric experiments, in-vivo measurement, instrumented implants, validated models, explant studies, and prospective or cross-sectional human evidence. This makes the evidentiary seams visible rather than turning technical cleanliness into apparent certainty.

The lumbar motion segment remains the required transfer test and the spine remains the archive's principal axial research program. Disc, endplate, facet, ligament, active support, neighboring segments, and external contacts make the lumbar system more coupled and its internal distribution more model-dependent. That complexity is the point of the transfer: the chapter asks which relations survive outside the cleaner joint construction, which quantities change meaning, and where cells must remain blank. A knee lead therefore serves the axial program by teaching a disciplined method before applying it to the system that matters most to the larger project.

The recovery has five layers:

1. **Direct Book I content:** system boundary, task, geometry, line of action, moment, contact, local exposure, dose, receiver state, alternatives, and null.
2. **Reconstructed Book I content:** alignment, axial collapse, cumulative strain, degeneration, and failure become staged propositions with evidence states and multiple histories.
3. **Protected case inventory:** unselected spinal, limb, craniofacial, conduit, pelvic, and postural contexts remain in the project inventory rather than disappearing when one case is chosen.
4. **Later-series hypotheses:** preferred configuration, posterior necessity, signed generalized modes, TCM correspondence, breath geometry, and correction remain available to later books but cannot close this narrative.
5. **Reversible quarantine:** deterministic posture-to-disease chains, universal optimal alignment, guaranteed prevention or reversal, diagnosis from form, and corrective instructions remain recoverable only after condition-specific evidence and application review.

Nothing is made safer by deleting the strong hypothesis from the internal project. It becomes publishable by assigning each part to the right evidence state and development layer.

## Source-obligation reconciliation

### BGB-S-1104 — alignment changes paths, moments, exposure, and compensation

**Preserved:** Spatial relations matter mechanically. For a declared task and boundary, changing joint orientation, curvature, contact, external support, or line of action can change moments, muscular demand, available contacts, and the distribution that must be resolved internally.

**Reconstruction:** *Alignment* is descriptive until a frame, phase, reference, task, and outcome are named. One visible configuration does not uniquely identify muscle forces, co-contraction, contact, tissue stress, or the person's history. The worked model must include more than one internally possible solution and at least one observation that distinguishes them.

**Protected future:** Book II may test whether a preferred global organization exists and whether posterior participation is necessary within its domain. Chapter 23 neither assumes nor disproves that program.

### BGB-S-1105 — magnitude, direction, repetition, and time create cumulative demand

**Preserved:** The receiver encounters a history, not merely a peak. Magnitude, mode, direction, distribution, rate, repetition, duration, sequence, variability, rest, prior exposure, and recovery interval can change the response.

**Reconstruction:** *Cumulative* cannot imply a universal scalar sum across forces, moments, stresses, strains, tasks, and tissues. The chapter should retain the smallest multidimensional history required by the selected receiver and response. A long exposure may remain within capacity; a short event may be consequential; repeated exposure may adapt, fatigue, repair, or produce no detected response.

### BGB-S-1110 — localized concentration, repeated strain, fatigue, degeneration, and failure

**Preserved:** Geometry and boundary conditions can produce local elevations in stress or strain within a heterogeneous receiver. Repetition, rate, material state, and recovery can make that local field biologically relevant. Fatigue, remodeling, degeneration, repair, and failure are legitimate candidate branches.

**Reconstruction:** Concentration is comparative: a local value relative to a stated reference distribution, region, or condition. It is not damage. Damage is not degeneration. Degeneration is not pain. A modeled peak is not a measured tissue history. The chain must retain ordinary exposure, adaptation, recovery, null response, and model artifact as affirmative alternatives.

### BGB-S-1115 — axial or global geometry as a failure family

**Preserved:** The spine and thorax are three-dimensional structures whose curvature, rotation, translation, support, and active control can affect balance, moment demand, contact, and regional mechanics. Axial geometry is central to the author's research program, not a cosmetic concern added for illustration.

**Reconstruction:** *Axial collapse* remains a candidate pattern rather than a diagnosis or single measured variable. A useful instance must specify what changed—height, curve, translation, rotation, balance, support, motion, or another geometry—and which local mechanical output is predicted. Systemic respiratory, vascular, neural, pelvic, cranial, energetic, and disease consequences require separate receivers and bridges.

### BGB-S-1117 — concentration, material capacity, time, and degenerative progression

**Preserved:** A repeated or retained local exposure can interact with the material and biological state of disc, endplate, facet, cartilage, bone, tendon, ligament, or another receiver. Mechanics can be part of degenerative progression rather than merely a response after the fact.

**Reconstruction:** The selected narrative must distinguish initiation, progression, current exposure, finding, symptom, and function. Age, development, morphology, genetics, inflammation, acute injury, total activity, disuse, pain-driven behavior, and care can alter the same nodes. A mechanical contribution can be strong without being exclusive, and it can be low for the declared endpoint even when the structure carries load.

### BGB-S-1121 — named spinal, craniofacial, conduit, joint, and postural examples

**Preserved:** The archive deliberately built a wide inventory to test whether the framework transfers across anatomy. Chapter 23 should use one lead system and one transfer system, not delete the unselected examples or turn them into a disease list.

**Allocation:** Lumbar disc–endplate–facet, scoliosis, sagittal variation, spondylolisthesis, stenosis, herniation, Schmorl's nodes, hip, knee, ankle, foot, craniofacial, pelvic, and other candidates remain in the series inventory. Chapter 24 owns thoracic-outlet constraint. Chapters 25–26 own instability and mixed compensation. Later books own directional and corrective hypotheses.

### BGB-S-1216 — coordinated transfer, continuity, glide, congruence, timing, and interruption

**Preserved:** Internal distribution depends on how skeletal contact, passive restraint, active tension, sliding interfaces, compliant structures, pressure, timing, and neighboring segments cooperate. A disrupted or unavailable relation can change the distribution and increase demand elsewhere.

**Reconstruction:** There is no assumed literal packet of load migrating through one tissue sequence. Each handoff must identify the force, moment, contact, pressure, deformation, or other conjugate relation being discussed. *Interruption* means that a predicted transfer, contact, excursion, or support relation differs under a declared comparison; it does not by itself establish dysfunction, tissue injury, or symptoms.

All seven assigned source obligations are directly represented, reconstructed, or protected. Adjacent archive obligations remain visible where they help preserve context, but they do not acquire Chapter 23 authority merely by proximity.

## Structural recovery from the archive

The archive contributes twelve durable moves:

1. The body is a mechanical system as well as a biological one.
2. Global and regional geometry can change the internal question posed by the same apparent external demand.
3. The skeleton, joints, passive restraints, active structures, compliant systems, and pressure participate in distribution together.
4. External demand, joint or segment resultant, contact, local stress or strain, tissue response, finding, symptom, and outcome are different objects.
5. A local concentration may be invisible in the external load or gross posture.
6. A local concentration acquires meaning only relative to a receiver, reference distribution, dose history, state, and capacity.
7. Adaptation, ordinary variation, adequate recovery, and no detected consequence are as real as fatigue, damage, or degeneration.
8. Axial geometry is an important application because local and neighboring spinal structures share demand within a three-dimensional system.
9. Disc, endplate, facet, bone, ligament, muscle, and neural or compliant receivers cannot borrow one another's mechanism.
10. A second anatomical region is required to discover which relations transfer and which are spine-specific.
11. Findings and symptoms can correspond, diverge, precede, follow, or remain incidental to the modeled exposure.
12. The framework succeeds when it can assign low priority or no added value to the geometry-to-strain account in a selected case.

## Clean conceptual recovery

### Preserve directly

- geometry is mechanically consequential when it changes a declared line of action, moment, contact, support, motion, or boundary condition;
- similar external tasks can produce different internal distributions;
- similar visible geometries can also support different internal strategies;
- local stress and strain are receiver- and frame-specific;
- stiffness contrast, contact area, material heterogeneity, anisotropy, fluid behavior, rate, and boundary conditions can alter local fields;
- dose includes time organization and recovery;
- material and biological capacity changes through development, adaptation, fatigue, injury, inflammation, care, and recovery;
- a local exposure can be biologically consequential;
- a coherent exposure can exist without a symptom or diagnosis;
- axial and limb systems can be compared only through declared homologous relations; and
- a null or lower-priority mechanical interpretation is a complete result.

### Reconstruct rather than erase

- “misalignment funnels stress into vulnerable tissue” becomes a task-specific geometry → moment/contact/active-demand → local exposure proposition with competing internal solutions;
- “minor postural deviation accumulates into damage” becomes a prospective or reconstructed dose history with receiver, recovery, and disfavoring observations;
- “axial collapse disrupts the primary load path” becomes an operational description of curve, height, balance, translation, rotation, motion, support, or contact and a predicted distribution difference;
- “disc degeneration is mechanical failure” becomes several questions about initiation, progression, current load sharing, material state, finding, symptom, and function;
- “herniation reveals the direction of chronic force” becomes a morphology and history hypothesis requiring material, temporal, and alternative evidence;
- “facet degeneration is posterior overload” becomes a coupled disc–facet–endplate distribution question under selected tasks and states;
- “Schmorl's nodes are proof of failed axial load management” becomes an endplate morphology and exposure-history question with developmental, material, trauma, and incidental alternatives;
- “soft tissue buffered failed skeletal support” becomes a candidate change in active tension, passive restraint, contact, or form-receiving exposure;
- “imaging lagged the mechanism” becomes a comparison between the construct measured by the image and the unmeasured dynamic or historical relation; and
- “restore alignment to reverse disease” becomes a later intervention hypothesis whose target, mediator, population, comparator, safety, and outcome remain to be established.

### Protect forward

- a preferred whole-system configuration and a generic efficiency metric;
- skeletal-primary routing as a first-order Book II modeling assumption;
- posterior participation as a possibly necessary but insufficient feature of preferred organization;
- signed axial, expansive, and rotational modes and their local-frame transformations;
- TCM hard/soft, Six-Division, channel-pair, force-line, and Yin/Yang correspondences;
- breath, pressure, phase, hyoid, bandha, and torus-related organization;
- systemic respiratory, circulatory, lymphatic, neural, autonomic, metabolic, inflammatory, immune, energetic, and recovery consequences;
- condition-specific diagnostic, preventive, prognostic, and treatment propositions;
- measurement and modeling systems capable of reconstructing longitudinal routing; and
- assessment, self-observation, correction, yoga, tai chi, rehabilitation, and professional practice.

Protection means that Chapter 23 should leave explicit research hooks when its bounded case reaches one of these questions. It does not mean the chapter should hint that the later answer is already known.

## Lead-system decision

The evidence comparison uses these two candidate architectures:

| Criterion | Lumbar motion-segment lead | Limb-joint lead |
|---|---|---|
| Fidelity to archive | strongest; directly carries axial geometry, disc, endplate, facet, and degenerative program | indirect; requires explicit protection of the axial program |
| Inspectable external task | must be selected carefully because trunk tasks include distributed muscle and neighboring-segment contributions | often cleaner ground, apparatus, and kinematic measurements |
| Internal-distribution evidence | rich but model-dependent and sensitive to follower load, muscle force, posture, motion, material state, and boundary conditions | potentially more direct contact and instrumented-joint evidence, still model- and task-dependent |
| Tissue bridge | disc, endplate, and facet evidence must remain receiver-specific | cartilage, bone, tendon, or meniscus may offer clearer receiver studies |
| Ordinary variation and null | common imaging findings and variable symptom correspondence provide strong null tests | asymptomatic morphology, variable alignment, and intervention nulls can also constrain |
| Transfer lesson | limb comparison reveals whether the grammar generalizes beyond the spine | lumbar transfer reveals where a cleaner joint model fails in a coupled spinal system |
| Principal risk | model complexity may be narrated as direct measurement, or posture controversy may consume the chapter | technical cleanliness may evade the book's intended axial thesis |

**Decision:** Lead with the knee as a multistudy reconstruction, centered on weight-bearing knee mechanics and articular-cartilage contact and strain. Transfer the same relational questions to the lumbar motion segment, centered on task- and geometry-dependent disc/posterior-element sharing and disc stress or pressure. The knee evidence more clearly separates external force, calculated moment, internal resultant, compartment force, contact pressure, and intratissue strain; it also supplies direct nulls in which a geometric alteration has little detected effect or an external surrogate changes without the internal quantity changing as expected. The lumbar evidence is sufficiently rich for a consequential transfer but not for pretending that posture or one segment resultant uniquely specifies disc, facet, muscle, ligament, or neighboring-level exposure.

The principal knee null is that geometry-sensitive external surrogates and even controlled interface alterations do not always change the selected internal quantity in the predicted direction or by a detectable amount. The principal lumbar null is that the effect of curvature or position varies by spinal level, mechanical quantity, boundary condition, active strategy, and tissue state rather than producing one global load score. Healthy activity can also create localized cartilage strain, and non-neutral alignment or structural findings can occur without symptoms. These results constrain the chain without making geometry irrelevant.

## Reader-facing sequence

1. **Open with the same apparent demand and two geometries.** Show only the task, contacts, and observed configuration before asking what changed inside.
2. **Declare the system.** Name the bodies, joints, tissues, passive restraints, active contributors, supports, environment, frame, phase, and timescale.
3. **Create a living evidence-state table.** Mark every consequential entry observed, measured, calculated, modeled, reconstructed, hypothesized, or unknown.
4. **Draw the free body.** Establish force and moment balance without pretending that equilibrium identifies the internal distribution.
5. **Let geometry change the question.** Trace line of action, moment arm, curve or orientation, contact, active demand, and neighboring support.
6. **Resolve one receiver.** Cross from a segment or joint resultant into a tissue-specific stress, strain, pressure, contact, or deformation field.
7. **Test sensitivity.** Show how active control, support, material properties, reference state, and boundary conditions change the estimated field.
8. **Add history and capacity.** Introduce rate, repetition, variability, rest, recovery, adaptation, fatigue, injury, inflammation, development, and care only where they alter a named node.
9. **Follow the biological bridge.** Separate response, retained material change, remodeling, repair, degeneration, failure, and null.
10. **Ask about findings and symptoms last.** Preserve asymptomatic findings, symptoms without the selected finding, and change without structural normalization.
11. **Run the transfer test.** Carry the same questions into the second anatomical system and leave nontransferable cells blank.
12. **Compare competing histories.** Include morphology, development, biology, acute injury, total exposure, pain-driven strategy, measurement error, and incidental structure.
13. **State what would disfavor the account.** Permit model insensitivity, exposure within capacity, no correspondence, another stronger explanation, duplication, and no added value.
14. **Close with the full chain.** Display the supported links, weakest consequential arrow, unresolved cells, and exact scope of the mechanical contribution.

No fictional patient is required. A source-grounded task model is preferable to a diagnostic vignette unless the case evidence provides the relevant chronology and clinical observations.
