## Drafting decision

Chapter 2 should be written from its current brief and definitions pack, not assembled from old prose. The archive preserves the intended breadth of the project: rigid-body and deformable mechanics, pressure and contact, active and passive carriers, exposure history, three-dimensional motion, energy transfer, load paths, and embodied force-line observations. It does not yet supply a technically safe paragraph that can bypass the current control system.

The recovery rule is therefore generous about **questions and conceptual kernels** and conservative about **wording, equations, causal completion, and evidentiary status**:

- preserve an older idea when it identifies a real mechanical object, candidate carrier, modeling question, metaphor, or later-book hypothesis;
- reconstruct the expression from the current notation pack and authoritative sources;
- keep later preferred-configuration, posterior, Yin/Yang, TCM, and three-mode hypotheses in their assigned series layer rather than requiring Chapter 2 to prove or reject them; and
- quarantine a defective equation or overconfident sentence without treating that defect as a verdict on the underlying research question.

No archived passage is approved for verbatim promotion. Generated prose and generated Markdown mirrors have no independent evidentiary value.

## Approved conceptual recovery

The following archive intentions are preserved in the first-draft plan:

1. The body can be examined as a material and mechanical system without claiming that mechanics exhausts living function.
2. Mechanical description must include moments, distributed contact, deformable media, active forces, pressure systems, and time—not only external force vectors and rigid levers.
3. Geometry matters because it changes relations such as moment arms, contact, boundary conditions, and available distributions; geometry alone does not complete a tissue or medical outcome.
4. Equal external demand need not imply equal internal resultants, contact distributions, stress, strain, or biological response.
5. Magnitude, direction, rate, duration, repetition, sequence, variability, rest, state, and recovery remain distinguishable exposure dimensions rather than entries in one invented score.
6. A load path can be linear, branching, distributed, active, pressure-mediated, or time-varying, provided that every carrier and quantity is named.
7. Embodied continuity is preserved as phenomenological evidence and a hypothesis generator rather than either being erased or declared a measured anatomical cable.
8. Posterior organization, coupled-motion hypotheses, and TCM-motivated interpretations remain protected later-series research questions. Chapter 2 supplies the grammar by which they can eventually be stated and tested.

## Passage-level promotion checklist

A recovered kernel may enter the Chapter 2 draft only when the new passage records or makes recoverable:

1. the archive locator and `SAL-02-##` disposition;
2. the selected system, boundary, scale, task, phase, and frame;
3. the exact mechanical object and unit rather than the umbrella word *load*;
4. the carrier or interface represented;
5. whether the content is observed, measured, calculated, inferred, modeled, hypothesized, or embodied;
6. every assumption connecting an external demand to an internal exposure;
7. a scope sentence stating what the example does not establish;
8. the authoritative source controlling the rebuilt mechanics or biomechanics; and
9. confirmation that no generated derivative has been mistaken for corroboration.

If one of these items is load-bearing and unavailable, narrow the passage, use an idealized example, move the question to its later destination, or omit it. Do not fill the gap with archive confidence.
