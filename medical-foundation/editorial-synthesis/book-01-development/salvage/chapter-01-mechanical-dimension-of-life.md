## Drafting decision

Draft Chapter 1 as a new synthesis. Preserve the archive's originating proposition—that health and medicine have a mechanical dimension and that the body operates as one living mechanical system—but do not promote archived sentences as finished prose.

The archive is most useful here for intent, scale, systems breadth, and the ambition to connect mechanics with medicine. It is least reliable where generated text turns that ambition into universal alignment, systemic-cascade, root-cause, or correction claims. The controlled draft should recover the larger question without importing those completed conclusions.

The chapter's reader-facing center is:

> A living body cannot opt out of mechanics, but the presence of mechanics does not tell us how much explanatory weight it deserves in a particular health question.

## Approved conceptual recovery

The draft should recover these source intentions:

- mechanics belongs in a serious account of embodied life before a diagnosis or rehabilitation episode;
- skeletal, active, tensile, compliant, sliding, pressure-bearing, neural, and vascular structures coexist in one living system;
- biological state changes mechanical behavior, and mechanical exposure can change biological behavior;
- the same event admits whole-person, segment, interface, tissue, and cellular descriptions;
- mechanical history may be discrete, cumulative, compensatory, or partly reconstructed;
- the surrounding task and environment participate in the exposure; and
- a mechanical lens can complement rather than replace established biological and whole-person reasoning.

These ideas are recovered as questions, distinctions, and bounded propositions. Their later specificity is not erased; it is routed to the chapters that can carry it.

## Quarantined expressions and the questions they protect

| Archived expression | Why it cannot enter as written | Protected question retained |
|---|---|---|
| “The human body is a mechanical system before it is a chemical one” | Creates an unsupported ontological and causal priority | How can mechanical state be investigated alongside biochemical and other biological state? |
| “Health arises” from seamless force distribution or integration | Converts a useful systems intuition into a universal health condition | Which functions depend on which mechanical relations, under what conditions? |
| “Optimal alignment,” “mechanical neutrality,” or evenly distributed load | No task-, tissue-, population-, or objective-specific comparator is defined | How does geometry redistribute demand, and when does that redistribution matter? |
| “Misalignment” produces systemic inflammation, fatigue, autoimmunity, impaired flow, or hormonal imbalance | Skips tissue-specific mechanical, physiological, temporal, and clinical links | Which proposed downstream branches are mechanically intelligible, measurable, and supported for a selected context? |
| Fascia as the governing universal network or memory of strain | Mixes anatomy, metaphor, material behavior, sensation, and clinical consequence | Which tissues and interfaces carry, transmit, constrain, sense, or retain mechanical history at each scale? |
| Mechanics is largely absent from modern medicine | Ignores established biomechanics across medicine and rehabilitation | What incremental value might longitudinal body-wide routing add to existing local mechanical reasoning? |
| Correction of structure prevents or reverses chronic disease | Introduces intervention and outcome claims outside Book I | What research and clinical-development program would be required to test modification, prevention, or treatment claims? |
