## Drafting decision

The `new` outlines contain one of the project's most important negative mechanical intuitions: a change in whole-body or regional organization can redistribute demand, and the resulting demand can become concentrated at a smaller interface or tissue region. That local state may be modest in external appearance yet consequential through magnitude, direction, repetition, duration, material state, or lost recovery.

The intuition should be preserved in full. The first-draft problem is not that the archive cares too much about geometry. It is that several different scale transitions were compressed into one sentence:

```text
visible misalignment
    -> altered force path
    -> focal stress
    -> tissue strain
    -> degeneration, pain, or systemic effect
```

Chapter 12 expands the missing middle without deleting the program:

```text
declared task + observed geometry + external contacts
    -> calculated regional forces and moments
    -> modeled sharing and interface conditions
    -> measured or modeled local field
    -> time-structured exposure relative to a named receiver's capacity
    -> later tissue-specific and clinical bridges
```

The controlled chapter can make a strong claim that geometry matters and that small global differences can sometimes produce large local differences. It must specify which geometry, which local quantity, which model, and which boundary conditions. Stronger claims about preferred alignment, systemic cascades, disease, reversal, and correction remain protected in later books and the research inventory rather than being removed.

## Structural recovery from the archive

The useful original movement is:

```text
skeletal organization
    -> path and leverage
    -> altered distribution
    -> focal stress or strain
    -> compensation and cumulative consequence
```

The controlled Book I movement is more explicit:

```text
task and whole-system configuration
    -> regional resultant and moment
    -> internal sharing and contact
    -> local field
    -> exposure history relative to capacity
    -> possible adaptation, tolerance, compensation, material change, or failure
```

This is not a weaker thesis. It makes the exact authorial intuition inspectable: a globally subtle change can matter locally because geometry, contact, and material boundaries can concentrate a field. It also provides a place for the equally real outcomes in which the change is absorbed, redistributed, tolerated, or irrelevant.

## Clean conceptual recovery

### Preserve directly

- Skeletal and whole-body geometry can change force paths, moments, contacts, and internal demand.
- External force magnitude alone does not determine local tissue exposure.
- The same regional resultant can be distributed differently over an interface.
- Curvature, contact location, congruence, surface shape, thickness, stiffness contrast, and constraint can change local fields.
- A local stress, strain, pressure, traction, or deformation field can be highly nonuniform.
- Apparently small geometric differences can produce materially different local results in a bounded model.
- Repetition, duration, rate, direction, variability, recovery, and material state influence whether a local field matters.
- Compensation or alternate support can preserve function while changing where demand is carried.
- Concentration, constraint, and instability are distinct but potentially co-occurring failure families.
- A mechanical account should trace from global observation to local receiver rather than stopping at a lesion name.

### Reconstruct rather than erase

- “Misalignment creates focal stress” becomes a declared geometry → regional force/moment → interface condition → local-field comparison.
- “Small deviations cause large consequences” becomes a normalized finite sensitivity between one geometric input and one local output, with nulls and boundary conditions.
- “Joint stress” becomes a spatial contact, traction, pressure, stress, or strain field with a specified surface or volume.
- “More contact area protects the joint” becomes a comparison that also tracks force, field distribution, thickness, material state, and contact location.
- “A stiffness mismatch causes injury” becomes a local-field question at a graded or sharp boundary; normal enthesis architecture provides the positive comparison.
- “A spinal curve concentrates load” becomes a task- and level-specific model with active, passive, contact, and pressure alternatives.
- “Repeated concentration causes degeneration” becomes a tissue-specific dose–capacity–response research question in Chapter 19.
- “Asymmetry means misrouting” becomes one candidate explanation among ordinary variation, active strategy, external support, development, reverse causality, and measurement error.

### Protect forward

- one preferred whole-body geometry that minimizes a defined global cost or local concentration;
- signed axial, opening/rotation, and expansive modes;
- posterior participation as necessary but insufficient for preferred organization;
- systematic relations between geometric discontinuity and “force-line” discontinuity;
- topographical or configuration transitions across whole-body load fields;
- TCM, Six-Division, channel, and hard/soft correspondence beyond Book I's mechanical polarity;
- breath and pressure as possible organizers of geometric state;
- prediction of systemic vascular, neural, organ, metabolic, inflammatory, or autonomic consequences;
- reversal of accumulated tissue change through reorganization; and
- assessment, correction, cueing, training, ergonomics, and individualized practice.

## Chapter 12 assembly plan

1. **Open with one resultant and two local fields.** Use the subject-specific hip and an idealized contact representation under a matched loading case.
2. **Return to the external task.** Close a simple object-distance free body and identify what changes at the regional level.
3. **Cross the first scale.** Move from external demand and whole-segment geometry to regional forces and moments.
4. **Cross the second scale.** Move from the regional resultant to contact and a local field with measured, calculated, modeled, and unknown labels.
5. **Treat curvature as conditional geometry.** Use normal lumbar or global sagittal variation without defining one ideal curve.
6. **Show a healthy stiffness boundary.** Use the enthesis to demonstrate normal grading, compliance, and nonuniform strain.
7. **Formalize amplification carefully.** Use a normalized sensitivity only for named inputs, outputs, references, and conditions.
8. **Branch the outcomes.** Include no amplification, another localization site, successful active or environmental redistribution, tolerated concentration, and uncertainty.
9. **Add time without performing biology.** Carry the local field into Chapter 10's exposure/capacity record and stop before Chapter 19's response.
10. **Hand off to constraint.** Concentration is a nonuniform field; Chapter 13 asks what relation keeps a compliant structure deformed or prevents required excursion.

## Draft audit obligations

The later first-draft audit must verify that:

- redistribution and concentration cannot be mistaken for the same scale;
- every geometry enters a free-body, contact, or material model before local consequence;
- the hip example distinguishes specimen measurement, applied loading, calculated quantities, modeled fields, and unknown living consequences;
- the same-resultant comparison does not imply that two living hips were experimentally altered;
- average pressure is not presented as a true spatial field;
- curvature and asymmetry remain task-, level-, and model-specific;
- the enthesis appears as a normal graded boundary rather than a pathological mismatch;
- normalized sensitivity declares references, units, range, and failure conditions;
- asymptomatic morphology, active/environmental redistribution, reverse causality, and tolerated concentration appear inside the argument;
- no concentration becomes damage, pain, diagnosis, or treatment without a separate bridge;
- inherited equations and decompression claims are rebuilt rather than polished; and
- Chapter 13 receives a clean concentration-versus-maintained-constraint distinction.

## Authorization

This salvage pass authorizes a full Chapter 12 draft after Chapters 10 and 11 supply their stable vocabulary. It preserves the archive's geometry, alignment, leverage, local-strain, cumulative-demand, compensation, degeneration, and whole-system amplification programs. It rebuilds their intermediate mechanics and routes later consequences without deleting them.

The chapter's positive center is strong: geometry changes mechanics, and local fields can differ greatly even when the gross task or resultant looks similar. Its limiting center is equally precise: geometry does not reveal the field by itself, and a concentrated field is not yet damage, disease, pain, or a correction target.
