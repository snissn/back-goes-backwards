## Drafting decision

The `new` outlines contain one of the project's most important original intuitions: gross demand should resolve through mechanically capable hard roles so that soft roles remain free to receive form, glide, contain, expand, and regulate. The archive develops that intuition through soft-to-hard transfer, posterior continuity, fascia, sequencing, “load leakage,” joint interruption, compensation, corridor constraint, and systemic consequence.

Chapter 11 should preserve that entire program. It should not flatten it into standard remarks about kinetic chains, and it should not publish the archive's compressed sequence as if each step were already demonstrated:

```text
changed posture or lost posterior support
    -> interrupted hard route
    -> load leaks into soft or ventral tissue
    -> local strain and compensation
    -> pain, organ dysfunction, or systemic decline
    -> correction through posterior engagement and breath
```

The controlled recovery expands the middle without erasing the intended direction:

```text
defined task, phase, and external balance
    -> candidate route availability and allocation
    -> observed or modeled handoff, bypass, overload, or substitution
    -> named local exposure and current capacity
    -> alternatives and disfavoring observations
    -> later concentration, constraint, compensation, biological, and medical bridges
```

The original sentence remains the chapter's center:

> Load wants to travel through the hard body so that the soft body can remain soft.

Here, “wants” is a heuristic about geometry, contact, relative stiffness, active control, pressure, and available paths. Hard and soft are changing mechanical roles. The distinction is motivated by TCM hard/soft and Yin/Yang concepts but is not definitionally identical to them.

## Structural recovery from the `new` outline

The archive's intended sequence is not a list of isolated claims. It is an escalation:

```text
integrated mechanical architecture
    -> hard and soft division of labor
    -> posterior anatomy as a major candidate system
    -> force transfer and energy management
    -> continuity, glide, congruence, and timing
    -> interrupted or poorly matched handoff
    -> local soft-role exposure
    -> compensation and cumulative demand
    -> medical and practical relevance
```

The current book preserves that sequence across Chapters 3–21. Chapter 11 is the hinge between the positive architecture of Parts I–II and the adverse-mechanism analysis of Parts III–IV. If it becomes merely a disclaimer about uncertain internal forces, the original argument disappears. If it declares one route established, the later research program is preempted. The chapter must do both jobs: state the division-of-labor hypothesis affirmatively and make route accounts inspectable.

## Clean conceptual recovery

### Preserve directly

- Load transfer is distributed across changing skeletal, active, tensile, compliant, sliding, pressure-bearing, and external roles.
- Gross structural demand and local soft-role freedom are distinct mechanical concerns.
- The hard/soft polarity names a division of labor, not a permanent tissue taxonomy.
- The distinction is motivated by TCM hard/soft and Yin/Yang concepts.
- Continuity can be mechanically important even when no single anatomical cable spans the task.
- Handoffs can change carrier, direction, scale, role, or phase.
- Glide, congruence, timing, contact, pressure, and active organization can affect transfer.
- A task can succeed through more than one internal allocation.
- Substitution can preserve an endpoint.
- An altered route may change local exposure even when gross task demand is unchanged.
- Embodied force-line experience is valid as an observation with its carrier and quantity still open.
- Routing hypotheses should be compared with alternatives and measurements.

### Reconstruct rather than erase

- “Load transfers from fascia and muscle to ligament and tendon to bone and joint” becomes one candidate handoff sequence among several interacting active, tensile, skeletal, pressure, contact, and external routes.
- “Continuity” becomes task- and phase-specific availability rather than a single foot-to-skull cable.
- “Load leakage” becomes a proposed increase or persistence of deformation, active tension, pressure, restricted glide, altered contact, changed moment, or other poorly matched exposure in a named receiver.
- “Soft tissue remains soft” becomes retained freedom to yield, conform, glide, contain, expand, and recover as required; it does not mean zero load or zero stiffness.
- “Interruption” becomes loss of a declared route requirement and remains distinct from bypass or overload.
- “Compensation” becomes an alternate carrier or strategy preserving something valuable before its cost is judged.
- “The kinetic chain is disrupted” becomes a task, phase, boundary, allocation, and exposure account.
- “Fascial continuity transmits force globally” becomes a scale ladder from anatomy, to bounded measured interaction, to task-level importance, to whole-system hypothesis.
- “An internal force line can be felt” remains a direct embodied observation that can motivate but not replace measurement.

### Protect forward

- posterior participation as necessary but insufficient for preferred organization;
- downward axial, opening/external rotational, and outward expansive Yang directions;
- a formal efficiency relation comparing candidate global configurations;
- exact hard/soft–Yin/Yang and Six-Division correspondence;
- the KI1–PC8 embodied force-line anchor and gross limb-role projection;
- breath, pressure, phase, and bandha as possible organizers or drivers of routing;
- torus, shell/core, network, field, tensegrity, metastability, and topology models;
- diagnostic meaning of observed discontinuity or substitution;
- individual tests, cues, corrective sequences, and training; and
- disease-specific neural, vascular, organ, pain, inflammatory, and systemic effects.

## Chapter 11 assembly plan

1. **Recall one phase, not the full recurring task.** Compare two successful object placements with and without added support.
2. **Separate balance from allocation.** Show that external equilibrium does not identify a unique internal route.
3. **State the native hard/soft intuition.** Translate it positively into shape-preserving and form-receiving freedoms and state its TCM motivation.
4. **Define continuity as availability.** Permit sequences, networks, distributed fields, changing dominance, and uncertainty.
5. **Make handoff concrete.** Use muscle–tendon–bone, joint contact, elastic storage, pressure, and external support without teaching one tissue ladder.
6. **Install the null branch.** Show healthy tendon or cartilage accepting load and deforming normally.
7. **Operationalize leakage.** Require receiver, quantity, history, mismatch, alternative, and predicted consequence.
8. **Separate transfer states.** Compare interruption, bypass, overload, substitution, rerouting, mixed states, and unresolved allocation.
9. **Show endpoint equivalence.** Use motor abundance and a measured assistance example to show different allocations without an adverse verdict.
10. **Stop inference.** Use the instrumented-knee example to separate external measures from internal contact force.
11. **Require a route account.** End with task, boundary, carriers, allocation, exposure, capacity, alternatives, and disfavoring observations.
12. **Hand off to Chapter 12.** Ask how a redistributed demand becomes concentrated near a smaller geometry or material transition.
