## Recovery decision

The archive intends the book's mechanical model to become usable: a clinician, researcher, movement professional, or attentive reader should be able to notice demand, support, configuration, compensation, and possible load distribution rather than treating the body as a static image. It also contains posture screens, palpation ideas, home tests, corrective movements, breathing cues, ergonomic changes, clinical interpretations, and implied diagnostic rules. Those materials express a real practice program, but they exceed Book I's present evidence and contract.

Chapter 27 should recover the observational ambition without prematurely validating the practice system. Its product is an ordered set of questions, not a score or protocol:

1. What demand entered the declared system?
2. What configuration and paths were available?
3. How were shape-preserving and form-receiving roles divided?
4. Where, if anywhere, might local exposure have accumulated?
5. What, if anything, did the changed strategy preserve?
6. What changes with task, support, time, or state?
7. What can actually be measured, and where should the inference stop?

The order matters. It prevents a visible posture or symptom from being treated as an internal route and prevents a plausible internal route from being treated as a diagnosis. Each question may support, weaken, redirect, leave unresolved, or stop a particular link. Asking a mechanical question does not give mechanics priority by default.

The chapter will use one compact callback to the recurring object-transfer task and one comparison across the four Part V narratives. The scaffold must produce different outcomes in those cases. It is an intellectual tool for organizing evidence, not an implied assessment waiting to be used clinically.

## Source-obligation reconciliation

### BGB-S-1212 — observable markers of function, capacity, and compensation

**Preserved:** Movement, balance, transition, effort, fatigue, pain, recovery, and task variation can provide useful observations about a person's current relation to a task. Repeated observation across conditions may reveal modulation that a static snapshot misses.

**Reconstruction:** An observation must name the observer, task, phase, context, direction, and ambiguity. It is not automatically a validated measure. A measured difference is not automatically clinically meaningful. A symptom or visible configuration is not an internal exposure. Posterior participation may be observed only through bounded anatomy and a declared task; it cannot be inferred as globally deficient from appearance.

**Protected forward:** Candidate posterior markers, transition tasks, palpation observations, breath relations, force-line reports, and embodied distinctions remain in Chapter 29's measurement program and Book V's practice inventory.

### BGB-S-1220 — hard/soft routing as a clinical lens

**Preserved:** Mechanical reasoning improves when it asks which elements establish or preserve shape and which receive, accommodate, or redistribute deformation in a particular interaction. A form-receiving structure may sometimes be required to act as a substitute boundary, and that possibility can motivate a local exposure question.

**Controlled definition:** The vocabulary is motivated by TCM hard/soft and Yin/Yang concepts but is not definitionally identical to them. A hard role is relatively shape-preserving in the declared direction, phase, and timescale. A soft role is relatively form-receiving: it yields, conforms, accommodates, flows, or redistributes under surrounding conditions. Roles are relational, not permanent tissue classes.

**Reconstruction:** The question is not “Is this tissue hard or soft?” It is “What role did this element take in this event, relative to what, and how do we know?” Bone, muscle, tendon, ligament, cartilage, pressure, contact, and external support can divide roles differently across tasks. The series intuition that load should be carried through a shape-preserving organization so form-receiving structures can remain available is preserved as a hypothesis generator, not a diagnostic shortcut.

### BGB-S-1225 and BGB-S-1302 — clinical limits and no protocols

**Preserved:** The framework belongs alongside ordinary history, examination, imaging, laboratory reasoning, rehabilitation, pharmacology, surgery, and referral rather than replacing them. It should make its own scope and stopping conditions explicit.

**Boundary:** A portable question scaffold is not a validated screen. It has no score, threshold, sensitivity, specificity, referral branch, prognostic class, treatment selector, or home use. Research instrumentation is not automatically a clinical measure. A question that cannot be answered reliably should remain a research question.

**Protected forward:** Safety, contraindication, practice progression, professional training, self-observation, individualized correction, and clinical protocols remain active later-book work subject to validation and medical-coauthor gates.

### BGB-S-1313 — routing as context, not diagnosis

**Preserved:** A mechanical account can change what questions are asked, which variables are tracked, and what alternatives are considered. It can be relevant even when it is not the primary cause of symptoms or disease.

**Reconstruction:** The scaffold should update individual links rather than issue a global mechanical verdict. Task modulation updates the proposition that the task or state matters; it does not prove original cause. Imaging noncorrespondence identifies an evidence gap; it does not validate a hidden route. A response to support or altered task can guide inquiry without selecting treatment.

### BGB-S-1609 — recognize compensation before correction

**Preserved:** A changed strategy often protects something: task completion, balance, confidence, a painful region, exposure, participation, or recovery. Understanding that job precedes any judgment about cost.

**Reconstruction:** A changed strategy may be useful, neutral, costly, symptom-led, transient, persistent, or unrelated. Cost requires a measured exposure or outcome, a denominator, a time course, and alternatives. Chapter 27 asks whether anything was preserved and what changed before calling the strategy compensation; it does not tell the reader how to undo it.

## Clean conceptual recovery

### Preserve directly

- begin with the task, environment, history, and declared system;
- keep whole-person, segment, joint, tissue, and interface scales distinct;
- configuration constrains mechanical possibilities without uniquely specifying internal distribution;
- contacts, supports, phase, direction, and reference frame matter;
- more than one viable strategy can satisfy the same external task;
- hard/soft roles are relational, directional, phase-specific, and exchangeable;
- posterior structures are substantial anatomical contributors without being the expected answer to every task;
- local exposure requires a tissue or interface, quantity, dose, and state;
- measured, calculated, modeled, inferred, reconstructed, hypothesized, and embodied information are different;
- the term *compensation* is earned by first establishing what, if anything, a changed strategy preserved;
- modulation updates a particular hypothesis rather than proving origin;
- alternatives and nulls enter before the conclusion;
- uncertainty changes the next question;
- an early unsupported link stops downstream inheritance of confidence;
- mechanics can be relevant to function while remaining secondary to symptom origin or diagnosis; and
- the scaffold may add little in a particular case.

### Reconstruct rather than erase

- “observe healthy alignment” becomes describe configuration, task, frame, phase, and ambiguity;
- “feel whether load enters the bones” becomes identify possible carriers and develop a validated measurement program;
- “posterior chain engagement” becomes bounded contribution of named structures under a declared task;
- “soft tissue overload” becomes tissue-specific stress, strain, pressure, deformation, activation, excursion, friction, or recovery question;
- “kinetic-chain compensation” becomes a measured relation across declared scales;
- “mechanical homeostasis” becomes a viable dynamic range for a named variable, not one posture;
- “the symptom changes with posture, so it is mechanical” becomes task or state modulation with simultaneous variables and alternatives;
- “the scan missed the mechanism” becomes a limit of the available measurement and a possible research question;
- “clinical test” becomes candidate observation until reliability, validity, utility, population, and purpose are established;
- “correct the compensation” becomes determine what, if anything, the changed strategy preserves, whether cost is demonstrated, and what evidence an intervention would require; and
- “root cause” becomes a ranked contribution to a specified endpoint and period.

### Protect forward

- preferred configuration and a generic efficiency metric;
- posterior participation as possibly necessary but insufficient for the later preferred-state hypothesis;
- skeletal-primary routing and local orthotropy or anisotropy models;
- axial, rotational, and expansive signed modes;
- TCM Six-Division and organ-channel mapping;

### Quarantine as publication moves

- posture or alignment supplies an internal route or diagnosis;
- posterior participation is always preferable or visibly deficient;
- hard tissues should always bear load and soft tissues should never bear load;
- one task has one normal solution;
- task modulation proves cause;
- an asymmetry establishes a kinetic chain;
- palpation reveals hidden force paths without validation;
- a research measure is clinically interpretable because it can be collected;
- more mechanical detail necessarily improves a decision;
- an unknown variable supports the project hypothesis;
- the seven questions form a clinical screen; and
- a mechanical explanation implies a mechanical treatment.

- self-tests, posture grading, palpation instructions, movement cues, breath cues, home corrections, exercise selection, or treatment sequencing;
- checklist scores, acronyms implying validation, thresholds, red flags, referral algorithms, or diagnostic classes;

## The seven-question scaffold

### 1. What demand entered the system?

Name the task, environment, contacts, supports, external loads, sequence, history, duration, repetition, rate, direction, variability, assistance, recent change, and recovery. Include access, disability, work control, and social constraints when they alter what occurred or which options existed. Replace labels such as “sedentary,” “active,” “weak,” or “poor posture” with inspectable exposure descriptions.

**Stop:** If the task and system cannot be described, do not assign an internal route.

### 2. What configuration and paths were available?

Describe what moved, what remained relatively fixed, which contacts changed, what neighboring regions contributed, and which frames or phases matter. List more than one mechanically viable strategy. Treat visible route lines as hypotheses unless carriers and quantities are measured.

**Stop:** If a visible configuration cannot be linked to internal distribution under explicit assumptions, keep the exposure unknown.

### 3. How were hard and soft roles divided?

Ask what established shape, actuated, restrained, transmitted, redirected, yielded, conformed, glided, contained, expanded, buffered, or regulated pressure. Apply each role to a relation, direction, phase, and timescale. Include posterior, anterior, lateral, deep, active, tensile, compressive, compliant, and pressure-mediated support as candidates rather than moral categories.

**Stop:** If roles cannot be operationalized, retain the vocabulary as an organizing analogy rather than a measured claim.

### 4. Where, if anywhere, might local exposure have accumulated?

Name the tissue or interface and the possible force, moment, contact, pressure, stress, strain, deformation, excursion, friction, rate, repetition, and recovery. Add material state, development, fatigue, inflammation, prior injury, health, and capacity. State whether the quantity is measured, calculated, modeled, inferred, or unknown.

**Stop:** If no valid bridge connects configuration to the local quantity, do not infer response, symptom, or disease.

### 5. What, if anything, did the changed strategy preserve?

Ask first whether anything continued or was protected, then what changed: tone, bracing, range, timing, balance, side-to-side transfer, effort, breathing, external assistance, task choice, or avoidance. Compare immediate protection, successful adaptation, persistent substitution, symptom-led behavior, neutral change, and irrelevance. Use *compensation* only when the proposed preserved function and changed strategy are both specified.

**Stop:** Do not call the change costly without a relevant exposure or outcome across a declared time.

### 6. What changes with task, support, time, or state?

Compare conditions only after naming everything changed simultaneously. A within-person change may raise mechanical relevance, expose a capacity limit, reflect attention or expectation, or remain nonspecific. Nonchange and paradoxical change are legitimate observations.

**Stop:** Do not promote modulation to origin, diagnosis, or treatment selection.

### 7. What can actually be measured, and what remains uncertain?

Separate ordinary observation, validated clinical measure, research measure, calculation, model output, retrospective reconstruction, hypothesis, and embodied report. Record reliability, validity, responsiveness, meaningful difference, confounds, missing periods, alternatives, nulls, and whether the result changes the decision under discussion.

**Stop:** End the route when a required observation is unavailable, unreliable, nonunique, clinically secondary, or outside scope. “Unknown” preserves the question; it does not support the answer.

## Required examples and comparison

### Recurring object-transfer callback

Use the person moving an ordinary object between supports and carrying it several steps. The callback should do only four jobs:

1. show how the system boundary changes from person-plus-object to person, segment, joint, and tissue;
2. show that the same visible task admits multiple strategies;
3. distinguish external observation from internal measurement and model; and
4. demonstrate one early stopping point.

It is not a lifting lesson, movement screen, or invitation to imitate a technique.

### Four narratives, four outcomes

The comparison must preserve the accepted Chapter 23–26 evidence state:

- **geometry and localized strain:** a stronger bounded geometry-to-local-exposure relation, with symptoms and longer outcomes remaining separate;
- **maintained thoracic-outlet constraint:** real anatomy and endpoint, but a reconstructed route whose weakest arrow remains decisive;
- **recurrent ankle instability:** a state-transition model with diverging material, perceived, task, and recurrence measures;
- **mixed chronic presentation:** a chronology that may yield a modest, reverse, low-priority, or unresolved mechanical account.

The scaffold is useful only if those cases do not converge on one verdict.

## Evidence-development obligations

The Chapter 27 evidence packet should be compact and method-centered. It must support:

1. the distinctions among observation, measurement, screening, assessment, diagnosis, and clinical utility;
2. reliability, validity, responsiveness, meaningful change, generalizability, and incremental value;
3. causal-order discipline and the role of alternatives, nulls, and stopping rules;
4. the limits of posture, visual observation, palpation, task modulation, and research instrumentation when relevant;
5. the mechanical correctness of the object-transfer boundary callback;
6. the accepted source limits of each Part V comparison; and
7. the possibility that a richer scaffold increases burden or confirmation bias without improving decisions.

No new disease bridge is needed. Condition-specific claims must be reused without expansion from their owning evidence packets.
