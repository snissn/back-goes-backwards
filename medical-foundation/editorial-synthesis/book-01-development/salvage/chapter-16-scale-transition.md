**Status:** controlled archive reconciliation as of 2026-07-11; use with the [Chapter 16 evidence packet](../evidence/chapter-16-scale-transition.md), [Chapter 16 brief](../chapter-briefs/BGB-U-0016-scale-transition.md), [source-coverage registry](../../series-synthesis/source-coverage.md), and [quarantine registry](../../series-synthesis/quarantine.md); not reader prose

## Salvage decision

The archive's whole-system ambition belongs in the project and can remain visible in Book I. Its underlying questions are not removed:

- Can a local or regional mechanical change recruit a wider organization?
- Can repeated compensation become a stable task-level starting state?
- Can a coherent whole-body organization return demand to a local receiver?
- Can mechanical reorganization alter tone, perception, pressure, flow, excursion, tissue response, effort, recovery, or reserve?
- Can the body undergo a consequential configuration transition that ordinary local comparisons fail to represent?

The required editorial change is to replace one compressed systemic cascade with a bidirectional multiscale map and several independent physiological branches. The change preserves more of the original program than a generic warning about medical overclaim because it records exactly what would make each proposed relation stronger.

The clean reader-facing center is:

> Mechanical organization can cross scales in both directions. A local change may recruit neighboring and task-level strategies; a task-level organization may concentrate demand locally. Sensory/control, pressure/flow/excursion, tissue-response, and energetic/regulatory consequences remain separate candidate branches. Each branch requires its own object, intermediate mechanism, chronology, measurement, alternatives, and null result.

## What is promoted into Chapter 16

### Bidirectional mechanical scale transition

Promote the full three-direction map:

```text
local -> regional
regional -> repeatable task-level organization
task-level organization -> local exposure
```

The arrows can form feedback, but each retains a declared quantity and alternative. The reader should see a positive knee-to-lumbar model comparison, a gait-restriction internal-force null, an interlimb arm-swing comparison, and a split-belt/handrail task-level comparison.

### A real whole-body reorganization question

Promote the question rather than hiding it. Repeated coupling among regions may produce a task-level organization that is not adequately described by one local variable. The publication candidate should call this *whole-task organization* or *multiregion reorganization*. The future program may test field continuity, sharp gradients, reversals, cross-midline routing, or configuration transitions. *Topology* remains deferred until a formal invariant and transformation class exist.

### Separate downstream branches

Promote four visible branches:

1. sensory and control;
2. pressure, flow, and excursion, subdivided by physical system;
3. tissue response; and
4. energetic and regulatory cost or benefit.

Support for one branch cannot be borrowed by another. Chapter 16 should include positive, null, reverse, beneficial, and common-cause paths.

### Cost can be a benefit or a reversal

Promote a robust energetic section. Arm-swing and exoskeleton experiments demonstrate that mechanical organization can change measured metabolic cost, that technological assistance can improve it, and that one higher tested assistance magnitude can reverse benefit. This gives *efficiency* operational content without pretending that Book I has found one generic efficiency metric or a universal help-to-hindrance threshold.

## What is named but remains non-load-bearing

- a preferred global configuration;
- posterior support as a necessary but insufficient later candidate for that configuration;
- a force-, moment-, stress-, or strain-field discontinuity as a possible marker of configuration transition;
- fascia as one possible long-range mechanical participant among several;
- interoception and autonomic regulation as separately measurable downstream questions;
- lymphatic and organ-excursion hypotheses awaiting system-specific evidence;
- chronic fatigue, diffuse pain, and resilience as possible contexts for research; and
- the possibility that several branches interact in a later integrated model.

Naming these ideas protects authorial intent. None is required for the Chapter 16 argument to succeed.

## What is quarantined from Book I reader prose

The following generated or archive formulations remain in provenance records but should not be stated as established:

- “fascia is the primary substrate of systemic regulation”;
- “misalignment directly impairs circulation, lymphatic drainage, neural signaling, immunity, or autonomic function”;
- “subtle postural deviation creates global physiological decline”;
- “chronic compensation depletes a universal energy reserve”;
- “diffuse pain or fatigue reveals hidden load misrouting”;
- “posterior loading restores flow or regulation”;
- “pressure change necessarily improves circulation”;
- “anatomical continuity proves force transmission across the whole chain”;
- “one local lesion reorganizes the whole body”; and
- any diagnostic, preventive, corrective, or treatment instruction.

Quarantine is not deletion. These propositions can re-enter a later publication layer if the intermediate relations, evidence, scope, and review justify them.

## Reader-facing sequence

1. Open with one local perturbation that changes a neighboring or distant regional measure.
2. Pair it immediately with a restriction experiment in which the proposed internal exposure remains relatively similar.
3. Move from interlimb coupling to task-level aggregation and show how hand contact changes the system boundary.
4. Reverse the arrow: a whole-task organization can focus demand locally only through the Chapter 12 bridge.
5. Introduce the four downstream branches in separate panels.
6. Develop sensory/control with a positive and null pair.
7. Compare arterial waveform, venous area, and nerve excursion only to demonstrate different obligations, not shared pathology.
8. Defer tissue response explicitly to Chapter 19.
9. Give energetic cost a substantive positive, nonlinear, beneficial, and reversal treatment.
10. Close with the branch registry and hand Part IV unanswered but testable medical questions.
