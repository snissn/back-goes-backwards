## Drafting decision

The `new` Section 2 outline contains a strong and coherent Book I sequence that should be preserved: define the posterior system, establish its anatomy, show its mechanical contributions in ordinary tasks, distinguish energy-management functions, connect those functions to performance and fatigue questions, and only then ask about health, failure, evolution, and application. Earlier synthesis compressed too much of that progression. Chapters 7–9 now restore it.

Chapter 8 owns the middle step. It should recover the archive's confidence that posterior structures are mechanically consequential in standing, walking, lifting, carrying, transitions, locomotion, propulsion, deceleration, stabilization, and elastic energy management. It should not neutralize that thesis into “all structures matter.” The defensible transformation is to make posterior importance task-, phase-, quantity-, and model-specific while keeping it substantial.

The archive's stronger claims are not discarded. They are separated by the work needed to support them:

1. task mechanics enter Chapter 8;
2. population, development, pregnancy, disability, illness, aging, and reserve enter Chapter 9;
3. failed handoff and adverse exposure enter Parts III–IV;
4. disease- and symptom-specific consequences enter worked narratives and the research inventory;
5. preferred posterior organization enters Book II;
6. Yang, channel, and Six-Division correspondence enter Book III;
7. breath as an organizing driver enters Book IV; and
8. tests, cues, training, correction, and practice enter Book V.

This is an inclusive recovery, not an ablation. The archive's questions remain active; Chapter 8 stops at the strongest layer it can presently carry.

## Structural recovery from the `new` outline

The archive's original movement can be retained without its universal causal language:

```text
posterior anatomy (Chapter 7)
    -> task-specific moments and support
    -> energy absorption / active work / storage / return / dissipation
    -> standing / sitting / gait / balance / object transfer
    -> performance / fatigue / stability / injury-exposure questions
    -> evolutionary explanation as a bounded hypothesis
    -> whole-body and lifespan integration (Chapter 9)
```

This sequence is more faithful than either of the two common editorial failures:

- reducing the posterior chain to one brief example because whole-chain clinical transmission is uncertain; or
- retaining “primary,” “optimal,” and “protective” language at every scale before the intermediate mechanics and outcomes have been established.

The controlled chapter can state that posterior structures are major contributors under named conditions. It need not decide whether the same structures are necessary or preferred in the all-Yang organization.

## Clean conceptual recovery

### Preserve directly

- Posterior structures are among the body's largest and most mechanically consequential active and tensile systems.
- Gluteal, hamstring, calf–Achilles, spinal-extensor, scapular, and related structures can generate or resist moments and change joint contact in defined tasks.
- Posterior muscle–tendon systems participate in propulsion, braking, controlled lowering, storage, return, redirection, and stabilization.
- Standing and sitting involve changing contacts, support, active state, and duration rather than one static posture label.
- Walking and balance recovery are whole-body, three-dimensional events with substantial posterior participation.
- Lifting and carrying change with object position, speed, support, asymmetry, assistance, environment, and prior state.
- Performance, fatigue, stability, and injury exposure are all legitimate research objects.
- Bipedalism, running, endurance, and posterior specialization form a legitimate evolutionary research program.

### Reconstruct rather than erase

- “The posterior chain is the primary force pathway” becomes “selected posterior structures make major task- and phase-specific contributions whose magnitude and alternatives can be measured or modeled.”
- “Posterior loading absorbs shock” becomes an explicit energy account separating active negative work, tendon storage and return, dissipation, redirection, and boundary crossing.
- “Healthy standing or sitting requires posterior engagement” becomes a comparison of contact, support, duration, motion, active state, and task demand without a posture verdict.
- “The posterior chain stabilizes gait” becomes phase-specific support, propulsion, swing, foot placement, ankle, trunk, arm, sensory, and pressure relations.
- “A strong posterior chain prevents injury” becomes separate performance, fatigability, stability, exposure, and injury-outcome questions.
- “Evolution designed the posterior chain for upright life” becomes a trait/function/evidence/alternative framework across comparative anatomy, fossils, living biomechanics, development, and ecology.

### Protect forward

- posterior participation as necessary but not sufficient for the preferred configuration;
- an efficiency functional that might rank whole-body organizations;
- posterior/Yang, exterior/interior, and hard/soft correspondences;
- breath-pressure coordination as a possible organizer of posterior support;
- population-specific technique, training, assessment, and retraining;
- posterior capacity and exposure as possible predictors of specific outcomes; and
- historical or clinical cases in which reorganizing posterior participation changes symptoms or function.

## Chapter 8 assembly plan

1. **Open with two successful transfers.** Same object and endpoint, different geometry and support; add one assisted configuration.
2. **Close one free body.** Show how a posterior contribution enters a declared force/moment balance without claiming a unique muscle solution.
3. **Separate energy verbs.** Use one lowering, placement, gait, or landing sequence to distinguish active work, storage, return, dissipation, and boundary crossing.
4. **Turn posture into support history.** Compare standing, supported sitting, unsupported sitting, leaning, and transition through contacts and duration.
5. **Use gait as the strongest positive case.** Name substantial gluteal, hamstring, plantar-flexor, and Achilles contributions alongside vasti, iliopsoas, lateral balance, arms, sensory control, and skeletal alignment.
6. **Use perturbation as the anti-cue case.** Show stepping, ankle, trunk, and external-support strategies rather than “engage the posterior chain.”
7. **Complete and retire the recurring task.** Trace all phases, variants, assistance, placement, and aftermath; do not replay it in later Parts.
8. **Break the outcome ladder.** Give performance, fatigue/fatigability, stability, and injury exposure separate measures and unsupported arrows.
9. **End evolution with open comparisons.** Trait, function, evidence class, alternative, tradeoff, and bounded prediction.
10. **Hand Chapter 9 a conditional result.** Posterior support is substantial; its whole-body role varies with anatomy, development, capacity, environment, disability, illness, and life stage.
