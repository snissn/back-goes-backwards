## Drafting decision

The `new` outlines preserve a substantial and important program: a body can complete a task while small relation losses, mistimed handoffs, repeated corrections, or persistent bracing change the mechanical history. The archive also compresses that program into a deterministic sequence:

```text
local instability or mistiming
    -> disrupted proprioception
    -> global bracing and inefficient compensation
    -> escalating tension and energetic cost
    -> pain, degeneration, recurrent injury, or systemic dysfunction
    -> correction through posterior engagement and breath
```

Chapter 14 should not delete that sequence. It should unpack it into independently testable links:

```text
declared task relation and perturbation
    -> measured maintenance, departure, or recovery
    -> measured timing and strategy
    -> correction or bracing as a potentially useful response
    -> repeated control-event history
    -> separately tested sensory, local-exposure, fatigue, pain, and biological branches
    -> alternatives, nulls, and disfavoring results
```

This keeps the author's original concern—small control failures and compensatory work can accumulate—while allowing variability, learning, protection, disability, environmental support, and pain-led change to remain legitimate branches.

## Structural recovery from the `new` outline

The archive's intended logic is broader than one chapter:

```text
three-dimensional architecture and hard/soft division of labor
    -> continuity and timing of transfer
    -> local relation loss or mistimed participation
    -> correction and bracing
    -> repeated demand and compensation
    -> changed local exposure, sensory state, and effort
    -> possible tissue, symptom, or systemic consequence
    -> later observation and practice
```

The current series preserves this sequence across Chapters 3–29 and Books II–V. Chapter 14 occupies a precise hinge: it converts *control failure* into an observable time series. If the chapter becomes only a warning against overinterpretation, the original thesis disappears. If it publishes the full cascade, timing evidence becomes a proxy for medical causation. The first draft must state the positive mechanism clearly while stopping at each missing bridge.

## Clean conceptual recovery

### Preserve directly

- Local or regional control can matter even when materials remain intact.
- Stability depends on maintaining or recovering a relation under a task, not on stillness.
- Timing, sequence, phase, and recruitment relation can matter independently of gross strength.
- A task endpoint can conceal repeated catching, correction, or alternate allocation.
- A small corrective event can become mechanically relevant through repetition when a named cumulative quantity changes.
- Bracing and co-contraction can be protective strategies that alter stiffness.
- Protection can preserve a valuable function before any cost is judged.
- Static shape and one trial can miss the defining control event.
- Sensory state, pain, expectation, fatigue, learning, and environment may change control.
- Whole-body participation can change when one region cannot perform its prior role.
- Measurement must include perturbation, timing, repetition, recovery, and alternatives.

### Reconstruct rather than erase

- “Instability” becomes failure to maintain or recover a named task relation within a declared tolerance.
- “Discoordination” becomes an independently measured timing, sequence, or phase relation linked to an exposure or outcome.
- “Micro-motion causes microtrauma” becomes a repeated-event record that requires a separate local exposure and biological bridge.
- “Deep stabilizers fail and global muscles compensate” becomes one allocation hypothesis among several, requiring task-, muscle-, and time-specific evidence.
- “Bracing is inefficient” becomes a comparison among relation preserved, internal force, effort, adaptability, and persistence.
- “Proprioceptive confusion escalates tension” becomes a branching sensory–control hypothesis with positive, null, reverse, and competing directions.
- “Timing failure disrupts the kinetic chain” becomes event marker, participant timings, relation, correction, and measured consequence.
- “Systemic consequences of local instability” becomes a scale-transition research program rather than one established cascade.

### Protect forward

- particular spinal, SI-joint, rib, trigger-point, or other diagnoses;
- local instability as the initiator of degeneration, recurrent injury, or chronic pain;
- proprioception, interoception, or altered neural signaling as the principal driver of chronic symptoms;
- chronic bracing as a body-wide energetic, autonomic, immune, or regulatory cost;
- posterior organization as the preferred solution to instability or discoordination;
- signed axial, rotational, and expansive coordination modes;
- traditional channel and Six-Division coordination;
- breath or bandha as the driver of phase and pressure organization;
- assessment thresholds, correction cues, stabilization, retraining, and graded practice; and
- disease-specific prevention, reversal, or treatment claims.

## Chapter 14 assembly plan

1. **Open after a successful endpoint.** Show one hypothetical perturbation recovered through a visible catch, then repeat it.
2. **Define the task relation.** Name support, contact, translation, rotation, or recovery without one ideal path.
3. **Separate instability from movement amount.** Include capable mobility and active stillness.
4. **Separate timing from material failure.** Use event-locked EMG, force, moment, and motion without collapsing them.
5. **Make variability affirmative.** Show several joint strategies preserving one task variable.
6. **Treat correction as a time series.** Record event, departure, response, recovery, and history; do not call it damage.
7. **Treat bracing first as a solution.** Show stiffness benefit and possible force/cost branch together.
8. **Build the sensory loop with branches.** Include pain-led change, null sensory findings, instruction, fear, fatigue, and inflammation.
9. **Compare matched states.** Capable variability, task-specific instability, discoordination, useful bracing, and unresolved evidence.
10. **Require dynamic disfavoring tests.** Alter perturbation, direction, support, repetition, and recovery.
11. **Stop before biological causality.** Deliver a defined control-event exposure to Chapter 19.
12. **Hand the successful strategy to Chapter 15.** Compensation begins as preservation, not failure.

## Draft audit obligations

The later first-draft audit must verify that:

- instability always names a task, relation, perturbation or demand, and recovery criterion;
- mobility, laxity, asymmetry, pain, variability, and imaging do not stand in for instability;
- discoordination requires timing or sequence evidence and a declared consequence;
- EMG, force, moment, motion, sensation, and inferred control remain separate objects;
- repeated correction names a measured event and history, not presumed microtrauma;
- capable variability and learning are substantive counterexamples;
- bracing appears first as a potentially useful strategy;
- any bracing cost names its denominator, exposure, time, and alternative;
- every sensory-loop arrow remains conditional, reversible, and separately measurable;
- pain-led, instruction-led, fatigue-led, inflammation-led, and null branches remain visible;
- disabled, aging, trained, assisted, and anatomically variable strategies are not deficit-coded;
- static appearance and one trial cannot carry the chapter's inference;
- no diagnosis, injury attribution, treatment, stabilization rule, test, cue, or protocol appears; and
- Chapter 15 inherits a successful protective strategy rather than a pathology label.

## Authorization

This salvage pass authorizes a full Chapter 14 draft once its evidence packet has passed independent review and Chapter 13 supplies the immediate seam. It preserves the `new` outline's instability, timing, sequencing, repeated correction, proprioception, bracing, compensation, energetic, posterior, breath, clinical, and practical ambitions. It redistributes their burden of proof across the series rather than deleting them.

The drafting center is constructive: living control is mechanical work performed through time. A completed task can contain relation loss, recovery, correction, and changing strategy that deserve measurement. The chapter succeeds when it makes that hidden history inspectable while showing with equal clarity that variability, bracing, and repeated correction can be capable forms of adaptation.
