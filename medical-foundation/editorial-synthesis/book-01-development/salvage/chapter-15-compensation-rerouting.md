## Drafting decision

The `new` outlines contain a central authorial sequence that must remain available to the book and series:

```text
low-grade deviation or constraint
    -> successful, initially silent compensation
    -> changed route and repeated demand
    -> changed starting state, reserve, or capacity
    -> possible delayed, distal, or systemic consequence
```

The sequence was written in anticipation of later research, not as a claim that every arrow was already demonstrated. Its useful structure should therefore be preserved generously. Chapter 15 does not delete “silent compensation,” “new baseline,” “cumulative demand without injury,” “distal consequence,” or “kinetic-chain rerouting.” It turns them into a branching chronology whose links can later be supported, softened, revised, or rejected individually.

The controlled version begins one step earlier—with the function the compensation preserves—and keeps several futures open:

```text
present demand, constraint, state, and valued function
    -> alternate support, route, timing, excursion, stiffness, or behavior
    -> successful task / partial success / no meaningful change
    -> recovery / learning / persistent useful strategy / replacement / repeated changed exposure
    -> adequate capacity / increased capacity / reduced options / named receiver exposure / unresolved state
    -> no consequence / functional benefit / transient response / later tissue or clinical bridge
```

This is a more complete expression of the original intent. It preserves the possibility that repeated compensatory routing can create real downstream cost while refusing to treat compensation itself as defect, concealed pathology, or proof of a remote cause.

## Structural recovery from the archive

The original material makes five important moves:

1. **Compensation can hide a changed mechanical organization because function continues.**
2. **A repeated solution can become the starting point for later tasks.**
3. **The later exposure may differ in location from the initiating constraint.**
4. **A meaningful history can accumulate without one dramatic injury.**
5. **The visible problem at the end of the history may not identify the origin.**

All five belong in the project. The controlled reconstruction adds the missing comparisons:

- task success may be achieved through several equally capable routes;
- compensation may be consciously chosen, externally assisted, or disability-specific;
- repeated exposure may build skill or capacity rather than consume it;
- a changed visible pattern may not change the proposed internal force;
- pain or disease may precede and create the strategy;
- a distal finding may share a common cause with the initiating observation; and
- a proposed chain may remain unresolved after appropriate measurement.

These branches do not neutralize the negative hypothesis. They make it possible to test.

## Clean conceptual recovery

### Preserve directly

- Compensation begins by preserving something valuable.
- The same task endpoint can be produced through different contacts, routes, timings, joint work, pressures, and supports.
- A strategy can remain unnoticed when ordinary function continues and no symptom is reported.
- Repetition makes a route part of an exposure history.
- A prior strategy can influence the starting organization expressed during a later task.
- External assistance and environmental design can become part of the load path.
- Acute fatigue, pain, constraint, anatomy, disability, skill, instruction, and preference can all change strategy.
- A route change can redistribute demand between limbs, regions, joints, muscles, devices, and the environment.
- A meaningful cumulative history does not require a discrete traumatic event.
- Delayed, distal, and contralateral hypotheses require explicit chronology and intermediate handoffs.
- Origin, adaptation, maintenance, consequence, and incidental context are distinct causal roles.
- Compensation may remain useful, become trained, resolve, be replaced, narrow options, or produce a measurable cost.
- In disability contexts, compensation is an optional analytical description, not a required identity or deficit label; use person-preferred language when known.

### Reconstruct rather than erase

- “Compensation masks dysfunction” becomes a task that remains successful while selected internal or behavioral variables change; hidden pathology is not inferred.
- “A new baseline becomes entrenched” becomes context- and time-specific retention, recurrence, or transfer demonstrated by repeated measures.
- “Compensation is energetically costly” becomes a comparison with a stated denominator: metabolic cost, mechanical work, activation, time, effort, recovery, or another selected outcome.
- “Minor imbalance accumulates into disease” becomes a link-by-link history through route, dose, receiver, capacity, biological response, and whole-person alternatives.
- “The other side takes the load” becomes measured contacts and forces, calculated moments or work, modeled internal sharing, and an explicit null possibility.
- “Distal symptoms reveal the original dysfunction” becomes a higher-burden hypothesis with chronology, spatial handoffs, covariation, manipulation, and reverse causality.
- “Kinetic-chain compensation” becomes a bounded multi-contact route rather than one anatomical cable or self-explaining chain.
- “Restore the original pattern” becomes an open comparison among persistent successful assistance, spontaneous replacement, chosen goals, and task-specific alternatives.

### Protect forward

- departure from a preferred all-Yang configuration as the initiator of compensatory rerouting;
- posterior participation as necessary but insufficient for preferred whole-body organization;
- axial, opening/rotational, and expansive signed modes;
- force-line discontinuity and configuration-transition models;
- Six-Division, channel, and TCM correspondence;
- breath and pressure as drivers of compensation or reorganization;
- autonomic, immune, endocrine, inflammatory, metabolic, organ, or whole-system consequences;
- specific disease, pain, degeneration, and imaging-lag claims;
- reversal of retained patterns or tissue changes through reorganization; and
- self-tests, correction, decompensation, retraining, cues, protocols, and individualized practice.

## Chapter 15 assembly plan

1. **Open with successful assistance.** Add a hand support or cane and state the valued function before any cost.
2. **Separate endpoint from route.** Show the same task with changed contacts, moments, work, or timing.
3. **Define silent narrowly.** Ordinary function or no reported symptom; no occult lesion.
4. **Show a useful external route.** Use cane or individualized assistance to make environmental support affirmative.
5. **Show acute internal redistribution.** Use fatigue with preserved performance and changed joint work.
6. **Insert the null.** A visible asymmetry or restriction need not increase the proposed internal force.
7. **Move from repetition to retained state.** Use split-belt adaptation and context-specific aftereffects.
8. **Build the cumulative timeline.** Put recovery, learning, support, capacity gain, and no consequence at every stage.
9. **Reconstruct spatial distance.** Name ground, device, limb, trunk, contact, receiver, and evidence state.
10. **Use the longitudinal null.** Show why a later contralateral finding does not validate its proposed route.
11. **Separate causal roles.** Origin, adaptation, maintenance, consequence, and incidental context receive different evidence.
12. **Return to three futures.** Persistent useful support, resolution/replacement, and demonstrated changed exposure.
13. **Stop before response.** Hand named exposures to Chapters 16 and 19 rather than completing a systemic or disease cascade.

## Authorization

This salvage pass authorizes a full Chapter 15 draft after the evidence packet passes independent review and Chapters 11 and 14 supply the immediate seams. It preserves the `new` outline's adaptation-to-dysfunction arc, new baseline, silent compensation, energetic and regulatory cost, gradual reorganization, kinetic-chain rerouting, nontraumatic accumulation, distal consequence, imaging-lag question, posterior hypothesis, breath hypothesis, clinical ambition, and practice ambition. It assigns each element a testable form and appropriate destination rather than narrowing the project to what is already settled.

The chapter's positive center is respect for living adaptation: a compensation is first a way of continuing. Its negative center remains real but conditional: when an alternate strategy recurs, it can change later demand, and that changed history may matter. The chapter succeeds when both truths can be investigated without making either one automatic.
