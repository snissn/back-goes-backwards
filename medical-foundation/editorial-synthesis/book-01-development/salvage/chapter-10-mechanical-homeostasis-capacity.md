## Drafting decision

The `new` outlines contain an important negative argument that the current Book I sequence should preserve: mechanical demand can be mismanaged gradually, without a single dramatic injury, when magnitude, direction, repetition, duration, geometry, support, and recovery interact with a changing living system. The archive also contains an important positive argument: living structure can accommodate, recover, learn, adapt, redistribute, and remain viable across changing demands.

Chapter 10 should recover both. It should not begin Part III by declaring hidden pathology. It establishes the neutral dynamic baseline from which later chapters can distinguish interrupted handoff, concentration, constraint, instability, substitution, accumulation, and compensation.

The archive's language often fused several steps:

```text
visible posture or modern environment
    -> misrouted load
    -> soft-tissue compensation
    -> depleted reserve
    -> degeneration, symptoms, or systemic disease
```

The controlled chapter does not delete that research program. It expands the missing middle:

```text
defined task and environment
    -> measured or modeled exposure history
    -> present, task-specific capacity
    -> observed accommodation, recovery, adaptation, fatigue, substitution,
       remodeling, injury, failure, or no material change
    -> only then, a bounded biological or medical interpretation
```

This is the generous transformation required by the project-model memo: preserve the hypothesis and add rigor at the publication layer where each bridge is claimed.

## Structural recovery from the archive

The useful original movement is:

```text
living structure under demand
    -> adaptation and compensation
    -> repetitive or sustained exposure
    -> reduced resilience or accumulated strain
    -> delayed failure and medical consequence
```

Chapter 10 should preserve the intuition while opening every compressed arrow:

```text
named system + task + environment
    -> multidimensional mechanical exposure through time
    -> present task- and outcome-specific capacity
    -> recovery history and changed state
    -> competing observed trajectories
    -> later failure-family and tissue-response analysis
```

The resulting chapter is not weaker. It gives the project a way to describe load mismanagement that is physically intelligible, testable, and compatible with ordinary adaptation and successful variation.

## Clean conceptual recovery

### Preserve directly

- A body is a living mechanical system whose present state affects how it meets a demand.
- Peak magnitude alone is insufficient; direction, rate, duration, repetition, variability, sequence, phase, and recovery can matter.
- The same nominal task can produce different internal exposures.
- Task completion and internal state are different observations.
- Recovery takes time and must be defined for a selected variable.
- Environmental contacts, constraints, tools, supports, and assistance change boundary conditions.
- Small repeated exposures can matter in ways a snapshot does not reveal.
- Acute failure and gradual accumulation are distinct histories.
- Adaptation and adverse accumulation are both possible responses to repeated demand.
- Mechanical reasoning should include chronology before attributing an outcome to age, posture, occupation, or diagnosis.

### Reconstruct rather than erase

- “The body adapts to load” becomes “defined cells, tissues, and task systems can exhibit several responses to specified load histories, including no observed change, accommodation, altered coordination, remodeling, fatigue, recovery, damage, or failure.”
- “Capacity is exceeded” becomes “a declared exposure no longer meets a declared performance, stability, integrity, symptom, or recovery criterion for the selected system.”
- “Cumulative load” becomes a multidimensional exposure history, not an unexplained sum.
- “Compensatory reserve is depleted” becomes a measured or explicitly inferred change in the ability to preserve a named function under additional demand.
- “Minor misalignment creates chronic strain” becomes a comparison of geometry, exposure, state, and time that retains a null outcome.
- “Modern environments cause collapse” becomes “environments change contacts, constraints, repetition, attention, support, and available strategies; their consequences require measurement.”
- “Repeated load causes degeneration” becomes a tissue- and outcome-specific research question after exposure, recovery, and alternatives are defined.
- “Rest restores the system” becomes a variable-specific recovery claim with a time course and criterion.

### Protect forward

- interrupted skeletal-primary routing as one candidate source of increased soft-role demand;
- “load leakage,” “strain entrapment,” and “failed handoff” after mechanical definitions are complete;
- concentration, constraint, instability, substitution, and compensation as distinct failure families;
- history-dependent configuration transitions and discontinuities in inferred load fields;
- an efficiency functional and preferred whole-body organization;
- posterior participation as necessary but insufficient within the Book II hypothesis;
- tissue-specific mechanobiology and systemic physiological bridges;
- condition-specific prognosis, prevention, treatment, ergonomics, and training; and
- TCM, Six-Division, breath-geometry, bandha, and practice applications in later books.

## Chapter 10 assembly plan

1. **Open with one task at four times.** The same object transfer appears rested, repeated, incompletely recovered, and familiar or assisted.
2. **Separate demand from exposure.** Show why object mass and visible motion do not locate internal tissue dose.
3. **Define homeostasis as a range.** Begin with bounded mechanobiology, then label the whole-task viable-range extension as project synthesis.
4. **Make capacity relational.** Qualify system, task, direction, outcome, state, and time; contrast completion with repetition and recovery.
5. **Turn dose into history.** Use matched peaks with different duration, direction, sequence, rate, variability, and rest.
6. **Treat recovery as observed change.** Use a narrow time-dependent tissue example and refuse universal rest periods.
7. **Place environment at the boundary.** Include supports and assistance; avoid a “modern life” polemic.
8. **Branch outcomes.** Preserve adaptation and adverse accumulation without making either automatic.
9. **Draw a conditional relation, not a score.** Keep task demand, resulting exposure, capacity, and boundary conditions separately inspectable through time.
10. **Hand off to failed routing.** Chapter 11 can now ask when demand shifts among carriers and what evidence would establish an interrupted handoff.

## Authorization

This salvage pass authorizes a full Chapter 10 draft. It recovers the archive's concern with cumulative exposure, silent change, recovery, compensation, environment, and delayed consequence while supplying the missing neutral baseline and intermediate steps. It preserves both possible directions of living response: successful accommodation and adaptation on one side, adverse accumulation and failure on the other.

The chapter's strongest defensible conclusion is not that every repeated demand causes harm. It is that a mechanical snapshot cannot carry a longitudinal judgment. Before calling a load viable, training, fatiguing, compensatory, injurious, or failing, the book must name the system, exposure history, present capacity, environment, response, and time course.
