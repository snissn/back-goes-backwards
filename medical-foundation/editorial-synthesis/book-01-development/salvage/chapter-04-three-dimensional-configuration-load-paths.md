## Drafting decision

Chapter 4 should be synthesized from the accepted brief, current project model, Chapter 2 mechanics controls, and the [Chapter 4 evidence pack](../evidence/chapter-04-three-dimensional-configuration-load-paths.md). It should not be produced by cleaning one legacy section or generated run.

The archive's protected intent is substantial and should remain visible:

- the body must be considered as a coupled three-dimensional mechanical system rather than only a collection of isolated planar levers;
- frames, bases, moments, rotational inertia, contact, and changing geometry belong in the project's engineering language;
- full-body movement may be decomposed in several useful ways;
- the author's axial, opening/rotational, and expansive coordinate research is a serious later-book model program;
- local spatial frames, generalized modes, material directionality, and stress fields may interact without being the same object; and
- traditional, embodied, breathing, and practice layers remain active series material even when they are not load-bearing in Book I.

The chapter's current causal spine is:

> selected system and phase → declared spatial frame and reference → three-dimensional geometry, contacts, constraints, active force, pressure, and material state → moments and available carrier relations → one or several bounded load-path representations → explicitly limited inferences

This normalization is preservative, not ablative. Material that exceeds Book I is routed to the book that can define and test it. Material that currently fuses unlike objects is decomposed into separately recoverable propositions. Only prompt contamination, unsupported equations, and conclusions that cannot be traced to a valid intermediate argument remain quarantined as formulations.

## First-draft disposition

The first draft may include:

- anatomical planes as useful but incomplete reporting conventions;
- global, segment, joint, task, surface, and tissue-local frames;
- one elementary change-of-basis example and one curved-frame warning;
- geometry-dependent moment arms, contacts, and rotational inertia;
- configuration as more than visible posture;
- a load path as a multicarrier graph, field, or time-varying representation;
- the difference between an external line of action and an inferred internal route;
- the four-description crosswalk: spatial frame, generalized modes, material-symmetry frame, and principal-stress frame;
- bounded rib, pelvic, and curved-column checks; and
- one direct, non-clandestine Book II signpost preserving the axial/opening/expansive program.

It may not include:

- a claim that three Euclidean dimensions derive the author's generalized modes;
- whole-body orthotropy as an established anatomical fact;
- principal-stress directions as meridians, force lines, or permanent paths;
- the old coupled-angular-velocity equation or a repaired-looking variant without a new derivation;
- a claim that rotation matrices establish spinal extension, decompression, posterior necessity, or health effects;
- preferred signs, Six-Division correspondence, bandha assignments, breath as a necessary driver, torus dynamics, or practice instruction; or
- diagnosis, movement correction, injury prevention, self-assessment, or protocol language.

## Quarantined expressions and their protected replacements

| Legacy expression | Why it cannot enter | Protected replacement or question |
|---|---|---|
| “Only a 3D approach can explain…” | Overstates the failure of valid planar models | Real tasks are 3D; the required dimensionality depends on the question and residual terms |
| “Three axes simplify every movement” | Conflates spatial basis, anatomical movement, and reduced modes | Several decompositions are possible; each must name its object and retained degrees of freedom |
| “Basis functions are the body's mechanical alphabet” | Treats one candidate decomposition as intrinsic and complete | A basis or reduced coordinate set is chosen for a declared representation and tested for adequacy |
| “Mass and tension determine moment of inertia” | Confuses mass distribution with active/internal dynamics | Mass distribution determines rigid-body inertia; active force, stiffness, damping, pressure, and constraints separately affect motion |
| “Rotation matrices reveal decompression” | A representation rule cannot establish a physical consequence | Matrices transform orientation/components; decompression requires a defined quantity and body-specific model |
| `omega_y = omega_x omega_z / cos(theta_x)` | Dimensionally inconsistent and unsupported | Preserve the coupled-mode question; derive a valid kinematic or dynamic model later |
| “Three orthogonal axes prove orthotropy” | Spatial dimensionality is not material symmetry | Orthotropy is a tissue-, scale-, state-, regime-, and evidence-dependent constitutive choice |
| “Principal stress reveals the body's force lines” | Stress eigenvectors are local, state-dependent, and sometimes nonunique | Compare principal-stress fields with candidate paths only through an explicit model and measurement program |
| “Pelvis opens like scissors” | Can imply large literal pelvic-ring separation | Define paired directors and a relative opening angle across named structures; distinguish width/area change |
| “Rib rotation is expansion” | Conflates angular and length/area/volume observables | Rotation may contribute to expansion; measure both and state their coupling |
