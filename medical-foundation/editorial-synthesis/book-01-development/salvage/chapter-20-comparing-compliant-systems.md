## Recovery decision

The archive contains a durable intuition that should be carried forward in full rather than reduced to a warning about overclaim:

> Load wants to travel through the hard body so that the soft body can remain soft.

In Book I, that phrase becomes a proposed **division of mechanical labor**, not a permanent classification of tissues. For a declared task, frame, direction, and timescale, some relations are relatively shape-preserving or shape-organizing; others are relatively form-receiving, yielding, accommodating, sliding, containing, or deforming. The vocabulary is **motivated by** TCM hard/soft and Yin/Yang concepts but is not claimed to be a definitionally exact biomedical translation of them.

The protected hypothesis is that a sufficiently organized load-bearing relation may allow a form-receiving structure to retain the freedom its function requires: caliber change, wall deformation, excursion, relative glide, pressure accommodation, or another receiver-specific freedom. This preserves the author's intended asymmetry. The primary question is not merely whether a passage appears narrow. It is whether an intrinsic, extrinsic, or mixed relation maintains deformation or limits freedom, and whether that changes the receiver's own mechanical output.

The archive also proposes consequences across fascia, vessels, lymphatics, nerves, organs, pressure systems, energy, inflammation, regulation, and chronic disease. Those ideas remain in the project. Chapter 20 promotes the system-specific mechanical questions that can now be stated and tested; it preserves the broader chains as research inventory with their missing links visible. It neither spends those hypotheses as current conclusions nor deletes them because their research phase remains deferred.

## Disposition legend

- **D — direct recovery:** the concept can enter Chapter 20 substantially as intended.
- **R — reconstruct:** preserve the intended question while replacing an overcompressed chain with explicit variables, alternatives, or evidence states.
- **P — protect forward:** retain the concept for another chapter, book, or research layer.
- **Q — quarantine:** retain the exact stronger proposition as a research obligation; do not place it in Book I as established fact.
- **X — exclude from reader prose:** editorial instruction, protocol, unsupported generated mechanism, or category collapse remains in provenance only.

`Q` and `X` are not deletion. They identify what must not silently become a Book I conclusion while keeping the author's larger program available for later formalization and research.

## Source-coverage reconciliation

### `BGB-S-1106` — tension, prestress, fascia, glide, pressure, and continuity

**Preserved:** The body is not exhausted by a bone-only lever model. Active tension, passive tensile structures, interfaces, pressure, contact, and relative glide can carry, redirect, accommodate, or modify demand. Fascia is retained where a named structure, interface, direction, scale, or measurement makes the claim inspectable.

**Reconstruction:** “One continuous tension network” becomes a set of possible local, regional, and longer-range relations. Structural continuity does not by itself establish the magnitude, direction, functional importance, or clinical consequence of force transmission. The larger continuity hypothesis remains available to later books and experiments.

### `BGB-S-1111` — vascular and lymphatic compression, kinking, tethering, glide, and flow

**Preserved:** Surrounding geometry, pressure, contact, course, movement, and tissue state can alter selected vascular or lymphatic mechanical outputs. Kinking and tethering remain useful candidate exposure descriptions when operationalized.

**Reconstruction:** Blood vessels and lymphatics receive separate rows. Vascular pressure–flow and wall behavior cannot supply lymphatic valve, pump, filling, or network conclusions. Lumen, waveform, flow, transport, perfusion, drainage, swelling, and clinical consequence remain different nodes. Ordinary position dependence, active response, restoration, redundancy, and no important change stay affirmative branches.

### `BGB-S-1112` — neural contact, tethering, excursion, signaling, proprioception, and bracing

**Preserved:** Nerves move, deform, contact interfaces, and operate within a changing mechanical environment. Excursion and local compression can be measured, and sufficiently imposed compression can affect intraneural physiology under bounded conditions.

**Reconstruction:** A nerve is not a hose and “neural flow” is removed. Excursion, strain, transverse deformation, contact pressure, circulation, axonal transport, conduction, sensation, motor output, proprioceptive response, bracing, and symptom are separate quantities or outcomes. Intrinsic neural state, systemic disease, reverse causality, and measurement artifact remain alternatives.

### `BGB-S-1119` — impingement, constrained space, tethering, and lost excursion

**Preserved:** Internal structures occupy courses and interfaces defined partly by surrounding anatomy. A persistent surrounding relation can maintain deformation or reduce excursion.

**Authorial refinement:** The consequential mechanical event is not simply “less space.” In an extrinsically maintained case, stress, contact, pressure, bending, or tethering imposed through the surrounding relation produces the deformation; a changed lumen, course, or available excursion is an intermediate state. A compliant structure may otherwise restore toward another equilibrium when the maintaining relation changes.

**Boundary:** A narrowed space or visible deformation does not identify the maintaining relation, prove persistence, establish abnormality, or complete the path to symptom or disease.

### `BGB-S-1219` — soft-tissue strain, maintained internal deformation, compression, and possible symptoms

**Preserved:** A form-receiving tissue can experience maintained strain, compression, contact, or lost freedom when its relationship to shape-organizing structures changes. That is a central Book I mechanical question, not material to be erased.

**Staging:** Chapter 13 establishes maintained deformation, Chapter 19 supplies receiver-specific tissue response, Chapter 20 compares unlike compliant systems, and Chapter 21 handles symptom and causal weight. The adjective “soft” never means weak, unloaded, passive, or unable to transmit substantial force.

### `BGB-S-1223` — geometry, course, lumen, pressure, excursion, and signaling

**Preserved:** These variables form a cross-system inventory.

**Required separation:** Geometry is not lumen; lumen is not flow; pressure is not one body-wide scalar; excursion is not strain; strain is not signaling; signaling is not symptom. The matrix retains separate columns and leaves unsupported cells blank.

### `BGB-S-1224` — thoracic outlet, pelvic basin, nerve roots, venous/lymphatic contexts, and chronic applications

**Preserved:** The archive intended a family of anatomical contexts rather than a single local illustration. All remain in the research program.

**Current allocation:** Chapter 24 owns thoracic outlet and its venous, arterial, and neural distinctions. Pelvic-basin, nerve-root, venous, lymphatic, and chronic-disease applications remain indexed candidates for later specialist-reviewed comparisons. Their absence from Chapter 20's reader narrative is a sequencing decision, not an editorial rejection.

### `BGB-S-1314` — thoracic-outlet corridor exemplar

**Preserved:** Thoracic outlet is a useful worked test of dynamic corridor geometry because different receivers traverse related regions and can respond differently.

**Boundary:** Chapter 20 may point to the case but cannot use provocative positional findings, one phenotype, or one receiver to establish another phenotype, etiology, diagnosis, or treatment. Chapter 24 carries the full comparison.

All eight assigned source obligations are represented, routed, or explicitly protected. No intended concept is silently discarded.

## Structural recovery from the archive

The archive contributes nine durable moves to Chapter 20:

1. **Mechanical support is a division of labor.** Some relations organize or preserve shape so that other structures can yield, contain, glide, or deform appropriately.
2. **The form-receiving body is active.** Vessels, lymphatics, nerves, connective-tissue interfaces, airways, and cavity boundaries have material, active, pressure, transport, or signaling behavior of their own.
3. **The maintaining relation comes first in an extrinsic account.** Lost space or lumen is an intermediate geometry, not the whole explanation.
4. **Freedom is receiver-specific.** Caliber, excursion, relative glide, pressure accommodation, and wall deformation are not interchangeable.
5. **Persistent states have histories.** Transient/restored, extrinsically maintained, intrinsically retained, and mixed paths require different evidence.
6. **Ordinary deformation is affirmative function.** A living compliant system often works by changing shape and restoring it.
7. **The common question transfers; evidence does not.** A shared matrix can expose comparable relations while preserving different equations, thresholds, and outcomes.
8. **Local mechanics can motivate wider research.** Mechanical plausibility is a beginning from which tissue, physiological, symptom, and disease links can be tested.
9. **The later directional program remains intact.** Skeletal-primary routing, posterior organization, TCM coordinates, force lines, breath geometry, and practice retain their book-level destinations without borrowing proof from this comparison.

## Clean conceptual recovery

### Preserve directly

- Shape-preserving and form-receiving are relational roles for a task, direction, frame, and timescale.
- The role language is motivated by TCM hard/soft and Yin/Yang concepts but is not definitionally identical to them.
- Form-receiving does not mean weak, passive, unimportant, forceless, or unloaded.
- A compliant structure may require bounded deformation or motion to perform its function.
- A maintained deformation requires an intrinsic, extrinsic, or mixed maintaining relation.
- Surrounding geometry and pressure can change selected receiver-specific outputs under bounded conditions.
- Active tissue state and intrinsic material change can oppose, amplify, or retain an externally initiated condition.
- A response to repositioning shows present modulation; it does not by itself establish original cause.
- Ordinary deformation, adequate restoration, compensation, low mechanical priority, and no consequential change are real outcomes.
- A common matrix can transfer disciplined questions across systems while leaving evidence in its own row.

### Reconstruct rather than erase

- “Load leaks into soft tissue” becomes: a specified demand, through a specified transfer or boundary relation, produces a measured exposure in a named receiver over a declared interval.
- “The soft body must stay soft” becomes: a form-receiving structure should retain the receiver-specific freedom needed for the named function within an appropriate range.
- “Bones compress a vessel” becomes: a surrounding relation may impose contact, bending, pressure, course change, or maintained deformation; wall state, internal pressure, collateral paths, and flow determine the later result.
- “Lymph is blocked” becomes a receiver-specific inquiry into filling, contraction, valve behavior, pressure, movement, propulsion, network transport, fluid balance, and alternatives.
- “A nerve is pinched” becomes separate questions about contact, excursion, strain, pressure, circulation, transport, conduction, sensation, motor function, and symptom.
- “Fascia transmits dysfunction globally” becomes testable local, regional, and longer-range continuity questions with measured force, deformation, glide, pressure, or coordination outcomes.
- “Impingement causes disease” becomes a staged chain from maintaining relation to receiver mechanics, tissue response, function, symptom, diagnosis, and outcome, with each arrow independently supported.
- “Posterior loading protects the organs” becomes a later hypothesis that a preferred shape-organizing route may preserve selected internal freedoms; Book I establishes neither the preferred route nor the medical consequence.

### Protect forward

- the formal definition of hard-body and soft-body roles across changing tasks;
- skeletal-primary routing as a first-order model;
- posterior support as necessary but not sufficient for the later preferred-configuration hypothesis;
- the axial, lateral/expansive, and rotational/moment framework;
- TCM Six-Division, channel-pair, hand–leg, and force-line correspondences;
- pericardium–kidney coordination and related embodied observations;
- fascia as a possible long-range force, pressure, sensory, or coordination medium;
- breath as an organizer or driver of pressure, shape, phase, or routing;
- pelvic-basin, craniofacial, hyoid, septal, airway, nerve-root, organ-mobility, venous, and lymphatic applications;
- systemic inflammation, autonomic regulation, immune function, energy, recovery, and chronic-disease hypotheses; and
- diagnostic, preventive, therapeutic, self-assessment, movement, bandha, yoga, tai chi, and corrective-practice programs.

These are inclusive forward obligations. Their publication layer and eventual claim strength depend on formalization, evidence, alternatives, and relevant expert review.

## Authorization

This salvage packet, together with the independently reviewed evidence packet, authorizes a full Chapter 20 draft. The chapter should be intellectually affirmative about the author's core idea. It should explain why a shape-organizing relation may matter precisely because living compliant structures need room to yield, contain, glide, change caliber, or accommodate pressure—not because soft structures are supposed to avoid load.
