## Recovery decision

The `new` outlines contain a durable idea that should remain visible in Book I:

> A person's present mechanical state is the latest point in a history. A useful account may need to follow how demands entered the person–environment system, how available structures shared them, how successful or costly compensations changed later distributions, and how local exposures developed across time.

That idea is broader than a posture observation and more specific than a general call to “consider biomechanics.” It also does not require the claim that every history is recoverable, that one route explains a finding, or that existing fields have ignored exposure and compensation. Occupational biomechanics, ergonomics, rehabilitation, movement science, and medicine already possess important parts of the record. The proposed addition is to make their relationship across scale and time explicit enough to inspect, compare, and test.

The archive's intent therefore survives in four layers:

1. **Established basis:** demand, exposure, repetition, recovery, task, environment, and compensation can matter across time.
2. **Organizing proposal:** observations and models held at different scales can be assembled into a provenance-labeled routing history.
3. **Medical hypothesis:** the assembled history may sometimes improve explanation, prediction, research design, communication, or a decision beyond existing local accounts.
4. **Later research program:** reliability, validity, biological links, clinical usefulness, preferred configuration, and intervention consequences require separate tests.

This reconstruction preserves the archive's stronger possibility rather than deciding against it in advance. It also gives that possibility a fair survival condition: a longitudinal routing record should earn its place by adding something defined and measurable relative to existing practice.

## Source-coverage reconciliation

### `BGB-S-1101` — mechanics as a health dimension

**Preserved:** Mechanical demand and its distribution belong alongside biological, psychological, social, developmental, and environmental context when a defined health or function question warrants them. The routing record includes structure, task, pressure, and time without pretending that they form a complete theory of health.

**Not made load-bearing here:** Breath as a universal organizer, pressure as a single hidden variable, or mechanical organization as sufficient for prevention or reversal of chronic disease.

### `BGB-S-1109` — low-grade deviation, cumulative compensation, and delayed consequence

**Preserved:** Small repeated differences can matter if they alter dose, recovery, distribution, or the next task state. Compensation can initially be successful, can remain useful, can become expensive in a defined sense, or can be unrelated to a later concern.

**Required reconstruction:** Every proposed sequence needs dates or intervals, an exposure quantity or observable proxy, a receiving structure, a state change, and alternative histories. “Low-grade” cannot itself mean harmful, and “delayed” cannot itself prove accumulation.

### `BGB-S-1114` — gradual reorganization without discrete injury

**Preserved:** Not every mechanically meaningful history begins with a memorable event. Repetition, changing work, growth, aging, assistance, pain, fatigue, recovery, or altered environment can change the available routes gradually.

**Required reconstruction:** Retrospective memory, job-level estimates, present findings, and modeled internal loads keep separate evidence labels. A smooth story assembled after the fact is not equivalent to repeated prospective observation.

### `BGB-S-1125` — a larger complementary role for biomechanics

**Preserved:** A shared record may help connect questions that are currently divided among local diagnosis, rehabilitation, device or surgical follow-up, occupational exposure, and environmental design.

**Required survival test:** The record must show incremental reliability, explanation, prediction, communication, research leverage, or decision value. More variables and a more comprehensive narrative are not automatically better.

### `BGB-S-1303` — answer the professional-identity objections

**Preserved:** Rehabilitation already studies movement, capacity, response, and adaptation; ergonomics and occupational medicine already study exposure and environment; biomechanics already models distribution; medicine already defines tissue states and local endpoints. The project is not diminished by those overlaps.

**Proposed increment:** A common, provenance-labeled chronology may make relations among these observations inspectable without assigning exclusive ownership to a discipline or creating a treatment system.

### `BGB-S-1305` — routing, cumulative demand, failure signatures, and scale

**Preserved:** A routing history can record global redistribution, local concentration, segmental instability, timing or handoff failure, constraint-maintained deformation or lost excursion, and compensatory rerouting. These families may overlap, alternate, resolve, or remain absent.

**Required research:** The taxonomy needs operational definitions, rater training, reliability testing, construct comparison, and evidence that its distinctions generate useful questions. Face validity does not establish a medical classification.

### `BGB-S-1309` — relevance upstream of rehabilitation

**Preserved:** The history may matter in how a question is framed before treatment, outside an injury episode, or across environmental and recovery contexts. “Upstream” is temporal or analytical, not a claim that rehabilitation is secondary in value.

**Required comparator:** For any proposed use, compare the record with ordinary history taking, occupational assessment, validated risk models, biomechanical testing, rehabilitation assessment, or the relevant standard practice.

### `BGB-S-1315` — explicit skeptic handling

**Preserved:** The serious objections are part of the model. The history may be nonunique, partially unobservable, unreliably reconstructed, already represented elsewhere, too burdensome, irrelevant to the immediate endpoint, or not incrementally useful.

**Interpretation:** Those are discriminating outcomes, not reasons to delete the research program. They determine the contexts and resolution at which the proposed object survives.

## Structural recovery from the archive

The source material contributes eight durable moves:

1. **Begin with a history rather than a static finding.** The question is how demand and response changed through time.
2. **Keep local and larger boundaries connected.** A local exposure can be placed within task, environment, regional distribution, and whole-person state without making the widest boundary causal by default.
3. **Treat compensation first as successful function.** Rerouting is often the way a task remains possible; any later cost must be specified rather than presumed.
4. **Permit gradual transitions.** A meaningful history need not contain one acute injury or one moment of failure.
5. **Use a small pattern vocabulary.** Constraint, concentration, instability, handoff failure, global redistribution, and compensatory rerouting organize observations without becoming diagnoses.
6. **Connect specialties without erasing them.** Different disciplines can contribute measurements and interpretations to the same dated record.
7. **Make the medical relevance earlier than a protocol.** The immediate product is a better question, comparison, or research design—not a correction sequence.
8. **End with testability.** Reliability, validity, alternatives, incremental value, and null results decide what survives.

All eight remain. The controlled synthesis adds three distinctions that the archive anticipated but did not consistently hold:

- a present endpoint, an exposure history, and the biological response to that exposure are separate records;
- observed, measured, calculated, modeled, inferred, and embodied information retain their provenance when combined; and
- a coherent candidate chronology is not a uniquely identified cause.

## Clean conceptual recovery

### Preserve directly

- Bodies encounter mechanical demands through tasks and environments across time.
- Peak exposure and cumulative exposure are different and sometimes complementary descriptions.
- Compensation can change subsequent distribution while preserving function.
- A present endpoint can be compatible with more than one history.
- A routing history can contain prospective observation, retrospective report, group-level exposure estimates, and model-derived quantities if each retains its evidence state.
- The six adverse-organization families are relational descriptors rather than diagnoses.
- Different disciplines can contribute different nodes to one record.
- The proposed object can be useful for explanation or research without being a clinical tool.
- No-added-value and unrecoverable-history results are valid outcomes.

### Reconstruct rather than erase

- “Local pathology misses global context” becomes a comparison: state what the local account explains, what wider history proposes to add, and what observation would distinguish them.
- “Cumulative stress precedes symptoms” becomes several branches: cumulative exposure with later response, successful adaptation, stable compensation, recovery, reverse causality, unrelated symptoms, and missing evidence.
- “Silent compensation” becomes maintained task performance before a selected endpoint, not an occult diagnosis.
- “Imaging lags mechanism” becomes a modality-, tissue-, finding-, and timing-specific question; an absent or common finding does not validate a proposed route.
- “Biomechanics is upstream of rehabilitation” becomes a candidate contribution to exposure and context questions that is compared with work already done in rehabilitation and adjacent fields.
- “One unified framework” becomes an interoperable research record whose usefulness may vary by question and scale.

### Protect forward

- biological effects of a defined exposure history, owned by Chapter 19;
- cross-system conduit, nerve, sliding-interface, and pressure comparisons, owned by Chapter 20;
- causal priority, symptoms, diagnosis, and whole-person alternatives, owned by Chapter 21;
- use inside clinical encounters or professional workflow, owned by Chapter 22;
- worked medical narratives, owned by Chapters 23–26;
- measurement reliability, construct validity, prediction, and incremental-value studies, owned by Chapter 29;
- skeletal-primary, posterior, and preferred-configuration hypotheses, owned principally by Book II;
- TCM coordinate correspondence and force-line elaboration, owned by Book III;
- breath-as-driver, bandhas, torus dynamics, testing, and corrective practice, owned by later books and dedicated research gates.

## Reader-facing sequence

1. **Open with one endpoint and several compatible histories.** Give none of them rhetorical priority.
2. **Name the proposed record.** Show task and environment → available paths → demand and distribution → local exposure → response or compensation → later endpoint.
3. **Label every link.** Preserve observed, measured, calculated, modeled, inferred, hypothesized, and embodied provenance.
4. **Enter the occupied landscape.** Show what ergonomics, exposure science, biomechanics, rehabilitation, and medicine already contribute.
5. **Use cumulative-exposure research affirmatively.** Establish that histories and metric choices can matter while retaining their limits.
6. **Make reconstruction error visible.** Contrast prospective observation, retrospective report, job-level priors, and model-derived quantities.
7. **Crosswalk the six families.** Use them as candidate relational descriptors across dates and scales.
8. **Restore the full compensation tree.** Include adaptation, recovery, stable substitution, no progression, reverse direction, and unrelated outcome.
9. **Assign disciplinary contributions without a new owner.** Keep conflict and missing nodes visible.
10. **Apply the incremental-value ladder.** End with results that support, narrow, duplicate, or disfavor the proposed object.
11. **Hand off the biological arrow.** Chapter 19 receives a bounded local exposure, not an assumed tissue response.

- Chapter 19 receives a local-exposure question whose biological link is still open.

## Authorization

This salvage pass, together with the completed independent source audit of its evidence packet, authorizes a full Chapter 18 draft. The draft should preserve the archive's central ambition: mechanical history may be a medically meaningful object that connects demand, structure, compensation, local exposure, recovery, and current state across time.

The draft should not apologize for that proposal or reduce it to a generic reminder that “context matters.” It should make the proposed record concrete, show the mature fields it joins, expose how its components could be measured or reconstructed, and state what would demonstrate useful integration. Equal care should be given to results that narrow its context, show duplication, or reveal that a history cannot be recovered reliably.

The clean handoff to Chapter 19 is a bounded proposition: even if a routing history is well described, the next arrow—from local physical exposure to a living tissue's response—still requires tissue-specific evidence. That boundary strengthens rather than diminishes the whole-system research program.
