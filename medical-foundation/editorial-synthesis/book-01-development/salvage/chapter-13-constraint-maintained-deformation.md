## Drafting decision

Chapter 13 should be drafted from the current project model, chapter brief, control records, and evidence pack rather than by revising a legacy chapter. Its causal spine is:

> defined system and reference → sustaining boundary conditions → contact, traction, pressure, bending, tethering, active force, or retained material state → tissue stress, deformation, course change, or lost excursion → separately supported system-specific function

The chapter's distinctive claim is not that reduced space is irrelevant. It is that geometry alone does not explain an extrinsically maintained state. The account must identify what currently maintains the shape or repeatedly recreates it, what restoring tendencies oppose that state, which tissue-specific freedom is altered, and which downstream quantity—if any—changes.

Hard and soft enter as relative mechanical roles. A hard role is comparatively shape-preserving or structurally resolving. A soft role is comparatively form-receiving or shape-conforming while retaining whatever yielding, glide, excursion, containment, expansion, pressure response, or recovery the task requires. This polarity is **motivated by** traditional Chinese medicine's hard/soft and Yin/Yang concepts; Book I does not claim definitional identity, complete historical equivalence, or scientific validation through correspondence.

The selected non-lumen calibration is the middle-finger flexor digitorum superficialis tendon and adjacent subsynovial connective tissue at the wrist during controlled healthy finger movement. It shows measurable, task-dependent co-motion and relative motion. It does not supply an adverse constraint, pathological threshold, recovery claim, health consequence, or corrective practice.

## First-draft review disposition

The first draft may now include:

- the generic distinction among transient, extrinsically maintained, intrinsically retained, and mixed states;
- the hard/soft division-of-labor hypothesis and its traditional motivation;
- the garden-hose analogy for causal order, under explicit fluid, wall, pressure, support, and biological limits;
- tendon excursion, SSCT excursion, and calculated relative motion as separate observables;
- ordinary co-motion, task variation, measurement error, intrinsic alternatives, and no downstream consequence as real counterexamples or nulls;
- the rule that kinematic return is not recovery after release; and
- candidate physiological chains only when every unsupported arrow remains visibly open.

It may not include:

- a claim that any smaller relative-motion value is loss, entrapment, injury, or load mismanagement;
- a claim that relative displacement measures local shear stress, frictional work, adhesion, capacity, or tissue health;
- a generic narrowing-causes-poor-flow or compression-causes-symptoms cascade;
- pooled vascular, lymphatic, neural, fascial, organ, or airway evidence;
- posterior organization, breath, or preferred configuration as the demonstrated correction;
- a diagnostic inference, reader self-test, exercise, manual technique, or treatment recommendation; or
- legacy certainty about systemic inflammation, autonomic effects, metabolism, immune surveillance, or disease prevention.

No external specialist review is represented as completed. Hand/wrist anatomy, ultrasound method, formal biomechanics, final figure, accessibility, rights, and publication wording remain later review gates. Those reviews may narrow or replace details without blocking a first draft that stays inside this sheet.

## Hard/soft observability and counterexample decision

For the selected interface, the tendon is a candidate tensile, shape-preserving carrier in the declared direction and task; the SSCT is a candidate form-receiving interface whose movement and relative excursion remain relevant. The observable is not either tissue label. It is the joint record of:

1. declared external task and achieved finger movement;
2. tendon excursion and, where available, imposed tendon force;
3. SSCT excursion;
4. calculated relative motion;
5. measurement error and ordinary task variation; and
6. any separately measured functional availability, substitute demand, or recovery.

The current source set supplies items 1 through 5 incompletely and does not supply an adverse item 6. The first draft may therefore present the relation as an inspectable hypothesis and neutral calibration, not as validation of the hard/soft model.

Required counterexamples and nulls are:

- SSCT normally moves and bears mechanical demand; softness does not mean zero load;
- healthy task changes alter relative motion without establishing dysfunction;
- tendon–SSCT relative displacement and mechanically measured frictional work are not interchangeable;
- a proposed maintaining term may change without an independent functional consequence; and
- matched task comparisons may ultimately be explained fully by ordinary geometry, activation, anatomy, stiffness, and measurement error, in which case the polarity narrows to a heuristic.

## Analogy and figure disposition

The hose and tendon–SSCT systems share a method, not a constitutive law: define the boundary conditions, state what maintains the current relation, measure the tissue-specific deformation or freedom, examine restoring behavior, and keep downstream function separate. Lumen, flow, transmural pressure, active vascular wall behavior, pulsatility, collateral circulation, tendon drive, layered SSCT, and relative glide do not transfer across systems.

The tendon–SSCT visual belongs as the controlled inset specified in `BGB-FIG-01-006`, not in the hose figure. A first-draft placeholder may show:

- an original longitudinal orientation schematic with the FDS tendon, adjacent SSCT tracking region, and ultrasound window;
- separate tendon and SSCT motion traces labeled as schematic unless sourced numerical data are redrawn under a recorded rights decision;
- two research task conditions without labeling either correct;
- `OBS`, `CALC`, project-interpretation or `HYP`, and `OPEN` evidence states; and
- an explicit null and unresolved recovery-after-release cell.

The caption must state that relative displacement is not local stress, frictional work, injury, adhesion, health, or recovery. The art must not resemble an exercise or self-test. Final anatomy, method, biomechanics, accessibility, and rights review remains a publication gate.
