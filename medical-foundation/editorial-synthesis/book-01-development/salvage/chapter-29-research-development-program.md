## Recovery decision

The archive proposes a scientific program, new measures, professional and public education, assessment and therapeutic methods, interdisciplinary implementation, changes to prevention, and eventually a reorganization of medicine around biomechanics. It also treats these ambitions as though they could rise together once the central mechanical idea is accepted.

Chapter 29 should preserve the full program by separating it into exactly six tracks:

1. **mechanistic and observational research**;
2. **measurement and validation**;
3. **education**;
4. **assessment and therapeutic development**;
5. **implementation**; and
6. **health-system development**.

The tracks coordinate, but none serves as a proxy for another. A mechanical relation can be real without yielding a clinically useful measure. A reliable measure can be nonspecific. Education can improve understanding without changing health. An intervention can help through a mechanism different from the one proposed. An effective intervention can fail in implementation. Successful adoption can occur without added clinical value. A negative result can retire one pathway without deciding the full series.

This architecture is not a retreat from the medical-overhaul ambition. It is the means by which the project can become more revisionary over time without using early-stage success as universal validation. Every handoff must carry provenance, population, construct, result, uncertainty, alternatives, and prohibited inference.

## Disposition legend

- **D — direct recovery:** the concept can enter Chapter 29 substantially as intended.
- **R — reconstruct:** preserve the concept while naming its track, output, evidence burden, alternative, stop, and handoff.
- **P — protect forward:** retain for Books II–V, a condition-specific program, or future institutional work.
- **Q — quarantine:** retain the stronger claim and re-entry conditions without presenting it as mature.
- **X — exclude from reader prose:** protocols, diagnostic systems, reform promises, and generated certainty remain in provenance only.

Quarantine is compatible with active development. It prevents a candidate from borrowing authority from the program that contains it.

## Source-obligation reconciliation

### BGB-S-1126 — research, collaboration, prevention, and health-system ambition

**Preserved:** The project seeks interdisciplinary studies that connect anatomy, mechanics, exposure, tissue response, symptoms, function, and longer outcomes; new ways to observe internal distribution; education across disciplines; prevention; and eventually routine mechanical reasoning in medicine.

**Reconstruction:** The ambition becomes a portfolio rather than one confirmatory project. Each study names the causal link it observes, the links it models, the links it infers, and the outcome it is qualified to change. Each development object has a negative-result route and a declared decision consequence.

**Protected future:** Disease-specific cohorts, systemic reserve, chronic illness, prevention, reimbursement, policy, and care redesign remain active only through their own biological, clinical, equity, and implementation bridges.

### BGB-S-1316 — bounded implications with explicit growth paths

**Preserved:** Book I can end with more than “further research is needed.” Its failure signatures, exposure grammar, hard/soft roles, compensation distinctions, worked cases, and clinical boundaries generate concrete projects now.

**Reconstruction:** A research question does not gain plausibility merely because it is testable. An application does not become ready because its ingredients are named. The chapter must state which early projects are possible, which later results they could support, and which conclusions remain unavailable.

### BGB-S-1616 and BGB-S-1617 — research and clinical integration

**Preserved:** The archive intends anatomy, biomechanics, traditional observation, breath, practice, and clinical experience eventually to contribute to research and care. It also intends collaboration among scientists, clinicians, teachers, engineers, and experienced practitioners.

**Reconstruction:** Different knowledge forms receive explicit provenance and appropriate methods. Embodied or traditional observations can generate constructs, descriptions, tasks, and hypotheses; they do not become anatomical or clinical validation through sincerity or longevity. Clinical integration begins with mature, purpose-specific outputs and studies added value against existing practice.

**Protected forward:** Preferred configuration, Six-Division and meridian mapping, force lines, breath geometry, bandhas, hyoid, torus, tensegrity, and individualized practices receive later-book programs. Chapter 29 defines their gates without previewing them as findings.

### BGB-S-1618 and BGB-S-1619 — institutions and lifelong iteration

**Preserved:** Schools, communities, workplaces, health systems, and public education are possible development settings. Embodiment and learning are intended as iterative processes without a perfection endpoint.

**Reconstruction:** Institutional programs require audience, content, training, fidelity, access, cost, equity, adverse-effect, and outcome evaluation. A lifelong practice orientation does not authorize continuous surveillance, one correct body state, or a commercial program.

### BGB-S-1620 and BGB-S-1621 — integrated later content and open contribution

**Preserved:** The eventual framework aims to integrate structure, breath, traditional observation, and practice, and to invite practitioners, teachers, and scientists to contribute observations and findings.

**Reconstruction:** Contribution requires provenance, reporting standards, consent where applicable, conflict disclosure, data governance, uncertainty, and a route for negative or contradictory findings. Community enthusiasm is not evidence, and program ownership cannot immunize a claim from retirement.

## Clean conceptual recovery

### Preserve directly

- Book I generates concrete research and development objects;
- the project is a portfolio rather than one theory awaiting one verdict;
- research, measurement, education, therapy, implementation, and health-system work produce different outputs;
- projects can span tracks, but every claimed output remains track-specific;
- no fixed linear sequence is required for all projects;
- provenance travels across every handoff;
- a result updates the exact proposition tested;
- negative, mixed, and inconclusive results have program destinations;
- a local pathway can fail without erasing the general mechanical framework;
- a general framework can remain useful while a preferred-state hypothesis fails;
- measurement reliability, construct validity, responsiveness, decision value, and diagnosis are separate;
- educational uptake, satisfaction, accuracy, behavior, and health outcomes are separate;
- feasibility, safety, efficacy, effectiveness, mechanism, comparative value, and implementation are separate;
- implementation and health-system value require access, equity, cost, governance, and unintended-effect evidence;
- existing disciplines and tools are active comparators, not obstacles; and
- the medical-overhaul ambition becomes incremental, inspectable institutional development.

### Reconstruct rather than erase

- “prove biomechanics matters” becomes test one declared relation, scale, task, state, and endpoint;
- “develop a load-path measure” becomes define construct, method, uncertainty, validity, and use;
- “teach the framework” becomes test comprehension, reasoning, communication, burden, and adverse effects;
- “assess misrouting” becomes develop an observation or tool without diagnostic status until validation;
- “correct the pattern” becomes specify population, intervention, comparator, dose, provider, outcome, mechanism test, harms, and scope;
- “integrate into medicine” becomes compare added value, duplication, workflow, reach, equity, cost, and governance;
- “prevent chronic disease” becomes a condition-specific causal and intervention program;
- “traditional knowledge validates the map” becomes provenance-bearing construct and hypothesis generation;
- “successful therapy proves the theory” becomes retain benefit while testing mechanism separately;
- “null result means the model was not measured deeply enough” becomes classify method limitation or disfavoring evidence by prespecified criteria; and
- “change the paradigm” becomes accumulate mature components and compare their system value.

### Protect forward

- preferred configuration and a generic efficiency metric;
- posterior participation as possibly necessary but insufficient for the later preferred-state program;
- skeletal-primary routing and local material anisotropy models;
- signed axial, rotational, and expansive modes;
- TCM Six-Division, organ-channel, hard/soft, Yin/Yang, and force-line correspondences;
- breath, pressure, hyoid, bandha, torus, tensegrity, and phase models;
- embodied observations and practitioner knowledge;
- candidate tests, screens, measures, cues, drills, exercise, breathing, and therapeutic practices;
- systemic resilience, fatigue, diffuse pain, chronic illness, and disease-specific applications;
- institutional curricula, professional roles, reimbursement, prevention, and policy; and
- eventual revision of medical models when component evidence warrants it.

### Quarantine as publication moves

- one positive mechanistic study validates the framework;
- reliability establishes validity, diagnosis, or clinical usefulness;
- a biomarker, image, sensor, or model output is meaningful because it is precise;
- educational enthusiasm establishes truth or benefit;
- an intervention effect proves its stated mechanism;
- efficacy proves effectiveness or implementation readiness;
- adoption proves value;
- a familiar traditional or embodied pattern proves anatomy or disease;
- preservation in the research program implies endorsement;
- a null can always be rescued by an unmeasured hidden mechanism;
- all six tracks must mature together; and
- one specialty, tool, practice, or doctrine should own biomechanical medicine.

### Exclude from Book I reader prose

- instructions for assessments, self-tests, palpation, posture grading, movement corrections, exercise, breath, bandhas, or therapeutic sequences;
- diagnostic thresholds, prognostic tools, referral rules, or clinical algorithms;
- promises of prevention, disease reversal, system savings, or medical transformation;
- exhaustive catalogues of candidate devices or therapies; and
- planning scaffolding, grant rhetoric, organizational ownership claims, and generated certainty.

## Six-track architecture

| Track | Central question | Immediate output | Principal evidence burden | Example stopping or redirection condition |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Research | Does a declared mechanical, biological, longitudinal, or causal relation occur under stated conditions? | bounded finding, estimate, model comparison, or revised pathway | design fit, measurement, alternatives, uncertainty, replication, transport | predicted relation absent, opposite, nonunique, or local only |
| Measurement | Can a construct be defined and measured well enough for a declared use? | qualified measure with use and limit | acquisition, reliability, validity, responsiveness, error, burden, decision value | reliable but nonspecific, unstable, burdensome, reactive, or not decision-relevant |
| Education | Does teaching change a direct learning or communication outcome safely? | audience-specific educational result | comparator, comprehension, calibration, communication, behavior where claimed, harms | liked but inaccurate, burdensome, fear-inducing, or no better than existing language |
| Therapeutic development | Is an assessment or intervention feasible, safe, effective, and appropriately scoped for a named population and outcome? | staged assessment or intervention evidence | population, comparator, dose, provider, adherence, outcomes, harms, mechanism, scope | no benefit, excess harm, mechanism discordance, or existing option performs better |
| Implementation | Can a mature object be delivered with fidelity, reach, access, equity, and sustainability in real settings? | setting-specific delivery evidence | workflow, training, cost, reach, fidelity, maintenance, equity, unintended effects | effective object cannot be delivered, reaches the wrong groups, or displaces greater value |
| Health-system development | Do mature components justify durable changes in training, workflow, financing, prevention, or policy? | incremental institutional decision | added value, opportunity cost, governance, overdiagnosis, workforce, distribution of benefit and harm | duplicates existing care, expands low-value care, worsens access, or lacks system benefit |

The matrix is a network. Education can use established mechanics before a new clinical measure exists. Measurement can mature for research without producing a therapy. Implementation requires a mature object but may expose failures that return it to design or therapeutic development. Health-system work can begin with shared language or data standards before disease-prevention claims mature. Every arrow is optional and gated.

## Maturity and provenance model

No single ladder fits every track, but each candidate should carry a record containing:

1. **origin and provenance** — direct authorial formulation, archive source, generated extrapolation, traditional correspondence, embodied observation, external evidence, or mixed origin;
2. **normalized proposition or construct** — what exactly is being claimed or measured;
3. **system and use** — population, task, scale, state, endpoint, and intended decision;
4. **method** — observation, measurement, calculation, model, experiment, cohort, trial, education study, implementation study, or synthesis;
5. **evidence state** — positive, negative, mixed, inconclusive, method-limited, not tested, or retired;
6. **alternatives and stop** — what competes and what result changes course;
7. **safety, ethics, access, and scope** — requirements proportionate to use;
8. **output qualification** — what the result licenses and forbids;
9. **handoff record** — destination track and retained limits; and
10. **publication layer** — internal hypothesis, research proposal, Book I synthesis, later-book model, practice development, clinical evidence, or system recommendation.

Re-entry after retirement requires new evidence, a materially revised construct, or a valid explanation of a method failure. It cannot consist only of redescribing the same null in more abstract language.

## Concrete cross-track pathway

Use Chapter 24's maintained-deformation pathway as the primary demonstration:

```text
surrounding boundary condition or applied force
    -> wall deformation, excursion change, or lumen/space change
    -> endpoint-specific physical consequence
    -> symptom, function, or clinical interpretation
```

The six tracks ask different questions:

1. **Research:** Does a declared configuration or force maintain deformation or reduce excursion under a defined task, and which alternative causes remain?
2. **Measurement:** Can wall geometry, excursion, pressure, flow, neural behavior, or relevant boundary conditions be measured reliably and validly for the proposed use?
3. **Education:** Can clinicians or designers learn to distinguish imposed demand, wall deformation, lumen, flow, symptom, and diagnosis without overinterpreting posture?
4. **Therapeutic development:** Does changing a declared boundary condition improve a patient-important outcome safely, and does the proposed intermediate change?
5. **Implementation:** Can a mature measure or intervention be delivered accurately, equitably, and within professional scope?
6. **Health-system development:** Does adding the mature element improve care enough to justify training, workflow, cost, and policy change?

A reliable geometry measure with poor symptom specificity remains a measurement result. An intervention that improves function while wall geometry does not change retains its benefit but weakens the proposed mediation. A mechanistic null may retire one posture-to-deformation route while leaving other maintained-constraint contexts intact.

## Required negative-result routing

| Result | Immediate program action | What remains unavailable |
|---|---|---|
| Predicted redistribution is not observed with adequate method and range | narrow or retire that relation; inspect task, state, and model sensitivity | hidden redistribution as default explanation |
| Local change appears but no regional carrier is measured | stop at the local result; design a bridge study only if consequential | kinetic-chain or systemic consequence |
| Reliable measure has low construct specificity | qualify for description only, revise construct, or retire for proposed use | diagnosis, mechanism, treatment selection |
| Educational program is liked but does not improve accuracy | revise language, audience, or format; do not claim educational success for reasoning | truth, behavior, or health effect |
| Intervention improves outcome while proposed mediator does not change | retain effect; test alternative mechanisms; narrow mechanistic claim | proof of project mechanism |
| Intervention has no benefit or unacceptable harm | stop, revise, or abandon for that population and use | continued recommendation because theory is plausible |
| Effective intervention fails reach, fidelity, equity, or sustainability | change implementation or setting; assess whether use remains justified | system readiness |
| Mature component adds no value over existing practice | do not integrate or displace current care | paradigm-change claim |

An inconclusive result should be labeled by why it is inconclusive: insufficient precision, invalid construct, inadequate range, missing exposure, attrition, conflicting outcomes, or genuine observational equivalence. “More research” is not a result category unless the next discriminating study is specified.

## Evidence-development obligations

The Chapter 29 evidence packet must support:

1. research-program and causal-pathway development;
2. construct definition and measurement validation;
3. direct educational-outcome evaluation and adverse effects;
4. staged assessment and intervention development, including mechanism–outcome discordance;
5. implementation science, fidelity, reach, cost, equity, and sustainability;
6. health-system added value, opportunity cost, overdiagnosis, governance, and distribution of benefit and harm;
7. negative-result interpretation, stopping, replication, and re-entry;
8. provenance and evidence-state preservation across handoffs;
9. exact reuse of one or two Book I pathways without expanding their medical claims; and
10. existing disciplines, measures, interventions, and system models as comparators.

Authoritative methods frameworks may define the tracks. Exact mechanistic, measurement, educational, therapeutic, implementation, and health-system claims require primary or domain-appropriate evidence. The packet should not become a bibliography of every possible project.
