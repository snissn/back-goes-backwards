## Drafting decision

The archive's integrated-system argument should be recovered generously. Its durable intent is that living support is neither a stack of bones nor a list of isolated tissues. Active muscle, tendon, ligament, fascia, cartilage, joint capsules, sliding interfaces, fluids, pressure, sensory control, and prior mechanical state cooperate across time. The archive also proposes that this cooperation can acquire a distributed baseline and that a tension-network or tensegrity model may eventually describe important whole-body organization.

Chapter 6 keeps all of those ideas in the project. It does not, however, make their strongest versions do foundational work before their scale and variables are defined. Four archive compressions require reconstruction:

1. anatomical continuity becomes a scale-controlled ladder rather than immediate proof of body-wide force transfer;
2. “baseline tone” becomes a family of local and regional state variables before it becomes a global set-point hypothesis;
3. pressure becomes a boundary-mediated mechanical contribution rather than a free-standing core force; and
4. tensegrity becomes a model to instantiate and compare rather than a conclusion obtained from visual resemblance.

This is not an ablation of the tension-network program. It gives that program a researchable foundation and protects its stronger forms for later books.

Chapter 6 therefore preserves:

- support as a coordinated, time-varying division of labor among many tissue and control systems;
- muscle as active, state-dependent, and more than a motor pulling a passive cable;
- tendons, ligaments, fasciae, and other connective tissues as real tensile and guiding structures;
- glide, deformation, containment, and pressure as positive mechanical functions;
- the candidate fascia/muscle → tendon/ligament → bone/joint handoff;
- baseline state and prestress as potentially consequential before visible movement;
- distributed-tension and tensegrity representations as live research models;
- the possibility of long-range mechanical organization, compensation, and retained state; and
- the traditional and embodied research motivations that later books develop.

## Recovery from the `new` Section 1 architecture

The newer Section 1 contains a strong conceptual progression:

```text
integrated structure and function
    -> skeletal architecture
    -> soft-tissue tension network
    -> baseline tone and tensegrity
    -> fascial continuity and internal relations
    -> adaptation, compensation, and clinical consequence
```

The current Book I retains this progression but resolves its layers:

- Chapters 1–4 establish the living mechanical system, quantities, roles, configuration, and paths;
- Chapter 5 develops skeletal geometry, contact, and restraint;
- Chapter 6 develops active, tensile, deformable, sliding, pressure, fascia, and prior-state relations;
- Chapters 7–9 apply those layers to posterior anatomy and task support;
- Chapters 10–19 develop capacity, adverse organization, retained state, and response; and
- Chapters 20–27 test bounded clinical systems before the educational and research synthesis.

This distribution preserves the archive's architecture. It avoids asking one introductory soft-tissue section to prove anatomy, whole-body mechanics, systemic physiology, chronic disease, and treatment at once.

## Recovery from the `new` Section 2 architecture

The durable soft-to-hard sequence is:

```text
active or form-receiving tissue accepts, generates, or redirects demand
    -> tendon, ligament, fascia, capsule, or pressure boundary transfers or constrains it
    -> a graded attachment, contact, or interface changes its local expression
    -> skeletal geometry and external support participate in structural resolution
```

The sequence should appear visibly in Chapter 6 because it is part of the project's authorial core. It is reconstructed in three ways:

1. **branching:** several active, tensile, pressure-mediated, and external-support paths can coexist, while inertial terms remain in the dynamic balance rather than becoming carriers;
2. **reciprocity:** force components can change sign or direction, energy can be stored and returned, and demand can be redistributed rather than migrating once in one direction; and
3. **phase dependence:** the structures and roles at acceptance, transport, placement, and recovery differ.

The stronger later hypothesis—that a preferred global configuration organizes these handoffs toward axial, opening/rotational, expansive, posterior, and Yang directions—remains fully protected. It is not needed to establish that handoffs and internal connective force relations are real questions.

## Clean conceptual recovery

### Preserve directly

- Living support depends on simultaneous skeletal, active, tensile, deformable, sliding, pressure, and control relations.
- Active muscle changes force, timing, direction, and the mechanical state of an assembly.
- Tendons and ligaments can carry substantial tension while deforming and changing role.
- Cartilage and other interfaces can support through controlled deformation and relative movement.
- Pressure can contribute to support through bounded tissues and geometry.
- Named fasciae can connect, partition, contain, provide attachment, permit glide, and transmit tension.
- The mechanical state before obvious movement changes the response to the next demand.
- The simple tissue handoff is a useful first approximation inside a branching living system.
- Tension-network and tensegrity models are legitimate candidates for formal comparison.

### Reconstruct rather than erase

- “One continuous tension network” becomes a set of named connections, measured transfers, coordinated tasks, and a separately tested whole-body model.
- “Baseline tone” becomes declared active, passive, pressure, contact, and history variables at local and regional scales.
- “Evenly distributed tone” becomes a possible model outcome requiring an endpoint and measurement, not a definition of health or efficiency.
- “Bones suspended in fascia” becomes a candidate tension-network representation compared with articular contact, linked-segment, continuum, and pressure models.
- “Fascia governs force transmission” becomes bounded connection, separation, containment, glide, sensing, and transfer claims.
- “Pressure is organized through the body” becomes pressure acting on named surfaces under declared boundary conditions.
- “Load flows from soft to hard” becomes a candidate changing handoff with branches, feedback, graded interfaces, and explicit quantities.
- “The body remembers tension” becomes material, neural, control, and remodeling hypotheses separated by timescale.

### Protect forward

- a preferred posterior or all-Yang whole-body configuration;
- axial, opening/rotational, and expansive signed modes;
- TCM channels, Six Divisions, K1–PC8 coordination, and internal force-line correspondences;
- breath, diaphragm, and pressure as a whole-body organizing driver;
- bandhas, torus circulation, and higher-dimensional geometric models;
- systemic vascular, neural, lymphatic, visceral, immune, metabolic, or autonomic effects;
- disease origin, prevention, recovery, and treatment claims; and
- assessments, self-tests, activation cues, breathing methods, fascial release, and corrective practice.
