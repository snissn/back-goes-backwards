# Pilot Evidence Map

**Scope:** BGB-U-0002, BGB-U-0013, and BGB-U-0024

**Status:** Editorial evidence stress test with focused selection packs; not yet an accepted chapter source pack

**Search date:** 2026-07-11

## Purpose and limits

This map tests whether the three pilot chapters can be developed without collapsing ordinary mechanics, the project's organizing synthesis, tissue physiology, and clinical interpretation into one claim. It is not a systematic review, does not establish prevalence or treatment effects, and does not advance any outline unit to E3 or R3.

The review follows four source-selection rules:

1. use primary experiments and observational studies for measured mechanical, physiological, imaging, and clinical findings;
2. use professional reporting standards, guidelines, and formal consensus documents for current terminology and diagnostic boundaries;
3. use systematic or technical reviews to identify established distinctions, methodological limits, and missing links; and
4. treat formal and bench models as evidence about the behavior of the specified model, not as evidence that a named human structure or condition follows it.

Inclusion records relevance, not blanket endorsement. A source supports only the quantity, population, protocol, and inference it actually examined. Absence of direct support at this stage creates a research or wording obligation; it does not erase the authorial model from the project.

## Executive synthesis

1. **The mechanical grammar is stable; its biological application is conditional.** System boundary, force, moment, pressure, stress, strain, deformation, stiffness, and equilibrium are standard concepts. Living tissues nevertheless require explicit material, geometric, temporal, and active-control assumptions.
2. **Hard and soft remain a viable organizing polarity, not an established tissue taxonomy.** The evidence supports heterogeneous, nonlinear, anisotropic, active, and state-dependent mechanical roles. The stronger proposal—that gross demand is generally best resolved through capable shape-preserving routes so form-receiving structures remain available for their functions—must be operationalized and tested at a stated scale, task, and direction.
3. **The generic maintained-constraint model is mechanically defensible.** External contact, internal pressure, wall properties, longitudinal conditions, tethering, and geometry can jointly maintain the state of a deformable structure. Which factor dominates cannot be inferred from shape alone.
4. **The downstream response is not monotonic or portable across systems.** A smaller lumen, altered course, or reduced excursion does not by itself establish impaired flow, conduction, symptoms, or disease. Regulation, collateral routes, internal state, dose, and time can change or reverse the response.
5. **Tube and nerve evidence must remain separate.** Both can illustrate a general boundary-condition schema, but vessel-wall deformation and fluid flow are not evidence for neural strain, excursion, intraneural circulation, or conduction, and vice versa.
6. **Thoracic-outlet phenotypes are distinct causal problems.** Neurogenic, nonthrombotic venous, thrombotic venous, and arterial presentations require different definitions, measurements, alternatives, and evidence chains even when they share a region.
7. **Positional compression is exposure evidence, not diagnosis.** Venous compression is frequent in asymptomatic volunteers in one controlled MR study, and arterial Doppler changes occurred in nearly three tenths of a small healthy sample. A dynamic image or waveform therefore cannot complete the clinical chain alone.
8. **Objective aTOS is the provisional lead; thrombotic vTOS retains a different job.** A focal bony relation, objective arterial pathology, and distal consequence provide the clearest endpoint chain, while repeated deformation → wall injury remains reconstructed and the definition/referral process enriches the correspondence. Thrombotic vTOS remains the stronger time-dependent transition from proposed exposure to retained intrinsic or mixed pathology.

## BGB-U-0002 — Working language of load

### Evidence posture

This chapter mostly controls definitions and reasoning habits. Its main risk is not that the terms are controversial; it is that familiar definitions may be applied to a living body while silently assuming small deformation, linearity, homogeneity, isotropy, passivity, or a directly measurable internal field.

| Proposition | Present disposition | Source role | Publication obligation |
|---|---|---|---|
| A system boundary determines which interactions are external and internal | Common mechanical ground | Standard mechanics plus a living-tissue application | Draw the boundary and show its reactions before inferring an internal exposure |
| Force, moment, pressure, stress, strain, deformation, work, and energy are distinct quantities | Common mechanical ground | Standard continuum mechanics and measurement literature | Define units, sign and frame conventions, and whether a quantity is imposed, measured, or inferred |
| Stiffness and compliance differ from strength, tolerance, reserve, and functional capacity | Mechanical distinction with biological qualification | Constitutive mechanics plus tissue-specific evidence | Do not assign one permanent scalar property to a living tissue or person |
| Mechanical exposure can differ by magnitude, direction, rate, duration, repetition, sequence, variability, and recovery | Defensible cross-tissue synthesis, not one universal dose function | Tissue-specific mechanobiology examples | Use examples to show dimensions; do not claim one dose metric predicts all tissue responses |
| A load path is a representation of how a defined demand is linked to balancing reactions | Project definition consistent with structural mechanics | Authoritative engineering teaching plus formal review | State demand, equilibrium condition, carriers, scale, and alternative paths |
| A force line may be measured, inferred, modeled, or experienced | Project-controlled distinction | Measurement literature plus explicit authorial vocabulary | Never render an embodied relation as a directly measured vector field without a measurement model |

Useful source anchors:

- [Measuring mechanical stress in living tissues](https://www.nature.com/articles/s42254-020-0184-6) distinguishes force, traction, stress, measurement, and inference in living systems and documents how method assumptions constrain the recovered field.
- [Biomechanics of soft biological tissues and organs, mechanobiology, homeostasis and modelling](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11775666/) reviews nonlinear, heterogeneous, anisotropic, viscoelastic, active, and remodeling behavior and treats mechanical homeostasis as a modeling program rather than one universal set point.
- [TU Delft: Main takeaways on load paths](https://ocw.tudelft.nl/course-readings/4-2-4-main-takeaways-on-load-paths/) provides an authoritative introductory engineering formulation linking an applied load to equilibrium forces; Book I's multicarrier biological use is broader and must be declared as such.
- [Adaptive and injury response of bone to mechanical loading](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3595617/) shows, within a specific tissue and experimental tradition, that magnitude, frequency, cycle structure, and inserted rest can alter response. It supports multidimensional exposure language, not a universal bodily dose equation.

### Hard and soft: evidentiary status

The compact formulation—

> Load wants to travel through the hard body so that the soft body can remain soft.

—should remain visible as the project's organizing proposal. Its defensible technical translation is relational:

- a **hard role** is relatively shape-preserving, constraining, force-transmitting, or structurally resolving for the defined demand;
- a **soft role** is relatively form-receiving and shape-conforming, remaining available to yield, glide, contain, expand, buffer, regulate, or change local shape; and
- the same tissue or assembly can exchange or combine roles across directions, scales, states, and tasks.

The source literature supports the ingredients—diverse material behavior, changing active state, multiaxial loading, and coupled solid-fluid function—but no cited paper establishes hard/soft as a universal biological law. That is acceptable at this stage. A reader-facing application passes the evidence gate only when it specifies:

1. the system, task, direction, scale, and time window;
2. the demand and the proposed hard-role carrier or assembly;
3. the soft-role function whose availability matters;
4. the measured or modeled exposure and comparison state;
5. the intermediate physiological link, if one is claimed; and
6. at least one alternative organization or explanation.

The formulation does **not** by itself establish that deformable tissues should be unloaded, that bone must always be the dominant carrier, that visible straightness is mechanically superior, or that a role assignment predicts health.

## BGB-U-0013 — Constraint-maintained deformation

### Exact model under review

The chapter's general causal order should be:

> imposed and intrinsic boundary conditions → contact, pressure, tethering, bending, traction, or shear → maintained tissue state → shape, wall stress, course, contact, or excursion → system-specific physiological effect → function, finding, or symptom

The first four terms form a mechanical model. The final two are separate biological and clinical propositions.

Maintained does not mean permanent. It means that a state persists or is repeatedly recreated for a declared interval because the relevant imposed conditions, intrinsic material state, active forces, or their combination continue to support it. A valid model therefore needs:

- a reference or comparison state;
- a time window and loading history;
- an account of restoring tendencies;
- explicit extrinsic, intrinsic, and mixed branches; and
- an observation that could distinguish the proposed maintaining relation from ordinary variation or reverse causation.

### Collapsible-tube mechanics

The model literature supports a pressure-area relation and a fluid-structure interaction problem, not a one-variable hose law.

| Source and design | What it supports | Important limit |
|---|---|---|
| [General tube law for collapsible thin- and thick-wall tubes](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jbiomech.2014.04.033): computational model with bench validation | Transmural pressure, wall thickness, material behavior, and cross-sectional area are coupled; collapse is nonlinear | Idealized cylindrical compliant tubes; a constitutive relation is not a patient-specific vessel model |
| [Buckling critical pressures in collapsible tubes relevant for biomedical flows](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10250313/): validated three-dimensional numerical study | Geometry, wall properties, length, and axial prestretch affect buckling thresholds and post-buckling states | The analyzed tube law is explicitly evaluated without flow; perfect-cylinder and material assumptions remain |
| [A novel tube law analysis under anisotropic external load](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39690160/): empirical model with laboratory experiments | Directionally nonuniform external loading can change the pressure-area relation and can be modeled rather than replaced by uniform external pressure | Validation used a urethra-like bench system, not a blood vessel or thoracic-outlet anatomy |
| [Unsteady flow in a collapsible tube subjected to external pressure or body forces](https://doi.org/10.1017/S0022112079001899): theory plus bench experiments | Unsteady flow, wall deformation, and external pressure or body force can interact nonlinearly | Thin-walled idealized tubes and model-specific regimes; no living regulation or clinical outcome |

These sources support the following bounded proposition:

> The state of a compliant conduit can be maintained by the relation among internal pressure, surrounding loading, wall properties, axial conditions, geometry, supports, and flow. Lumen area is both an outcome of that coupled state and one determinant of the subsequent fluid problem.

They do not establish which anatomical structure supplies the external demand in a person, that the skeleton is always the dominant source, or that modest positional narrowing causes impaired flow or symptoms.

### Flow is a separate, non-monotonic link

The chapter should not use a fixed-radius Poiseuille relation as though a living, deforming conduit were rigid, circular, steady, unregulated, and without collateral routes. Pressure gradient, viscosity, length, geometry, wall behavior, pulsatility, active vascular tone, surrounding tissue pressure, gravity, and alternative outflow paths can all matter.

[External compression increases forearm perfusion](https://journals.physiology.org/doi/full/10.1152/japplphysiol.00965.2004) is a useful counterexample. In nine healthy young men, distributed sleeve pressure of 13–23 mmHg increased measured forearm arterial inflow under the studied conditions; pressures above 50 mmHg reduced flow in the limited observations reported. The experiment concerns distributed mild forearm compression, not local arterial kinking, venous outflow obstruction, or thoracic-outlet syndrome. Its value here is narrow but important: external compression does not have one monotonic effect on living perfusion.

The required language is therefore:

- deformation can change the pressure-flow relation under specified conditions;
- the direction and size of that change must be measured or modeled for the system at issue; and
- geometry, maintaining demand, regulation, collateralization, and clinical consequence remain different causal levels.

### Neural mechanics is a separate model

Peripheral nerves move, change shape, and change measured stiffness with limb and joint position. That establishes ordinary mechanical responsiveness, not a clinical entrapment chain.

- [In vivo effects of limb movement on nerve stretch, strain, and tension](https://doi.org/10.3233/BMR-169720) found complex longitudinal and transverse excursion and diameter change across heterogeneous studies; increased strain was not a dominant general result.
- [In vivo effects of joint movement on nerve mechanical properties](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10855485/) found position-related increases in shear-wave stiffness in healthy nerves, with segment, joint position, equipment, and limited study counts constraining interpretation.
- [Impact of neurodynamic sequencing on the median nerve and brachial plexus](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39767243/) measured sequence-dependent shear-wave velocity changes at the median nerve and C5–C6 roots in 31 healthy participants. Shear-wave velocity is a stiffness-related measurement, not direct proof of tensile force, strain, impaired excursion, or pathology.
- [Effects of graded compression on intraneural blood flow](https://doi.org/10.1016/S0363-5023(81)80003-2) found pressure-dependent microcirculatory changes in acutely compressed rabbit tibial nerves. Species, site, pressure application, duration, and injury conditions prevent direct transfer to a human brachial-plexus symptom mechanism.

A constraint-maintained neural claim still needs direct evidence that a defined surrounding relation changes contact pressure, course, excursion, intraneural physiology, or conduction in the relevant human state. Symptom reproduction alone cannot supply that evidence.

### What genuinely generalizes

The tube and neural models share only this abstract schema:

> In a defined system, surrounding and intrinsic boundary conditions can maintain a deformable or sliding structure in a state different from an appropriate reference. That state may include altered shape, wall stress, course, contact, or excursion. Whether it changes flow, conduction, signaling, function, or symptoms is a separate system-, dose-, and time-specific proposition.

The statement is stronger than “space might matter” because it names the maintaining relation. It is narrower than “constraint causes dysfunction” because each downstream arrow remains open to measurement and alternatives.

### Selected non-lumen calibration

The [focused comparison](chapter-13-non-lumen-comparison.md) provisionally selects flexor tendon–SSCT relative glide during controlled healthy finger motion. Separate tendon excursion, SSCT excursion, and relative motion make the interface inspectable without using lumen or flow. Median-nerve excursion during controlled elbow motion remains the reserve.

This selection calibrates the variables and preserves a null branch. The reviewed evidence establishes ordinary task-dependent motion and protocol-specific measurement, not an adverse maintained loss, pathological threshold, recovery after release, symptom mechanism, or intervention. Kinematic reversal must not be called recovery, and relative displacement must not be called shear stress or friction.

### Required countermodels

| Countermodel | Why it matters | Discriminating evidence needed |
|---|---|---|
| Intrinsic material or pathological state | The conduit or nerve may hold its state because of wall disease, thrombosis, scar, edema, adhesion, or remodeling | Imaging, material or physiological findings, history, and response after external conditions change |
| Ordinary functional deformation | Shape change or excursion reduction may be a normal task-dependent state | Asymptomatic controls, within-person comparison, duration, reversibility, and functional endpoint |
| Regulation or collateral compensation | A local geometry change may not produce the expected downstream effect | Pressure, flow, perfusion, volume, conduction, or other direct functional measurement |
| Protective or adaptive constraint | Reduced excursion or increased stiffness may limit another exposure | Task-level outcome, time course, and comparison to an alternative state |
| Reverse causation | Pain, inflammation, weakness, thrombosis, or disease may change control and geometry | Temporal evidence and independent markers of the proposed initiating exposure |
| Measurement artifact or model mismatch | Position, probe pressure, image plane, constitutive assumptions, or surrogate choice may create the apparent relation | Reliability, validity, sensitivity analysis, and an independent modality where feasible |

## BGB-U-0024 — Thoracic outlet worked narrative

### Phenotype boundary

Thoracic outlet syndrome is a regional umbrella, not one mechanism. The [Society for Vascular Surgery reporting standards](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0741521416301914) treat neurogenic, venous, and arterial forms as separate entities that may coexist or overlap. The Book I narrative must go one level more specific:

| Candidate phenotype | Required defining feature | What remains uncertain |
|---|---|---|
| Neurogenic TOS | A phenotype-specific clinical pattern involving the brachial plexus, with alternatives evaluated under an accepted criterion set | No single definitive biomarker; criteria, provocative findings, imaging, and treatment-response evidence remain contested |
| Nonthrombotic venous TOS or McCleery syndrome | Intermittent positional upper-extremity swelling or venous outflow impairment without acute thrombosis | Positional venous compression is common; diagnostic thresholds, natural history, and high-quality cohorts are limited |
| Thrombotic venous TOS or effort thrombosis | Axillary-subclavian upper-extremity deep venous thrombosis with a supported thoracic-outlet mechanical etiology | Thrombosis is objective, but mechanical attribution still requires separation from catheter, cancer, thrombophilia, idiopathic UEDVT, and other causes |
| Arterial TOS | An objective subclavian-artery abnormality caused by extrinsic compression and subsequent injury, under the SVS reporting definition | Positional pulse or waveform change without objective ischemia or arterial injury does not meet that definition; the condition is rare and often anatomy-specific |

Relevant boundary sources:

- The [SVS reporting standards](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0741521416301914) supply the core phenotype separation and reporting language.
- The [2024 INTOS recommendations](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39206209/) are a Delphi consensus among specialist hand surgeons, not validation of a definitive neurogenic test.
- The [2022 European Association of Neurosurgical Societies systematic review and consensus](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9514726/) documents anatomy, classification, and diagnostic disagreement in neurogenic presentations.
- The [ACR Appropriateness Criteria](https://acsearch.acr.org/docs/3083061/Narrative/) organize imaging by neurogenic, venous, and arterial variants; they guide modality selection, not causal interpretation of a positive positional finding.
- The [2026 American Venous Forum guideline for upper-extremity DVT](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41780672/) emphasizes etiology-based classification and identifies continuing evidence gaps in risk stratification and long-term outcomes. It is relevant to thrombotic venous cases, not proof that every primary UEDVT is mechanically caused.

### Positional anatomy and the asymptomatic comparator

The evidence strongly supports dynamic geometry and strongly rejects diagnosis from geometry alone.

1. In a 2003 MR comparison of 35 asymptomatic volunteers and 54 symptomatic patients, [venous compression after the postural maneuver](https://pubs.rsna.org/doi/10.1148/radiol.2272012111) occurred in 47% versus 63% at the prescalene space, 54% versus 61% at the costoclavicular space, and 27% versus 30% at the retropectoralis-minor space. No arterial or nervous compression was reported in the volunteer group under that study's definitions. The small, older study shows substantial overlap for venous geometry; it does not estimate population prevalence.
2. In a 2021 cross-sectional study of 19 healthy volunteers, [28.9% of the sample experienced a Doppler waveform interpreted as significant arterial compression in at least one provocative position](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33868673/). The sample is small and the protocol-specific rate is not a prevalence estimate, but it directly warns against isolated provocative ultrasound interpretation.
3. A 2025 retrospective reliability study of 51 CT and 58 MR examinations found that a [standardized area-change method](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40659084/) had good CT but poor-to-moderate MR inter- and intra-observer reliability. The study assessed measurement reliability in referred imaging cohorts, not diagnostic validity, symptom causation, or an abnormality threshold.
4. In 424 patients referred for suspected TOS, a [venous photoplethysmography measure](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/cardiovascular-medicine/articles/10.3389/fcvm.2022.803919/full) had limited accuracy for ultrasound-defined positional compression. The authors emphasized that ultrasound depicts compression whereas plethysmography estimates hemodynamic consequence, and that collateral veins can dissociate the two. This distinction directly supports the chapter's evidence ladder.

No single percentage should be generalized across modality, maneuver, threshold, population, or phenotype.

### Evidence ladder for the selected case

| Level | Question | Minimum evidence |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Regional anatomy | Which structure traverses which bounded region? | Authoritative anatomy plus the selected person's or cohort's relevant variant |
| 2. Imposed exposure | What task, position, external load, active tension, or anatomy changes the boundary conditions? | Kinematics, contact, pressure, imaging across states, or a clearly labeled formal model |
| 3. Local tissue state | What deformation, wall injury, contact, strain, tethering, or excursion change occurs? | Tissue-specific measurement; geometry cannot substitute for neural or vascular physiology |
| 4. Physiological consequence | What changes in pressure, flow, volume, perfusion, intraneural circulation, conduction, or another function? | Objective tissue-appropriate endpoint measured in the relevant state |
| 5. Clinical contribution | Does the physiological change contribute to the defined presentation? | Phenotype-specific clinical evidence, time course, alternatives, and medical review |
| 6. Vignette interpretation | Is the complete chain supported in this case? | Traceable observed findings, explicit inferences, missing links, and competing accounts |

Evidence at one level does not license the next. In particular:

- anatomical possibility does not establish exposure;
- positional narrowing does not establish impaired flow;
- impaired flow does not establish symptom attribution;
- symptom reproduction does not identify the affected structure; and
- response to movement or treatment does not retroactively prove the proposed cause.

### Candidate leading cases

#### Objective arterial injury

An arterial presentation with a cervical rib, anomalous first rib, healed fracture, or another defined external structure plus objective subclavian-artery injury can provide the cleanest complete chain:

> defined anatomy and task → repeated external arterial deformation → wall injury or post-stenotic change → objective hemodynamic or distal arterial consequence → clinical presentation

Its strengths are a visible external constraint, tissue-specific pathology, and an SVS definition that requires objective arterial abnormality rather than positional compression alone. Its limits are rarity, selection into specialist cohorts, and dependence on a focal anatomical lesion. It would demonstrate a mechanical principle, not ordinary posture or a common universal route.

#### Thrombotic venous TOS

Effort thrombosis can show a richer temporal transition:

> repeated costoclavicular exposure plus individual susceptibility → endothelial and mural injury, altered outflow, or stasis → thrombosis → retained intrinsic obstruction, collateralization, and a changed response to future external demand

The objective thrombus makes the clinical state inspectable, but the initiating chain is often reconstructed rather than observed prospectively. The [TROTS registry protocol](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371/journal.pone.0279708) is especially useful as a map of the evidence gap: the literature is dominated by small retrospective specialist cohorts, diagnostic criteria for the compression component are not uniform, compression can be physiological, and vascular damage does not map simply to symptoms. This case is strongest if Chapter 24 wants to show how an initially extrinsic relation can become mixed and path-dependent.

#### Nonthrombotic venous obstruction

McCleery syndrome offers the most direct reversible sequence from position to outflow and swelling without introducing thrombus as an intrinsic state. However, the named literature is small. A [2014 retrospective surgical series](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/1538574413512380) included 19 patients presenting with intermittent arm swelling, three of whom had chronic thrombus. That cohort can establish that a selected clinical group has been described; it cannot define diagnostic accuracy, natural history, population frequency, or the general causal effect of decompression. The phenotype should not lead unless the focused pack finds stronger objective hemodynamic and control data.

#### Neurogenic comparison

Neurogenic TOS is best retained as a contrast rather than the first hose-inspired demonstration. It can show why contact pressure, stiffness, strain, excursion, intraneural circulation, conduction, symptoms, and diagnostic criteria cannot be substituted for one another. Its uncertainty is informative, but a generic neurogenic narrative would blur rather than clarify the pilot method.

### Provisional editorial recommendation

The [focused phenotype comparison](thoracic-outlet-phenotype-comparison.md) supports this working arrangement:

- **Lead:** objective aTOS using a source-grounded composite with focal bony anatomy, objective adjacent artery-wall pathology, and a documented distal embolic or ischemic consequence.
- **Weakest arrow:** repeated imposed deformation → wall injury, always labeled reconstructed unless direct evidence is found.
- **Required negative comparison:** healthy provocative arterial waveform change and bony variation without aTOS.
- **Time-dependent comparison:** thrombotic vTOS as a compact event-to-retained-state timeline.
- **Reserve:** nonthrombotic venous obstruction.
- **Within-region contrast:** neurogenic TOS, used to show that neural variables require a different chain.

The direction remains provisional pending author and medical-coauthor approval. Its purpose is not to make aTOS representative of thoracic-outlet presentations; it gives Chapter 24 one bounded case whose strong and weak arrows can both remain visible.

### Phenotype-specific alternatives

| Phenotype | Alternatives that must remain inside the narrative |
|---|---|
| Arterial | ordinary provocative waveform change, embolic disease from another source, intrinsic arterial disease, trauma, vasospasm, measurement artifact, and an incidental bony variant |
| Thrombotic venous | catheter or device, cancer, thrombophilia, inflammatory or systemic prothrombotic state, idiopathic UEDVT, trauma, and physiological positional compression incidental to the clot |
| Nonthrombotic venous | ordinary positional compression, collateral compensation, heart-level and gravitational effects, lymphatic or other causes of swelling, intrinsic stenosis, and measurement disagreement |
| Neurogenic | cervical radiculopathy, distal entrapment, other plexopathy, shoulder disorder, pain sensitization, trauma, systemic neuropathy, and mixed vascular-neural presentation |

## Review gates before drafting

| Gate | Pass condition |
|---|---|
| FORM | The Chapter 2 notation sheet and Chapter 13 tube and non-lumen interface models have technical review; every equation states assumptions, variables, units, and boundary conditions |
| DEF / CLIN | Chapter 24 names one leading phenotype, the distinct job of every retained comparison, and the exact criterion set used for each clinically classified presentation |
| EVID | Focused source packs record search date, databases, inclusion logic, study design, population, endpoint, limitations, and conflicts or funding where material |
| LINK | Every arrow in the selected causal chain has direct support, a labeled formal inference, or an explicit unresolved status; evidence is not borrowed from another phenotype |
| ALT | At least one live competing explanation is attached to every medically exposed transition, with a proposed discriminating observation |
| MEAS | The vignette identifies observable exposure, tissue-state, physiological, and functional variables and the timing of each |
| PROV / CLIN | Every vignette fact is traceable to a published case, approved source-grounded composite, or clearly hypothetical element |
| CLIN | The medical coauthor approves terminology, phenotype, alternatives, and exclusions; vascular expertise reviews the condition-specific chain, and hand/wrist anatomy and biomechanics review the tendon–SSCT comparison |
| VIS | Figures visually distinguish observed anatomy, measured quantity, model output, authorial synthesis, and unresolved causal inference |
| AUD / EVID | The claim ledger records the strongest permissible reader-facing wording for each medically exposed proposition |

If a gate fails, the default response is to narrow the claim, change the leading phenotype, expose the missing arrow, or retain the mechanism as a research program. Failure of one clinical instantiation does not by itself invalidate or delete the generic hard/soft or maintained-constraint model.

Until those choices and the remaining review gates are approved, the pilot remains a well-specified editorial and research program rather than draft-ready medical prose.
