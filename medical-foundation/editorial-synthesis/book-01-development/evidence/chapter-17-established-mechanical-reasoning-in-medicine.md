## Editorial decision

Chapter 17 should concede its strongest skeptical objection immediately:

> Medicine already reasons mechanically. It measures and changes pressure, flow, volume, patency, stability, contact, geometry, support, and force distribution; it also substitutes for mechanical functions that a person cannot presently perform. These practices establish that mechanical conditions can be legitimate medical endpoints. They do not, by themselves, reconstruct the complete route and history by which the current condition arose. Endpoint management and longitudinal routing are therefore related but non-identical objects. The latter earns a place only where it adds something testable to existing practice.

This is common ground, not a novelty claim. Cardiopulmonary care, surgery, orthopedics, rehabilitation, prosthetics, ergonomics, and biomechanics already contain mature forms of mechanical reasoning. The chapter's job is to name a small representative set accurately, then expose a narrower inverse problem: several prior histories may be compatible with the same present endpoint.

The archive's proposed gap remains intact but changes status. “Medicine lacks a unified load-routing framework” is an authorial integration hypothesis to be tested against existing disciplines in Chapter 18. It is not a factual premise authorized by this chapter. A useful routing history may improve explanation, prediction, recurrence analysis, research design, or communication in some contexts. It may be unnecessary for the immediate decision in others.

## The endpoint record

The chapter needs one recurring record rather than a catalogue of procedures:

```text
clinical or functional problem
    -> selected system boundary
    -> measured or inferred mechanical variable
    -> intended local or system-level mechanical change
    -> achieved endpoint and uncertainty
    -> biology and person-level context retained
    -> prior route and history known / partly reconstructed / unnecessary / unresolved
```

The mechanical variable must remain specific. Pressure is not flow. Flow is not perfusion. Tidal volume is not tissue strain. Compression depth is not coronary perfusion pressure. Plate stiffness is not interfragmentary strain at every point. Socket pressure is not total interface traction, comfort, or tissue tolerance. A clinical endpoint may integrate several of these quantities, but it does not make them interchangeable.

The last line prevents two opposite errors. A local intervention can work without revealing one initiating history. Conversely, the fact that history is unresolved does not make the local endpoint unreal or its management intellectually incomplete. Chapter 18 must show where an additional routing record changes a question that matters.

## Pressure, flow, and mechanical substitution are already ordinary medicine

The 2025 American Heart Association adult basic-life-support guideline offers a direct example. During cardiac arrest, chest compressions are required for forward flow; pauses are followed by a rapid fall in coronary perfusion pressure, and rate, depth, recoil, interruptions, ventilation, rhythm, and other care interact.[^kleinman2025] This is unambiguously mechanical reasoning with a medical purpose. It also resists reduction to one variable. Compression depth is a controllable input, coronary perfusion pressure is a physiological-mechanical intermediate, and return of spontaneous circulation and survival are distinct outcomes.

The example establishes substitution in its clearest form. External compression temporarily performs part of a pump function that the arrested circulation is not providing. It does not identify why arrest occurred, repair myocardium, restore every regulatory process, or guarantee survival. Nor does a more elaborate whole-body routing reconstruction need to precede the immediate act. This is a required counterexample to the claim that upstream history is always necessary for useful care.

Ventilation provides a second, more visibly reciprocal example. The same AHA guideline states that ventilation is necessary for oxygen delivery and carbon-dioxide elimination in respiratory arrest with spontaneous circulation.[^kleinman2025] A pressure or volume cycle can therefore support a necessary physical function while the initiating disease or event remains separately investigated.

Yet support itself changes exposure. The official ATS/ESICM/SCCM guideline for adults with acute respiratory distress syndrome recommends lower tidal volumes and limited inspiratory plateau pressures for that defined population, while explicitly retaining disease state and individual clinical features.[^fan2017] The 2024 ATS update adds conditional recommendations involving positive end-expiratory pressure, extracorporeal support, corticosteroids, and neuromuscular blockade, and recommends against prolonged recruitment maneuvers in moderate-to-severe ARDS.[^qadir2024] Together they support the following bounded synthesis rather than a new clinical recommendation:

- pressure, volume, flow, timing, gas exchange, chest-wall mechanics, lung state, circulation, and biological injury are coupled but different;
- external support can be lifesaving and can also create or redistribute mechanical demand;
- the same nominal ventilator setting does not imply the same regional tissue exposure in every lung; and
- mechanical management remains embedded in inflammatory, infectious, hemodynamic, neurological, pharmacological, and whole-person care.

The reader-facing prose should not teach CPR or ventilator settings. It should use these official examples to show that mechanical substitution is accepted medical reasoning and that a mechanical endpoint can be necessary without becoming a total account of illness.

## Fixation controls a mechanical environment without replacing biology

Fracture fixation provides a structural example with unusually explicit boundary conditions. AO Surgery Reference distinguishes compression plating, in which the plate produces fracture-site compression for absolute stability, from bridge plating that provides relative interfragmentary stability, restores length and transverse and rotational alignment, and leaves the fracture zone relatively undisturbed while callus forms.[^ao-plating] In the bridge-plating model, external force is transferred through the plate-and-screw construct; if the gap closes, load sharing between construct and bone changes.

The legitimate endpoint is not “the bone is mechanically solved.” It is a selected relation among fragments and construct: alignment, continuity, restraint, permitted motion, and load sharing appropriate to a fracture pattern and healing strategy. The same page preserves blood supply and soft-tissue disturbance as design concerns. Mechanical stability and biological environment are cooperating requirements, not competing explanations.

A recent large-animal experiment makes that interaction measurable. Ten sheep received experimental tibial osteotomies and active fixators that generated a spatial gradient from 2.5 to 25 percent interfragmentary strain. The sheep were randomly assigned: five were loaded from the first postoperative day and five from day 22. At five weeks, high-resolution computed tomography analyses indexed to strain level quantified repair-tissue area and density within the osteotomy and the radial span of periosteal tissue. Those outcomes varied with strain magnitude and loading time; lower tested strains favored more tissue within the gap, while higher strains shifted calcified repair tissue toward external callus.[^barcik2026] The strain gradient was generated by the experimental construct; the tissue outcomes were measured from the images. This experiment does not define a human treatment threshold or validate a one-dimensional strain theory for every fracture. It shows that a mechanical descriptor, spatial distribution, biological response, and timing can be experimentally separated.

Fixation therefore contributes three propositions:

1. medicine intentionally constructs a load path and controls relative motion;
2. a targeted mechanical state can be a legitimate prerequisite for healing; and
3. the endpoint does not erase fracture biology, vascularity, tissue damage, infection, medication, nutrition, age, behavior, or time.

For Chapter 17, no operative technique, fracture classification, or numerical strain target should enter reader prose. The fracture example exists to establish ordinary medical reasoning about continuity, stability, and load sharing.

## Decompression changes a local condition without recovering one unique cause

Carpal-tunnel pressure studies provide a bounded decompression example because the mechanical endpoint was measured before and after release. Sanz, Lizaur, and Sánchez del Campo measured carpal-canal pressure in a deliberately narrow cohort of 103 women aged 20–50 with unilateral idiopathic carpal tunnel syndrome and in 25 controls, across neutral, flexed, and extended wrist positions. Preoperative pressure was higher in the clinical group, changed with wrist position, and fell markedly immediately after open release. Pressure rose somewhat by the second postoperative month but remained within the control range during one-year follow-up.[^sanz2005]

This supports a narrow claim: dividing the transverse carpal ligament altered a measured pressure condition within the selected canal. The study also recorded symptoms, tests, and grip strength through time, but improvement in those measures does not turn pressure into the only state variable. Participants were selected for a clinical procedure, the comparison was not a randomized causal decomposition of every possible mechanism, and the intervention cannot reconstruct the complete origin of each presentation.

Ahn and colleagues make the non-equivalence explicit. In an operative sample of 48 wrists from 39 people undergoing endoscopic release, most with severe or extreme electrodiagnostic grades, maximum static carpal-tunnel pressure measured with the wrist neutral fell from 56.7 to 7.4 mm Hg after release. Preoperative maximum pressure did not correlate with electrodiagnostic severity or median-nerve cross-sectional area, although the latter two correlated with each other.[^ahn2009] A mechanically dramatic endpoint and other clinically relevant measurements therefore did not form one scalar severity axis.

The chapter should use this example to distinguish four objects:

| Object | What the cited studies can show | What remains unresolved |
|---|---|---|
| Local geometry and position | Pressure differs by wrist position and release state | One universally optimal wrist geometry |
| Canal pressure | A measured drop follows ligament release in selected cohorts | Complete nerve loading through time or one causal threshold |
| Nerve structure and function | Cross-sectional area and electrodiagnostic measures can be recorded | Direct equivalence with pressure or guaranteed reversibility |
| Symptoms and performance | They can change after the intervention | Unique initiating cause, prognosis for one person, or body-wide route |

This is decompression without a metaphysical claim about “space.” A changed constraint can change pressure, contact, deformation, and the ability of tissues to occupy and move within a local region. The cited studies directly measured pressure; which other variables mediated a clinical change remains a separate question. The example is not a diagnostic or treatment template.

## A living interface is more than a device that survives

A prosthetic socket must transmit forces and moments between a device and living tissue. Component integrity is necessary but not sufficient. The interface has finite area; pressure and shear vary with region, task, phase, fit, material, suspension, motion, volume, and tissue state.

Paternò and colleagues recorded in-socket pressure at anterior, lateral, posterior, and medial regions in ten men with unilateral transfemoral amputation using three socket designs across level walking, ramp ascent and descent, and stair ascent and descent. Pressure patterns and extrema varied by location and task; sensor failure reduced the available sample for several tasks, and the small design subgroups supported only qualitative comparison.[^paterno2024] The study demonstrates local distribution and task dependence. It does not establish a universal fit metric, comfort threshold, tissue-injury threshold, or preferred socket design.

In a single person with transtibial amputation, Devin and colleagues changed sock layers to simulate looser and tighter fit and measured pressure and shear at three anatomical sites during standing and level walking. Small fit changes produced site- and phase-specific changes, and pressure and shear did not move as one uniform quantity.[^devin2023] The single-case design makes the finding illustrative rather than general. It is especially useful because it prevents “pressure distribution” from becoming shorthand for the entire interface traction state.

An older single-participant alignment study supplies a temporal distinction. Jia and colleagues compared three sagittal prosthetic-alignment settings while measuring pressure at five residual-limb sites. Duration above 90 percent of peak pressure and time–pressure integral changed more clearly than peak pressure alone.[^jia2008] One subject cannot establish clinical consequences, but the result shows why peak, duration, and integrated exposure answer different questions.

Together the interface studies establish a familiar medical-engineering problem:

```text
external task and contact
    -> device geometry, alignment, suspension, and control
    -> distributed pressure and shear through time
    -> local tissue deformation and perfusion not fully observed
    -> comfort, skin state, participation, and device use separately observed
```

The device may be structurally intact while the living interface is poorly matched. It may also create a capable, comfortable, and valued extension of the person. Neither state can be inferred from a pressure peak alone. Assistance belongs inside the system boundary, consistent with Chapters 9, 14, and 15; it is not an artificial deviation from a supposedly true unsupported body.

## Endpoint and history are complementary only when both earn their keep

The chapter should compare its domains through the same columns:

| Domain | Selected mechanical endpoint | What the endpoint establishes | Biology and context retained | Upstream history that may remain unknown |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Resuscitation | compression-supported forward flow and perfusion conditions | an external action can substitute part of pump function | rhythm, cause, oxygenation, drugs, delay, injury, system of care | why arrest occurred; often unnecessary before immediate support |
| Assisted ventilation | bounded pressure, volume, flow, gas-exchange, and protection targets | a device can support breathing or gas exchange | lung and chest-wall state, inflammation, circulation, neurological state, cause | how the current respiratory failure developed; may or may not change immediate settings |
| Fracture fixation | alignment, continuity, restraint, relative motion, and load sharing | a construct can create a selected healing environment | blood supply, soft tissue, infection, biology, time, behavior | full injury and loading history; often partly known from trauma |
| Carpal decompression | reduced local canal pressure after release | the targeted constraint mattered to the measured local endpoint | nerve state, symptom history, anatomy, systemic contributors, recovery | one unique origin or complete prior route |
| Prosthetic interface | task- and site-specific pressure and shear distribution | device fit and task change local interface mechanics | tissue state, comfort, goals, volume, adaptation, environment | prior compensations; sometimes irrelevant to immediate fit problem |

One endpoint can follow several histories. A high local pressure might follow anatomy, swelling, position, external contact, retained tissue change, or combinations. A fracture construct may be selected after different injury mechanisms and tissue states. A similar socket-pressure pattern may occur under different gait strategies, fit histories, or tissue conditions. The endpoint does not contain a unique recording of its past.

That is an underdetermination claim, not proof that a complete history can be recovered. A routing account can itself be wrong, overfitted, or unnecessary. It must specify its measurements, modeled links, alternatives, and incremental result. A more expansive story is not better because it contains more regions.

## Required counterexamples and nulls

The reader-facing chapter should retain five checks against self-sealing integration:

1. **Immediate support without history.** CPR can be required before arrest etiology or whole-body routing is reconstructed.
2. **Local endpoint without one severity axis.** Carpal-tunnel pressure can fall sharply while pressure, nerve cross-sectional area, and electrodiagnostic severity remain non-equivalent.
3. **Mechanical environment plus biology.** Fixation can establish continuity and stability while blood supply, soft tissue, tissue response, and time still shape healing.
4. **Distribution without outcome.** A socket pressure map can be accurate yet insufficient to predict comfort, skin response, participation, or long-term tissue state.
5. **No incremental routing value.** If a routing history does not improve explanation, prediction, recurrence analysis, measurement, communication, or research design beyond existing practice, Chapter 18's proposed addition has not earned relevance in that context.

These are not concessions added after the thesis. They define the thesis's domain.

## Measurement and disfavoring program

| Question | Minimum record | What would narrow or disfavor the proposed interpretation |
|---|---|---|
| Is a mechanical variable already an accepted medical endpoint? | current specialty source, exact variable, boundary, intended result | the example uses mechanical language rhetorically but does not measure or intentionally alter the variable |
| Is function being mechanically substituted? | failed or unavailable function, external action or device, measured intermediate, clinical purpose | support does not produce the proposed physical intermediate or the function is biologically rather than mechanically defined |
| Did fixation or decompression change the selected local condition? | before/after geometry, pressure, motion, stability, or load-sharing measure | no reliable change, incompatible measurement, or endpoint defined only by symptom response |
| Does interface distribution matter? | task, geometry, pressure/shear location and time, tissue or user result | distribution is not reproducible, does not covary with fit/task, or adds no useful information over global load |
| Does upstream history add value? | endpoint-only account, routing-history account, declared prediction or decision, prospective comparison | equal explanation or outcome, unstable reconstruction, added false positives, or no actionable research difference |

## Authorization

This completed independent source and chapter-boundary audit authorizes a controlled Chapter 17 draft organized around four representative domains. External specialty and medical-coauthor reviews remain publication gates. The packet authorizes the claim that mechanics is already inside medicine and the narrower claim that a present local endpoint does not uniquely encode its upstream history. It does not authorize the claim that medicine lacks biomechanics, that longitudinal routing is novel or always valuable, that response to a mechanical intervention proves a mechanical origin, or that the selected procedures validate the project's later preferred-configuration program.

The chapter succeeds when a skeptical clinician can agree with nearly all of it before deciding whether Chapter 18's proposed addition is useful.
