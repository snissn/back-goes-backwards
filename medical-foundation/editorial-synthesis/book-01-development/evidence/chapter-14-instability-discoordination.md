## Editorial decision

Chapter 14 should preserve the archive's strong intuition that small failures of control, repeated correction, mistimed participation, and protective bracing can matter even when a task is completed and no material lesion is visible. It should make that intuition testable without turning unfamiliar motion into pathology:

> Instability is a task-specific failure to maintain or recover a named mechanical relation within a declared tolerance. Discoordination is a task-specific mismatch in timing, sequence, or phase among participants that are otherwise available. Either state may provoke correction or bracing, but neither follows from movement amount, variability, pain, or muscle activity alone.

The chapter owns a **control-event account**, not a diagnosis. Its primary observable is a time series around a perturbation or task transition:

```text
initial task state
    -> declared perturbation or changing demand
    -> relation and timing response
    -> corrective action or alternate strategy
    -> recovery, nonrecovery, or successful adaptation
    -> repetition under a defined history
```

The final endpoint is only one measurement in that sequence. A successful endpoint can conceal extra corrections, but extra corrections can also be ordinary exploration or learning. Bracing can increase mechanical stiffness and preserve a relation; it can also increase internal force, restrict options, or become irrelevant to the outcome. Those possibilities must remain branches, not a predetermined cascade.

## Operational definitions

### Stability requires a relation, not a look

For a declared task, let `y(t)` name one measured relation: contact state, joint translation or rotation, center-of-mass relation to support, segment relation, or another bounded performance variable. Let `Y_task(t)` name the task-relevant acceptable set rather than one ideal path. A perturbation begins at `t_p`.

The chapter can ask:

- whether `y(t)` remains inside the declared set;
- if it leaves, how far and for how long;
- whether and when it returns;
- which contacts, forces, moments, motions, and activations accompany recovery; and
- whether the same response recurs as demand, uncertainty, or fatigue changes.

This is a record, not a universal scalar stability score. The acceptable set can be asymmetric, phase-dependent, person-specific, and altered by a device or environment. A large movement may be controlled; a small movement may occur near a consequential contact boundary; a relatively still body may be actively stabilized. The chapter should use *instability* only when the required relation and recovery criterion are stated.

This operational use is narrower than many clinical uses of *mechanical*, *functional*, or *perceived* instability. It does not convert laxity, a giving-way report, pain, or imaging into the same object. Those findings may motivate different measurements, but they cannot fill the relation record by themselves.

### Discoordination requires a timing comparison

Discoordination is not a synonym for instability. It names a candidate relation among time-varying participants:

```text
event or phase marker
    -> participant A onset, peak, offset, or contact transition
    -> participant B onset, peak, offset, or contact transition
    -> task-relevant overlap, gap, or order
    -> measured mechanical or performance consequence
```

The event marker can be perturbation onset, heel contact, release of an object, a change in support, or another observable boundary. The participants can be muscles, joints, contacts, pressure compartments, or segments, but their measured quantities must not be collapsed. EMG onset is an electrical observation affected by method; it is not muscle force. Joint moment is a net calculation; it is not one muscle's contribution. Phase difference becomes consequential only if it predicts the declared relation, exposure, correction, or outcome.

Healthy young adults exposed to unexpected waist-pull perturbations showed direction- and magnitude-dependent timing in lower-limb muscle activity, joint moment, and motion; larger perturbations produced faster muscle responses but not a uniform change in all kinetic or kinematic measures.[^zhu2022] In another healthy overground-walking experiment, fifteen participants used several recovery strategies—foot placement, ankle inversion or eversion, push-off, and trunk adjustment—with their contributions depending on perturbation direction.[^karabin2024] These studies establish that recovery is coordinated across time and regions. They do not define one correct sequence.

### Correction and bracing are responses, not failure classes

A correction is an observed response that attempts to maintain or restore the declared relation after an actual or anticipated disturbance. It becomes identifiable only relative to an event marker and that relation. Bracing is an active strategy that changes task-specific stiffness or available motion, often through co-contraction but not defined by one muscle pattern. It can precede a perturbation and occur without demonstrated instability.

Instability and discoordination can coexist, but neither may be inferred from the other. A correction can respond to relation loss, a timing mismatch, uncertainty, or ordinary task error; bracing can be anticipatory, corrective, or protective. None of these observations establishes pathology or a poorly matched strategy without a named consequence and comparison.

## One endpoint, several legitimate strategies

Motor variability must be divided into variation that changes a task result and variation that preserves it. In a virtual throwing task, Müller and Sternad decomposed learning-related improvement into noise reduction, exploitation of task tolerance, and covariation among execution variables.[^muller2004] In a redundant reaching experiment, participants could achieve the same endpoint through different shoulder, elbow, and wrist configurations; reinforcement shifted the joint-space solution without changing the task goal.[^mehler2017]

A direct balance comparison makes the point more concrete. Twenty-four healthy young adults recovered successfully from lateral platform perturbations in both single-leg and bipedal stance. The two conditions had similar damping effectiveness but different directional recovery actions.[^petro2018] The endpoint and one performance measure therefore did not identify one movement solution.

Chapter 14 should use this as the required null branch:

```text
visible variation
    -> task relation preserved
    -> strategy adapts when the perturbation changes
    -> no important rise in the selected exposure
    -> capable variability, not demonstrated instability
```

Variation may also be exploratory, measurement noise, anatomical accommodation, device interaction, or a learned specialty. A strategy is not disfavored because it differs from a group mean. It becomes a failure candidate only when the declared task relation, adaptability, exposure, or recovery criterion corresponds.

## Bracing is a mechanical strategy before it is a cost

Co-contraction can change a system's response to perturbation. In seven healthy participants, strong ankle antagonist co-contraction produced greater measured joint stiffness than plantar-flexion activation at similar strength, although reflex behavior and weak co-contraction results were not uniform across people.[^nielsen1994] In ten healthy men performing lifting tasks, Granata and Marras combined measured motion, reaction loads, and trunk EMG with a spine model. Greater antagonistic co-contraction was associated with both greater modeled spinal compression and a larger modeled stability margin.[^granata2000]

The correct conclusion is conditional:

- bracing can be a useful way to maintain a relation;
- its benefit and cost depend on the task and model;
- increased stiffness can reduce some movements while changing forces or available options;
- increased activation does not establish fear, pathology, inefficiency, or sensory deficit; and
- lower activation does not establish improvement.

The chapter should compare at least four bracing states:

| State | What may be preserved | What might change | What would keep the judgment open |
|---|---|---|---|
| Anticipatory task stabilization | contact or relation before a known demand | stiffness, internal force, effort | equivalent outcome without bracing may not be available |
| Reactive protection | relation after pain, surprise, or perturbation | excursion, correction, activation | short-lived protection may be appropriate |
| Learned economical strategy | endpoint with reduced unnecessary correction | activation pattern, timing | economy is outcome- and denominator-specific |
| Persistent or poorly matched bracing | one relation while another option or exposure changes | force, fatigue, variability, contact | persistence and consequence require longitudinal measurement |

This table preserves the author's concern that chronic bracing may become costly while refusing to infer that cost from the presence of activation alone.

## Repeated correction can be recorded as a dose without being called damage

Repeated perturbation experiments show why one successful trial is inadequate. In twenty-six healthy young adults exposed to ten stance-slip perturbations, several muscle-synergy activation features changed between early and late trials while other synergy structures changed little.[^wang2024] In twenty-three people with chronic ankle instability and twenty-three controls performing repeated mediolateral single-leg perturbations, practice changed margin-of-stability and hip-torque coordination in the instability group, while co-contraction adaptation differed by muscle pair and group.[^xu2026] The latter study is a clinical-group experiment, not evidence that any correction causes injury or that its group labels generalize to the reader.

For one named corrective event `k`, the chapter can retain a record such as:

```text
time of event t_k
perturbation magnitude and direction
departure of the declared relation
recovery time
contact, force, moment, motion, or activation response
modeled local exposure, if available
state before the next event
```

Across `N` events, cumulative count, time, work, impulse, activation, excursion, or another quantity can be summarized only when that quantity answers the bounded question. There is no generic “correction dose.” Nor may the draft call a kinematic oscillation *microtrauma*. To reach fatigue, pain, injury, or degeneration, the account must identify a receiver, local exposure, capacity criterion, biological response, chronology, and alternative explanation. Chapter 19 owns that bridge.

Repeated correction also has an affirmative branch. Practice can reduce, reorganize, or better target a response. A learner may initially make many useful corrections. A disabled or aging person may use a device or alternate joint strategy that makes a task possible. An athlete may accept greater activation for precision. A correction history is evidence about control work, not a moral score.

## The sensory–bracing loop remains a branching hypothesis

The archive proposes a loop in which changed mechanics alters proprioceptive information, uncertainty increases guarding or bracing, bracing changes motion and sensory input, and the altered state reinforces further protection. Each link is plausible in some bounded settings, but the full loop is not established by any single line of evidence.

Experimental pain provides both positive and null branches. Across four experimental pain conditions completed by ten to twelve participants per condition (twelve healthy adults total; nine completed all four), movement-direction detection was impaired at some anatomically relevant thumb muscle or skin sites but not at nearby control sites; selected other perceptual judgments were unchanged.[^weerakkody2008] An ankle experiment found movement-detection thresholds changed only with relatively high pain induced in two antagonist muscles, while position recognition remained unchanged.[^matre2002] In twenty-eight participants given nerve growth factor injections that produced sustained forearm pain and hyperalgesia, wrist joint-position error did not change over the measured days.[^summers2019]

These studies support four controls:

1. pain can precede a sensory change, so sensory difference need not be the original cause;
2. sensory effects are site-, measure-, intensity-, and time-dependent;
3. a null result for one sensory measure does not establish normality of every sensory process; and
4. a sensory difference does not by itself establish instability, bracing, or a mechanical consequence.

The reader-facing loop should therefore branch:

```text
changed position, motion, tissue state, pain, expectation, or instruction
    -> selected sensory measure changes / does not change / is not measured
    -> activation or bracing changes / does not change / precedes the sensory finding
    -> stiffness, contact, effort, or options change / remain adequate / improve
    -> protection resolves / persists / is replaced / has no demonstrated consequence
```

Reverse sequences remain eligible: instruction, expectation, pain, or task demand can provoke bracing first, after which changed motion or contact may alter a sensory measure.

The full feedback hypothesis gains support only if time order is measured, its component arrows covary as predicted, manipulation of one term changes the next, and competing accounts do less explanatory work. It should be narrowed if pain, task instruction, fatigue, inflammation, habit, environmental constraint, or measurement error explains the observations without the proposed loop.

## Primary worked comparison

Use a hypothetical healthy participant in a repeated mediolateral support-surface perturbation task. The scene is constructed from the variables used in the cited perturbation studies, not presented as one study participant or patient.

### Declared task

- stand or step within a defined support condition;
- receive perturbations with known onset, direction, and magnitude;
- preserve or recover a declared center-of-mass/support or segment relation;
- permit any capable ankle, step, hip, trunk, upper-limb, or device-assisted strategy; and
- record several trials so adaptation and drift remain visible.

### Matched states

1. **Capable variable recovery:** the relation returns within the criterion through different joint combinations.
2. **Task-specific instability candidate:** the relation repeatedly leaves the accepted set or recovery time lengthens under matched demand.
3. **Discoordination candidate:** the endpoint succeeds, but an independently measured timing gap predicts a repeated correction or selected exposure.
4. **Useful bracing:** anticipatory co-contraction changes stiffness and preserves the relation under a known perturbation.
5. **Unresolved:** the observed kinematics do not identify internal force, timing reliability is poor, or the proposed exposure was not measured.

The figure must label measured perturbation, motion, force, and EMG separately from calculated moments, modeled stiffness or exposure, and unknown biological consequence.

### Required counterfactuals

- alter perturbation direction or magnitude rather than repeating one trial;
- compare early and later repetitions;
- preserve the endpoint while allowing strategy changes;
- change environmental support where safe and experimentally appropriate;
- include pain or fatigue only as separately measured conditions; and
- treat a null or adaptive result as informative.

## Measurement and disfavoring program

| Question | Minimum observations | What would count against the proposed interpretation |
|---|---|---|
| Was the system unstable for this task? | declared relation, perturbation, departure, recovery, repeatability | relation remains controlled across relevant perturbations |
| Was the problem discoordination? | event marker, participant timing, reliability, linked exposure or outcome | timing variation does not predict the relation or selected consequence |
| Did correction create a meaningful exposure? | event definition, local or regional quantity, history, receiver, uncertainty | corrections are rare, small, adaptive, or unrelated to the proposed receiver |
| Was bracing protective? | activation, mechanical stiffness or response, task success, force/cost measure | activation rises without improving the selected relation |
| Did protection become costly? | longitudinal persistence plus a defined cost or restricted option | strategy resolves, remains well tolerated, or improves performance |
| Does the sensory loop operate? | time-resolved sensory measure, activation, mechanics, manipulation, alternatives | arrows lack time order, fail to covary, or a competing initiator explains them |

Static posture, imaging, and one successful trial cannot distinguish these states. Surface EMG alone cannot establish muscle force or neural strategy. Center of pressure alone cannot identify every regional strategy. A clinical label cannot replace the actual perturbation, relation, and recovery record.

## Claim and model actions for later registry synchronization

No stable identifiers should be allocated until the packet is independently reviewed. The later control pass should create or amend records for:

- task-specific instability as failure to maintain or recover a named relation;
- discoordination as a timing or sequence relation with a measured consequence;
- repeated correction as a candidate exposure history, not presumed microtrauma;
- bracing as a potentially useful stiffness strategy with task-specific cost;
- the conditional sensory–control feedback hypothesis;
- an instability/discoordination paired-state model; and
- a repeated-correction time-series model that reuses the Chapter 10 temporal descriptor.

- **Chapter 19 owns** tissue and biological response to a defined exposure.
- **Chapter 21 owns** pain, fear, behavior, environment, causality, and whole-person alternatives.
- **Chapter 25 owns** the later integrated recurrent-instability narrative.
- **Books II–V own** preferred coordination, traditional mapping, breath-driven timing, assessment, and retraining.

The chapter's strongest defensible conclusion is not that hidden instability explains chronic illness. It is that control has mechanics and history: when a task relation is lost, recovered, and lost again, the correction itself can be observed and quantified. The equally important null is that variability, bracing, and correction can be capable, adaptive, and harmless.
