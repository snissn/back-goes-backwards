# Chapter 13 Non-Lumen Comparison

**Decision:** `BGB-DEC-0002`

**Units and records:** `BGB-U-0013`, `BGB-X-0004`, `BGB-M-0006`, `BGB-C-0012`–`BGB-C-0014`

**Status:** Decision-grade focused comparison; not a systematic review or a chapter source pack

**Search date:** 2026-07-11

## Recommendation

Use the **middle-finger flexor tendon–subsynovial connective tissue interface during controlled finger flexion and extension** as Chapter 13's primary non-lumen comparison. Describe the structure precisely as a flexor tendon–subsynovial connective tissue (`SSCT`) relation at the wrist rather than calling it a generic “tendon sheath.” The most useful observable is relative longitudinal motion: tendon excursion, SSCT excursion, and the difference or ratio between them under a declared hand and wrist task.

Retain **median-nerve excursion at the wrist during controlled elbow motion** as the reserve. It has strong recent ultrasound-validation evidence and is an intelligible example of a structure accommodating limb movement. It is not the preferred pilot because its immediate functional endpoint is less directly measured, healthy excursion is highly task- and reference-dependent, and it would sit too close to Chapter 24's required neurogenic contrast.

This is a **moderate-confidence editorial recommendation**:

- confidence is high that both systems exhibit measurable, task-dependent motion relative to surrounding tissue;
- confidence is moderate that the tendon–SSCT relation is the clearer Book I teaching example;
- confidence is low that the reviewed healthy-motion studies establish an adverse maintained constraint, a pathological loss-of-excursion threshold, or recovery after release of such a constraint.

The example should therefore teach the variables and the test, not complete a dysfunction narrative.

## Decision boundary

The [Chapter 13 brief](../chapter-briefs/BGB-U-0013-constraint-maintained-deformation.md) asks whether the maintained-constraint model transfers from a tube to a structure whose relevant freedom is excursion or glide. The [decision docket](../decisions/pilot-decision-docket.md) requires comparison of measurement quality, ordinary variation, maintaining conditions, recovery, functional relevance, and duplication with later chapters. The [example registry](../example-registry.md) additionally excludes the inference that reduced glide is automatically entrapment, injury, a symptom source, or an indication for a technique.

The selected example does not need to prove disease. It does need to make the following distinction visible:

> A structure can show less motion in one ordinary task than in another. Calling that difference a consequential loss of excursion requires a declared reference, a maintaining relation, persistence or reproducible re-creation, a relevant functional comparison, response when the proposed relation changes, and alternatives.

This pack compares neutral or healthy movement studies for that purpose. Clinical populations appear only where they were used to test a measurement method; no diagnostic, prognostic, or treatment inference is imported.

## Candidate A — flexor tendon relative to subsynovial connective tissue

### Candidate identity

The proposed pilot is the **middle-finger flexor digitorum superficialis tendon and adjacent SSCT at the wrist during standardized finger flexion and extension**. The SSCT is a thin, layered tissue surrounding structures in the carpal tunnel. It can be tracked separately from the tendon in longitudinal ultrasound, although the thin layered target makes its measurement less secure than measurement of the tendon itself.

This terminology matters. The reviewed work measures the tendon and identified SSCT or, in a separate zone-V study, “surrounding tissue.” It does not justify treating *tendon sheath*, *SSCT*, *retinaculum*, and *surrounding soft tissue* as interchangeable anatomical or mechanical objects.

### Reference state

Use a reproducible movement cycle rather than an ideal resting shape:

- wrist neutral;
- forearm supinated;
- elbow extended;
- shoulder position declared;
- middle-finger or four-finger flexion and extension performed at a controlled cadence; and
- the tendon and the same adjacent SSCT layer tracked through the same cycle.

The reference is the specified task and its starting phase. A useful within-person perturbation is grip diameter: in a study of ten healthy volunteers and twenty wrists, three cylinder diameters constrained the available finger motion while the wrist and upper limb were stabilized. Speckle tracking distinguished the resulting tendon–SSCT relative-motion patterns ([Yoshii et al., 2009](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2789985/)). Adjacent-finger configuration and active versus passive drive are other relevant perturbations, but they should not be merged into the same experimental record.

### Normal excursion and deformation

The evidence supports **ordinary task-dependent motion**, not one normative value.

- In a reliability study using standardized third-finger flexion-extension, the healthy control group showed roughly 1.1 cm of flexor-tendon excursion per flexion or extension movement. The authors cautioned that finger range, hand dimensions, image quality, and protocol affect the absolute value ([Festen-Schrier et al., 2019](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6389416/)).
- In the grip-diameter experiment, the reported shear index—defined as tendon excursion minus SSCT excursion, divided by tendon excursion—was `39.4% ± 13.3%`, `31.3% ± 9.9%`, and `22.3% ± 9.4%` for the small, medium, and large cylinders. The result shows that relative motion depends on the imposed task; it does not identify a better cylinder, an ideal shear index, or a pathological threshold ([Yoshii et al., 2009](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2789985/)).
- In a separate zone-V study of eleven healthy participants, median absolute long-finger flexor digitorum profundus excursion ranged from `7.6` to `23.4 mm` across active, passive, and adjacent-finger configurations. Surrounding tissue moved as well, so absolute tendon motion and tendon-relative-to-surroundings motion gave different descriptions of the same task ([Korstanje et al., 2012](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6882526/)).

These values should appear, if at all, as examples of protocol dependence. They should not be pooled into a normal range because they involve different tendons, locations, tasks, and analysis methods.

### Candidate maintaining relation

The candidate maintaining relation is the **declared hand configuration plus active or passive tendon drive within a bounded anatomical route**. Finger joint angles, grip diameter, adjacent-digit position, wrist state, muscle activation, and the surrounding corridor jointly determine the observed excursion and relative motion for that phase.

This is a mechanically inspectable, reproducibly re-created state. It is not yet an adverse constraint. The healthy studies do not directly measure contact pressure, friction, retinacular force, local tissue stress, or a surrounding structure continuously imposing a harmful deformation. They show that changing boundary and drive conditions changes motion. A stronger Chapter 13 claim would require a direct link from a specified maintaining relation to a retained or consequential loss of relative excursion.

### Measurement validity and reliability

The measurement case is strong for tendon displacement and adequate but qualified for SSCT motion.

1. A dedicated longitudinal ultrasound speckle-tracking method was tested on porcine tendons, a human cadaveric flexor tendon, and an in-vivo flexor tendon. Average errors were `0.08 mm`, `0.05 mm`, and `0.3 mm`, respectively, under the tested conditions ([Korstanje et al., 2010](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/20152983/)).
2. An SVD-filtered method was compared with metal-marker ground truth in six cadaver arms. Its relative displacement error was `12 ± 16.9%`, lower than the comparison algorithms, but still large enough that method-specific uncertainty cannot be ignored ([Bandaru et al., 2019](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29993671/)).
3. In sixteen healthy controls, intra-rater, inter-rater, and test-retest ICCs were `0.94`, `0.95`, and `0.89` for tendon displacement; `0.93`, `0.82`, and `0.82` for SSCT displacement; and `0.87`, `0.74`, and `0.73` for the relative-motion shear index. The SSCT is less than a millimeter thick and layered, which makes region selection and out-of-plane motion important limitations ([Festen-Schrier et al., 2019](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6389416/)).
4. A direct intraoperative comparison reported moderate agreement for tendon and SSCT excursion and stronger agreement for the relative-motion index, but intra-rater reliability varied by outcome. It supports feasibility, not a universal measurement standard ([van Doesburg et al., 2012](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3898937/)).
5. A cadaver study using color Doppler rather than speckle tracking imposed a known `20 mm` tendon excursion at three velocities. Mean displacement errors increased from `-0.05 mm` at `50 mm/s` to `-2.36 mm` at `150 mm/s`, making acquisition rate and motion velocity explicit parts of validity ([Tat et al., 2015](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25792584/)).

The chapter should distinguish three quantities: absolute tendon excursion, SSCT excursion, and a calculated relative-motion index. Reliability of one does not validate the others, and a relative-motion index is not direct measurement of local shear stress, friction, injury, or tissue capacity.

### Recovery

The reviewed studies provide **kinematic reversal and repeated cycles**, not recovery from a maintained adverse state.

Finger flexion followed by extension shows that motion changes direction; repeated clips support protocol repeatability. The cadaver color-Doppler study observed a different loading and unloading pattern of relative displacement, including a return during extension ([Tat et al., 2015](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25792584/)). None of this establishes:

- return to an individual baseline after prolonged constraint;
- a recovery time constant;
- restoration after unloading;
- hysteresis under living physiological conditions; or
- recovery of a functional or biological endpoint.

The recovery cell must remain visibly unresolved. The pilot should use *return phase* or *kinematic reversal* where that is all that was measured.

### Functional endpoint

The immediate endpoint is completion of the specified finger movement. Tendon excursion and joint rotation are mechanically proximate enough that one can discuss input, transmission, relative motion, and observed output without importing a disease or symptom claim.

That endpoint remains limited. The studies do not show that a particular shear index is more efficient, comfortable, resilient, healthy, or necessary. They also do not establish how much relative motion is required for long-term tissue function.

### Ordinary variation

Ordinary variation is central rather than residual:

- cylinder diameter changes available joint motion and relative tendon–SSCT motion;
- active and passive drive produce different excursion;
- adjacent-finger position changes the measured route and displacement;
- the surrounding tissue moves with the tendon to a task-dependent degree;
- participant anatomy, hand size, speed, and achieved joint range affect the result; and
- two-dimensional imaging can lose out-of-plane motion.

The neutral lesson is that “less excursion” names a comparison, not a dysfunction. It becomes a loss only after the relevant freedom, reference, magnitude, duration, restoring behavior, and functional consequence have been established.

### Countermodels and alternative explanations

Any proposed reduction in relative motion must remain compatible with at least these accounts:

1. **Different task geometry:** the fingers or wrist moved through a different range.
2. **Different drive:** muscle activation, passive tension, or movement velocity changed.
3. **Ordinary coupling:** surrounding tissue appropriately co-moved with the tendon.
4. **Anatomical variation:** tendon path, moment arms, or tissue-layer geometry differed.
5. **Intrinsic state:** scar, adhesion, material change, or local inflammation altered the interface; this cannot be called extrinsic constraint without evidence.
6. **Protective or adaptive organization:** reduced motion may limit another exposure or reflect a chosen motor strategy.
7. **Reverse causation:** discomfort, guarding, or another state changed the movement that produced the measured excursion.
8. **Measurement artifact:** probe pressure, image plane, region selection, frame rate, or tracking failure changed the estimate.

The healthy pilot should foreground items 1 through 4 and 8. The clinical alternatives belong only as future discriminators, not as implied explanations of the neutral movement.

### Fit with the hard/soft relational model

This candidate offers a strong but nonliteral translation of the project's organizing statement:

> Load wants to travel through the hard body so that the soft body can remain soft.

At the declared scale and direction, the tendon can perform a comparatively tensile, force-transmitting, and shape-preserving role while the surrounding interface remains form-receiving and available for deformation and relative motion. The simultaneous movement of tendon and SSCT also prevents a crude cable-in-empty-space picture: the soft-role tissue is loaded and moves, and the tendon itself deforms and changes role in other directions.

The candidate observable is not the tissue label. It is whether the defined tensile transfer and the defined relative freedom can coexist under the task. Absolute tendon excursion alone cannot answer that question; measuring surrounding motion as well is what makes the example useful.

No reviewed source establishes that more relative motion is always better, that the tendon should carry all demand, or that SSCT loading is undesirable. The example supports a division-of-labor question, not a universal answer.

### Duplication with Chapter 24

Duplication risk is low and useful. The [Chapter 24 brief](../chapter-briefs/BGB-U-0024-thoracic-outlet.md) already plans a short callback to the selected Chapter 13 system through `BGB-X-0008`. A tendon–SSCT example gives that comparison a different constitutive behavior and immediate function from the arterial-led thoracic-outlet chain. Shared rows can include boundary conditions, maintaining relation, deformation or excursion, restoring behavior, measurement, and alternatives; the system-specific rows remain relative glide and finger motion rather than lumen, flow, neural conduction, or symptoms.

The tendon example also leaves Chapter 24's short neurogenic contrast free to explain why contact pressure, strain, stiffness, excursion, intraneural physiology, conduction, symptoms, and diagnosis are separate variables. Selecting a nerve in Chapter 13 would make those two editorial jobs harder to separate.

### Figure feasibility

Figure feasibility is high. A controlled inset can use:

1. a longitudinal anatomical schematic of tendon and adjacent SSCT;
2. extension, flexion, and return phases;
3. separate displacement traces for tendon and SSCT;
4. a relative-motion bracket or calculated index;
5. two declared task geometries, such as different grip diameters, without labeling one correct;
6. an observed-versus-inferred legend; and
7. an unresolved recovery-after-release cell.

The figure must not draw unmeasured contact pressure, friction, stress, adhesion, or injury as observed. It should label the carpal tunnel only as the anatomical measurement site and should not resemble a hand exercise or self-test.

## Candidate B — median-nerve excursion during neutral limb movement

### Candidate identity

The reserve is the **median nerve at the wrist during controlled elbow movement**, measured longitudinally and, where possible, relative to named adjacent tissues. “Neutral” here means a healthy volunteer and a standardized nonprovocative movement protocol. It does not mean that the nerve is unloaded, straight, or in one universal neutral state.

Elbow motion is preferable to a composite upper-limb neurodynamic test because it allows the task, starting position, and distal measurement site to be stated more plainly. It also avoids importing the language of a named entrapment syndrome or a therapeutic maneuver.

### Reference state

Use a declared initial elbow angle, stabilized forearm and wrist, and selected deep and superficial reference tissues. A 2026 healthy-volunteer study compared middle-finger, wrist, and elbow motion while tracking median-nerve movement at the wrist. The study's elbow movement began from a relatively low-tension configuration, and its robotic device standardized wrist motion and forearm stability ([Tomažin et al., 2026](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12969963/)).

The reference tissue is part of the result. Absolute nerve displacement and displacement relative to a deep or superficial tissue are different measurements. The chapter should not call either one “true glide” without naming the frame.

### Normal excursion and deformation

- In twenty healthy adults, controlled elbow motion produced `3.8 ± 1.2 mm` of absolute median-nerve displacement at the wrist and `3.6 ± 1.2 mm` relative to deep tissue. Finger and wrist movements produced different values, and relative motion against superficial tissue did not follow the same pattern ([Tomažin et al., 2026](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12969963/)).
- In a separate feasibility sample of eight healthy adults, a validated gradient-optical-flow method measured mean median-nerve displacement of `3.3 ± 1.0 mm` during elbow extension and shoulder anteflexion ([Snoj et al., 2025](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39477744/)).
- In 197 healthy participants performing composite finger flexion, transverse median-nerve movement was generally within approximately `1 mm` vertically and `3 mm` horizontally, yet no predominant transverse direction emerged. Investigators visually categorized `52.5%` as sliding independently from tendons, `26.9%` as moving with tendons, and `21.3%` as showing no visible longitudinal slide. These are a different task and partly qualitative outcome, so they demonstrate heterogeneity rather than a normative comparison value ([Yao et al., 2022](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8671362/)).

The values cannot be combined across tasks, planes, reference tissues, algorithms, or joint sequences.

### Candidate maintaining relation

The candidate relation is the **limb configuration and motion of surrounding tissues that set the nerve's course, slack, tension, and relative displacement for the selected phase**. The 2026 study directly measured nerve and reference-tissue displacement and calculated interface-related shear quantities. It did not directly measure contact pressure, tethering force, friction, or local intraneural load.

The neutral evidence therefore supports a configuration-dependent kinematic state. To establish constraint-maintained loss of excursion, a future study would need to identify the surrounding relation, show that it changes the relevant nerve motion beyond ordinary variation, and show what happens when that relation changes while competing variables are controlled.

### Measurement validity and reliability

The recent validation evidence is a principal strength of this candidate.

- A gradient-optical-flow speckle-tracking method was tested in seven fresh-frozen cadaveric forearms against an air-bubble marker while `20 N` traction moved the median nerve. With a five-frame backtrace, automatic tracking underestimated displacement by `4% ± 4%` across the tested `0–4.50 mm` range. In eight healthy participants the method measured `3.3 ± 1.0 mm` of movement with an inter-rater ICC of `0.87`. It could not detect longitudinal strain at the tested displacements ([Snoj et al., 2025](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0301562924003855)).
- The 2026 joint-comparison study used the validated algorithm to calculate absolute displacement, displacement relative to named adjacent tissues, and a two-dimensional shear-strain field. Only the best-quality one of three recorded cycles was analyzed, which improves interpretability of the selected clip but can underrepresent ordinary acquisition failure or variability ([Tomažin et al., 2026](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12969963/)).
- A systematic reliability review found generally moderate-to-high reliability across peripheral-nerve ultrasound-excursion studies but substantial protocol and analysis variation. That synthesis supports feasibility, not interchangeability of methods or values ([Ellis et al., 2018](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0301562917322470)).

Displacement remains distinct from tensile strain, contact pressure, stiffness, conduction, and symptoms. A calculated interface shear field does not by itself identify a pathological tether or a consequential constraint.

### Recovery

The reviewed neutral studies provide movement reversal and repeated trials, not recovery from a maintained imposed state. They do not establish a return time, persistence after unloading, restored conduction, or a functional recovery threshold. This candidate has the same unresolved recovery cell as the tendon candidate.

### Functional endpoint

Median-nerve excursion accommodates changes in course and length during limb movement. That is a legitimate mechanical function. The reviewed neutral studies do not show whether the measured amount is necessary or sufficient for nerve conduction, intraneural circulation, comfort, or task performance.

The first defensible endpoint is therefore **mechanical accommodation during the specified movement**, not neurological health. Any conduction, physiological, symptom, or clinical link needs a separate source and does not belong in this generic comparison unless it is explicitly used to show a missing bridge.

### Ordinary variation

Ordinary variation is at least as important here as in the tendon system:

- joint and movement sequence change the excursion;
- the deep and superficial reference tissues produce different relative measurements;
- healthy people differ in transverse direction and visible longitudinal sliding pattern;
- starting angle changes slack and tension;
- active finger motion and passive joint motion are not equivalent inputs;
- nerve anatomy and surrounding-tissue motion vary; and
- probe pressure, tracking window, out-of-plane motion, and clip selection affect measurement.

No reviewed evidence supplies a context-free minimum excursion or a threshold at which reduced movement becomes imposed, consequential, or pathological.

### Countermodels and alternative explanations

1. **Ordinary slack take-up:** less translation may accompany more local strain, or vice versa, depending on configuration.
2. **Moving reference tissue:** apparent absolute excursion may partly reflect motion of the surrounding field; relative results depend on the chosen reference.
3. **Different task or starting state:** elbow, wrist, finger, and shoulder inputs cannot be treated as equivalent doses.
4. **Protective motor behavior:** an individual may limit motion without a fixed external tether.
5. **Intrinsic nerve or surrounding-tissue state:** material change can alter displacement without the proposed skeletal or extrinsic relation.
6. **Reverse causation:** discomfort or another state may alter the movement and therefore the measured nerve motion.
7. **Measurement artifact:** probe drift, pressure, out-of-plane motion, image quality selection, and tracking assumptions can change the estimate.
8. **No downstream consequence:** the kinematic difference may remain within ordinary functional variation.

### Fit with the hard/soft relational model

The nerve is an intuitive form-receiving structure: it changes course, shape, and relative position while the limb moves. That makes it useful for explaining why available excursion can matter without a lumen.

The present evidence is less useful for identifying the corresponding hard-role assembly. Limb bones, joints, muscles, connective tissues, and external support all contribute to the boundary conditions, but ultrasound excursion alone does not reveal which carries or resolves the gross demand. Without a measured surrounding-load relation, the example risks relabeling ordinary nerve kinematics as load entering the soft body.

The fit is therefore real but incomplete: strong for *soft-role freedom*, weaker for *division of labor* and *adverse substitution*.

### Duplication with Chapter 24

Duplication risk is material. Chapter 24 must already distinguish neural contact pressure, strain, stiffness, excursion, intraneural physiology, conduction, symptoms, and diagnosis from the selected vascular chain. A median-nerve pilot would be useful background for that distinction, but it could also make neutral wrist-level nerve excursion appear to support a brachial-plexus or thoracic-outlet mechanism that it did not study.

If the nerve becomes the pilot, Chapter 24 would need a conspicuous separation between:

- `BGB-X-0004`, healthy median-nerve kinematics at the wrist;
- the short neurogenic thoracic-outlet variable contrast; and
- `BGB-X-0008`, the formal transfer record.

The tendon candidate avoids this three-way seam.

### Figure feasibility

A nerve figure is feasible but carries more explanatory load. It would need:

1. initial and final elbow states without resembling a prescribed maneuver;
2. the wrist-level nerve measurement window;
3. absolute, deep-relative, and superficial-relative displacement shown separately;
4. an indication that displacement is not strain, contact, stiffness, conduction, or symptoms;
5. ordinary between-person variation; and
6. an unresolved recovery cell.

This is valuable material for a technical note or reserve inset. It is less immediately legible than the two-track tendon–SSCT comparison.

## Pilot formulation and explicit limits

The Chapter 13 comparison can be built around this bounded formulation:

> During ordinary finger movement, a flexor tendon and its adjacent subsynovial connective tissue move together to a degree and also move relative to one another. The amount of absolute and relative motion changes with the declared task. This supplies a measurable example of excursion without a lumen. It does not by itself identify a loss, an adverse constraint, or a preferred amount of glide.

The next paragraph can state the model test:

> A claim of constraint-maintained loss of excursion would need more: a task-specific reference; a named surrounding or intrinsic maintaining relation; a change larger than measurement error and ordinary variation; persistence or reproducible re-creation at a stated timescale; response when the proposed relation changes; and a separately measured functional consequence. The neutral example shows what must be observed, including the null branch in which task-dependent relative motion remains ordinary.

Do not infer from the example that:

- SSCT should not carry stress or move;
- greater excursion or a larger relative-motion index is inherently better;
- a grip diameter, finger position, or wrist position is mechanically correct;
- reduced motion identifies adhesion, entrapment, injury, pain, or disease;
- ultrasound displacement measures local stress, friction, capacity, or tissue health;
- the selected interface establishes a body-wide hard/soft law; or
- a movement, exercise, or manual technique should be used to change it.

## Reserve formulation

If editorial review rejects the tendon–SSCT system, use this reserve:

> During controlled elbow movement, the median nerve normally changes position at the wrist relative to surrounding tissues. The measured excursion depends on starting position, joint input, reference tissue, individual, and method. This provides a measurable example of course accommodation without a lumen, not a threshold for healthy glide or evidence of entrapment.

The reserve should use the 2025 validation study and the 2026 controlled joint-comparison study as its principal measurement sources. It should not use a named neurodynamic test, symptom provocation, a diagnostic cutoff, or a carpal-tunnel treatment study to make the neutral mechanism appear clinically established.

## Figure and Chapter 24 handoff

For Chapter 13, add the tendon–SSCT comparison as a controlled inset or a small dedicated figure only if it remains legible beside `BGB-FIG-01-006` through `008`. The figure should expose the model's empty cells rather than visually completing them.

For Chapter 24, `BGB-X-0008` can reuse the same baseline variables without reusing the generic example as clinical evidence:

| Shared model row | Tendon–SSCT system | Objective-aTOS system |
|---|---|---|
| Required freedom or function | Relative excursion through a movement cycle | Artery-wall integrity and distal arterial supply in the selected context |
| Maintaining or initiating relation | Declared hand configuration, drive, and bounded path | Focal anatomy and proposed repeated arterial deformation, explicitly reconstructed |
| Measured state | Tendon motion, SSCT motion, relative-motion index | Objective wall lesion; thrombus, embolus, or occlusion where present; distal consequence |
| Restoring or retained behavior | Kinematic reversal observed; recovery after adverse constraint unresolved | Current pressure, flow, regulation, collateral response, and retained lesion do not directly recover the initiating exposure |
| Immediate function or consequence | Specified finger movement | Distal arterial or ischemic consequence and clinical presentation kept separate |
| Important failure of analogy | No lumen, flow, active vascular wall, collateral circulation, or vascular physiology | Relative glide cannot be replaced by patency or flow language |

That comparison earns transfer only at the level of declared boundary conditions, a maintained or repeated state, a tissue-specific freedom, a restoring account, measurement, and alternatives. It does not establish shared anatomy, material behavior, physiology, clinical importance, or intervention.

## Work required before closing `BGB-DEC-0002`

1. **Authorial review:** Confirm that the tendon–SSCT relation preserves the intended hard/soft teaching job and that “load entering the soft body” is not being reduced to any ordinary interface shear.
2. **Anatomical review:** Confirm the exact tendon, imaging level, SSCT naming, and relation to retinaculum and surrounding tissues. Avoid a generic tendon-sheath label unless a source studies that exact structure.
3. **Measurement review:** Select one primary acquisition and analysis protocol. Record its frame rate, movement cadence, region definition, reference tissue, error, repeatability, and out-of-plane limitations.
4. **Variation extraction:** Recover participant-level or distributional data where available. Do not present study means as normative thresholds.
5. **Maintaining-relation gate:** Decide whether the neutral task effect is sufficient as a calibration example. If prose asserts an adverse maintained loss, add direct evidence connecting a defined constraint to relative motion and response when that constraint changes.
6. **Recovery gate:** Keep recovery explicitly unresolved unless a study measures behavior after release or unloading at a stated timescale. Do not substitute reverse movement for recovery.
7. **Functional gate:** Keep the endpoint at specified finger movement unless direct evidence connects the measured interface variable to another function. No symptom or health claim is needed for the pilot.
8. **Figure prototype:** Draft an original two-track schematic with observed, calculated, inferred, and unresolved elements visually distinct. Test that it cannot be mistaken for an exercise instruction.
9. **Chapter 24 seam review:** Confirm that `BGB-X-0008` reuses the comparison's grammar but not its evidence as support for the thoracic-outlet case.
10. **Control-record consistency:** Keep the docket, example registry, Chapter 13 brief, Chapter 24 transfer record, and Figure 012 planning record aligned to the same provisional selection. This comparison file does not itself close the decision or advance evidence status.

## Decision posture

**Present recommendation:** flexor tendon–SSCT relative glide selected provisionally; neutral median-nerve excursion retained as reserve.

**Reason:** the tendon–SSCT system provides the clearer non-lumen transfer, a direct relative-motion observable, a mechanically proximal functional endpoint, a strong hard/soft relational test, a simpler figure, and less neural duplication in Chapter 24. The nerve remains evidentially credible and should be preserved because its recent measurement validation is strong and it may become preferable if anatomical, figure, or interface-measurement review exposes a fatal weakness in the tendon selection.

**What the decision does not close:** an adverse maintained-loss example, a recovery measure, a pathological threshold, a clinical claim, or an intervention. Those remain deferred evidence and editorial gates rather than implied conclusions.
