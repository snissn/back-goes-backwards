## Editorial decision

Chapter 27 can compress Book I into seven portable questions, provided the questions remain a reasoning scaffold rather than an implied clinical instrument. Their value is not that they produce a mechanical answer. Their value is that they force an account to move in order—from task and history, through system boundary and configuration, toward a specified local quantity—and to stop when an intermediate bridge is missing.

The scaffold's present contribution is therefore methodological:

> Begin with the task, environment, history, and changing state. Declare the system and phase. Distinguish observed configuration from candidate internal distribution; specify relational hard and soft roles; name a local receiver and quantity before proposing response; ask what, if anything, a changed strategy preserved; use modulation to update only the arrow it tests; and finish by stating what was measured, what remains inferred, which alternatives survive, and where the route stops.

That sequence is a project synthesis. No source below validates the seven questions as a clinical tool. A future tool would require operational definitions, user and setting specifications, reliability and agreement studies, construct- and criterion-specific validity work, responsiveness and interpretability studies where change is claimed, comparison with existing practice, decision-consequence evidence, burden and harm assessment, and external validation. The chapter may name that development path; it may not borrow the authority of those methods before completing it.

The chapter should be memorable without being mnemonicized into a score. Each question has five possible effects on one named proposition:

1. **support** the arrow under review;
2. **weaken** that arrow;
3. **redirect** inquiry to a competing account;
4. **leave the arrow unresolved**; or
5. **stop** the present route.

No total score is calculated. Confidence does not flow past an unsupported early arrow merely because later observations are vivid or medically consequential.

## The observation-to-use architecture

The chapter needs a stricter distinction than “observation versus clinical test.” A technically repeatable output can remain a research measure; a valid measure can remain irrelevant to a particular decision; and a clinically useful assessment need not be a population-screening program or a diagnosis.

### Six different objects

| Object | Minimum definition for this chapter | Evidence needed before promotion | What it does **not** become automatically |
|---|---|---|---|
| Ordinary observation | A report or description tied to observer, task, phase, setting, comparison, and ambiguity | clear operational wording and provenance | reproducible measure, hidden internal quantity, cause, or diagnosis |
| Reproducible measure | A specified measurand or construct, procedure, instrument or rater conditions, output, units or categories, and quantified repeatability/reproducibility or agreement | repeated-measure study under declared conditions; error and uncertainty characterization | valid measure of a different construct, clinical assessment, meaningful change, or utility |
| Validated clinical assessment | A measure or structured assessment shown fit for a declared construct, purpose, population, task, setting, interpreter, and use | purpose-specific validity plus relevant reliability, error, interpretability, and implementation evidence | screening program, diagnosis, prognosis, treatment selector, or benefit from using it |
| Screening | An organized effort to identify higher-risk people in an apparently healthy target population so that an evidence-supported next step can follow | target condition and population, test performance, pathway, quality assurance, benefit-harm balance, feasibility, equity, and program evaluation | diagnosis at the moment of the screen or a casual synonym for “look at” |
| Diagnosis | An iterative explanation of a person's health problem developed from history, examination, tests, clinical reasoning, communication, follow-up, and the surrounding care system | condition-specific clinical evidence, professional competence, work-system support, and revision over time | one mechanical observation, one score, or one image |
| Decision-relevant utility | Evidence that using information changes a meaningful decision or outcome enough to justify error, burden, cost, opportunity cost, and possible harm | comparison with the existing decision process and explicit consequences | a property conferred by accuracy, statistical association, novelty, or explanatory elegance alone |

The International Vocabulary of Metrology defines measurement as experimentally obtaining quantity values that can reasonably be attributed to a quantity, and defines the measurand as the quantity intended to be measured. It also distinguishes measurement results, uncertainty, repeatability conditions, and reproducibility conditions.[^jcgm2012] Book I should borrow the discipline, not pretend every clinical or embodied construct is a physical quantity. A visible change in trunk angle, a clinician's category, a pressure trace, a pain report, and a modeled stress field can all be legitimate records, but they are not interchangeable measurements of “load routing.”

The FDA–NIH BEST resource distinguishes biomarkers from clinical outcome assessments and distinguishes patient-, clinician-, observer-, and performance-based outcomes. It also makes validation dependent on proposed use.[^best2025] Chapter 27 can use that separation to keep images, sensor outputs, reports, observations, and performances in their own columns. The regulatory terminology is not adopted wholesale, and no project-native observation acquires validation by being placed in a table.

WHO defines screening at the level of an apparently healthy target population and an organized program that includes follow-up, quality assurance, costs, burdens, benefits, and harms.[^who2020] The seven questions therefore cannot be called a screen. They do not specify a target condition, threshold, confirmatory pathway, or population-level program.

The National Academies represents diagnosis as an iterative, collaborative process over time within a health-care work system, not as the output of one isolated measure.[^nasem2015] This is the chapter's decisive boundary: the scaffold can organize a mechanical contribution to an account but cannot issue the account's diagnosis.

### Measurement properties answer different questions

The terminology below is deliberately purpose-specific. The question is never simply “Is this measure validated?” It is “For what construct, comparison, population, setting, task, interpreter, timeframe, and decision has which property been established?”

| Property | Question answered | Example of a legitimate result | Promotion that is not licensed |
|---|---|---|---|
| Repeatability or reproducibility | How close are results under specified same or changed conditions? | similar instrument values across repeated trials under a declared protocol | truth, clinical meaning, or stable performance outside those conditions |
| Agreement and measurement error | How close are repeated values in the units or categories that matter? | expected within-person variation or rater disagreement is quantified | ability to distinguish people or detect meaningful change |
| Reliability | How well does the measure distinguish variation among people or objects relative to error in the sampled population? | a reliability coefficient under specified raters, sample, and conditions | small absolute error, validity, or portability to a different population |
| Content validity | Do the items or observations adequately represent the intended construct and purpose? | users and experts judge task-specific content relevant and comprehensible | structural, criterion, predictive, or clinical validity |
| Construct or criterion validity | Does the result behave as expected relative to specified hypotheses or an appropriate reference? | prespecified relations or diagnostic-accuracy estimates under a defined design | causal mechanism, general utility, or validity for an untested use |
| Responsiveness | Can the measure detect change in the construct over the intended interval? | expected change and stability hypotheses are met | that the change matters to the person or exceeds measurement error |
| Meaningful change and interpretability | What magnitude or category of change has an interpretable consequence for the relevant person or group? | change is anchored to a declared external judgment or outcome | universal threshold, individual certainty, or intervention efficacy |
| Generalizability or transportability | Do performance and interpretation hold in other people, tasks, raters, settings, times, and prevalence conditions? | external performance remains adequate in a new setting | context-free validity |
| Incremental value | Does the new information improve discrimination, calibration, explanation, communication, or another declared target beyond what is already known? | prespecified improvement against an existing account | better decisions or outcomes without consequence analysis |
| Decision-relevant utility | Does using the information improve choices or outcomes enough to justify its consequences? | comparative benefit under explicit tradeoffs | a diagnosis, treatment rule, or mandate for use outside the evaluated context |

The COSMIN consensus separates reliability, measurement error, validity, and responsiveness, and treats interpretability as distinct from measurement properties.[^mokkink2010] Its taxonomy was developed for health-related patient-reported outcomes, so Chapter 27 should not treat it as a complete standard for force plates, imaging, palpation, or model outputs. It supplies a clean warning against compressing several evaluation questions into the word *valid*.

Agreement and reliability are not synonyms. De Vet and colleagues show that agreement concerns closeness of repeated scores while reliability concerns the ability to distinguish among persons and is influenced by between-person heterogeneity.[^devet2006] If a proposed observation is intended to track change in one person, absolute error and agreement can matter more than a favorable reliability coefficient from a heterogeneous sample.

The GRRAS guidance requires the intended reliability or agreement parameter, rater and subject selection, measurement process, blinding, independence, sample-size rationale, statistical method, and uncertainty to be reported.[^kottner2011] This supports an explicit development gate for any Chapter 27 category. It does not provide acceptable cutoffs or validate any observation in advance.

Responsiveness and meaningful change must remain separate. Jaeschke, Singer, and Guyatt's foundational work defines a minimally important difference through the perspective and consequences of change rather than statistical detectability alone.[^jaeschke1989] The exact historical method should not be universalized, but the chapter needs its central distinction: a detectable change can be too small, irrelevant, or too uncertain to matter.

Generalizability is also a separate question. Rothwell's analysis of randomized trials emphasizes that eligibility, setting, outcome, follow-up, protocol, and the characteristics of participants and care can constrain application beyond the study.[^rothwell2005] The source is trial-specific; Chapter 27 transfers only the requirement to state where a measurement or relation was established before applying it elsewhere.

### Accuracy and association do not supply utility

Diagnostic-accuracy reporting itself requires intended use, target condition, participant selection, index test, reference standard, thresholds, blinding, flow, timing, and applicability. STARD makes those dependencies visible.[^bossuyt2015] It is a reporting guideline, not a validation certificate. The seven questions specify none of those elements and therefore cannot inherit diagnostic status from a future sensitivity or specificity estimate attached to one component.

Vickers and Elkin developed decision-curve analysis because conventional accuracy measures do not by themselves encode the clinical consequences of false-positive and false-negative decisions.[^vickers2006] Chapter 27 need not prescribe that method. It should preserve the broader point that a new measure or model can discriminate statistically while adding little or causing net burden in the decision context under review.

Pauker and Kassirer's threshold model similarly places testing between action thresholds: additional information has value when it can change a choice under explicit benefits, harms, and probabilities.[^pauker1980] Their decision-analytic formulation is not a universal bedside rule and is not imported as a numerical algorithm. Its bounded use here is a stopping question: *Could the answer to this mechanical question change the declared interpretation or decision?* If not, more measurement may be research-interesting but presently low priority.

## Evidence state, provenance, and inference

The project evidence states remain controlling:

| State | Minimum record | Permitted statement | Common overpromotion |
|---|---|---|---|
| `OBS` — observed | observer or reporter, task, phase, time, context, comparison, ambiguity | what was seen, heard, felt, or reported | internal exposure, stable trait, cause, or diagnosis |
| `MEAS` — measured | measurand or construct, instrument or rater, protocol, units/categories, uncertainty | result under declared conditions | different scale, tissue, timeframe, or outcome |
| `CALC` — calculated | inputs, equation, sign convention, assumptions, propagated uncertainty | derived quantity for the declared model | direct observation or unique internal sharing |
| `MOD` — modeled | boundary, representation, parameters, calibration/validation domain, sensitivity | behavior of a stated model | the one true anatomical route |
| `INF` — inferred | observations used, alternatives, logic, disfavoring result | proposed relation with scope | measured fact or completed causal chain |
| `HYP` — hypothesized | testable proposition, predicted observations, null and replacement conditions | research claim | established mechanism or clinical tool |
| `EMB` — embodied report | reporter, experience, task, phase, prompting, comparison, ambiguity | first-person felt organization or change | measured force line, anatomy, diagnosis, or universal mapping |

**Reconstructed** remains a qualifier attached to one or more base states. It does not become an eighth evidence rank. A reconstruction can be strong, weak, or useful, but it is not continuous observation of the interval it joins.

### Observation quality is parameter-specific

Bao and colleagues found that interrater reliability for video-based posture observations varied by the posture parameter and categorization scheme; broader categories could improve percentage agreement while changing resolution.[^bao2009] This does not show that all visual observation is unreliable. It shows why “observe posture” is not an operational method. A candidate observation must specify the segment, plane or frame, task phase, category boundary, rater preparation, and intended use.

Manual observation has the same constraint. Nolet and colleagues' systematic review of low-back palpation found poor reliability for bony and joint-mobility palpation, inconsistent reliability for soft-tissue palpation, and limited validity evidence across the included procedures.[^nolet2021] The result is not a ban on palpation and cannot be generalized to every body region or purpose. It does prevent Chapter 27 from treating palpation as direct access to an otherwise hidden body-wide force path.

### Confidence attaches to arrows, not narratives

The chapter should use the following update grammar:

| Update | Meaning | Required wording discipline |
|---|---|---|
| Supports | the observation is more expected under the named arrow than under at least one declared alternative | state which arrow, comparison, and conditions; do not say “supports the mechanical model” globally |
| Weakens | the predicted observation is absent, reversed, imprecise, or better explained by an alternative | preserve power, error, and measurement limits |
| Redirects | another chronology, scale, receiver, or nonmechanical account fits better | name the new question without treating redirection as failure |
| Unresolved | the available evidence cannot discriminate among live accounts | unknown is neutral, not latent support |
| Stop | a required bridge is unavailable, nonunique, outside scope, clinically secondary, or unable to affect the declared purpose | stop downstream inheritance while leaving narrower observations intact |

## The seven-question evidence map

| Question | Required input | Forward bridge | Legitimate outputs | Principal stop |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1. What demand entered the system? | task, environment, contacts, supports, external load, phase, history, dose, recovery, relevant constraints | a declared system and inspectable exposure record | measured external demand, bounded qualitative history, or unresolved exposure | task, phase, or system cannot be specified well enough to assign an internal route |
| 2. What configuration and paths were available? | named frame and scale, contacts, motion, constraints, more than one viable strategy | explicit mechanical relation between geometry and candidate distribution | bounded set of possible carriers or models | appearance alone cannot discriminate internal distribution |
| 3. How were hard and soft roles divided? | declared interaction, direction, phase, timescale, element and relation | operational role tied to measurable behavior | candidate shape-preserving and form-receiving division | role language remains analogy because no operational observation or quantity is available |
| 4. Where, if anywhere, might local exposure have accumulated? | named receiver or interface, quantity, state, dose, time | measured/calculated/modeled path from demand to receiver | challengeable local-exposure hypothesis | no bridge from configuration or role to the local quantity; no receiver-specific object |
| 5. What, if anything, did the changed strategy preserve? | dated change, task preserved, possible protected quantity, exposure and outcome | measured correspondence or chronology between changed strategy and later demand/outcome | successful adaptation, immediate protection, persistent substitution, neutral change, or unresolved change | cost is asserted without a relevant measured exposure/outcome and time course |
| 6. What changes with task, support, time, or state? | controlled or well-described comparison and simultaneous changes | specific modulation prediction with alternatives | support, weakening, redirection, paradoxical result, nonchange, or unresolved modulation | change is nonspecific, uncontrolled, below error, or unable to distinguish origin |
| 7. What can actually be measured, and what remains uncertain? | evidence states, measurement properties, alternatives, nulls, scope, purpose | purpose-specific validity and decision relevance | bounded contribution, research question, low-priority result, or stop | evidence is unavailable, unreliable, invalid for purpose, nonunique, clinically secondary, burdensome, or outside scope |

The order is logical rather than a required encounter script. A clinician, researcher, engineer, or reader may learn a diagnosis, imaging result, symptom, or task history first. The chapter's claim is narrower: no internal mechanical route becomes stronger than its missing prerequisites merely because information arrived out of order.

## Question-by-question development

### 1. What demand entered the system?

The first question replaces adjectives with events. “Sedentary,” “active,” “strained,” “poorly aligned,” and “repetitive” are compressed interpretations. A usable record names, as available:

- task and environment;
- system boundary and phase;
- contacts, supports, carried or applied loads, assistance, and tools;
- direction, rate, duration, frequency, repetition, variability, and rest;
- prior events, training, illness, care, devices, and changing state;
- whether each detail was observed, recorded, measured, estimated, recalled, or unknown; and
- access, disability, work control, caregiving, resources, and environmental constraints when they change the event or the available alternatives.

The goal is not exhaustive history. It is a denominator for the proposed arrow. A symptom count without task opportunity, a recurrence count without time at risk, or a claim about cumulative demand without recovery and changing capacity cannot carry the intended meaning.

The first question can end the mechanical route early. If the exposure is unknown, the account may still preserve a symptom, diagnosis, finding, or current mechanical response. It may not fill the historical interval by reading the present configuration backward.

### 2. What configuration and paths were available?

Configuration is a relation under a declared frame, scale, and phase. It can change moment arms, line of action, contact, available excursion, boundary conditions, and the set of plausible carriers. It does not uniquely solve internal load sharing.

The question should ask:

- What moved, and relative to what?
- What remained relatively fixed over the selected interval?
- Which contacts appeared, disappeared, or changed support?
- Which neighboring regions and external supports could participate?
- Which global, segment, joint, or local material frame is being used?
- What other strategy could satisfy the same external task?
- Which carrier or internal quantity was measured, and which remains modeled or inferred?

Chapter 27 should not use “path” as a literal packet of load traveling through a cable. A path is a representation of how force, moment, pressure, contact, deformation, energy, or another named quantity is transmitted or distributed through a declared model. Different quantities can have different apparent paths. Changing the system boundary changes which interactions are internal or external.

### 3. How were hard and soft roles divided?

Hard and soft are relational mechanical roles **motivated by** TCM hard/soft and Yin/Yang concepts but not offered as exact definitions of those traditions. In this book:

- a **hard role** is relatively shape-preserving in a declared relation, direction, phase, and timescale; and
- a **soft role** is relatively form-receiving: it yields, conforms, accommodates, flows, glides, contains, expands, or redistributes under surrounding conditions.

The same tissue, assembly, pressure field, or external support can combine roles in different directions or exchange roles between phases. Bone can deform. Muscle and tendon can preserve or change a relation. Cartilage can receive form while contributing to joint constraint. A fluid-filled compartment can transmit pressure while changing shape. A brace, chair, floor, or carried object can become part of the boundary.

The project intuition—*load wants to travel through the hard body so that the soft body can remain soft*—is preserved here as a hypothesis generator. Its operational translation is not “hard tissues should bear all load.” It is:

> Did the interaction provide an adequate shape-preserving route for the named demand, or was a relatively form-receiving element required to act as a substitute boundary in a way that changed its local exposure?

That question can be productive even when the answer is no, unknown, or task-dependent. It requires a declared relation and quantity before it can advance.

Posterior participation belongs inside this third question as one bounded candidate. Named posterior muscles, tendons, ligaments, skeletal relations, and pressure-mediated interactions can contribute materially to selected tasks. Chapter 27 may ask whether they participated, how that participation was observed, and whether its contribution changed with the task. It may not infer a global posterior deficit from appearance, identify posterior as synonymous with hard or Yang, or treat posterior contribution as the scaffold's expected answer.

### 4. Where, if anywhere, might local exposure have accumulated?

This is the main translation gate. The chapter should refuse the phrase “soft-tissue overload” unless it can be decomposed into a receiver and a quantity. Candidate local objects include:

- force or moment at a declared boundary;
- contact location, area, or pressure;
- normal or shear stress;
- tensile, compressive, or shear strain;
- deformation, excursion, curvature, torsion, friction, or gliding relation;
- active force, activation, shortening or lengthening behavior;
- intraluminal or compartment pressure and wall deformation;
- rate, cycle count, dwell time, variability, recovery, and accumulated history; and
- material or biological state that changes the receiver's response.

The route must state whether each quantity is `MEAS`, `CALC`, `MOD`, `INF`, or unknown. A joint moment does not establish one muscle force. A contact force does not establish a pressure field without contact geometry. Pressure does not establish tissue strain without material and boundary relations. Strain does not establish injury, symptom, degeneration, or diagnosis without receiver-specific time and biological bridges.

### 5. What, if anything, did the changed strategy preserve?

If the term *compensation* is used, begin by asking whether anything continued or was protected. The first record should be the task, balance relation, painful region, confidence, participation, exposure, healing interval, or other function that may have been preserved. Only then should the account ask what changed and whether *compensation* is the appropriate description.

Candidate changes include tone, bracing, timing, range, gait, side-to-side transfer, external support, breath behavior, task choice, avoidance, pace, attention, effort, and recovery. These can represent:

- immediate protection;
- successful adaptation with no demonstrated cost;
- learned or externally assisted strategy;
- symptom-led behavior;
- persistent substitution associated with a measured later exposure;
- an epiphenomenon or shared response; or
- an observation unrelated to the selected endpoint.

The term *cost* requires more than difference. It needs a relevant quantity or outcome, chronology, denominator, and alternatives. A strategy that appears unusual can be efficient for that person and task. A common strategy can still be costly under a selected dose. Chapter 27 recognizes compensation before any later work considers correction.

### 6. What changes with task, support, time, or state?

Modulation can be informative because it tests whether an observation travels with a changed condition. Comparisons can include supported and unsupported, early and late, rested and fatigued, one task and another, familiar and unfamiliar, or one environmental arrangement and another. But the comparison must record everything that changed simultaneously.

A change can reflect:

- changed external demand or boundary condition;
- changed internal distribution or available capacity;
- learning, attention, expectation, confidence, or threat;
- natural fluctuation, regression toward an average, or measurement error;
- medication, care, sleep, illness, or other concurrent state;
- a nonspecific response shared by several accounts; or
- an interaction not yet represented.

The permitted conclusion is local: “This observation changed with this condition under these circumstances.” A stronger arrow requires specificity, control, temporal order, mechanism-relevant measurement, replication, and alternatives. Immediate symptom change does not recover historical origin; nonchange does not prove the absence of every mechanical contribution.

### 7. What can actually be measured, and what remains uncertain?

The final question is not an instrumentation shopping list. It closes the route by asking:

1. What exact proposition is still in dispute?
2. What observation could discriminate it from the strongest alternative?
3. Is that observation available as an ordinary report, reproducible measure, validated assessment, calculation, model, or not at all?
4. What are its error, validity domain, responsiveness, interpretability, and generalizability limits?
5. What result would support, weaken, redirect, leave unresolved, or stop the arrow?
6. Would obtaining the information change the declared interpretation or decision enough to justify burden and risk?
7. Is another medical, biological, psychological, social, environmental, or safety concern more important now?

The chapter earns its clinical boundary by accepting four legitimate endings:

- mechanics is a substantial part of the selected account;
- mechanics contributes modestly or modifies the current task without establishing origin;
- the direction may be reverse or shared;
- the mechanical route is currently low priority or unresolved.

None of those endings selects treatment.

## Alternatives, confirmation pressure, nulls, and stopping

The scaffold increases the number of mechanically framed questions. That can improve resolution, but it can also manufacture coherence: once a reader suspects a route, each later observation can be interpreted as confirmation of it. Alternatives must therefore be generated before the result is known and retained at the arrow they challenge.

Mendel and colleagues experimentally studied psychiatrists and medical students evaluating a case and found that confirmatory information search was associated with persistence in an incorrect preliminary diagnosis.[^mendel2011] The population, specialty, experimental task, and outcome are narrow; the study does not quantify confirmation bias for biomechanics. It justifies the development rule that every mechanical hypothesis must declare at least one serious alternative and a disfavoring observation before interpretation.

Kostopoulou and colleagues found that family physicians' evaluation of incoming diagnostic evidence could be distorted toward a leading hypothesis in experimental vignettes.[^kostopoulou2012] Again, this is not a universal estimate. It supports a complementary control: record the observation and its ambiguity before assigning it to the preferred route, and ask whether the same observation would be described differently if another chronology led.

### Alternative families required at each consequential arrow

| Proposed arrow | Minimum alternatives | Example of a discriminating observation | Stop condition |
|---|---|---|---|
| Task/history → configuration | ordinary strategy variation; environmental constraint; symptom-led change; measurement artifact | matched task before/after the proposed exposure or an independent task record | chronology cannot be recovered and present appearance is doing all historical work |
| Configuration → internal distribution | multiple active strategies; different contact or material assumptions; external assistance; model nonuniqueness | direct internal measure where available, sensitivity analysis, or an independently predicted resultant | several models fit the external observation and no discriminator is available |
| Distribution → local exposure | wrong receiver; wrong quantity; load sharing elsewhere; insufficient dose; recovery | receiver-specific measure or validated calculation with condition-specific inputs | no named receiver or no bridge from resultant to local field |
| Local exposure → tissue response | normal adaptation; successful recovery; inflammation or tissue state from another source; insufficient temporal order | prospective response measure and dose relation with alternatives | response was not measured or preceded the proposed exposure |
| Tissue response → finding/symptom/function | asymptomatic finding; symptom without selected finding; central or contextual modulation; shared cause | condition-specific correspondence and noncorrespondence evidence | endpoint is being inferred from the mechanism rather than observed |
| Changed strategy → later cost | successful adaptation; neutral variation; reduced exposure; reverse symptom-led change | dated outcome/exposure relation with denominator | difference is the only evidence of cost |
| Modulation → historical cause | expectation, learning, natural fluctuation, concurrent care, nonspecific support | controlled and specific manipulation with temporal and mechanism-relevant measurement | present change cannot discriminate origin |

### Nulls are results with conditions

The ASA statement on *p*-values emphasizes that a *p*-value does not measure the probability that a hypothesis is true, the size or importance of an effect, or by itself provide a complete evidentiary conclusion.[^wasserstein2016] Chapter 27 should therefore avoid both familiar errors:

- “not statistically significant” is not proof that two states are identical; and
- “statistically significant” is not proof that the difference is large, causal, reproducible, or useful.

A null or noncorrespondence record should include:

| Field | Required content |
|---|---|
| Proposition tested | exact arrow or expected relation |
| Measure and contrast | quantity, protocol, task, groups or conditions, and timeframe |
| Estimate and uncertainty | effect estimate and interval where available; not only a threshold label |
| Resolution | what magnitude or pattern the design could plausibly detect |
| Scope | population, setting, receiver, phase, and model assumptions |
| Consequence | which arrow is weakened or stopped and which narrower observations remain |
| Residual question | whether a different quantity, scale, or task is justified rather than merely imagined |

The last field prevents a null from becoming an endless escape hatch. A proposed unmeasured route should be retained only if it generates a distinct, feasible, and potentially discriminating observation. Otherwise the current route stops.

### Stopping is a positive method

The following are legitimate stopping conditions:

1. the task, time, phase, or system boundary is not recoverable;
2. the observation is not operationally defined or cannot be reproduced adequately for its purpose;
3. configuration does not discriminate among candidate internal distributions;
4. no receiver-specific quantity connects the proposed distribution to exposure;
5. the exposure–response interval is unobserved and alternatives predict the same endpoint;
6. a result does not exceed relevant error or cannot be interpreted meaningfully;
7. task or state modulation is nonspecific;
8. the new information adds nothing beyond the established account;
9. burden, harm, inequity, opportunity cost, or false reassurance outweighs likely value;
10. another clinical or whole-person concern carries priority;
11. the question lies outside the user's professional, research, or editorial scope; or
12. the relevant domain already has a validated tool that should replace the generic scaffold.

A stop applies to the unsupported arrow, not to mechanics everywhere. The account can retain the task, finding, symptom, measured force, or other narrower fact without completing the chain.

## Mechanical correctness of the object-transfer callback

The callback uses only the established BGB-X-0001 scene: a person moves an ordinary object from one support to another and carries it several steps. It does not teach technique, invite imitation, specify an ideal posture, or compare a “good” and “bad” performance.

### Governing balance and the boundary rule

For a declared system $S$, Newton–Euler accounting can be written schematically as

\[
\sum \mathbf{F}^{(S)}_{\mathrm{ext}} = \frac{d\mathbf{P}_S}{dt},
\qquad
\sum \mathbf{M}^{(S)}_{O,\mathrm{ext}} = \frac{d\mathbf{H}_{O,S}}{dt},
\]

where $O$ is fixed in the declared inertial frame. The same physical hand–object interaction is internal to a person-plus-object system and external to a person-only or object-only system. Its physical existence does not change; its classification in the balance does.

Winter's biomechanics text develops free-body, force, moment, segment, energy, and inverse-dynamics reasoning for human movement.[^winter2009] The source supports this boundary callback and the distinction between measured external quantities and calculated segment resultants. It does not establish the book's hard/soft language, a posterior route, tissue exposure, or any clinical claim.

| Selected system | External interactions represented | What can be concluded | What remains unavailable |
|---|---|---|---|
| Person plus object | gravity on both, ground reactions, first or second support contact when present, and any other external assistance | whole-system momentum and angular-momentum balance | hand–object contact as an external term; local joint or tissue sharing |
| Person alone | body weight, ground reactions, object-on-hand contact, and other external support | demand entering the person and whole-person resultant relations | unique internal path, individual muscle force, tissue stress, or biological response |
| Object alone | object weight, hand-on-object contact, first/second support contact | acceptance, acceleration, approximate equilibrium during a selected interval, deceleration, and release | how the person produced the hand contact |
| Selected segment | segment weight, external contact if present, and neighboring joint/contact resultants at the cuts | net segment force and moment balance under declared kinematics and inertial parameters | unique muscle, tendon, ligament, articular, or fascial sharing |
| Joint or interface | resultants plus candidate muscle/tendon action, contact, restraint, and geometry | load-sharing hypotheses and, with enough inputs, selected calculated or modeled quantities | unique pressure or stress field without geometry, material, activation, and boundary assumptions |
| Tissue or local region | boundary traction, pressure, active state, constraint, and material relation | modeled or measured stress, strain, pressure, deformation, or excursion under the selected representation | adaptation, injury, symptom, diagnosis, or prognosis without further evidence |

### Four required corrections to intuitive reading

1. **Zero acceleration is not zero force.** During an interval in which the object is approximately steady, its net external force may be approximately zero while weight and hand forces remain nonzero and internal muscular and contact forces persist.
2. **The carry phase is not mechanically constant merely because the object looks steady.** Repeated foot contacts, balance corrections, segment accelerations, and changing internal sharing can coexist with a visually stable object.
3. **A net joint resultant is not one tissue's force.** Inverse dynamics calculates a net residual compatible with the model and inputs; it does not uniquely resolve co-contraction or individual muscle and passive-tissue forces.
4. **Moving inward changes the model, not just the zoom.** Segment, joint, interface, and tissue questions require new geometry, constitutive, activation, and contact information.

Erdemir and colleagues reviewed model-based estimation of muscle forces and show why the muscle-force problem is indeterminate without recruitment or optimization assumptions, model parameters, and validation evidence.[^erdemir2007] This supports the nonuniqueness statement. It does not imply that every model is equally plausible or that internal forces can never be estimated usefully.

### Why the four outcomes must differ

The scaffold fails editorially if it produces “mechanics confirmed” four times. Its comparison should show:

1. a relatively strong bounded internal-distribution relation with downstream clinical bridges still open;
2. co-observed anatomy and endpoint with one decisive reconstructed historical interval;
3. a branching recurrent-state model in which material, task, experience, and later event do not collapse; and
4. a source-grounded but explicitly hypothetical mixed chronology whose answer can be mechanical, reverse, shared, adaptive, low-priority, or unresolved.

The difference arises from evidence state and completeness, not from declaring one diagnosis inherently mechanical and another inherently nonmechanical.

## Development path before any clinical-tool claim

Skivington and colleagues' updated Medical Research Council framework emphasizes context, program theory, stakeholder perspectives, uncertainty, refinement, implementation, and economic considerations across development and evaluation.[^skivington2021] Chapter 27 is not necessarily a complex intervention, but the framework supports iterative development rather than immediate release of an attractive question set.

A defensible future program would include:

1. **Concept and content work:** define users, purposes, constructs, excluded uses, and the causal model; conduct patient, clinician, researcher, engineer, disability, and accessibility review.
2. **Observation specification:** operationalize each candidate observation; define task, phase, frame, category or quantity, provenance, ambiguity, and competing interpretations.
3. **Feasibility and burden:** study comprehension, time, missingness, equity, workflow fit, opportunity cost, and unintended posture or self-blame messages.
4. **Reliability and agreement:** evaluate raters, repeated conditions, learning, blinding, independence, error, and relevant precision.
5. **Validity and discrimination:** test prespecified hypotheses and alternatives at each arrow rather than validate a global “mechanical” construct.
6. **Responsiveness and meaning:** where tracking change is intended, distinguish detectable, real, and meaningful change.
7. **External validation:** repeat in new populations, tasks, settings, professions, and prevalence conditions.
8. **Incremental-value comparison:** compare the scaffold with existing histories, examinations, rehabilitation, ergonomic analysis, and documentation.
9. **Decision-consequence evaluation:** determine whether use improves a declared decision or outcome and whether benefits justify harms and burden.
10. **Replacement and retirement rules:** remove questions that are unreliable, redundant, biasing, burdensome, or superseded by validated domain-specific tools.

This program belongs primarily to Chapter 29. Chapter 27 can show why it is needed.
