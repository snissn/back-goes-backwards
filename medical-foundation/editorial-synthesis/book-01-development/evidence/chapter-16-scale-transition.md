## Editorial decision

Chapter 16 can state a positive whole-system proposition without pretending that *systemic* is one mechanism:

> A whole-body mechanical pattern need not be reducible to one local variable or one initiating lesion. A change in one region can alter the participation of neighboring regions; repeated regional solutions can contribute to a stable task-level organization; and task-level geometry, contacts, and control can focus demand back onto a local receiver. These relations are bidirectional, conditional, and measurable. A mechanical reorganization may then have sensory, pressure-and-flow, tissue-response, or energetic consequences, but those are separate branches. Evidence for one branch does not establish the others.

This formulation preserves the archive's intuition that a person can undergo a consequential, system-level reorganization rather than merely accumulate unrelated local changes. It also makes the idea researchable. *Whole-body organization* means a repeatable relation among declared segments, contacts, forces, timing, or task outcomes. It does not yet mean a preferred all-Yang configuration, a topological invariant, a fascial master network, or a medical cause.

The chapter's affirmative center is therefore stronger than “everything is multifactorial.” Mechanical changes can cross anatomical regions and levels of description. The evidence below demonstrates several bounded transitions. What remains hypothetical is whether a given transition persists, how far it travels, which physiological branch it enters, and whether it has medical consequence.

## The multiscale grammar

The chapter should use four levels without treating them as interchangeable:

```text
local receiver or relation
    <-> neighboring or regional participation
    <-> task-level organization across several regions and contacts
    <-> whole-person state, environment, and valued outcome
```

The arrows are not causal facts. Each proposed transition must add the information that the next level requires:

- **local:** named surface, volume, interface, joint, muscle, nerve, vessel, or relation and a measured or modeled quantity;
- **regional:** declared segments, contacts, resultants, timing, recruitment, or work allocation;
- **task:** endpoint, whole-body momentum, support geometry, external work, speed, stability relation, or another explicit aggregate;
- **whole-person and environment:** pain, fatigue, attention, expectation, skill, disability, assistance, instruction, terrain, tool, social demand, and the person's selected outcome.

A local quantity cannot be obtained by merely zooming into a task-level measure. Conversely, a whole-task pattern cannot be inferred by enlarging one local field. The bridge may be mechanical, sensory, behavioral, environmental, or some combination. The chapter should show what was measured at each level and leave an arrow absent when its intermediate relation is unknown.

## The nearest scale transition is real—and conditional

An induced knee restriction supplies a direct positive example. Seventeen healthy adult men walked normally and with the right knee restricted by a brace at nominal 15- and 30-degree settings. Motion and ground-reaction measurements entered a musculoskeletal model of the lumbar region. Walking speed fell under both restriction conditions. Trunk alignment changed; the 30-degree condition increased the modeled lumbar extension moment during defined phases, and selected modeled paraspinal muscle forces and vertical and anterior L4–L5 joint-reaction-force components increased across restriction comparisons.[^nakatsuji2022]

This is a bounded local-to-regional result. The knee condition preceded a measured gait change and a modeled lumbar change, but the result belongs to the changed gait solution rather than an isolated knee-angle effect. It does not show that a knee restriction always overloads the back, that the modeled force was harmful, or that a naturally occurring contracture has the same history as an experimental brace. It also does not decide whether the best description runs “knee to back” or “a new whole-task solution changes both.” The task and model couple them.

A second restriction experiment provides the necessary null. Eight unimpaired participants walked with unilateral and bilateral ankle, knee, or combined restrictions. Participant-scaled musculoskeletal models used measured kinematics and ground reactions, with EMG-informed timing constraints, to estimate lower-limb muscle and joint-reaction forces. Some restrictions changed ground-reaction peaks and loading rates, but the modeled lower-limb joint-reaction forces remained relatively similar because modeled muscle forces changed during loading response.[^mccain2023]

The two studies should be taught together:

```text
selected regional restriction
    -> visible or measured task change
    -> another region may change / may not change
    -> selected internal exposure may rise / fall / remain similar / remain unknown
```

Neither study cancels the other. They examine different receivers, models, and conditions. Together they establish the correct research posture: cross-region propagation is mechanically intelligible and experimentally observable, but its destination and magnitude cannot be read from the visible perturbation alone.

## Regional participation can become a whole-task organization

Walking supplies a useful multiregion system because arms, trunk, legs, ground contacts, and environmental support can be changed and measured separately. In sixteen healthy young adults, unilateral and bilateral manipulations of arm swing changed the amplitude of the opposite arm and altered the phase-coordination index of the lower limbs during treadmill walking.[^bondi2017] The result supports interlimb coupling under the tested instructions. It does not identify one spinal, fascial, or neural carrier, and attention to the unusual instruction remains a plausible contributor.

Whole-body angular momentum makes aggregation explicit. In a split-belt experiment, twenty-four healthy young adults adapted while either holding a handrail or walking with free arm swing. Initial belt asymmetry changed whole-body angular momentum through both changes in segmental momenta and changes in their cancellation. Handrail contact reduced the early perturbation, altered the coupling between angular momentum and step-length asymmetry, and produced a smaller early aftereffect.[^park2022]

This is a strong example of environmental support becoming part of whole-task organization. The handrail did not merely add “stability” in the abstract. It changed the system boundary and the relations among segmental motion, aggregate momentum, asymmetry, and adaptation. It also shows why a visually coherent pattern does not imply one origin: belt speeds, hand contact, learning, and segmental coordination all participate.

An aggregate can conceal different internal solutions. Twelve healthy adults walked with in-phase, restricted, normal, and several exaggerated arm-swing conditions. Increasing arm-swing amplitude generally reduced vertical angular momentum and ground-reaction moment up to a point, but the most exaggerated swing did not continue to reduce metabolic cost and instead raised it.[^degraaf2019] Segmental organization, task-level mechanics, and energy use therefore changed in related but nonidentical ways.

For Book I, a *stable whole-task organization* should mean only that a declared multiregion relation recurs across a stated interval or set of tasks. It may be learned, constrained, assisted, comfortable, protective, culturally practiced, disability-specific, or transient. A durable reorganization beyond the laboratory remains a longitudinal hypothesis. It should be tested with repeated multiregion measures rather than inferred from one posture or image.

## The reverse path—global to local—must remain equally available

A whole-task configuration changes local exposure only through intermediate mechanics. Chapter 12 supplied the governing sequence: task and environment, regional forces and moments, interface sharing, local field, and separately tested response. Chapter 16 adds feedback. If several regions settle into a repeated timing, support, and contact pattern, that organization can alter a named local moment, pressure, strain, activation, or excursion at the next task.

The knee-restriction example can be read in both directions. Locally imposed restriction changed the whole gait solution; the resulting task-level solution changed modeled lumbar quantities. The split-belt example works similarly: an environmental asymmetry recruited segmental changes into whole-body angular momentum, while hand contact changed the task-level state and the adaptation expressed by individual steps. “Local causes global” and “global causes local” are therefore not competing creeds. They are candidate segments of a feedback history.

Every global-to-local claim should include at least four alternatives:

1. the visible whole-body change does not materially change the selected local exposure;
2. a different local receiver changes instead;
3. active control, pressure-mediated support, a tool, or environmental contact redistributes the demand before it reaches the proposed receiver; or
4. one common task, state, or instruction changes both the global pattern and local measure without either causing the other.

This structure preserves the author's proposed configuration-transition program. A discontinuity, sharp gradient, reversal, or cross-midline rerouting in a future force-, moment-, or strain-field representation can remain a protected research question. Book I does not call it a topological change or assume that visual displacement reveals the field. It asks which representation, continuity condition, and observation would distinguish a true reorganization from ordinary movement within a capable configuration.

## Sensory and control consequences form their own branch

Mechanical support and sensory information can be experimentally separated imperfectly but usefully. In ten healthy adults standing with eyes closed, a robot applied approximately one newton of trunk-velocity-dependent light touch at the upper back. The light-touch conditions reduced selected center-of-pressure sway measures; added position-dependent vibration affected some outcomes differently.[^saini2019] The study supports a task-specific control response to a small, information-bearing contact. Because small contact forces and the robot's mechanical behavior remained part of the condition, it does not isolate a purely sensory carrier, establish a body-wide proprioceptive deficit, demonstrate an autonomic effect, or identify a universal sensory mechanism.

Experimental pain provides a positive and null pair. In a series of thumb conditions completed by ten to twelve participants per condition, local muscle or skin pain impaired passive-movement detection at some anatomically relevant sites while several control comparisons remained unchanged.[^weerakkody2008] In twenty-eight participants with sustained experimental forearm pain and hyperalgesia after nerve-growth-factor injections, wrist joint-position error did not change over the measured days.[^summers2019]

These findings protect the sensory–bracing hypothesis while bounding it. A local state can alter a selected sensory measure, and a small sensory contact can alter control. But pain may precede the sensory change; another sensory measure may remain unchanged; instruction or expectation may alter activation first; and no study here establishes the full sequence from mechanical strain to sensory uncertainty to chronic bracing to medical consequence.

The branch registry should therefore keep separate rows for:

- muscle activation or task-specific tone;
- perceived effort;
- proprioceptive discrimination or position sense;
- interoceptive report;
- peripheral nerve excursion or conduction;
- anticipatory, corrective, or protective control; and
- any autonomic variable, which requires its own physiological measure.

The word *signaling* cannot connect those rows by itself.

## Pressure, flow, and excursion are several branches, not one fluid story

Position can change a vessel's geometry and measured flow pattern, but the sign and meaning depend on the vessel, posture, method, and output. In nineteen healthy participants assessed bilaterally, provocative arm positions produced Doppler waveforms classified as significant arterial compression in at least one position in 28.9 percent of participants. Peak systolic velocity and systolic rise time were variable and did not correlate with the compression classification; positional findings in healthy people would have generated many false-positive thoracic-outlet inferences.[^bishop2021]

In a different study of forty-nine healthy volunteers, moving the arm into a specified abducted, flexed, externally rotated position increased the measured cross-sectional area of the subclavian vein on average rather than narrowing it.[^sadek2016] The study was designed around ultrasound visualization, not symptom causation or venous-outflow performance. Paired with the arterial study, it demonstrates why “arm position affects the vasculature” is only the beginning of a question.

Nerve mechanics is a different object again. Ultrasound measurements in healthy participants found median-nerve excursion from movements at the wrist, elbow, shoulder, and neck, ranging in the reported locations and conditions from 0.3 millimeters during contralateral neck side-flexion to 10.4 millimeters during elbow extension. Reported additional forearm strain varied by maneuver, and the investigators judged the tested extended-limb strain likely below levels expected to impair blood flow or conduction.[^dilley2003] Normal excursion is therefore an affirmative mechanical function, not evidence of entrapment.

These examples should remain in separate panels:

| Object | Candidate input | Intermediate measure | Output that still requires support |
|---|---|---|---|
| Artery | position, contact, external pressure, active state | lumen geometry, Doppler waveform, pressure gradient | tissue perfusion, symptom, diagnosis |
| Vein | position, pressure, muscle pump, valve state | cross-sectional area, velocity, pressure, volume change | venous return, swelling, clinical significance |
| Lymphatic | deformation, intrinsic contraction, external pressure, valve state | segment-specific pressure or flow | drainage, edema, immune or inflammatory outcome |
| Peripheral nerve | bed length, contact, tether, joint motion | excursion, strain, shear, conduction | sensory or motor finding, symptom, diagnosis |
| Tendon or fascial interface | force, motion, contact, material state | relative glide, shear strain, pressure | tissue response, function, symptom |
| Pressure-bearing cavity | geometry, muscular boundary, phase, external demand | pressure and expansion pattern | support, ventilation, continence, circulation, or symptom |

The lymphatic, sliding-interface, and cavity rows are protected research obligations here, not evidence-backed conclusions from the three studies above. Chapter 20 must source each one independently. No evidence about a nerve validates a vessel claim; no arterial waveform validates a lymphatic consequence; and no change in lumen or excursion is automatically adverse.

## Tissue response is not the next automatic arrow

The tissue-response branch begins only after an exposure has been named. Sensitization, inflammatory signaling, remodeling, repair, fatigue, wear, degeneration, and injury are different responses with different time courses and evidence. A local mechanical change can occur without any of them. A biological state can also alter material properties, pain, activation, movement, and geometry before the mechanical observation.

Chapter 16 should not import one token inflammation citation and use it to authorize a cascade. It should hand Chapter 19 a structured question:

```text
named receiver and local exposure history
    -> measured material or cellular intermediate
    -> time-resolved tissue response
    -> functional or clinical outcome, if any

coequal branches:
    no measurable response / useful adaptation / recovery / reverse direction /
    common biological cause / exposure mismeasured
```

The archive's inflammation and gradual-reorganization ideas are preserved by this handoff. They are not rejected; they are assigned the evidence program capable of strengthening, narrowing, or disfavoring them.

## Energetic cost and regulatory demand require named denominators

Energy is the best-supported downstream branch in this packet because mechanical assistance, motion, muscle activation, and oxygen-based metabolic measures can be recorded together. It is also the clearest demonstration that no one visible pattern is intrinsically efficient.

The arm-swing experiment showed a nonlinear relation: changes that reduced whole-body angular-momentum and ground-reaction-moment measures did not continue to reduce cost of transport when arm swing became excessive.[^degraaf2019] Mechanical aggregation and metabolic outcome were related but not identical.

External assistance can lower a measured cost. In seven participants walking with a 6.8-kilogram rucksack, a multi-joint soft exosuit used person-specific control parameters. Powered individualized assistance reduced measured metabolic cost relative to both walking without the device and walking with it unpowered.[^lee2018] The individualized profiles differed among participants. This blocks any rule that an altered, technologically assisted, or noncanonical route must be less efficient.

Assistance can also cross from useful to costly. Twelve healthy men walked under several assistance magnitudes from an energy-removing exoskeleton. Moderate assistance reduced measured metabolic cost by 3.4 percent relative to normal walking and reduced measured hamstring activity. Under the higher tested magnitude, metabolic cost exceeded normal walking and selected muscle activity rose relative to lower-assistance comparisons.[^shepertycky2023] The experiment demonstrates a task-, device-, timing-, magnitude-, and sample-specific help-to-hindrance reversal; it does not locate a universal or person-specific threshold.

The term *cost* must therefore name its numerator and denominator: muscle activation over what interval, mechanical work by which segment, net metabolic power at what speed, oxygen cost per distance, perceived effort, recovery time, attentional demand, or loss of task options. A strategy can improve one while worsening another. *Reserve* can mean capacity to repeat, vary, recover, or tolerate a perturbation for a declared task. It is not a hidden energy tank.

*Regulatory* remains a research heading, not a synonym for autonomic dysfunction. A candidate claim must name heart-rate variability, blood pressure, ventilatory work, endocrine response, temperature regulation, attentional control, or another defined variable, then support the mechanical bridge and whole-person alternatives independently. Fatigue, diffuse pain, and low resilience remain contexts in which such measurements may be useful; they are not evidence that mechanics is the primary cause.

## Archive preservation and correction

The archive contains a valuable set of questions that should survive:

- soft-tissue tone and glide as possible participants in long-range organization;
- repeated compensation changing effort, perception, and the organization presented to later tasks;
- pressure and available space affecting vessels, nerves, sliding interfaces, and cavities in system-specific ways;
- cumulative reorganization without a discrete injury;
- a local mechanical state interacting with sensory and physiological processes;
- energetic cost, recovery demand, and resilience as possible consequences; and
- the broader proposal that a body can reorganize coherently rather than only fail at isolated sites.

The evidence does not support carrying forward the archive's compressed arrows as publication claims: fascial continuity does not by itself prove body-wide force transmission; alignment does not establish circulation, lymphatic, autonomic, inflammatory, immune, or chronic-disease effects; activation does not establish a depleted reserve; and a positional vascular or nerve finding does not establish symptoms or treatment.

The correction is additive rather than ablative. Every ambitious relation remains in the project as a branch with an object, measurement, chronology, alternative, and destination. Stronger preferred-configuration, Six-Division, breath-geometry, and practice claims remain protected in Books II–V, where they can receive their own formal and evidentiary work.

## Claim and model actions for later registry synchronization

No stable identifiers should be allocated until the packet is independently reviewed. The later control pass should create or amend records for:

- conditional local-to-regional recruitment;
- repeatable regional-to-task organization without a single-lesion requirement;
- global-to-local concentration through the Chapter 12 bridge;
- physiological branch separation and the prohibition on evidence borrowing;
- task-specific energetic cost, benefit, reserve, and dose reversal;
- a bidirectional multiscale map;
- a branch-obligation registry; and
- a cost model with named numerator, denominator, task, interval, and alternative.

## Boundaries and handoffs

- **Chapter 12 owns** the bridge from task geometry through regional balance and interface sharing to a local field.
- **Chapter 14 owns** task-specific relation loss, correction, bracing, and sensory-control loops.
- **Chapter 15 owns** persistence, transfer, rerouting, and delayed mechanical history.
- **Chapter 16 owns** bidirectional scale transitions and the separation of downstream research branches.
- **Chapter 17 owns** where such mechanics already has accepted medical uses.
- **Chapter 18 owns** the proposed contribution of route history and multiscale representation.
- **Chapter 19 owns** exposure-to-tissue-response evidence.
- **Chapter 20 owns** system-specific vessel, lymphatic, nerve, sliding-interface, and cavity comparisons.
- **Chapter 21 owns** causal role, symptoms, whole-person alternatives, and clinical interpretation.
- **Books II–V own** preferred configuration, TCM coordinate mapping, breath geometry, assessment, and practice.

The chapter's strongest defensible conclusion is affirmative: regional mechanics can couple into a coherent whole-task organization, and whole-task organization can return demand to local receivers. The next physiological step is not one systemic cascade. It is a set of separable, testable branches, any one of which may prove important, beneficial, null, reversed, or unrelated.
