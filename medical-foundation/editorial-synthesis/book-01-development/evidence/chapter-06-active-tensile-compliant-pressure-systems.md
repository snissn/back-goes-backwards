## Editorial decision

Chapter 6 can be drafted around a bounded positive proposition:

> Skeletal geometry becomes usable living support through changing cooperation among active muscle, tensile tissues, deformable contacts, sliding interfaces, pressure-bearing boundaries, sensory control, and prior mechanical state. Each relation can be real at a named scale without proving one continuous body-wide cable or one global control variable.

This formulation preserves the archive's central insight that the body is not a stack of isolated bones actuated by independent motors. It also preserves the stronger research intuition that distributed tension, pressure, and prior state may create system-level organization. What changes is the order of proof. Anatomical continuity, task-level coordination, measured local transfer, regional prestress, and a whole-body tension-network model are separate propositions. Support for one is not silently promoted to the next.

The chapter should not become a tissue catalogue. The recurring object transfer supplies one changing event. Every component enters because it performs a job in a declared phase: initiating or modulating force, transmitting tension, restraining a relation, distributing contact, receiving form, permitting excursion, containing pressure, sensing state, or preparing the assembly for what happens next.

## Quantity, boundary, and scale controls

Chapter 6 inherits the Chapter 2 quantity stack. The words *tension network*, *pressure support*, *tone*, and *force transfer* do not relax it.

```text
task and external contacts
    -> segment forces and moments
    -> active and passive component forces
    -> interface traction, contact pressure, or cavity pressure
    -> local stress, strain, deformation, or excursion
    -> separately evidenced biological response
```

The chapter may move among levels, but every move must name what was measured, calculated, modeled, or inferred. Surface electromyography is not muscle force. Fascial marker displacement is not a whole-body stress field. Intracavity pressure is not the force on a structure until a surface, normal, area, and geometry are declared. Contact pressure is not material stress everywhere beneath the contact. A coordinated task is not proof that one tissue network transmitted the decisive force.

Three scale transitions require explicit gates:

1. **component to local assembly:** a muscle, tendon, interface, joint, or cavity participates in a bounded relation;
2. **local to regional:** several named components transfer or redistribute a measurable quantity across a declared anatomical region; and
3. **regional to whole body:** the regional relation remains consequential after other carriers, control strategies, contacts, and scales are included.

The archive's integrated field and global-set-point ideas remain legitimate hypotheses at the third level. They cannot borrow certainty from evidence at the first.

## Source-grounded mechanical kernels

### Active muscle changes both force and system state

Muscle can shorten, remain approximately isometric, or lengthen while active. Its force depends on activation, length, shortening or lengthening velocity, architecture, passive elements, prior contraction, and the boundary conditions of the muscle–tendon unit.[^lieber2011] Recent reviews of history-dependent force describe residual enhancement after active lengthening and depression after active shortening, while also noting unresolved mechanisms and uncertainty about in-vivo functional consequences.[^hahn2023] These findings are sufficient to reject a motor-plus-cable picture in which a named muscle simply emits one force value from its anatomy.

The same active system can accelerate, hold, brake, redirect, alter joint contact, or change the force–displacement response of an assembly. Agonist–antagonist coactivation can increase apparent joint stiffness, but its relation to action stability depends on the task and the modeled effector; more coactivation is not automatically more stable, efficient, or healthy.[^latash2018]

Surface electromyography records electrical activity through a measurement process; it does not directly measure force. A review of the surface-EMG–force relation emphasizes confounding factors and the difficulty of estimating force, especially in dynamic applications.[^disselhorst2009] Visible motion is further underdetermined because many muscle-force combinations can satisfy the same net joint demand.

**Permitted use:** active state changes candidate paths, contacts, resistance to perturbation, and the system's starting condition.

**Prohibited inference:** visible effort, posture, or an EMG amplitude reveals a unique internal route, an ideal activation pattern, or a global tone value.

### Tendon is a dynamic tensile participant, not a lossless wire

Tendon can carry longitudinal tension while deforming and interacting with muscle work. At a finer scale, fascicles and the interfascicular matrix can permit relative sliding; experiments comparing two equine tendons found greater interfascicular sliding in the tested energy-storing tendon than in the tested positional tendon.[^thorpe2012] Roberts and Azizi's review describes biological springs as capable of energy conservation, power amplification, power attenuation, and rapid mechanical feedback, with the function determined by timing and energy flow rather than by the tissue label alone.[^roberts2011] Experimental work in landing turkeys showed that temporary tendon energy storage reduced muscle-fascicle lengthening velocity and power absorption during a particular high-rate task.[^konow2012] It is a powerful bounded example of a form-receiving element altering how demand reaches active tissue; it is not a general human injury-protection rule.

Direction and interface matter. Experiments on two porcine wrap-around tendons found region- and orientation-dependent responses under longitudinal tension, transverse compression, and combined loading.[^bol2024] The findings permit a single tendon to participate in a comparatively shape-preserving relation in one direction while receiving form in another; they do not supply generic values for human tendons or every task.

Ligaments share some tensile and time-dependent features but have a different anatomical job. They guide and restrain relative motion through configuration-dependent recruitment. Capsules contain and guide a joint, contribute passive resistance, and provide an interface between articular, ligamentous, and surrounding tissues. Neither structure should be reduced to a passive cord or assigned a permanent hard role.

**First-order handoff:** active tissue can produce or gather force; tendon or a recruited restraint can transfer or constrain it; an enthesis, bone, and joint contact can enter structural resolution.

**Reality check:** several active tissues, tendons, ligaments, contacts, and external supports act simultaneously, and energy or force can branch, return, or change sign. The first-order handoff is a readable trace, not a literal conveyor.

### Deformation and excursion are part of support

Articular cartilage is a fluid-saturated solid. Ateshian's review describes theoretical and experimental evidence that interstitial-fluid pressurization can carry a large fraction of applied contact demand under specified cartilage tests and can contribute to low friction.[^ateshian2009] Park and colleagues directly measured high peak fluid-load-support fractions in human and bovine cartilage specimens under unconfined compression and linked that behavior to tension–compression nonlinearity.[^park2003] The measured fractions belong to those specimens and protocols; they are not universal percentages for living joints.

This example establishes a durable chapter principle: useful support can require deformation, time dependence, fluid movement, and a preserved sliding or contact relation. Bursae, tendon sheaths, fascial planes, and other interfaces provide relations across which neighboring structures can move relative to one another. That movement is not necessarily a gap in support; it can be one condition of support.

The chapter should distinguish four objects in every interface example:

- gross task motion;
- relative excursion or glide between named structures;
- local deformation of each structure; and
- contact pressure, traction, or stress inferred or measured at the interface.

Chapter 13 later asks what happens when a required form-receiving freedom is persistently replaced by a maintained boundary. Chapter 6 supplies the neutral positive baseline: ordinary deformation and excursion are not pathology, and less movement is not automatically worse.

### Pressure acts through bounded surfaces

Pressure contributes a surface traction. For an idealized pressure field on a declared boundary portion, its resultant contribution is represented by

\[
\mathbf F_p = \int_A -p\,\mathbf n\,dA,
\]

where the selected surface \(A\), sign, and normal convention must be declared. The equation prevents “pressure” from floating free of a boundary. Uniform pressure over a complete closed boundary has no net translational resultant even though its distributed traction can stress the wall and resist deformation. Geometry, pressure differences, wall behavior, active control, external contact, and constraints determine the structural consequence.

Hydrostatic skeletons provide a clear biological existence proof. They use internal pressure, muscular or connective-tissue control, and approximately volume-conserving contents to generate support, movement, and variable stiffness without rigid skeletal elements.[^kier2012] That architecture demonstrates what pressure-mediated support can mean. It does not establish equivalence between an invertebrate hydrostat and a human abdominal or thoracic cavity.

In three human participants, Hodges and colleagues used phrenic-nerve stimulation to raise intra-abdominal pressure without concurrent abdominal or back-extensor activity and reported an 8–31% increase in the study's prone lumbar-stiffness measure.[^hodges2005] This is a bounded existence proof that pressure can change a measured structural response in that setup. The sample was very small, the method invasive, the participants prone, and the endpoint was not posture, pain, health, or whole-body organization. The study does not establish voluntary breathing as a treatment or master driver.

Cartilage fluid pressurization, a hydrostatic skeleton, and intra-abdominal pressure illustrate different systems at different scales. They should be shown side by side precisely so the reader does not pool them into one phenomenon. The transferable proposition is only that pressure can share structural work when the boundary and mechanism are specified.

### Fascia belongs in the model at named scales

The term *fascia* is used inconsistently across anatomical and clinical literatures. A nomenclature editorial distinguished a named anatomical fascia from the broader “fascial system,” and a 2025 *Journal of Anatomy* proposal again noted the absence of clear consensus while proposing a layered, multiscale system.[^stecco2016][^stecco2025] These are terminology proposals, not evidence that one body-wide route carries a consequential amount of force.

At bounded scales, fascia can connect, separate, contain, partition, provide an attachment, and permit or constrain relative movement. Barker and colleagues applied 10 N traction to named muscle attachments in eight unembalmed cadavers and measured tension and marker movement in the posterior and middle lumbar fasciae.[^barker2004] The experiment demonstrates regional tensile transmission under its dissection and loading conditions. It does not quantify in-vivo whole-body route dominance, neural control, clinical relevance, or a traditional channel.

Two 2016 systematic reviews clarify the next steps. Wilke and colleagues found varying anatomical support for proposed myofascial chains and identified functional relevance as a separate question.[^wilke2016] Krause and colleagues reviewed nine studies and concluded that tension can transfer between some adjacent structures, while emphasizing methodological heterogeneity, anatomical variation, and the need for in-vivo functional work.[^krause2016]

A 2026 meta-analysis of 15 studies reported remote stiffness increases during local lengthening and contraction, but rated certainty very low for lengthening and moderate for contraction; methods were heterogeneous, reporting bias was suspected, and only eight studies monitored unwanted muscle activity with electromyography.[^kretschmer2026] Remote stiffness is an endpoint compatible with passive transfer, active control, and measurement-specific effects. It does not by itself isolate fascial transmission, identify one route, or establish clinical consequence.

The chapter should use a **continuity ladder**:

1. named anatomical tissues are connected;
2. a local or regional perturbation produces measurable deformation or tension elsewhere;
3. the magnitude is consequential during a specified living task;
4. the relation materially changes a task-level or clinical endpoint after other carriers and control are considered; and
5. a body-wide network supplies a better model than competing linked-segment, continuum, pressure, or control accounts.

Evidence on one rung does not automatically establish the next. This ladder preserves the research program more effectively than either calling fascia irrelevant or declaring it the governing substrate in advance.

### Initial state is plural before it is global

The object transfer begins before the hand touches the object. Passive tissue tension, active muscle state, joint contact, ligament recruitment, pressure, support conditions, and recent movement history all help define what system receives the next demand. Muscle thixotropy and history-dependent force provide direct reminders that mechanically relevant state can persist even when the visible pose is the same.[^lakie2019]

The chapter should separate three levels:

| Level | Permitted meaning | Example observables | What remains unproved |
|---|---|---|---|
| Local baseline state | Active and passive state of a named tissue or small assembly before the event | EMG with limits, force, displacement response, muscle length/history, local pressure | A global neural or mechanical setting |
| Regional prestress or preparation | Pre-existing tensions, compressions, contacts, coactivation, or pressure in a declared region | perturbation response, pressure, joint response, component force estimates | Whole-body uniformity, optimality, or one control variable |
| Global set-point hypothesis | Coordinated whole-person state that constrains later routing across regions | requires simultaneous multiscale measurements and a formal model | Existence, scalar form, preferred value, health effect, and causal controller |

In mechanics, *prestress* means stress present before an added external demand in a declared model. It does not mean readiness, health, or tensegrity by definition. *Baseline tone* should be used only after stating whether it refers to neural activation, passive muscle behavior, connective-tissue tension, palpated resistance, or a model parameter. The archive's global mechanical set point remains protected as a later hypothesis; Chapter 6 should neither assert it nor erase it.

### Tensegrity must become a model, not a synonym for every tension network

Formal tensegrity structures are not defined by looking interconnected. They require specified nodes, tension and compression members, connectivity, boundary conditions, member behavior, and a stable prestressed state in self-equilibrium under the declared constraints. Prestressability and subsequent static or dynamic response are formal problems rather than visual judgments.[^sultan2001][^murakami2001]

The body contains many distributed tensile and compressive relations, so tensegrity is a reasonable candidate representation. It may help ask how prestress changes stiffness, how a perturbation redistributes through a network, or how a shape persists without simple stacked compression. It cannot be selected because bones look like struts and fascia looks continuous. Skeletal segments transmit compressive resultants through articular contacts; tissues carry multiple loading modes; topology changes with contact; muscle is active; and control, pressure, and external supports matter.

A broader **tension-network model** need not satisfy the stricter definition of tensegrity. It may include contacted compression carriers, multiaxial tissues, active members, pressure boundaries, and changing topology. That freedom preserves more of the archive's research intuition, but it also requires the model to name its carriers, state variables, constitutive or control rules, boundaries, and predictions. Calling the model broader cannot make it exempt from comparison or falsification.

The controlled comparison is:

| Model family | Natural object | Required inputs | Potential advantage | Stop rule |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Linked-segment / multibody | segment motions, joint resultants, external contacts | geometry, inertia, constraints, active-force model | efficient task-level force and moment accounting | internal tissue question remains unresolved |
| Continuum / finite element | local fields in a selected body or tissue | geometry, constitutive law, boundary and initial conditions | stress, strain, deformation, contact fields | inputs or validation insufficient; whole-person scale impractical |
| Muscle–tendon / control or synergy | active force and coordinated actuation | activation/control model, muscle–tendon geometry and properties | explains state-dependent force and redundancy | does not by itself specify all passive and pressure carriers |
| Pressure-network | cavities or fluid-saturated tissues | pressure field, boundary geometry, wall/material behavior | explains bounded pressure-mediated support | no defined boundary or pressure difference |
| Broad tension-network | named tensile, compressive, active, contact, or pressure relations | carrier map, component laws, state variables, boundaries, contact and control rules | may integrate relations that strict model families separate | merely relabels the components or yields no discriminating prediction |
| Formal tensegrity | prestressed network of declared tension and compression members and nodes | topology, member laws, prestress, constraints, and contact rules | may predict distributed response and shape persistence | stable prestressed mapping cannot be made or adds no discriminating prediction |

The broad tension-network hypothesis and formal tensegrity both remain in Book I as research-generating models, but they are not synonyms. Each survives only where a bounded implementation outperforms or clarifies alternatives—not because it can absorb every observation after the fact.

## Integrated object-transfer model

The recurring object transfer should be traced through five phases without assigning one tissue to one permanent role.

1. **Approach and contact.** The object still has its original support. Skin and local interfaces conform; active muscle positions the hand; skeletal geometry creates contact possibilities; prior tone and pressure are initial conditions.
2. **Acceptance and departure.** Support transfers progressively. Active force rises or changes direction; tendons transmit and deform; joint contacts migrate; ligaments and capsules may recruit; the original surface remains a parallel carrier until separation.
3. **Transport or hold.** External acceleration may be small while internal active force remains necessary. Depending on the selected boundary and task, several muscles, tendons, joint contacts, pressure boundaries, and external contacts may share the demand. Visible stillness does not imply internal inactivity.
4. **Placement and deceleration.** The new surface progressively accepts demand. The muscle–tendon unit may store, return, or dissipate energy, while tendon changes the timing and rate at which active muscle receives it; contact and glide change; placement is not simply departure played backward.
5. **Release and aftermath.** Active state, tissue deformation, pressure, and contact recover on different time scales. The ending configuration may resemble the start while the internal state differs.

The first-order handoff from active or form-receiving tissue toward tensile and skeletal resolution should appear as one highlighted route inside a branching diagram. Alternative carriers, simultaneous feedback, pressure, and external supports remain visible; inertial terms stay in the task's force-and-moment balance rather than being drawn as anatomical paths. If the figure can be read as a single packet moving unaltered from fascia to bone, it has failed.

## Counterexamples, nulls, and falsifiers

| Tempting inference | Counterexample or alternative | Drafting consequence |
|---|---|---|
| More muscle activity means more force or stability | Surface EMG is not force; coactivation effects depend on task and model | Name measurement and endpoint; do not praise maximal bracing |
| Tendon merely transmits muscle force | Tendon can store, return, delay, redirect, or attenuate power and can receive multiaxial deformation | Treat energy and phase explicitly |
| Soft tissue deformation means load failed to reach the skeleton | Cartilage fluid–solid support and tendon deformation can be ordinary parts of successful support | Distinguish useful form reception from adverse maintained substitution |
| Pressure supports the body, therefore breathing is the master organizer | Pressure consequences require a boundary; the human lumbar experiment is tiny and bounded | Keep ordinary pressure mechanics here and breath-driver claims in Book IV |
| Anatomical continuity proves a long force chain | Local connection, measurable regional transfer, task consequence, and whole-body dominance are different rungs | Name fascia, scale, perturbation, magnitude, and competing paths |
| Baseline tone is one evenly distributed body-wide scalar | Local activation, passive tension, pressure, and contact can vary independently | Preserve global set point only as a model to be built and tested |
| Tensegrity is more holistic and therefore more accurate | A conventional model may predict the same observations with fewer assumptions | Require a declared topology and discriminating prediction |
| An integrated system must have one preferred solution | Multiple configurations and carrier combinations can satisfy the same task | Book II owns preference; Chapter 6 establishes architecture only |

The integrated-support model is weakened for a selected task when component balances cannot be reconciled, a proposed carrier shows no predicted participation, a claimed remote transfer is indistinguishable from active control or external-contact changes, or a simpler conventional model predicts the measured outcome equally well. A global tension-network claim must be narrowed when its boundaries, state variables, and scale cannot be declared without changing them after the observation.

## First-draft authorization

A controlled Chapter 6 draft is authorized if it:

- follows one task through changing active, tensile, contact, sliding, pressure, and control relations rather than listing tissues;
- keeps muscle activation, measured motion, EMG, force, moment, stiffness, and stability distinct;
- presents tendon and ligament as deformable, state- and direction-dependent participants rather than passive cords;
- treats deformation and excursion as potentially positive functions before discussing adverse constraint;
- gives pressure a surface, boundary, geometry, and scale every time it performs structural work;
- uses named fasciae and the continuity ladder rather than a single undifferentiated cable;
- distinguishes local baseline state, regional prestress, and global set-point hypothesis;
- displays the simple soft-to-hard handoff inside a branched and time-varying system;
- keeps a broad tension-network hypothesis distinct from formal tensegrity, compares both with at least three conventional alternatives, and includes a real stop rule; and
- hands a complete but neutral tissue architecture to Chapter 7 without deriving posterior preference.

It is not authorized to claim that fascia is the body's governing substrate, that pressure or breath regulates all structure, that baseline tone is optimally even, that the skeleton floats in continuous soft-tissue tension, that tensegrity proves health or preferred configuration, or that any reader should activate, brace, breathe, release, or move in a specified way.
