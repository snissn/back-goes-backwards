**Companion controls:** [Chapter 15 brief](../chapter-briefs/BGB-U-0015-compensation-rerouting.md), [Book I contract](../book-contract.md), [Chapter 10 evidence packet](chapter-10-mechanical-homeostasis-capacity.md), [Chapter 11 evidence packet](chapter-11-continuity-handoff-substitution.md), [Chapter 14 evidence packet](chapter-14-instability-discoordination.md), `BGB-C-0005`, `BGB-M-0003`, `BGB-M-0004`, and `BGB-X-0001`

## Editorial decision

Chapter 15 can preserve the archive's longitudinal intuition in a stronger and more inspectable form:

> Compensation begins as a solution. It is a change in route, timing, stiffness, excursion, support, or behavior that preserves something valuable under a present demand or constraint. If the solution recurs, it can change the exposure and starting state of later tasks. That change may remain useful, become trained, resolve, transfer, narrow available options, produce a measurable cost, or prove irrelevant. A delayed or distant consequence is credible only when the intermediate route, chronology, exposure, receiver, response, and alternatives are separately supported.

This is not a chapter about concealed pathology. *Silent* means that the selected task remains possible or that no symptom is reported; it does not mean that an occult lesion exists. Nor is this a chapter about restoring one original pattern. An assistive device, disability-specific strategy, learned gait, altered support, or persistent asymmetry may be the best available organization for the person's task and goals.

The chapter owns the mechanical history between a successful substitution and a possible later exposure. It does not own tissue injury, degeneration, pain causation, diagnosis, or corrective practice. Those bridges remain in Chapters 19–21 and Book V.

## The compensation-history grammar

The chapter should use one branching sequence consistently:

```text
present task, environment, state, and valued outcome
    -> immediate strategy and protected function
    -> stated change in route, timing, support, work, force, motion, or effort, with evidence state attached
    -> repeated exposure / recovery / task variation / external assistance
    -> retained, resolved, transferred, replaced, or further changed strategy
    -> possible later exposure at a named receiver
    -> separately evidenced material, biological, functional, or clinical response
```

Every arrow can branch to no meaningful change. Recovery, learning, additional capacity, environmental support, and ordinary variability are not footnotes to a progression story; they are coequal outcomes. The last arrow is not established by the earlier mechanics. If a later symptom, lesion, or diagnosis is introduced, Chapter 19 must supply the tissue-response bridge and Chapter 21 must test the causal role and whole-person alternatives.

The timeline should carry evidence-state labels:

- **observed or measured:** task, contact, motion, force, activation, device force, performance, symptom report, or other recorded quantity;
- **recalled:** a person's report about prior onset, behavior, exposure, or recovery;
- **calculated:** moment, work, impulse, symmetry, or another quantity derived through a declared method;
- **modeled:** internal force, muscle contribution, or other estimate that depends on a stated model;
- **hypothesized:** an unobserved handoff, initiating relation, or later consequence proposed for testing; and
- **unknown:** a link or state that the available record does not resolve.

Chronology is necessary but not sufficient. A strategy occurring before a later finding does not establish that it caused the finding.

## Compensation begins with what it preserves

A cane makes the chapter's positive center visible. In twenty-seven adults with late-stage unilateral hip osteoarthritis, Tazawa and colleagues compared walking with and without a contralateral T-cane. During cane use, the calculated affected-side peak external hip-adduction moment fell from an average 0.76 to 0.57 N·m/kg, the calculated affected-side moment impulse fell by about fifteen percent, and reported walking pain decreased. The device added a ground contact and an opposing moment through the arm and trunk. That contact was part of the successful walking system rather than evidence that task success was false.[^tazawa2022]

The observed and calculated result is bounded. Hip adduction moment is not a direct cartilage stress field. The study was an acute, nonrandomized comparison in a selected clinical group and did not establish long-term tissue protection. Mean impulses on the nonaffected side were numerically higher with the cane but not statistically different. The useful conclusion is therefore mechanical and immediate: an added support can preserve walking while changing the route and reducing selected calculated affected-side measures.

The same example prevents a simplistic benefit-versus-cost verdict. In fifteen healthy young men instructed to place ten or twenty percent of body weight through a cane, Kawase and colleagues found that the higher prescribed cane-support condition changed calculated upper-limb joint work nonuniformly and increased positive work at the shoulder and elbow.[^kawase2024] That experiment does not show injury, disability experience, or the long-term consequence of prescribed cane use. It does show why a route change must name both what it relieves and which regions or external supports now participate.

An assistive exosuit supplies an affirmative environmental branch. Seven participants walking with a 6.8-kg rucksack used an autonomous multi-joint soft exosuit whose selected control parameters were tuned for each person. With powered individualized assistance, measured metabolic cost was lower than in both no-device and unpowered-device conditions, and the selected assistance profiles differed among participants.[^lee2018] The experiment did not isolate the benefit of individual tuning from the benefit of powered assistance itself. The study is small and task-specific, but it provides a direct counterexample to the equation of altered or externally assisted routing with inefficiency. The environment can become part of a successful load path. Chapter 15 uses metabolic cost only as a task-level outcome here; Chapter 16 owns any proposed energetic or regulatory scale transition.

The reader-facing opening should therefore record four things before evaluating any compensation:

1. the valued function—walking, balance, work, comfort, safety, communication, participation, object control, or another chosen outcome;
2. the present constraint or demand, if known;
3. the new support, route, timing, or behavior; and
4. the immediate result and unresolved quantities.

If the protected function cannot be named, *compensation* is only a description of difference.

In disability contexts, *compensation* is an analytical term, not a required identity or deficit label. Reader-facing language should follow the person's own goals and preferred description whenever those are known.

## Task success can coexist with a changed internal distribution

Fatigue provides a clear within-person example. Vial and colleagues analyzed the first three steps of maximal acceleration before and after a high-speed multidirectional fatiguing protocol in thirteen semiprofessional male soccer players. Acceleration was maintained, but observed ground-contact time and posture and calculated impulses, joint moments, power, and positive work changed. The hip-to-knee work redistribution was step- and limb-specific, while no change was detected in the study's ankle-joint measures.[^vial2023]

The task outcome alone would miss the redistribution. The altered joint pattern alone would miss the preserved performance. Neither observation establishes a chronic burden. The experiment did not follow a multiweek history, demonstrate tissue injury, or determine whether the post-fatigue strategy persisted after recovery. Chapter 15 should use it to show the record needed for one episode:

```text
same declared performance outcome
    + changed state after fatigue protocol
    + changed joint-specific work and timing
    + unknown recovery course
    + unknown tissue consequence
```

The same structure applies outside sport. A shorter excursion may preserve comfort. A hand on a table may preserve balance. A different grip may preserve object control. A prosthesis or orthosis may make a task possible. A slower pace may preserve participation. These strategies can be deliberate, learned, reflexive, environmentally imposed, or simply one of several capable solutions. The chapter must not infer an original failed route when no such route was measured, or impose the label *compensation* when it adds no explanatory value for the person or task.

## A changed visible route may leave the proposed internal exposure unchanged

The strongest null comes from induced restriction. McCain and colleagues recorded eight adults without reported gait impairment walking while braces restricted the ankle, knee, or both, unilaterally and bilaterally. Participant-scaled musculoskeletal models estimated muscle forces and joint reaction forces from measured kinematics and ground reaction forces with EMG-informed timing constraints. Unilateral knee restriction increased ipsilateral ground-reaction-force peak and loading rate, yet modeled lower-limb joint reaction forces remained relatively unchanged because modeled muscle forces fell during loading response.[^mccain2023]

This is not proof that restriction is harmless. It is proof that one conspicuous external change and one increased external measure do not fix the internal result. A compensation narrative that observes asymmetry, assumes more joint force, and then predicts damage has skipped the very allocation problem the book is trying to expose.

A clinical-group null makes the same caution concrete. In forty people with knee osteoarthritis and nineteen matched controls, Bakker and colleagues found no group-level interlimb differences in calculated frontal-plane knee and hip moments during comfortable treadmill walking when limbs were classified by each participant's most painful knee.[^bakker2021] Subgroups differed in the direction of knee-moment asymmetry, and hip moments did not differ between limbs. Pain, diagnosis, and apparent sidedness therefore did not supply one compensatory loading pattern.

These nulls should remain inside the affirmative argument. They refine it: compensation is a hypothesis about a changed task solution. The relevant exposure must still be measured or modeled, and it may increase, decrease, move, or remain materially similar.

## Repetition can change the next starting state

Split-belt treadmill experiments make a retained state observable without invoking damage. Across several experiments, Vasudevan and Bastian exposed healthy adults to belts moving at different speeds. Participants adapted step-length relations during exposure, and a reversed asymmetry appeared when the belts returned to equal speed. Aftereffects were largest near the slower training speed, and 23–38% remained after washout at the other tested speed, demonstrating context-sensitive retention rather than one global new gait.[^vasudevan2010]

Selgrade and colleagues calculated mechanical work during a ten-minute, three-to-one split-belt task. Leg work rose abruptly on initial exposure and then declined as participants adapted; the distribution between pendular and step-to-step work also changed.[^selgrade2017] The endpoint, mechanical work, and learned coordination therefore followed different time courses.

In eleven people more than six months after a single unilateral stroke and eleven matched controls, Reisman and colleagues found partial transfer of split-belt aftereffects to overground walking in both groups. Double-support transfer was larger in the poststroke group; the between-group step-length result was a trend. Among poststroke participants classified as asymmetric at baseline, step-length and double-support asymmetry showed a strong trend toward improvement (*P* = .06). The study remained an acute adaptation experiment rather than proof of durable treatment benefit.[^reisman2009]

Together these experiments support a limited but important statement: what happened during prior repetitions can affect the organization expressed at the next exposure, and the effect can depend on context. They do not establish a permanent set point, a hidden structural change, or a necessary progression. For Chapter 15, *new baseline* should mean only the repeatedly encountered starting organization for the next defined task and time window.

The route-history record should include:

| Field | Minimum content |
|---|---|
| Protected function | selected task and person-valued outcome |
| Immediate state | pain, fatigue, anatomy, assistance, instruction, environment, skill, or unknown |
| Route change | contacts, timing, motion, activation, work, pressure, or modeled force |
| Repetition | count, duration, frequency, task variation, and interruptions |
| Recovery | time course, rest, sleep, support change, training, or unmeasured state |
| Retention or transfer | same task, new task, new speed, new environment, or no persistence |
| Later exposure | named receiver and measured or modeled quantity |
| Outcome | useful, neutral, training, costly, insufficient, replaced, or unresolved |

There is no universal compensation score. A strategy can reduce metabolic cost while increasing selected joint work, preserve performance while reducing variability, or improve safety while slowing speed. The judgment depends on the declared outcome and time horizon.

## Cumulative demand does not require a discrete event—and does not guarantee a consequence

The archive's “degeneration without injury” intuition should be retained as a branching history rather than a concealed diagnosis:

```text
low-grade or ordinary change
    -> task remains successful through an alternate strategy
    -> strategy recurs under a defined exposure
    -> selected route, dose, or recovery state changes
    -> capacity remains adequate / improves / becomes uncertain / is exceeded
    -> no consequence / useful adaptation / transient symptom / material response / failure
```

No trauma is required for a cumulative exposure to exist. Repeated steps, reaches, lifts, holds, supports, and postures all have histories. But the absence of trauma does not prove that a later condition was mechanically accumulated. To make that bridge, the account must name the receiver, local quantity, dose, material or biological response, chronology, and alternatives.

The cumulative branch should also preserve state changes that reduce demand: skill learning, greater capacity, a better tool, external support, rest, task redesign, assistance, and spontaneous resolution. Repetition may teach a more economical solution, as in the split-belt work experiment. It may stabilize a useful disability-specific strategy. It may make a device easier to use. Or it may have no meaningful effect.

The term *reserve* remains task-specific. It can be represented by the remaining ability to vary a strategy, repeat the task, tolerate a perturbation, or recover within a declared time. It is not one hidden energy tank, and continued task completion is not proof that reserve is depleted.

## Delayed, distal, and contralateral accounts carry a higher burden

Spatial distance can make a hypothesis interesting, but it does not make the hypothesis self-validating. A credible contralateral account must reconstruct the path:

```text
initial state or constraint
    -> measured change in support or behavior
    -> changed contacts and regional resultants
    -> changed internal distribution at the proposed receiver
    -> repeated exposure and recovery history
    -> tissue-specific response
    -> later finding
```

The route may pass through the ground, a cane, the opposite limb, the pelvis, the trunk, or an external support. Every intermediate handoff must be named. “The kinetic chain” is not a substitute for those links.

Longitudinal evidence shows why this burden matters. Aljehani and colleagues collected biomechanical measures from forty people six to twenty-four months after unilateral primary total knee arthroplasty, then tested those measures against contralateral radiographic progression at follow-up a mean 8.8 years after surgery. Contralateral radiographic progression was common, but the selected biomechanical variables did not differ between progressors and nonprogressors and did not predict progression in the regression analyses.[^aljehani2022] This study does not show that cumulative mechanics never matter. It shows that temporal order, a plausible story, and a later contralateral finding are insufficient when the measured predictors do not support the proposed link.

The chapter should require alternatives at every distant account:

- common upstream anatomy, age, biology, disease, medication, activity, occupation, or environment affecting both regions;
- pain, fatigue, fear, instruction, or social demand initiating the visible strategy;
- the later problem causing the observed compensation rather than resulting from it;
- task selection, walking speed, assistance, or treatment changing both exposure and outcome;
- recall error about onset or persistence;
- a different unmeasured mechanical quantity than the proposed one; and
- incidental co-occurrence.

A remote hypothesis should be narrowed when the route does not change as predicted, the secondary exposure does not covary with the strategy, the chronology reverses, recovery does not alter the finding, or a common cause explains the observations with fewer unsupported links.

## Origin, adaptation, maintenance, consequence, and context are different roles

One observed strategy can occupy different positions in different histories:

| Role | Question | Evidence needed |
|---|---|---|
| Origin | What first changed the mechanical problem? | pre-change record, perturbation, injury, environment, anatomy, or other initiating evidence |
| Adaptation | What solution preserved the task? | matched task comparison and protected outcome |
| Maintenance | Why did the solution persist? | repeated exposure, environment, learning, pain, preference, support, or capacity history |
| Consequence | What later exposure or outcome did the strategy produce? | temporal and spatial path with a named receiver and response |
| Incidental context | What was present without doing causal work? | null change, disfavoring manipulation, or better competing explanation |

The roles must not be fused. Pain can originate a gait change, be modified by it, maintain it, follow it, or coexist with it. A cane can be an adaptation and an environmental support without being the origin of the condition. An asymmetry can be a stable preference or anatomical accommodation rather than a consequence waiting to occur.

## Primary worked comparison

Use one hypothetical person performing the book's recurring object-control task, then adding a hand support during a period of fatigue or discomfort. The scene is constructed to teach the evidence record; it is not a patient case.

### Declared task and value

- move or hold the object safely;
- preserve balance and chosen comfort;
- permit a shorter excursion, wider stance, support surface, opposite hand, or slower pace; and
- avoid treating visual symmetry as the endpoint.

### Three time courses

1. **Persistent successful assistance:** support remains chosen, the task stays capable, recovery is adequate, and no adverse secondary exposure is demonstrated.
2. **Resolution or replacement:** fatigue or discomfort resolves, the support is no longer needed, or a different strategy becomes preferable.
3. **Changed later exposure:** support recurs, a named regional work or force measure changes, and the next task begins from a measurably different state; any later biological or clinical outcome remains unestablished until separately bridged.

### Required evidence states

- observed contacts, task outcome, and timing;
- measured external force or motion where available;
- calculated moment, impulse, work, or exposure descriptor;
- modeled internal sharing labeled as modeled;
- recalled onset labeled as recalled; and
- unknown local tissue and long-term consequences retained as unknown.

The figure should also show a reverse branch in which pain or a later condition precedes and produces the visible strategy.

## Measurement and disfavoring program

| Question | Minimum observations | What would count against the proposed interpretation |
|---|---|---|
| What did the compensation preserve? | task, valued outcome, present demand, alternatives, and constraint if one is claimed | no selected function can be identified, or a constraint essential to the account is absent |
| Did the route change? | before/after contacts, motion, force, work, activation, pressure, or modeled sharing | proposed distribution is unchanged or another representation fits better |
| Did repetition change the next dose? | repeated measures, time, recovery, task variation, and selected exposure | change resolves, fails to transfer, or remains too small for the proposed receiver |
| Did a useful strategy become costly? | persistent strategy plus a defined cost and comparator | performance, recovery, options, and exposure remain adequate or improve |
| Is a later finding mechanically linked? | chronology, intermediate handoffs, receiver exposure, tissue response, alternatives | wrong temporal order, absent covariation, failed manipulation, or common cause |
| Is the strategy the origin? | pre-onset evidence and plausible initiating event | pain, disease, environment, treatment, or later impairment preceded the strategy |

Static posture, one gait cycle, imaging, and symptom location cannot reconstruct this history. Surface EMG cannot by itself establish force, metabolic cost, or tissue exposure. A joint moment is not a contact-stress map. A model estimate must retain its assumptions. A person completing the task does not establish that the strategy is costless, and persistence does not establish that it is harmful.

## Claim and model actions for later registry synchronization

No stable identifiers should be allocated until the packet is independently reviewed. The later control pass should create or amend records for:

- compensation as a change that protects a declared function under a present constraint or demand;
- silent task success as distinct from hidden pathology;
- repeated compensation as a possible change in the next exposure or starting state;
- cumulative demand without a discrete event as a branching, nonprogressive-by-default history;
- delayed, distal, and contralateral reconstruction with an explicit link burden;
- origin, adaptation, maintenance, consequence, and incidental-context roles;
- a branching compensation timeline that reuses `BGB-M-0003`;
- a route-before/route-after comparison that inherits `BGB-M-0004`; and
- a task-specific reserve representation with no universal score.

## Boundaries and handoffs

- **Chapter 10 owns** demand, dose, capacity, recovery, environment, and exposure history.
- **Chapter 11 owns** availability, participation, handoff, interruption, substitution, and route states within a task.
- **Chapters 12–13 own** concentration and maintained deformation.
- **Chapter 14 owns** relation loss, correction, bracing, timing, and the first protective response.
- **Chapter 15 owns** protected function, persistence, retained starting state, plural outcomes, and temporal/spatial reconstruction.
- **Chapter 16 owns** local-to-regional and regional-to-global transitions in sensory, pressure, effort, and regulatory questions.
- **Chapter 18 owns** the assembled routing-history method.
- **Chapter 19 owns** tissue and biological response.
- **Chapter 21 owns** causal role, pain, behavior, social context, and whole-person alternatives.
- **Chapter 26 owns** the later mixed chronic worked narrative.
- **Book II owns** preferred configuration; **Book V owns** assessment and practice.

The chapter's strongest defensible conclusion is affirmative: living systems preserve valued function by changing how a task is solved, and those solutions can have measurable histories. Its limiting conclusion is equally important: a successful compensation is not a hidden lesion, and a later or distant finding does not reveal its own mechanical origin.
