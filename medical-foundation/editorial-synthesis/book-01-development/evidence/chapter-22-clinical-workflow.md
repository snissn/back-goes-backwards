**Companion controls:** [Chapter 22 brief](../chapter-briefs/BGB-U-0022-clinical-workflow.md), [Book I contract](../book-contract.md), [causal-chain and evidence-state grammar](../planning/causal-chain-and-evidence-state-grammar.md), [Chapter 18 evidence packet](chapter-18-longitudinal-load-routing.md), [Chapter 21 evidence packet](chapter-21-causal-scope-whole-person.md), and BGB-C-0014

## Editorial decision

Chapter 22 can show where a mechanical-routing inquiry might enter an ordinary care process, but it cannot present a book-authored clinical workflow as established practice. The additive sequence is a conceptual and editorial model. It exists to keep the proposed lens from replacing diagnosis, professional judgment, current care, or the person's priorities, and to make the worked narratives in Part V comparable.

The chapter should therefore make two claims of different strength:

1. Existing authoritative frameworks already treat diagnosis and care as iterative, collaborative, person-centered, contextual, and distributed across professions. Physical therapy, rehabilitation, ergonomics, imaging, medication, procedures, and surgery already reason about movement, function, task, environment, anatomy, exposure, and change. The book cannot claim this territory was empty.
2. The project proposes that an explicit longitudinal representation of load history and routing may add value for selected questions. That incremental value is unproven. It must be compared with existing practice and may consist in clearer chronology, a shared cross-scale vocabulary, a better research object, or no important addition.

This distinction preserves the author's ambition. “Additive” describes the current publication posture, not a permanent promise that the framework can only ever be an adjunct. If later work establishes reliable observations, discriminating hypotheses, clinical utility, and favorable outcomes, the model could justify a larger change in education or care. Book I first needs a representation that can be evaluated without smuggling in a diagnosis or protocol.

The proposed entry rule is:

> Begin from the person's declared problem, established safety and diagnostic processes, current care, function, goals, and professional scope. Add a mechanical history only for a named question. Preserve provenance, alternatives, and missing cells. Stop when the account cannot discriminate, changes nothing, or another concern carries priority.

This is a reasoning boundary, not advice to a patient and not a substitute for applicable professional standards.

## Existing clinical reasoning comes first

The National Academies describes diagnosis as an iterative, collaborative activity that unfolds over time within a health-care work system. Its model includes information gathering, integration and interpretation, a working explanation, communication, and outcomes, with patients and families participating in the diagnostic team.[^nasem2015] This supports the chapter's refusal to place a new mechanical pattern before the health problem or outside existing care. It does not supply a detailed encounter sequence for every profession and does not validate a routing inquiry.

NICE defines shared decision making as collaboration between a person and a health professional around care decisions, including discussion of options and their risks, benefits, and consequences.[^nice2021] WHO defines rehabilitation around optimizing functioning and reducing disability for people with health conditions in interaction with their environment, and describes it as person-centered and directed to goals and preferences.[^whorehab2024] These sources support early attention to what matters to the person, current function, environment, and care context. They also prevent a mechanical explanation from becoming the sole definition of success.

The reader-facing entry should be phrased as a set of prerequisites, not a book-authored safety protocol:

| Existing clinical object | Why it precedes the added lens | What Chapter 22 may say | What it may not supply |
|---|---|---|---|
| presenting concern and functional effect | defines what needs explanation or support | identify the declared question and relevant outcome | diagnose the concern |
| timely and safety-sensitive context | another problem may require priority | established professional processes govern | red-flag list, triage rule, contraindication, or referral threshold |
| known or considered diagnosis and tissue state | constrains plausible histories and care | preserve rather than replace the diagnostic account | confirm, reject, or relabel a diagnosis |
| medication, surgery, procedure, device, and rehabilitation history | can change biology, anatomy, symptoms, task, and routing | enter care as dated nodes and interventions | recommend changing or sequencing care |
| function, activity, participation, goals, and preferences | determine relevance and tradeoffs | make the outcome person- and task-specific | infer etiology from goals |
| professional role and setting | determines authority, measurement, and responsibility | disclose scope and source of each observation | create a universal scope map |

The order is conceptual. In a real history, task, pain, diagnosis, medication, and goals can be discussed together. The non-negotiable point is logical priority: a routing hypothesis cannot override established safety obligations or acquire diagnostic authority by appearing first on the page.

## Selective task and exposure history has established precedents

The proposed history does not begin from nothing. Occupational ergonomics already connects task description with force, repetition, posture, intensity, frequency, duration, work organization, and psychosocial context. NIOSH guidance also emphasizes job and task analysis, worker reports, work records, changes over time, and multiple concurrent factors.[^niosh2024] Those practices support a bounded task record:

- what task or environment is under review;
- what external demand, contact, repetition, duration, variability, and recovery are known;
- when the task or concern changed;
- what equipment, assistance, schedule, or work organization altered the demand;
- what was reported, observed, or directly measured; and
- which internal quantities remain modeled or unknown.

The transfer limit matters. NIOSH material is designed for occupational hazard identification and program development. It does not validate a retrospective whole-body route in one person, an optimal posture, a tissue threshold for every worker, or a clinical causal conclusion. It shows that exposure is task- and dose-structured and that physical, organizational, and psychosocial conditions can coexist.

The same selectivity applies outside work. A developmental, sport, caregiving, domestic, travel, recovery, or device history should enter only when it changes a named node. Chapter 22 should not ask every reader to reconstruct every activity. More fields can increase burden and retrospective storytelling without improving discrimination.

## The proposed contribution is a longitudinal routing object

Chapter 18 defines the proposed object as a dated chain:

| Layer | Candidate entry |
|---|---|
| task and environment | demand, contact, support, surface, tool, schedule, assistance |
| configuration and available paths | observed relation at a named scale and phase |
| external demand and internal distribution | measured input plus modeled or inferred distribution |
| local receiver exposure | stress, strain, pressure, excursion, contact, activation, or another named quantity |
| receiver response | material, cellular, neural, vascular, functional, or other system-specific change |
| time and recovery | repetition, variability, rest, repair, retained state, and capacity |
| finding, symptom, and function | separate endpoints with potentially different correspondence |
| whole-person context | biological state, expectations, behavior, work, resources, environment, care, meaning, goals |

The object could add value if it preserves chronology and cross-scale distinctions that are otherwise lost. It could also duplicate a good rehabilitation history, an ergonomic analysis, a surgical or imaging account, or ordinary clinical documentation. Its proposed novelty is not “asking about movement.” The exact comparison is:

> Does an explicit, evidence-state-labeled routing record improve clarity, reliability, discrimination, communication, research design, prediction, decision relevance, or outcome relative to the existing account?

Book I can state that as a research question. It cannot answer it through the elegance of the diagram.

## Observation, provenance, and interpretation must remain separate

The workflow needs a light provenance ledger because the proposed chain mixes sources that are easy to conflate. The FDA–NIH BEST terminology distinguishes biomarkers from clinical outcome assessments and distinguishes patient-, clinician-, observer-, and performance-based assessments.[^best2021] The exact regulatory taxonomy is not transferred wholesale, but its underlying discipline is useful: an image, pressure measurement, report of pain, observed movement, and performance test are not interchangeable evidence for one hidden construct.

The project's evidence-state grammar can be translated into the following internal record:

| State | Entry records | Permitted job | Common overpromotion |
|---|---|---|---|
| observed | who saw or reported what, when, during which task | establish an observation or experience | inferred internal stress or history |
| measured | instrument, protocol, units, site, time, uncertainty | establish the measured quantity under conditions | different tissue, scale, state, or outcome |
| calculated | inputs, equation, assumptions, uncertainty | derive a quantity from declared inputs | direct observation |
| modeled | system boundary, parameters, validation domain | compare candidate distributions or mechanisms | unique true route |
| inferred | reasoning from observations and alternatives | propose an arrow | measured fact |
| hypothesized | testable relation and disfavoring result | direct future observation or experiment | causal conclusion |
| embodied report | first-person sensation, connection, or change | preserve experience and generate questions | tissue measurement, diagnosis, or universal mapping |
| reconstructed | dated synthesis of several base states | organize a possible history | continuous recording of the past |

“Reconstructed” should remain a qualifier rather than a new evidence rank. A reconstruction can contain direct measurements and reports, but the route connecting them remains a synthesis unless it was observed continuously at the relevant scales.

If any observation becomes a score, classification, or test, reliability and agreement require their own study. The GRRAS statement was developed because reporting and knowledge of error in clinical ratings and classifications were often inadequate.[^kottner2011] COSMIN work similarly separates content validity, reliability, measurement error, construct validity, and responsiveness when selecting instruments.[^mokkink2016] These sources do not validate this ledger. They support the decision not to release it as a clinical form by implication.

## Reports and embodied observations have bounded but real jobs

A person's report of a task, symptom, perceived support, fatigue, or internal connection is not an inferior substitute for every instrument. Some endpoints—pain, meaning, confidence, ease, and lived functional burden—are available only through the person. The FDA glossary explicitly distinguishes patient-reported outcomes from clinician observations, biomarkers, and performance measures.[^best2021]

The workflow should preserve:

- the exact experience or observation;
- the task, phase, time, and comparison;
- whether the observation was spontaneous, prompted, repeated, or altered by expectation;
- concurrent care or environmental changes;
- its relevance to the person's goal; and
- the next proposition it motivates.

It should not translate “I feel a line of support” into a measured anatomical force line, “this position feels easier” into universal efficiency, or “symptoms changed” into a recovered cause. First-person evidence is allowed to remain exactly what it is. That is more respectful and more analytically useful than either dismissing it or literalizing it.

## Modulation informs the present without proving the past

Current change can be valuable. If a task, support, rest period, position, device, medication, procedure, explanation, attention shift, or therapeutic interaction changes a symptom or function, that observation can identify a relation worth retaining. It can also reflect natural fluctuation, regression toward an average, learning, expectation, measurement error, co-intervention, or several simultaneous mechanisms.

The Chapter 21 source set supplies three useful boundaries:

- Shih and colleagues manipulated expectation cues in healthy participants and found changes in pain ratings and neural responses under the experiment.[^shih2019] Expectation can enter the symptom relation without implying that pain is unreal or that physical exposure is absent.
- Hinman and colleagues found no added pain or function benefit of unloading shoes over conventional walking shoes in their knee-OA trial.[^hinman2016] A mechanically labeled intervention does not guarantee the intended clinical difference.
- Paterson and colleagues found no significant mediation by the proposed biomechanical variables in the full trial cohort; subgroup signals were exploratory.[^paterson2018] Intervention assignment, measured mechanical change, clinical response, and mediation are separate propositions.

The workflow should record present modulation as:

| Field | Question |
|---|---|
| assigned or naturally occurring change | what changed, and who selected it? |
| comparison | compared with what state, task, or interval? |
| immediate measured output | what symptom, performance, or mechanical quantity changed? |
| persistence | how long did the change remain? |
| alternatives | expectation, learning, natural fluctuation, concurrent care, fatigue, measurement error |
| permitted conclusion | what present relation is supported? |
| historical boundary | which origin claims remain unresolved? |

This is not a home test. It is a structure for interpreting already available evidence in a worked narrative.

## Imaging and other findings occupy specific nodes

Imaging is neither a mechanical verdict nor meaningless noise. The ACR Appropriateness Criteria are condition- and scenario-specific and explicitly frame imaging choice around the clinical condition.[^acr2021] Brinjikji and colleagues' synthesis of asymptomatic spinal imaging shows that several degenerative findings become common with age even in people without symptoms.[^brinjikji2015] Together these support a bounded rule:

> Place a finding at the node it measures, then establish separately what it contributes to diagnosis, anatomy, tissue state, exposure, symptom, function, prognosis, or decision.

The workflow should allow at least six possibilities:

1. the finding is central to an established diagnosis or procedural question;
2. it constrains anatomy or a mechanical model without explaining the symptom;
3. it corresponds with a selected symptom or function under condition-specific evidence;
4. it is downstream of another biological or mechanical history;
5. it is common or incidental for the declared endpoint; or
6. its relevance is unresolved.

The reverse error is equally important. No routine imaging finding does not establish that no mechanical exposure occurred. Many exposures, responses, and dynamic relations are not represented by a selected static image or its reporting protocol. Chapter 22 can state this logically but must avoid implying that an unmeasured preferred route is therefore present.

Laboratory, examination, and functional findings receive the same treatment. Each has a construct, protocol, uncertainty, and condition-specific interpretive role. A finding can be valid and still fail to select among competing histories.

## The proposed lens overlaps substantially with existing professions

World Physiotherapy describes physiotherapists as working to develop, maintain, and restore movement and function across life and across promotion, prevention, treatment, and rehabilitation, with attention to physical, psychological, emotional, and social well-being.[^worldphysio2026] APTA materials place movement within a specific context and describe examination of function, systems, movement, communication, cognition, affect, and learning, with collaboration or referral when appropriate.[^apta2026a][^apta2026b] WHO rehabilitation likewise includes health conditions, function, environment, goals, multidisciplinary work, and a wide range of interventions.[^whorehab2024]

AOTA's domain-and-process materials place daily activity, participation, work, environmental and personal context, performance, and adaptation within occupational-therapy practice. The International Ergonomics Association defines ergonomics around human-system interaction and design, including tasks, jobs, tools, environments, and organization.[^aota2026][^iea2026]

The chapter should say plainly that physical therapists, physiotherapists, rehabilitation clinicians, biomechanists, ergonomists, occupational therapists, surgeons, sports-medicine professionals, pain clinicians, and others already perform parts of the proposed work. That overlap is an asset because it supplies comparators and potential collaborators.

### Care-interface matrix

| Existing domain | Established contribution relevant here | What the routing object might add | What it cannot claim |
|---|---|---|---|
| diagnosis and medical evaluation | defines health problems, conditions, urgency, differential possibilities, tests, and care | a bounded exposure or history question | diagnostic replacement or superior priority |
| imaging and laboratory medicine | anatomy, tissue state, biomarkers, and condition-specific findings | explicit placement of a finding in a longitudinal graph | reinterpretation outside expertise or criteria |
| medication | changes biological state, symptom, capacity, and sometimes movement | dated intervention node and changed boundary conditions | medication recommendation or causal simplification |
| surgery and procedures | change anatomy, stability, pressure, contact, and available paths | before/after routing research question | procedure indication or outcome prediction |
| physiotherapy and rehabilitation | movement, function, capacity, task, education, adaptation, graded activity, environment, goals | explicit cross-scale history or shared evidence-state language, if useful | novelty by ignoring existing practice |
| occupational therapy and ergonomics | activity, participation, work, tool, environment, task demand, and adaptation | longitudinal integration with receiver-specific mechanics | ownership of work design or exposure science |
| psychology and pain care | expectation, learning, coping, meaning, behavior, symptom, and participation | placement of these relations inside the same causal graph | reduction to confounding or a mechanical modifier |
| person, family, and community | lived experience, goals, constraints, resources, and choices | provenance for observations and outcomes that matter | extraction of a route without consent or relevance |

The WHO interprofessional framework supports collaborative practice as a health-system strategy, not the primacy of any one vocabulary.[^whoipe2010] The proposed mechanical representation should be judged partly by whether it improves communication across these domains without erasing their constructs.

## “This is just physical therapy” is an empirical question

The objection contains at least four possible conclusions:

1. Existing physical therapy or rehabilitation already performs the full useful job; the book's representation adds no value.
2. Existing practice performs the substantive work, while the book contributes a clearer cross-disciplinary vocabulary or research record.
3. The routing object reveals a chronology, scale transition, or receiver-specific relation not represented reliably in current practice.
4. The project proposes a distinctive hypothesis but cannot yet observe or validate it.

Chapter 22 must not choose among these through rhetoric. It can specify comparisons:

- Can different reviewers reconstruct the same route from the same record?
- Does the record distinguish candidate histories better than ordinary documentation?
- Does it add a measurable exposure or receiver relation rather than merely more words?
- Does it improve communication across professions or only introduce unfamiliar terms?
- Does it change a prespecified research or care question?
- What time and burden does it add?
- Does the result generalize across settings, tasks, and populations?
- Does any downstream action improve outcomes relative to existing care?

The book may still be valuable if its first contribution is theoretical synthesis. It should name that contribution accurately rather than borrowing clinical utility it has not established.

## Clinical deployment requires a development program

If the conceptual workflow ever becomes a professional instrument, several stages become necessary. Skivington and colleagues' updated MRC framework for complex interventions emphasizes context, program theory, stakeholder perspectives, key uncertainties, refinement, comparative resources and outcomes, feasibility, evaluation, and a legitimate decision to stop.[^skivington2021] GRRAS, COSMIN, and STARD identify separate reporting and validation requirements for ratings, measures, and diagnostic-accuracy studies.[^kottner2011][^mokkink2016][^stard2015]

Those sources support a development boundary, not a mandated design:

| Development question | Example evidence obligation |
|---|---|
| construct | what exactly is “routing history,” and which decisions or research questions could it affect? |
| content | do affected people and relevant disciplines agree that the record includes what matters and omits burdensome irrelevancies? |
| feasibility | can the record be completed accurately in the intended setting and timeframe? |
| reliability and agreement | do repeated observations and independent reviewers produce comparable entries where they should? |
| validity | do fields correspond to the constructs, measurements, and external evidence they claim to represent? |
| discrimination | can candidate histories make different predictions that observations resolve? |
| incremental value | does the object outperform or complement an explicit comparator? |
| utility and outcomes | does using the object improve a meaningful process or outcome without unacceptable burden or harm? |
| equity and access | who is excluded, misread, burdened, or disadvantaged by the language, tools, time, or required measurements? |
| governance | who may use it, for what purpose, with what accountability, revision, privacy, and safety process? |

Book I is at the construct and narrative-record stage. It must not borrow the authority of later stages.

## Incremental value and stopping rules

More information is not automatically better. A routing narrative can add cognitive load, documentation burden, false coherence, and opportunities for overinterpretation. Vickers and Elkin's decision-curve work makes a general distinction between predictive performance and decision consequence.[^vickers2006] Chapter 22 does not need to teach that method. It needs the principle that a more detailed account earns practical relevance only if it changes a meaningful comparison under acceptable tradeoffs.

### Continue the mechanical inquiry when

- the declared question is not already answered by the current account;
- a candidate mechanical relation is specific enough to measure or discriminate;
- chronology or task information could distinguish plausible histories;
- the receiver and outcome are named;
- the proposed record can be completed without displacing higher-priority care;
- the result could improve research, communication, prediction, or a later decision; and
- uncertainty is visible rather than converted into a global story.

### Narrow or stop when

- established evaluation and care already address the relevant question;
- another medical or contextual concern has priority;
- the history is too nonunique for the available observations;
- the proposed internal route cannot be measured or distinguished;
- an image, response, or posture is doing all the causal work;
- the proposed account merely renames existing practice;
- adding fields does not improve a prespecified result;
- burden, privacy, access, or interpretive risk exceeds the likely value; or
- the question belongs to research rather than the present encounter.

These are not instructions to a patient or referral criteria. They are validity conditions for whether the book's proposed representation should continue to occupy the narrative.

## Minimum worked-narrative record for Part V

The record should be complete enough to expose missing links and compact enough not to simulate a clinical intake form.

| Record row | Required content | Evidence-state requirement | Failure to prevent |
|---|---|---|---|
| declared question | origin, current exposure, response, symptom, function, recovery, prediction, or decision | explicit | one story answering several different questions |
| person and context | relevant population, setting, goals, care, and scope | observed/reported with provenance | generic body detached from a person |
| safety and care boundary | applicable established context and what the narrative does not decide | sourced boundary | implicit medical guidance |
| task and environment | phase, surface, tool, support, schedule, demand, and comparator | observed, measured, or reported | posture without a task |
| configuration and available paths | scale, frame, phase, and candidate alternatives | observed/measured versus modeled | visible form treated as internal force |
| external demand and distribution | applied forces/contacts plus inferred or modeled sharing | each quantity labeled | load as an undefined substance |
| local receiver exposure | tissue or system, quantity, dose, and uncertainty | measured/calculated/modeled/inferred | whole-body claim without receiver |
| response through time | immediate, repeated, retained, restored, or remodeled state | dated evidence | chronology invented from coexistence |
| finding, symptom, and function | separate outcomes | source and construct stated | finding equals symptom |
| whole-person entries | only factors attached to named nodes or arrows | evidence state and timeframe | biopsychosocial boilerplate |
| alternatives | forward, reverse, reciprocal, common-cause, context-mediated, and incidental where relevant | explicit competing graph | preferred story by omission |
| null or low-priority branch | ordinary variation, adequate restoration, no added value, or other-primary account | affirmative | every narrative “finding” mechanics |
| discriminator | observation or experiment that separates candidate structures | prospective where possible | unfalsifiable explanation |
| missing cells | consequential unknowns | visibly blank | rhetoric filling the gap |
| bounded interpretation | strongest permitted conclusion and weakest consequential arrow | explicit | coherence becoming diagnosis |
| next destination | research question, ordinary care context, or stop | no recommendation unless sourced and authorized | hidden protocol |

The record is an editorial and research scaffold. It should appear in Part V as a transparent method, not as a form readers are asked to apply to themselves.

## Neutral composite architecture

The chapter can use a constructed, source-grounded illustration:

> A person has a task-limited concern, a documented medical context, ongoing care, and a goal involving one recurring activity. The symptom and performance vary, but the available observations do not establish why.

Run the same illustration to three outputs:

### Output A — a bounded present relation

A task feature and a measured or observed change correspond repeatedly. The current relation is worth recording or measuring. Origin, tissue state, and treatment remain open.

### Output B — a useful but incomplete history

Chronology and task change support a candidate exposure sequence, but the receiver quantity or biological bridge is missing. The result is a researchable hypothesis, not a clinical conclusion.

### Output C — no added value

The mechanical reconstruction is nonunique, duplicates the existing account, or changes no meaningful question. The workflow stops while ordinary care and the person's goals remain intact.

The composite must not contain invented diagnostic detail, a dramatic rescue by posture, or a disguised recommendation. No actual patient is represented.

## Support, narrowing, and disfavor tests

### Results supporting further development

- independent reviewers can use the same record with acceptable agreement;
- the routing object captures chronology or cross-scale relations absent from the comparator;
- candidate histories make different predictions and the record discriminates them;
- measurements correspond to the constructs and receivers they claim to represent;
- people and relevant professions find the representation understandable and useful;
- added information improves communication, research design, prediction, or decision consequence;
- benefit persists across relevant contexts without unacceptable burden; and
- the system supports a null outcome rather than forcing a positive mechanical account.

### Results narrowing the contribution

- the representation is useful only for one task, condition, profession, scale, or research setting;
- it improves documentation but not prediction or outcomes;
- it preserves useful first-person or longitudinal information but cannot identify internal distribution;
- its vocabulary helps cross-disciplinary communication while substantive work remains established practice;
- it generates research questions but is too burdensome for routine care; or
- hard/soft language is useful for explanation but adds no independent measurement construct.

### Results disfavoring current use

- reviewers cannot agree on consequential entries;
- the record encourages unsupported causal reconstruction;
- proposed fields cannot be measured or validated;
- the same conclusions follow from a simpler existing framework;
- the tool changes attention or care without improving meaningful outcomes;
- burden, inequity, privacy risk, or confusion increases;
- scope varies too much for responsible standardization; or
- it functions as a diagnostic or treatment system before validation.

These results refine the project. They do not determine whether all future preferred-configuration, TCM, breath, or practice hypotheses fail.

## First-draft authorization and limits

The reviewed source set can authorize a reader-facing chapter that:

- opens inside an ordinary, collaborative, person-centered clinical context;
- labels the additive sequence conceptual and unvalidated;
- adds task and load history selectively for a named question;
- separates reports, observations, measurements, calculations, models, inferences, embodied observations, and reconstructions;
- treats present modulation as evidence without turning it into origin;
- gives imaging and other findings bounded, condition-specific jobs;
- acknowledges substantial overlap with physical therapy, rehabilitation, biomechanics, ergonomics, and other fields;
- states the proposed incremental contribution as a research question;
- supplies an explicit no-added-value branch and stopping rule;
- creates the minimum record used by Chapters 23–26; and
- preserves the longer medical-overhaul ambition as contingent future work.

It does not authorize a safety checklist, diagnostic label, screening score, red-flag list, referral threshold, prognosis, treatment, care sequence, patient self-test, preferred-configuration assessment, TCM clinical mapping, breath test, or corrective practice.
