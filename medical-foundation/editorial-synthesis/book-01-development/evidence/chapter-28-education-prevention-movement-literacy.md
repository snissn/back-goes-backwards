## Editorial decision

Chapter 28 can take a bounded affirmative stance. A public language for task demand, support, duration, repetition, variability, capacity, fatigue, recovery, and available options is worth developing. Professionals can test whether that language improves communication across existing disciplines. Environments and institutions can change the boundary conditions under which people act. Those propositions do not require a universal posture, a hidden fault model, a self-correction system, or proof of the series' preferred-configuration hypothesis.

The chapter's positive claim is:

> Mechanical literacy can help people and institutions describe what a task asks, which supports and alternatives are realistically available, how demand changes through time, and where uncertainty remains. Education and design should be judged by the specific outcomes they are intended to change and by their effects on access, agency, burden, and harm—not by agreement with a mechanical theory.

The evidence does not support a generic claim that education, ergonomics, participation, or “more movement” prevents harm. Primary studies show a deliberately mixed pattern: knowledge or professional behavior can improve while patient outcomes do not; measured exposure can change while most health outcomes do not; a workstation feature can reduce a specified incident disorder in one work setting; participatory programs can be null, incompletely implemented, or improve one endpoint while worsening another; and a recurrence intervention can reduce a defined recurrence while increasing lower-extremity adverse events. The chapter must retain those distinctions rather than average them into optimism or pessimism.

The wider ambition also survives. Schools, workplaces, communities, professional curricula, health systems, designers, practitioners, teachers, and scientists can become sites of development. That is an institutional and cultural research program, not evidence that the present framework is ready to scale.

## Operational distinctions

### Mechanical literacy is not an instruction system

For this chapter, **mechanical literacy** means the ability to describe an ordinary person–task–environment relation using:

- the task and its phase;
- contacts, external supports, assistance, reach, and visual relation;
- duration, rate, repetition, variability, and pace;
- capacity, fatigue, recovery opportunities, and changing state;
- who controls the task, schedule, equipment, and alternatives;
- what was observed, measured, reported, modeled, inferred, or left unknown; and
- which realistic options are available to this person in this setting.

It does not mean learning to feel tissue stress, locate a unique force path, identify a mechanical fault, screen for disease, diagnose oneself or another person, select treatment, hold a correct posture, or implement the preferred configuration. Awareness is not presumed harmless. Language and imagery can enlarge agency, but they can also induce continuous monitoring, body moralization, fear, guilt, or misplaced certainty.

WHO's health-literacy framework treats literacy as social practice shaped by community norms, culture, organizations, political conditions, and available support, not merely information deposited in an individual.[^who-hl2022] Chapter 28 can transfer that structural lesson: a person cannot use an option that a schedule, workplace, cost, disability access barrier, safety rule, or caregiving duty removes. WHO does not validate *mechanical literacy* as defined here, and the chapter must not borrow that authority.

### Four applications with separate outcomes

| Application | Object being changed | First defensible outcomes | Promotion that is not automatic |
|---|---|---|---|
| Public education | comprehension, vocabulary, uncertainty, recognized options | comprehension, recall, interpretation, communication, burden, vigilance | behavior, exposure, symptom, injury, disease, or population benefit |
| Professional education | shared questions, documentation, handoffs, scope recognition | knowledge, confidence, communication accuracy, behavior, duplication, burden | improved diagnosis, treatment, patient outcome, or system efficiency |
| Environmental or task design | contacts, assistance, reach, timing, tools, support, pace, task sequence, access, control | measured exposure, usability, comfort, performance, fatigue, participation, tradeoffs | lower tissue stress, symptom prevention, injury prevention, or universal fit |
| Prevention program | one named adverse endpoint | incidence, severity, duration, recurrence, disability, or participation as prespecified | benefit at a different endpoint or proof of the proposed mechanism |

Clinical use is a separate downstream object, not an outcome inherited from any row. Professional education asks what professionals learn or do; public education asks what an audience understands; prevention asks whether a named adverse endpoint changes. Using mechanical literacy in care would instead require its own clinical purpose, comparison, patient-facing outcomes, scope controls, burdens, and harms. Nothing in this packet validates that use or converts Chapter 27's reasoning questions into a patient tool.

### Option space, universal design, accommodation, and assistance

An **option space** is the set of strategies a person can realistically use given the task, body, health, pain, fatigue, disability, pregnancy, age, culture, assistance, cost, schedule, safety, job control, and competing priorities. It is not maximal joint range, maximal choice in the abstract, or a count that is always better when larger.

The UN Convention on the Rights of Persons with Disabilities defines universal design as products, environments, programs, and services usable by all people to the greatest extent possible without adaptation or specialized design, while explicitly preserving assistive devices for particular groups where needed. It separately defines reasonable accommodation as necessary and appropriate modifications in a particular case that do not impose a disproportionate or undue burden.[^crpd-art2] Chapter 28 must preserve all three objects: inclusive baseline design, person-specific accommodation, and assistance. None should be coded as biomechanical failure.

WHO's ICF locates functioning and disability in context and includes environmental factors.[^who-icf] WHO disability policy further requires consultation with persons with disabilities and integration of disability across design, implementation, monitoring, and evaluation, including attention to intersecting discrimination.[^who-disability2021] The WHO–UNICEF assistive-technology report frames appropriate products and services as supports for functioning, participation, and inclusion while documenting large access gaps.[^great2022] Together these authorize disability and access as central design variables. They do not show that more options, one device, or one environment improves every mechanical or medical outcome.

## Evidence for education and public mechanical literacy

### Public messages can change some beliefs, but the outcome chain remains open

Buchbinder, Jolley, and Wyatt evaluated a statewide public back-pain campaign using nonrandomized before-and-after surveys with an adjacent state as comparison, including 4,730 members of the public and 2,556 general practitioners. Belief scores improved in the intervention state. Back-related compensation claims, compensated days, and medical payments also declined descriptively during the campaign.[^buchbinder2001] The study supports the possibility that sustained public messaging can change beliefs. Its nonrandomized design and descriptive claims analysis do not isolate the campaign as the cause of disability or cost changes, and its back-pain content does not validate this project's mechanical model.

The result does not generalize automatically to digital education. In a stepped-wedge cluster-randomized trial of 779 people with nonspecific low-back pain, Suman and colleagues found no between-group improvement in back-pain beliefs, disability, or absenteeism from a multifaceted eHealth strategy relative to a digital information letter. Completion was lower in the intervention group, and estimated cost utility was promising without significant cost savings.[^suman2019] Chapter 28 should use the contrast as a design lesson: medium, context, reach, engagement, comparator, and outcome matter. “Education works” and “education does not work” are both overstatements.

Geldhof and colleagues' two-school-year quasi-experimental program among 193 intervention and 172 control children aged 9–11 improved posture knowledge and observed material-handling behavior and reduced time in trunk flexion and neck torsion. Fear-avoidance beliefs did not change, and pain showed only a nonsignificant trend in boys.[^geldhof2006] The study separates knowledge, observed behavior, exposure proxies, beliefs, and symptoms. The intervention taught back posture and postural dynamism; it is not evidence that the chapter should teach those commands, that measured posture changes reduced internal tissue exposure, or that childhood education prevents later pain.

Public or professional language can also have adverse effects. In semistructured interviews with 12 people with acute and 11 with chronic low-back pain, Darlow and colleagues found that clinicians' messages were recalled as influential. Some were interpreted as implying vulnerability and a need for protection and were connected in participants' accounts with vigilance, worry, guilt, or frustration; reassurance could increase confidence.[^darlow2013] This primary qualitative study supplies a harm hypothesis and a reason to test interpretation. It does not estimate incidence, establish that any phrase caused an outcome, or license replacing biopsychosocial communication with a mechanical story.

### Pedagogy obligations for the project-native vocabulary

The archive's awareness → engagement → integration sequence (`BGB-S-1613`) and its preference for jargon-free, visual, metaphorical, tactile, autonomy-supporting teaching (`BGB-S-1614`) remain usable development hypotheses. Before promotion, the material needs audience-specific evaluation of:

1. comprehension of demand, support, time, variability, capacity, recovery, options, and uncertainty;
2. whether visuals and metaphors preserve the observation–inference boundary;
3. whether people can apply the language without detecting hidden faults;
4. accessibility across language, disability, literacy, age, culture, and medium;
5. changes in confidence, recognized options, burden, vigilance, shame, or blame;
6. comparison with simpler existing language; and
7. retention and transfer across tasks without assuming health benefit.

The chapter can use ordinary examples and multiple representational modes. Tactile or embodied examples remain reports and learning devices, not measurements of internal force, torque, stress, strain, pressure, or “force lines.” Individual cues, tests, drills, posterior organization, breath instruction, TCM correspondence, and self-experimentation remain protected for Book V.

## Evidence for professional education

WHO's interprofessional education framework treats collaborative learning and practice as a workforce and health-system strategy that must be adapted to local needs.[^who-ipe2010] It supports the legitimacy of studying cross-disciplinary language. It neither endorses this book's grammar nor shows that adding mechanical detail improves handoffs, decisions, or patient outcomes.

The FREE pragmatic cluster-randomized trial provides a useful endpoint split. Eight practices, 63 general practitioners, and 226 patients participated in a clinician education and management program for low-back pain. Intervention clinicians improved attitudes, knowledge, confidence, and guideline-concordant behavior, but the groups did not differ on the primary six-month disability outcome or secondary patient outcomes; the adjusted Roland–Morris difference was 0.57 points (95% CI −0.64 to 1.78). One activity-related gluteal tear occurred in the control group.[^darlow-free2019] The trial supports a precise statement: professional education can change professional outcomes without demonstrating superior patient recovery. Because the intervention also changed clinical management, it cannot be used as a pure test of education or as proof for the proposed shared mechanical vocabulary.

A future professional-education study should prespecify the professions, baseline language, content, comparison, setting, handoff or reasoning task, burden, scope errors, and patient-facing consequences. Early outcomes may be documentation completeness, agreement about task and evidence state, appropriate stopping, or reduced unsupported inference. Diagnosis, treatment selection, and health outcomes require their own downstream evaluation.

## Evidence for occupational exposure and environmental design

### Systems architecture

The International Ergonomics Association defines human factors/ergonomics around interactions among people and other system elements and distinguishes physical, cognitive, and organizational domains.[^iea-definition] The ILO/IEA principles for HFE design and management similarly place people, tasks, tools and technologies, work organization, and environment inside one work system; they emphasize worker involvement, individual differences, continual evaluation, and redesign.[^ilo-iea2021] These sources support Chapter 28's person–task–assistance–organization–environment model. They are guidance, not proof that any selected feature reduces exposure or harm.

NIOSH's Total Worker Health hierarchy prioritizes elimination, substitution, and redesign of work conditions over education that asks workers to manage hazards personally.[^niosh-twh2024] The chapter may therefore contrast “hold yourself correctly” with changes to support, reach, pace, assistance, task sequence, staffing, or control. It may not imply that every source can be eliminated, that education is useless, or that a redesign has succeeded before outcomes and tradeoffs are measured.

### Primary studies show endpoint-specific benefits, nulls, and redistribution

In a one-year four-arm randomized trial among 182 call-center operators, Rempel and colleagues compared ergonomics training alone with training plus a trackball, forearm support, or both. Forearm support was associated with lower incidence of a defined neck/shoulder musculoskeletal disorder (hazard ratio 0.49, 95% CI 0.24–0.97) and lower neck/shoulder and right-upper-extremity pain; 63 incident diagnoses occurred overall.[^rempel2006] This is a bounded positive example: a specified support changed specified pain and incident-disorder outcomes in a defined workforce. It does not establish universal dimensions, a correct posture, a tissue route, benefit for every user, or transfer to another task.

Edwardson and colleagues randomized 78 clusters containing 756 office workers to usual practice, a multicomponent SMART Work and Life intervention, or the same intervention plus a height-adjustable desk. At 12 months, sitting decreased by 22.2 minutes/day without the desk and 63.7 minutes/day with it relative to control; the desk group sat 41.7 minutes/day less than the no-desk intervention. Small selected improvements appeared in stress, wellbeing, vigour, and lower-extremity pain, while most cardiometabolic, fatigue, musculoskeletal, and work outcomes showed no noticeable intervention effects.[^edwardson2022] This is the chapter's cleanest exposure lesson: successfully changing the measured behavior does not make every health endpoint move or establish the mechanism assumed by the intervention.

The Stay@Work participatory-ergonomics trial shows that design can redistribute demand. Thirty-seven departments and 3,047 workers were cluster randomized. Decision latitude and decision authority improved modestly, but reported exposure to awkward trunk postures increased (odds ratio 1.86, 95% CI 1.15–3.01), other physical and psychosocial risk-factor outcomes were null, and follow-up loss was 35% and 29%.[^driessen-exposure2011] In the companion pain analysis, the program did not reduce low-back- or neck-pain prevalence, intensity, or duration and did not show primary prevention for low-back pain (OR 1.23, 95% CI 0.97–1.57) or neck pain (OR 1.01, 95% CI 0.74–1.40). Recovery from low-back pain improved (OR 1.41, 95% CI 1.01–1.96), while recovery from neck pain did not.[^driessen-pain2011] The same intervention thus produced different exposure, symptom-incidence, and recovery results. None can stand in for the others.

Among 504 workers in 119 kitchens, Haukka and colleagues reported that a participatory program generated and implemented 402 changes but produced no systematic difference in musculoskeletal pain, local fatigue, musculoskeletal sick leave, or perceived physical workload during the intervention or one-year follow-up.[^haukka2008] Implementation activity is not itself a beneficial outcome.

In the TOY wait-list cluster trial, 190 workers from 16 childcare institutions received a 20-week participatory-ergonomics training program or usual practice. Physical exertion, maximum pain, pain interference, and number of painful regions did not differ discernibly. Musculoskeletal-pain-related sickness absence decreased by 0.48 days/month (95% CI −0.8 to −0.1), while self-efficacy worsened by 0.2 points (95% CI −0.3 to −0.0).[^rasmussen2020] A program can improve an attendance endpoint while leaving the targeted exposure and symptom endpoints unchanged and worsening a psychological measure.

These trials do not show that participatory design is futile. They show why the chapter must distinguish participation in identifying changes, fidelity of implementation, measured changes to work, and each intended outcome. Participation without decision authority or resources can also transfer responsibility to workers while leaving the source of demand intact.

## Participatory and universal design, accessibility, and participation

The design model should contain at least five coupled elements:

```text
person and changing state
    <-> task and performance requirement
    <-> assistance, tools, and supports
    <-> organization, schedule, resources, and control
    <-> built, social, and information environment
```

No element is presumed to be the problem or the correction target. A standardized design can fit one person and constrain another. A support can reduce one demand while increasing reach, pressure, visual effort, cognitive burden, stigma, cost, or dependence on unavailable assistance. A person's preferred strategy can be safe and capable even when it differs from a normative unsupported posture.

Oude Geerdink and colleagues tested Individual Placement and Support and a Participatory Workplace Intervention in a 2×2 factorial trial of 120 municipal clients with work disabilities over 18 months. The workplace intervention did not improve time to sustainable employment (hazard ratio 0.89, 95% CI 0.48–1.64); mental-health scores were slightly worse (difference −4.07, 95% CI −7.93 to −0.22), and most secondary outcomes were null. Only 16 of 60 assigned clients received the workplace intervention according to protocol, in part because its main workplace component could begin only after employment had been obtained; delivery remained incomplete even among clients who found work.[^oudegeerdink2025] The result demonstrates both an adverse endpoint and a structural implementation problem. It does not show that accommodation or participation is ineffective in general, nor that disability should be treated as an individual mechanical deficit.

Lambeek and colleagues randomized 134 workers who had been sick-listed for at least 12 weeks with chronic low-back pain to usual care or integrated care combining participatory workplace intervention with graded activity. Median time to sustainable return to work was 88 days versus 208 days, with a return-to-work hazard ratio of 1.9 (95% CI 1.2–2.8); functional status improved, while pain did not differ.[^lambeek2010] This is a positive disability/participation result for an integrated clinical and workplace program. It cannot isolate the workplace component, does not authorize a public education claim, and does not imply that pain or a proposed mechanical route caused the work outcome.

Access review must ask who defines the desired outcome, who can refuse, who controls the setting, what assistance is available, which costs are transferred, and whether surveillance or disclosure is required to receive support. Disability, pregnancy, aging, chronic illness, fatigue, pain, body diversity, culture, job security, caregiving, resources, and safety are not after-the-fact caveats; they determine the available system.

## The outcome-specific prevention ladder

The word *prevention* is incomplete without an object. Every claim must record population, baseline state, intervention, comparator, outcome definition, time at risk or follow-up, measurement, adherence or implementation, relevant competing events, adverse outcomes, and alternative explanations.

The study names in this ladder refer to the source-bijected primary-study descriptions above; the table separates their outcome objects without creating additional source jobs.

| Prevention object | What must be counted or measured | Positive or mixed primary example | Null, adverse, or boundary result | What cannot be inherited |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Exposure** | named behavior, force, posture, support relation, duration, frequency, or other exposure under declared measurement conditions | SMART Work and Life reduced measured sitting time at 12 months | Stay@Work modestly improved decision variables while awkward-trunk-posture exposure increased and most exposure outcomes were null | symptom, injury, disability, disease, or benefit from the assumed route |
| **Symptom** | incidence, severity, duration, interference, recovery, and denominator for the specified symptom | Forearm support reduced selected upper-body pain in call-center operators | Participatory programs were null for multiple pain outcomes; Stay@Work improved low-back-pain recovery but not incidence or neck recovery | diagnosed injury, historical cause, tissue mechanism, or general health |
| **Injury or defined disorder** | prespecified case definition, ascertainment, exposure opportunity, competing events, and time | Forearm support lowered incident physician-ascertained neck/shoulder disorder in one trial | A pain, posture, comfort, or exposure result is not an injury result; the other selected trials do not supply a generic injury-prevention claim | symptom absence, disease prevention, or transfer to another task/device/population |
| **Disability** | specified function or disability measure, context, meaningful change, and follow-up | Integrated care improved functional status and accelerated sustainable return to work | FREE changed clinician outcomes without changing patient disability; eHealth education changed neither beliefs nor disability | pain reduction, isolated ergonomic efficacy, or mechanical causation |
| **Recurrence** | recovered baseline state, exact recurrence definition, surveillance interval, censoring, and adverse events | In WalkBack, 701 recovered adults receiving individualized progressive walking plus education had lower activity-limiting low-back-pain recurrence (HR 0.72, 95% CI 0.60–0.85; median 208 versus 112 days)[^pocovi2024] | Lower-extremity adverse events were more numerous in the intervention group (100 versus 54), although participants with any adverse event were similar; components cannot be separated | primary prevention, education alone, a universal movement dose, preferred configuration, or recurrence of another condition |
| **Participation** | person-valued involvement in work, school, home, community, or another life situation, including sustainability and access | Integrated care improved sustainable return to work | The disability workplace trial did not improve sustainable employment and slightly worsened mental health under low implementation | symptom or tissue improvement, independence without assistance, or universal institutional benefit |

The ladder is not a hierarchy of human value. Comfort, knowledge, access, or participation may matter greatly even if they do not prevent injury. The discipline is semantic and causal: state what changed.

## Equity, burden shifting, surveillance, stigma, and access

### Responsibility follows control

The chapter should state directly that an individual cannot be held solely responsible for conditions controlled by employers, schools, transport systems, housing, care arrangements, device procurement, or public design. The NIOSH hierarchy and ILO/IEA work-system principles described above support moving inquiry upstream toward task and organizational design. Education may help people name a problem or participate in redesign, but it must not become a cheaper substitute for staffing, equipment, schedule, assistance, or hazard control.

### Monitoring is not neutral awareness

EU-OSHA's guidance on smart digital monitoring describes potential real-time hazard recognition but also warns about surveillance, performance measurement, privacy, data ownership and security, discrimination, algorithmic management, substitution for stronger controls, and transfer of collective responsibility to individuals.[^euosha2022] WHO's public-health-surveillance ethics guidance supplies wider duties around necessity, proportionality, privacy, autonomy, equity, transparency, and community engagement.[^who-surveillance2017] These are governance boundaries, not proof that every wearable, camera, posture alert, or exposure sensor is harmful.

Any monitoring proposal must specify:

- the hazard and intended outcome;
- why monitoring is necessary rather than a direct design change;
- who owns, accesses, combines, retains, and deletes data;
- whether workers or learners can refuse without penalty;
- whether data can affect employment, insurance, care, discipline, or access;
- false alerts, missingness, algorithmic bias, and whose bodies defined thresholds;
- whether feedback increases options or commands conformity;
- collective controls that remain funded; and
- independent routes for challenge, correction, and redress.

### Stigma and normative bodies

Mechanical language can turn difference into fault if symmetry, stillness, unsupported movement, independence, youth, thinness, strength, or a presumed posture becomes the unspoken norm. The chapter should describe function and access before deviation. Assistance, accommodation, rest, sitting, or a stable specialized strategy may be capable solutions. The reader should never be asked to display, disclose, or correct a body to earn participation.

Equity evaluation must include reach, comprehension, usable options, time and financial costs, language, digital access, disability access, work control, job security, caregiving, culture, and who benefits from any productivity gain. An average benefit can coexist with exclusion or harm in a subgroup.

## Institutional and cultural ambition

The archive's program is intentionally larger than one chapter. Health systems, schools, workplaces, communities, and public culture (`BGB-S-1618`) remain legitimate development sites. Embodiment as lifelong iteration without a perfection endpoint (`BGB-S-1619`) remains an important philosophical guard. Later integration of structure, breath, and traditional observation (`BGB-S-1620`) and the invitation to practitioners, teachers, and scientists to contribute findings (`BGB-S-1621`) remain explicit future work.

The updated Medical Research Council framework for complex interventions requires attention to context, program theory, stakeholder engagement, uncertainty, refinement, evaluation, and implementation.[^skivington2021] Applied here, institutional ambition should proceed through separate tracks:

1. **co-definition:** identify audience, problem, existing practice, desired outcome, alternatives, and people likely to be excluded;
2. **content development:** test comprehension, interpretation, accessibility, uncertainty language, and adverse vigilance;
3. **bounded feasibility:** measure reach, adoption, fidelity, burden, resources, and unintended effects in one setting;
4. **comparative evaluation:** compare against existing education or design, with prespecified outcomes and harms;
5. **implementation study:** examine governance, professional scope, cost, sustainability, equity, and adaptation;
6. **outcome-specific scale evaluation:** test only the educational, exposure, symptom, injury, disability, recurrence, participation, or system outcome actually claimed; and
7. **revision or withdrawal:** revise when understanding fails, harms appear, burden shifts, access narrows, or existing approaches perform better.

This staged program preserves the cultural ambition without implying that Book I is a curriculum ready for schools, a competency standard for professions, or a public-health intervention ready for policy. Chapter 29 owns development-track detail and institutional implementation questions.

## Evidence state, stopping rules, and disfavoring results

### Present evidence state

- The field-level definitions and rights principles are authoritative guidance (`FND`), not intervention-effect estimates.
- The intervention findings are direct reports from primary studies (`OBS`/`MEAS` as applicable), bounded by their populations, content, comparators, measures, follow-up, implementation, and harms.
- The layered application model, modern-exposure sequence, option-space model, and prevention ladder are project syntheses (`SYN`).
- Mechanical literacy as a distinct pedagogy and the shared Book I professional grammar remain hypotheses (`HYP`).
- Institutional and cultural transformation remains a protected development program (`HYP/APP`), not an established outcome.
- Preferred configuration, posterior/Yang organization, force-line, breath, pressure, TCM correspondence, and practice claims remain non-load-bearing for Chapter 28 and protected for later books.

### Stop the claim when

1. the audience, task, intervention, comparator, outcome, timeframe, or measure is unnamed;
2. a knowledge or belief result is used as a behavior, exposure, or health result;
3. a measured exposure change is used as a symptom or injury result;
4. comfort, preference, or fit is used as proof of tissue loading or prevention;
5. an average design result erases access, subgroup, or tradeoff effects;
6. participation is used as proof of symptom or tissue improvement;
7. an individualized multicomponent intervention is reduced to education or one mechanical mechanism;
8. implementation activity or adoption is used as efficacy;
9. monitoring replaces direct control or requires ungoverned surveillance;
10. responsibility is assigned to people who do not control the relevant conditions; or
11. the proposed educational or design change performs no better than simpler existing practice or produces disproportionate burden or harm.

### Results that would disfavor the project-native educational model

- learners cannot distinguish observation from inference after instruction;
- the vocabulary performs worse than ordinary language for comprehension or communication;
- teaching increases vigilance, fear, shame, blame, false diagnosis, or unnecessary care;
- professionals show more unsupported mechanical inference, scope crossing, or documentation burden;
- accessibility or comprehension gaps widen;
- recognized options do not become realistic options because institutional constraints are ignored;
- a design repeatedly transfers demand or excludes users without compensating benefit;
- the program requires surveillance or individual vigilance where direct controls are available; or
- selected outcomes remain null while stronger public claims continue.

These results would require revision, narrowing, replacement, or withdrawal of the relevant component. They would not by themselves falsify all biomechanics or the later preferred-configuration research program.
