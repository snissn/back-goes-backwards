## Editorial decision

Chapter 20 can compare several unlike compliant systems through one disciplined question:

> What bounded freedom does this object need for a declared function; what intrinsic, extrinsic, or mixed relation could limit that freedom; what receiver-specific mechanical output would change; and what evidence would distinguish an important constraint from ordinary deformation, restoration, compensation, or no consequential change?

The comparison is compatible with the author's central hard/soft intuition without turning it into a fixed tissue taxonomy. In the project's language, shape-preserving and form-receiving are relational roles for a task, frame, direction, and timescale. The vocabulary is **motivated by** TCM hard/soft and Yin/Yang concepts but is not definitionally identical to them. Bone can deform and receive form; tendon, muscle, fascia, fluid, or pressure can transmit large demand or help preserve shape. A structure can change roles across phases and directions.

The authorial shorthand—“Load wants to travel through the hard body so that the soft body can remain soft”—can be translated into a bounded division-of-labor hypothesis:

> For selected tasks, demand may be better organized when shape-organizing structures carry or distribute enough of it that form-receiving structures retain the excursion, caliber, glide, pressure accommodation, or other deformation their function requires.

“Better” requires a named output and comparator. The proposition does not say that compliant tissues should be unloaded, that skeletal load is always primary, or that more compliance is always preferable. Its strongest null is that conventional descriptions of geometry, stiffness, contact, pressure, active control, and material response explain the selected output fully and the hard/soft language adds no discriminating value.

## The transferable object is controlled freedom—not softness without load

A compliant object is not useful because it avoids force. It may bear tension, pressure, shear, contact, or bending while changing shape. Its relevant freedom is the deformation or relative motion needed for a named function within a bounded range.

Examples make the differences explicit:

- a vascular conduit may need caliber and wall deformation compatible with a pressure–flow relation;
- a collecting lymphatic needs filling, wall contraction, valve operation, and transport against changing pressure;
- a peripheral nerve needs excursion, strain accommodation, and physiological integrity while crossing moving interfaces;
- a tendon–subsynovial connective-tissue interface needs task-dependent relative motion without possessing a lumen;
- an upper airway needs a pressure–flow relation shaped by wall state, active dilator behavior, lung volume, and surrounding geometry; and
- a pressure-bearing cavity needs a boundary that can contain pressure, deform, and share structural work for a declared task.

These are not six versions of one hose. A vessel carries fluid; a nerve signals and contains its own microcirculation and axonal transport; a tendon interface permits relative glide; an airway carries gas through an actively controlled collapsible passage; a cavity distributes pressure traction across a boundary. The common question survives only if those outputs remain different.

The role pair should therefore be encoded on relations rather than painted permanently onto tissues:

```text
declared task, frame, direction, and timescale
    -> candidate shape-organizing relation
    -> form-receiving freedom that remains available
    -> receiver-specific output
    -> separately supported functional or biological consequence

required alternatives:
    ordinary deformation / adequate restoration / intrinsic receiver state /
    active compensation / different receiver / measurement artifact /
    low mechanical priority / no consequential change
```

This is the testable form of the hard/soft intuition. The shape-organizing relation must be measured or at least operationally specified. The form-receiving function must have an observable. If the proposed division of labor predicts nothing beyond the conventional variables, it remains a useful explanatory vocabulary rather than a validated independent model.

## Persistence must be classified before consequence

The garden-hose analogy performs one limited job: an externally maintained kink precedes the change in lumen and flow. The hose often restores when the maintaining relation is removed. Anatomy adds active tissue, internal pressure, anchoring, growth, inflammation, remodeling, collateral paths, changing tasks, and nervous control. Those additions make four histories possible:

1. **Transient and restored.** The object deforms during an ordinary task and returns when pressure, contact, phase, or position changes.
2. **Extrinsically maintained.** A surrounding relation continues to impose or recreate the deformation or loss of excursion.
3. **Intrinsically retained.** Material change, wall state, tone, swelling, scar, adhesion, lesion, thrombosis, or another receiver-specific state persists after the original surrounding relation has changed.
4. **Mixed.** Extrinsic exposure changes the receiver, or an intrinsic state changes how surrounding structures load it; later persistence belongs to both histories.

An observed narrow lumen, reduced excursion, altered pressure, or changed waveform does not decide among them. A response to repositioning supports present modulation only. Persistence after repositioning can reflect intrinsic retention, inadequate change in the proposed boundary, a different maintaining relation, or measurement error. The first draft should show time explicitly:

```text
state A -> maintaining relation applied -> deformation/output measured
        -> relation removed or changed -> restored / partly restored / retained / unknown
```

The causal sequence stays receiver-specific:

```text
intrinsic or extrinsic relation
    -> maintained geometry, strain, contact, or lost excursion
    -> receiver-specific mechanical output
    -> receiver-specific biological or functional response
    -> finding, symptom, diagnosis, or outcome, if independently established
```

No downstream noun can replace an untested arrow. “Impingement,” “compression,” “tether,” and “pressure” are beginnings of descriptions, not complete mechanisms.

## Vascular conduits: geometry is one variable inside a pressure–flow system

Collapsible-tube experiments establish that external pressure, wall behavior, lumen geometry, upstream and downstream pressures, and flow can form nonlinear relations. Low and Chew observed flow limitation in an experimental collapsible tube during a collapsed phase, including conditions in which flow became relatively independent of further increases in driving pressure.[^low1991] Griffin and colleagues used water flowing through an unsupported silicone tube under a gravity-generated pressure gradient. In that apparatus, lumen-area reduction had to become very large before flow fell by more than half, and the tested length of a 74-percent area reduction did not materially change mean flow.[^griffin2018] These experiments are useful precisely because their boundaries are explicit. They do not model pulsatile blood, vascular tone, branching, collateral routes, autoregulation, blood rheology in vivo, or clinical perfusion.

Human positional studies show why lumen or waveform cannot be assigned one moral direction. In nineteen healthy participants, provocative arm positions produced Doppler waveforms classified as significant arterial compression in at least one position in 28.9 percent of participants; other velocity measures varied, and the method would have produced many false-positive thoracic-outlet inferences in an asymptomatic sample.[^bishop2021] In forty-nine healthy volunteers, a specified abducted, flexed, externally rotated arm position increased mean subclavian-vein cross-sectional area rather than narrowing it.[^sadek2016] The studies concern different vessels, outputs, and protocols. Together they establish ordinary position dependence and the need for a clinical boundary; neither identifies a beneficial or harmful routing state.

A vascular row therefore needs at least:

```text
surrounding relation + wall/active state + internal and external pressure
    -> lumen and wall geometry
    -> local pressure gradient, resistance, and waveform or flow
    -> collateral/autoregulatory response
    -> tissue perfusion or venous return
    -> clinical consequence, if supported
```

The evidence here supports selected early links, not the whole chain. Intrinsic wall disease, thrombosis, valve state, systemic pressure, volume status, autonomic regulation, and ordinary anatomical variation remain alternatives. Chapter 24 owns the thoracic-outlet application and its arterial, venous, and neural distinctions.

## Lymphatic transport is an active valve-and-pump system

Lymphatic pathways cannot inherit vascular equations or conclusions merely because they contain a lumen. Collecting lymphatics possess valves and active wall contraction; transport can depend on intrinsic pumping, external movement, pressure gradients, tissue-fluid formation, and the state of a network.

In isolated rat mesenteric lymphangions, Davis and colleagues independently controlled input and output pressure, measured intralymphangion pressure, tracked diameter and valve positions, and found a time-dependent increase in contractility as afterload rose. Stronger segments maintained positive work over a wider tested range; weaker segments reached pump failure differently.[^davis2012] A related isolated-vessel study found pressure-dependent constriction and changes in valve behavior after selective elevation of downstream pressure.[^scallan2013] These are direct demonstrations that the receiver changes its own mechanical state. A narrowing lymphatic can be an active response rather than evidence that an external skeletal relation has simply crushed a passive tube.

Human studies also show extrinsic participation without reducing transport to compression. Havas and colleagues injected radiolabeled albumin into the vastus lateralis of eight men and found that dynamic and isometric knee-extension tasks increased clearance relative to rest, with differences between the tested muscle configurations.[^havas1997] The measure was tracer clearance at an injection site, not complete network flow, tissue-fluid balance, or a clinical outcome. It nevertheless establishes that surrounding muscle activity and deformation can change a transport-related measure under bounded conditions.

The lymphatic row requires:

```text
tissue-fluid input + intrinsic contraction + valve state + external movement/contact
    + upstream/downstream and tissue pressure
    -> segment diameter, filling, ejection, reflux, or propulsion measure
    -> network transport and fluid balance
    -> swelling or other clinical endpoint, if independently supported
```

Ordinary loading can assist rather than obstruct. Increased afterload can evoke compensation before failure. A systemic or intrinsic lymphatic state can dominate. One accessible measure may sample only part of the network. The hard/soft question is therefore whether surrounding organization preserves or disrupts the receiver's required pumping and filling freedoms—not whether all external pressure is undesirable.

## Nerves: excursion, strain, contact, circulation, transport, and signaling remain separate

Peripheral nerves move relative to surrounding tissues during ordinary joint motion. In healthy participants, Dilley and colleagues used ultrasound to measure median-nerve excursion during wrist, elbow, shoulder, and neck movements. Reported excursion depended strongly on the maneuver and measurement location, while additional forearm strain remained small under several tested movements.[^dilley2003] Normal excursion is an affirmative function and a source of ordinary variation; it is not evidence of entrapment.

Controlled animal studies establish that sufficiently imposed compression can affect a different set of outputs. Rydevik, Lundborg, and Bagge directly observed intraneural microcirculation in rabbit tibial nerves during graded local compression. Venular, capillary, and arteriolar flow changed at different applied pressures, and high, prolonged compression produced persistent microcirculatory impairment under the experimental conditions.[^rydevik1981] That study supplies a pressure-to-microcirculation bridge in exposed rabbit nerve. It does not provide a human postural threshold, explain a symptom, or show that visually reduced space produces the same local pressure.

The nerve row must separate:

```text
joint motion, bed length, contact, tether, or local pressure
    -> excursion, elongation, transverse deformation, strain, or shear
    -> intraneural pressure, circulation, axonal transport, or conduction
    -> sensory or motor function
    -> symptom or diagnosis, if independently supported
```

The variables are not interchangeable. Less translation can accompany different local strain depending on slack and reference frame. A nerve can move while under contact; it can show altered signaling through an intrinsic or systemic process without a selected mechanical constraint. Pain, sensitivity, or disease can change movement first. Measurement artifacts include moving reference tissue, probe pressure, image-plane loss, and protocol differences. A corridor change can therefore make a neural question intelligible without establishing neuropathy.

## Sliding interfaces preserve freedom without a lumen

The middle-finger flexor tendon–subsynovial connective-tissue relation from Chapter 13 is the strongest transfer test because its key output is relative motion rather than flow. In twenty healthy wrists, Yoshii and colleagues tracked flexor tendon and adjacent SSCT during gripping of three cylinder diameters. Both moved, and the calculated relative-motion pattern changed with task geometry.[^yoshii2009] The result does not establish one ideal amount of glide or identify an adverse constraint.

Chapter 13's full evidence review further distinguishes absolute tendon excursion, SSCT excursion, relative displacement, and frictional work. In a cadaver experiment spanning wrist posture, tendon force, and velocity, ultrasound-derived relative displacement and mechanically measured frictional work did not respond as interchangeable quantities.[^tat2016] A smaller relative-motion index is therefore not a measurement of shear stress, adhesion, injury, capacity, or symptom source.

The sliding-interface row needs:

```text
tendon drive + joint/task geometry + surrounding material and contact state
    -> tendon excursion and surrounding-tissue excursion
    -> relative glide, shear, contact, or frictional work as separately measured quantities
    -> finger movement or another declared function
    -> tissue response or symptom, if independently supported
```

Two nulls remain mandatory: changed geometry with retained functional relative motion, and reduced relative motion primarily explained by intrinsic material state rather than an external skeletal relation. Tendon itself can perform a high-load tensile role while participating in a form-receiving interface in another direction. The example refutes any rule that “soft” means unloaded.

## Airways: wall, posture, active control, pressure, and gas flow interact

The upper airway resembles a collapsible tube only at a coarse level. Its pressure–flow behavior depends on anatomy, wall mechanics, surrounding tissue, lung volume, sleep or anesthetic state, and pharyngeal muscle activity.

Walsh and colleagues studied fifteen healthy volunteers under propofol anesthesia and measured pharyngeal critical closing pressure while the head was flexed, neutral, extended, or rotated. Data from three participants were excluded because genioglossus activity persisted. Among the analyzed conditions, flexion made the passive airway more collapsible and extension less collapsible relative to neutral, while rotation did not show the same group effect; the site of collapse varied in some participants.[^walsh2008] This is unusually direct evidence that a surrounding geometric relation can change a defined airway-mechanics output. It remains a small anesthesia study, not a general posture recommendation or an explanation of obstructive sleep apnea.

Sleep-stage research shows that even “passive” collapsibility measures are state-dependent. In adults with and without obstructive sleep apnea, Carberry and colleagues found differences in critical closing pressure and genioglossus activity across sleep stages, with pharyngeal muscle activity increasing after the pressure reductions used to estimate Pcrit.[^carberry2016] This keeps active compensation and measurement protocol inside the model.

The airway row requires:

```text
head/neck and surrounding geometry + wall/tissue state + lung volume
    + active dilator state + internal/external pressure
    -> lumen and critical closing pressure or resistance
    -> airflow and gas exchange
    -> sleep, anesthetic, or clinical outcome, if independently supported
```

The chapter may use the airway as evidence that posture can matter mechanically while still refusing the jump from geometry to disease, causality, or treatment. Intrinsic tissue state, active tone, sleep stage, body composition, lung volume, measurement state, and no clinically meaningful consequence remain live branches.

## Pressure-bearing cavities require a boundary, volume relation, and task

Pressure is not a free-standing force. It contributes distributed traction on a declared boundary; geometry, pressure difference, wall behavior, active muscle, contained material, and external support determine the result. Chapter 6's bounded example showed that experimentally raised intra-abdominal pressure changed a measured lumbar stiffness response in three prone participants, but did not establish a breathing method, posture rule, or whole-body driver.[^hodges2005]

More recent measurement makes ordinary task variation visible. Soucasse and colleagues used an ingestible intragastric pressure sensor in twenty healthy volunteers during supervised tasks and daily activity. Pressure peaks and rates differed by task, and brief high values occurred during ordinary life.[^soucasse2022] The study measured a pressure proxy over time, not wall stress, spinal support, pelvic-floor load, organ deformation, injury, or an optimal range.

Joppin and colleagues paired dynamic MRI with an ingestible pressure sensor in eighteen healthy participants during breathing, coughing, and Valsalva tasks. Relations between abdominal-wall displacement and pressure differed by participant, task, and muscle region, and their pseudo-compliance estimate varied widely.[^joppin2024] That heterogeneity is the chapter's important result: one pressure value does not identify one boundary response, and one visible expansion does not recover the pressure field or load share.

The cavity row needs:

```text
contained volume/material + boundary geometry and stiffness + active muscle
    + external demand and phase
    -> pressure field and boundary deformation
    -> distributed traction, support, ventilation, continence, or another named function
    -> tissue or clinical outcome, if independently supported
```

A compliant boundary can be appropriate; a stiffer boundary can sometimes preserve shape; either can become limiting depending on the task. High pressure can be brief and ordinary. Low pressure need not mean poor support. Breath-as-organizing-driver and pelvic-health practice claims remain later-book work.

## What would support or disfavor the division-of-labor hypothesis

The hard/soft hypothesis gains content only when it predicts something beyond a relabeling of stiffness or load share. A useful experiment would declare the task and external demand, measure a candidate shape-organizing relation, measure the form-receiving freedom, and compare a prespecified output while accounting for active control and receiver state.

Support would become stronger if, under comparable tasks:

- greater participation of the proposed shape-organizing route predicted retained excursion, caliber, glide, or pressure accommodation;
- changing that route changed the receiver-specific output in the predicted direction and the output restored when the relation changed;
- the relation discriminated among intrinsic, extrinsic, and mixed histories better than ordinary variables alone; and
- the added model improved a defined explanation, prediction, or research decision out of sample.

The hypothesis would narrow or fail for a selected system if:

- the proposed hard-role measure had no reliable relation to the required freedom;
- the observed output was explained by intrinsic state, active control, task geometry, or measurement error;
- greater “hard” participation reduced rather than preserved the named function under the relevant conditions;
- ordinary deformation restored fully and no consequential output changed; or
- conventional variables predicted the outcome equally well without the polarity.

Those are successful research outcomes. They keep the authorial hypothesis generous while making its publication strength earned rather than assumed.

## First-draft authorization and limits

The reviewed source set authorizes a reader-facing chapter that:

- discloses the TCM hard/soft and Yin/Yang motivation without claiming exact identity;
- states the division-of-labor hypothesis in bounded, testable terms;
- distinguishes transient, extrinsically maintained, intrinsically retained, and mixed states;
- compares six systems through needed freedom, maintaining relation, output, measure, alternative, and null;
- uses vascular, lymphatic, nerve, tendon-interface, airway, and cavity evidence only for the row actually studied;
- shows ordinary deformation, active compensation, restoration, and no consequential change as affirmative branches; and
- hands Chapter 21 partial causal chains whose explanatory weight may be substantial, modest, low priority, or null rather than predetermined.

It does not authorize a diagnosis, prognosis, disease explanation, treatment, self-test, posture rule, universal soft-tissue cascade, preferred configuration, Six-Division mapping, breath driver, or claim that skeletal routing is already established as primary. Thoracic outlet remains a Chapter 24 case. Pelvic-basin, nerve-root, systemic, and chronic-disease applications remain inclusive research inventory until their receiver-specific bridges are built.
