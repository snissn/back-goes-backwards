## Editorial decision

Chapter 29 should make the project more ambitious by making its future work divisible. It should not present one pipeline in which a plausible mechanism inevitably becomes a measure, a treatment, a curriculum, and finally a medical system. It should present six different kinds of work:

1. **research** tests a declared anatomical, mechanical, biological, longitudinal, or causal relation;
2. **measurement** qualifies an observable quantity for a declared use;
3. **education** tests a selected learning, reasoning, or communication outcome;
4. **therapeutic development** evaluates an assessment or intervention for a named population, outcome, and setting;
5. **implementation** evaluates delivery of a sufficiently mature object in real settings; and
6. **health-system development** evaluates whether mature components justify durable institutional change.

The six tracks are a network, not a staircase. A project may occupy more than one track, but each question, method, output, and inference remains named. The Medical Research Council's updated complex-intervention framework explicitly treats development, feasibility, evaluation, and implementation as iterative phases and asks investigators to revisit context, programme theory, stakeholders, uncertainties, refinement, and economic considerations rather than advance automatically.[^skivington2021] The NIH Stage Model is likewise recursive rather than prescriptive or linear.[^onken2014] These frameworks support conditional movement among kinds of work. They do not establish this book's exact six-track taxonomy, which remains an editorial architecture designed to prevent claim migration.

The chapter's positive thesis is:

> A broad research program becomes tractable when each project earns one declared output, carries its provenance and limits forward, and lets positive, negative, mixed, and inconclusive results change the exact proposition tested.

The chapter should return repeatedly to one physical pathway already developed in Chapter 24:

```text
boundary condition or applied force
    -> wall deformation or excursion change
    -> endpoint-specific physical consequence
    -> finding, symptom, function, or clinical interpretation
```

That pathway is useful because every track can ask a different question without strengthening the owning chapter's medical claim. Chapter 29 must not turn a maintained-deformation narrative into a diagnosis, screening tool, treatment, prevention promise, or institutional mandate.

## Why six tracks rather than one translational ladder

The chapter's six tracks divide outputs that are routinely fused:

| Track | Immediate question | Immediate output | What the output does **not** automatically authorize |
|---|---|---|---|
| Research | Does a declared relation occur under stated conditions? | bounded finding, estimate, model comparison, or revised pathway | measure qualification, diagnosis, treatment, implementation, or policy |
| Measurement | Can a construct be acquired and interpreted well enough for one declared use? | qualified measure and uncertainty statement | causal interpretation, screening, clinical utility, or treatment selection |
| Education | Does teaching change a selected learning or communication outcome safely? | audience- and outcome-specific educational result | truth of the framework, behavior change, health benefit, or clinical competence outside the tested domain |
| Therapeutic development | Does an assessment or intervention help a named population on a declared outcome with acceptable harms? | staged feasibility, safety, efficacy, effectiveness, or mechanism result | a stronger stage, a different population, the proposed mechanism, or routine use |
| Implementation | Can a mature object be delivered with acceptable reach, fidelity, cost, access, and sustainability? | setting-specific delivery result | efficacy, mechanism, equity, or system value |
| Health-system development | Do mature components add enough value to justify durable institutional change? | transparent training, workflow, financing, or policy decision | ownership of a field, universal adoption, or proof of the larger theory |

The NIH Stage Model and the MRC framework both allow iteration and return. Curran and colleagues show that effectiveness and implementation questions can be combined prospectively in hybrid designs while retaining separate aims and outcomes.[^curran2012] The permission to combine does not permit fusion: a study can test an intervention and an implementation strategy together only by defining both.

## The cross-track provenance record

Every candidate claim, model, measure, curriculum, assessment, intervention, implementation strategy, and institutional proposal should carry the same minimum record:

1. **origin:** direct authorial formulation, archive source, generated extrapolation, traditional correspondence, embodied observation, external evidence, or mixed origin;
2. **normalized object:** the exact proposition, construct, tool, or intervention being developed;
3. **track and immediate use:** the present question and the decision the output is meant to inform;
4. **domain:** population, anatomy, task, state, scale, setting, endpoint, and time horizon;
5. **method:** what is observed, measured, calculated, modeled, manipulated, taught, delivered, or inferred;
6. **evidence state:** positive, negative, mixed, inconclusive, method-limited, not tested, or retired;
7. **alternatives:** competing mechanisms, explanations, tools, interventions, and existing practice;
8. **uncertainty and stopping condition:** result that narrows, redirects, or retires the object at its present strength;
9. **safety, ethics, access, and governance:** obligations proportionate to the use;
10. **licensed output:** what the result permits and explicitly forbids;
11. **handoff:** destination track, receiving question, and retained limits; and
12. **publication layer:** internal hypothesis, research proposal, Book I synthesis, later-book model, practice-development object, clinical evidence, or system recommendation.

The NIH Data Management and Sharing Policy requires prospective planning for management and appropriate sharing of scientific data, with attention to standards, repositories, and justified limits.[^nihdms2020] Chapter 29's record is broader: it also preserves claim provenance and prohibited inference. The NIH policy supports transparent data stewardship; it does not establish this exact editorial schema or remove privacy, consent, cultural-governance, and interpretive obligations.

Provenance must survive a favorable result. If an embodied observation generated a task that later produces a reproducible measurement, the measurement's origin may remain EMB, while the measured relation receives its own external evidence state. The later result does not retroactively turn the original experience into anatomical proof. If a generated prompt supplied an equation that is later corrected and validated, the validated equation receives a new record; the generator does not become a scientific source.

## Track 1 — mechanistic and observational research

### Immediate job

Research tests one declared relation along the Book I chain:

```text
task and environment
    -> configuration and available paths
    -> external demand and internal distribution
    -> local exposure
    -> material or biological response
    -> finding, symptom, function, participation, or outcome
```

A study should state which link it observes, which it calculates, which it models, and which it does not examine. The six adverse-organization families—global redistribution, local concentration, instability, coordination failure, maintained deformation, and compensatory rerouting—supply candidate questions, not confirmed causes.

### Design fit

Different questions need different designs:

| Question | Candidate design | Output | Principal noncollapse rule |
|---|---|---|---|
| Does a material or anatomical relation exist? | anatomy, cadaveric, imaging, material test, modeling | bounded structural or mechanical relation | existence is not clinical importance |
| Does demand alter distribution under a task? | perturbation, within-person experiment, in-vivo mechanics | task-specific contrast | surface motion or net moment is not complete internal routing |
| Does a local exposure precede response? | repeated measurement, longitudinal cohort, controlled exposure where ethical | temporal estimate | temporal order is not unique mechanism |
| Does one state modify response to later demand? | interaction, stratified perturbation, longitudinal state model | state-dependent estimate | subgroup result is not an individual prognosis |
| Does a change travel between regions? | simultaneous multiregion measurement or intervention on a declared carrier | cross-scale relation | adjacency or timing alone is not transmission |
| Does a mechanical history add information? | prospective comparison against existing model | incremental estimate and calibration | association is not clinical utility |

The MRC framework requires a programme theory, key uncertainties, context, and stakeholder perspectives to remain active throughout complex-intervention research. Nosek and colleagues describe preregistration as a means of distinguishing planned confirmatory tests from exploratory analysis.[^nosek2018] Chapter 29 should use prespecification proportionately: exploratory anatomy and construct development remain legitimate, but their outputs should not be rewritten as if they were confirmatory.

### Negative and nonunique results

A predicted redistribution may not occur. A local deformation may not alter the selected endpoint. Several configurations may produce similar distributions. A compensation may lower rather than raise exposure. A relation may disappear when task, receiver state, or exposure opportunity is measured. A proposed carrier may fail to connect scales.

A nonsignificant test alone does not establish absence. Equivalence testing requires a substantively justified smallest effect size of interest and a design capable of testing whether effects within that range can be rejected.[^lakens2018] The chapter should therefore distinguish:

- **negative within a prespecified meaningful range:** evidence that lowers priority;
- **mixed:** endpoints, populations, tasks, or methods disagree;
- **inconclusive:** precision, construct validity, exposure range, attrition, or design cannot discriminate;
- **nonunique:** several models or routes fit the observation; and
- **retired:** adequate evidence repeatedly disfavors the proposition in its declared domain.

An unmeasured hidden body-wide mechanism cannot be the default explanation for every null.

## Track 2 — measurement and validation

### One signal supports several different claims

The measurement track must keep at least these questions separate:

1. Can the signal be acquired under a declared protocol?
2. How much disagreement or error occurs within and between observations?
3. Does the signal represent the intended construct for the intended use?
4. Does it distinguish relevant states or change when the construct changes?
5. Is the change interpretable relative to error and a meaningful reference?
6. Does using the measure improve a research, educational, clinical, or system decision?
7. What burden, reactivity, exclusion, privacy, or surveillance cost does measurement create?

The COSMIN consensus taxonomy separates reliability, measurement error, validity, and responsiveness and treats interpretability and feasibility as additional concerns.[^mokkink2010] GRRAS distinguishes reliability from agreement and requires transparent reporting of design, sample, raters, measurement process, and estimates.[^kottner2011] The FDA–NIH BEST taxonomy distinguishes biomarkers from clinical outcome assessments and makes context of use central.[^best2025] These sources were developed for particular classes of health measures. Their distinctions transfer; their thresholds and regulatory meanings do not automatically apply to every biomechanical sensor, image, model output, or composite.

### Minimum measurement record

| Field | Required content | Failure prevented |
|---|---|---|
| Construct | quantity or latent object, unit, direction, boundary, scale, phase, and time | naming a device output as “load routing” without definition |
| Intended use | description, group comparison, state tracking, mechanism test, prognosis, diagnosis, decision, or outcome | one validation result migrating to another use |
| Acquisition | sensor or observer, placement, calibration, sampling, preprocessing, missingness | precision implied by a digital output |
| Reliability and agreement | source of variation, repeated conditions, estimate, interval, error | repeatability confused with truth |
| Validity evidence | content, structural, convergent, discriminant, criterion, known-groups, or predictive evidence as appropriate | a familiar-looking signal treated as construct proof |
| Responsiveness | expected change, comparator, time, and error-aware interpretation | any change treated as meaningful |
| Reference | within-person baseline, task, population, external criterion, or decision threshold | hidden norm and postural moralization |
| Generalizability | bodies, devices, settings, assistance, disability, tasks, operators | narrow sample becoming universal |
| Burden and reactivity | time, cost, fatigue, attention, privacy, altered behavior | measurement assumed neutral |
| Decision consequence | action, comparator, benefit, harm, and uncertainty | a valid measure becoming a useful clinical tool by implication |

No single “whole-body efficiency” or “misrouting” score should enter Book I. Book II may develop candidate measures only after defining the state, domain, generalized modes, transformation rules, and disfavoring evidence.

## Track 3 — education as an independently tested intervention

### Educational success is outcome-specific

Chapter 28 owns the substantive movement-literacy and prevention program. Chapter 29 supplies the development rule: state what is taught, to whom, for what immediate outcome, compared with what, and with what possible burden.

WHO treats health literacy as a relation between personal knowledge and competencies and the organizational structures and resources that enable people to access, understand, appraise, and use information.[^whohealthliteracy2025] That framing supports shared responsibility rather than locating failure in the learner. Cook, Bordage, and Schmidt distinguish descriptive, justification, and clarification purposes in medical-education research; a study can describe what happened, compare whether an intervention worked, or investigate how and why.[^cook2008] Neither source proves that Book I's vocabulary improves learning.

Candidate direct outcomes include:

- accurate separation of task, configuration, external demand, internal distribution, local exposure, response, symptom, function, and diagnosis;
- correct use of relational hard/soft roles without assigning tissues permanent classes;
- ability to identify when a multiscale route lacks a carrier;
- quality of load-history questions;
- uncertainty calibration and willingness to stop a mechanical account;
- interdisciplinary description of a person–task–environment problem; and
- avoidance of posture moralization, fear, blame, or one-correction reasoning.

Satisfaction, recall, comprehension, reasoning, communication, behavior, clinical outcome, and system outcome are distinct endpoints. A memorable phrase can be inaccurate. A correct answer can fail to change practice. A behavior change can have no health benefit. A curriculum can improve one audience's reasoning while increasing burden or fear in another.

Required adverse outcomes include pain vigilance, fear, shame, body surveillance, professional overconfidence, jargon burden, loss of existing disciplinary nuance, exclusion of disabled or assisted movement, and redistribution of responsibility from institutions to individuals. An educational null changes the language, format, audience, or claimed outcome; it does not decide the underlying mechanics.

## Track 4 — assessment and therapeutic development

### A candidate practice remains a development object

The archive preserves observation methods, manual approaches, movement and strength work, coordination training, yoga, Pilates, breath practices, bandhas, Tai Chi, ergonomic changes, and other applications. Chapter 29 should preserve this inventory without making any item ready for use.

The updated MRC framework divides complex-intervention work into iterative development or identification, feasibility, evaluation, and implementation phases. The NIH Stage Model similarly distinguishes intervention generation and refinement, controlled efficacy work, effectiveness research, and implementation-related work while allowing movement backward and sideways. TIDieR requires a reproducible account of why, what, who provided, how, where, when and how much, tailoring, modification, and fidelity.[^hoffmann2014] SPIRIT and CONSORT require prospective and transparent specification and reporting of trial questions, methods, outcomes, analyses, and deviations.[^spirit2025][^consort2025]

For each candidate assessment or intervention, the record must name:

- intended population and reason for selection;
- target task, impairment, symptom, function, participation, or valued outcome;
- proposed mechanism and its independently measured mediator;
- comparator, including existing practice or a simpler intervention;
- provider, training, professional scope, and fidelity;
- materials, dose, progression, tailoring, and co-interventions;
- feasibility, acceptability, safety, and stop rules;
- primary and secondary benefits and harms;
- timeframe, adherence, exposure, and follow-up;
- disability, access, cost, language, cultural, and equity implications; and
- what result would revise or retire the candidate.

CONSORT Harms 2022 requires harms to be planned, collected, analyzed, and reported alongside benefits, including how harms were elicited and how recurrent or serious events were handled.[^junqueira2023] A report of no observed harm is interpretable only in relation to ascertainment, sample, duration, and precision.

### Benefit does not prove the proposed mechanism

MRC process-evaluation guidance separates implementation, mechanisms of impact, and context.[^moore2015] Imai, Keele, and Tingley show that causal mediation requires explicit definitions and identification assumptions rather than a simple before-and-after association between mediator and outcome.[^imai2010] A practice can improve comfort or function through general activity, expectation, attention, therapeutic alliance, skill, exposure change, or another route. If benefit is real and the proposed mechanical mediator does not change, the benefit remains and the mechanism claim weakens.

Conversely, a laboratory manipulation that changes a mechanical intermediate does not establish patient benefit. Therapeutic development must measure the outcome it claims.

### Efficacy and effectiveness remain purpose-dependent

PRECIS-2 asks trial designers to align eligibility, recruitment, setting, organization, delivery, adherence, follow-up, primary outcome, and analysis with an explanatory or pragmatic purpose.[^loudon2015] A tightly controlled study may answer whether an intervention can produce a change under selected conditions; it may not answer whether it helps in ordinary care. A pragmatic result may estimate real-setting performance without isolating a mechanism. Neither orientation is inherently superior; the design must fit the question.

Book I supplies no individualized protocol, test, corrective cue, exercise, breath instruction, or treatment-selection rule.

## Track 5 — implementation and interdisciplinary care

### Delivery is a different empirical object

Proctor and colleagues distinguish eight implementation outcomes—acceptability, adoption, appropriateness, feasibility, fidelity, implementation cost, penetration, and sustainability—from service and clinical outcomes.[^proctor2011] RE-AIM separates reach, effectiveness, adoption, implementation, and maintenance and emphasizes representativeness at both participant and setting levels.[^glasgow1999] CFIR organizes determinants across intervention, outer setting, inner setting, individuals, and process.[^damschroder2009] These are complementary lenses, not interchangeable scores.

The implementation track begins only with a sufficiently mature object. That object may be an educational module, measurement procedure, documentation element, environmental design, or therapeutic intervention. The implementation question names the setting and strategy separately from the object being implemented.

Minimum outcomes include:

| Domain | Question |
|---|---|
| Reach | Who is offered, excluded, reached, and retained? |
| Adoption | Which clinicians, organizations, schools, workplaces, or communities take it up? |
| Appropriateness | Do stakeholders judge fit for the problem and setting, and against what alternative? |
| Feasibility | Can it be delivered with available time, skills, infrastructure, and resources? |
| Fidelity and adaptation | What core function is preserved, what form changes, and who decides? |
| Cost | What implementation and opportunity costs fall on patients, workers, professionals, and systems? |
| Equity | How do access, burden, benefit, harm, and decision power vary across groups and settings? |
| Maintenance | Does the object and its benefit persist, decay, adapt, or displace something else? |
| Unintended effects | Does delivery increase surveillance, stigma, workload, low-value care, or exclusion? |

Curran's hybrid typology permits effectiveness and implementation aims to be studied together, but it requires both to be specified a priori. Successful delivery does not establish effectiveness. Poor delivery does not automatically refute the intervention's mechanism. A good outcome under poor measured fidelity may mean the fidelity construct, the intervention theory, the outcome, or the implementation account needs revision.

### Equity is not a late subgroup check

The Health Equity Implementation Framework places recipients, providers, the clinical encounter, inner and outer context, and societal influence inside implementation analysis.[^woodward2019] Chapter 29 should therefore ask who defined the problem, who designed the object, whose movement or body is treated as normative, who bears data and behavior burdens, who can access alternatives, and who receives benefit or harm.

Implementation in primary care, rehabilitation, specialty medicine, occupational health, sport, schools, workplaces, and community settings remains separate. A result in one setting does not authorize another.

## Track 6 — health-system development

### Biomechanical medicine as an incremental institutional program

The project's institutional ambition should remain direct: medicine may eventually benefit from a more coherent account of demand, routing, exposure, capacity, recovery, and person–task–environment relations across disciplines. The ambition is not limited permanently to a small adjunct. Its present development posture is additive and comparative because system change must show what it adds, replaces, duplicates, or worsens.

The GRADE Evidence-to-Decision framework for health-system and public-health decisions includes effects, resources, equity, acceptability, feasibility, and implementation considerations and makes the basis of judgments explicit.[^moberg2018] WHO defines health-technology assessment as a systematic, multidisciplinary evaluation of direct and indirect consequences and describes clinical, economic, ethical, social, and health-system implications as relevant to policy decisions.[^whohta] These frameworks support a transparent system question; they do not determine what society should value or whether this project is ready.

Possible milestones include:

1. a shared vocabulary that demonstrably improves a declared reasoning or communication outcome;
2. reproducible measures qualified for research or clinical uses;
3. better histories or task descriptions that add decision-relevant information;
4. interventions with favorable benefit–harm profiles against existing alternatives;
5. equitable, feasible implementation in declared settings;
6. improved interdisciplinary coordination without duplication or scope confusion;
7. justified training, documentation, referral, reimbursement, prevention, design, or policy changes; and
8. cumulative evidence strong enough to revise broader medical models.

No single specialty, device, assessment, therapy, traditional system, or preferred-state model must own the field.

### System harms and opportunity costs

System proposals must examine:

- displacement of effective existing care;
- testing and labeling of inconsequential variation;
- overdiagnosis and overtreatment;
- financial, time, cognitive, and workforce burden;
- disability exclusion and norm enforcement;
- unequal access to assessment, redesign, and care;
- privacy, surveillance, and commercial use of movement data;
- professional boundary conflict and fragmented care;
- incentives that reward volume rather than benefit; and
- distribution of benefit, harm, decision power, and opportunity cost.

Brodersen and colleagues define overdiagnosis narrowly as a correctly identified condition that would not cause symptoms or harm in the person's lifetime, while distinguishing it from false positives, misdiagnosis, overdetection, and overtreatment.[^brodersen2018] Chapter 29 should use the exact term sparingly. Many risks in this program are better described as low-value measurement, labeling normal or inconsequential variation, unnecessary follow-up, overtreatment, or burden without demonstrated benefit.

The system track asks whether mature components improve care enough to justify their full consequences. Adoption, professional enthusiasm, or a compelling theory is not the endpoint.

## One pathway across six tracks

Chapter 24's maintained-deformation account provides a controlled demonstration:

```text
surrounding boundary condition or applied force
    -> deformation, excursion change, or lumen/space change
    -> endpoint-specific physical consequence
    -> symptom, function, or clinical interpretation
```

| Track | Declared question | Candidate immediate result | Inference still unavailable |
|---|---|---|---|
| Research | Under a named task, does a declared boundary condition maintain wall deformation or alter excursion, and which alternative causes remain? | task- and structure-specific relation, null, or revised pathway | diagnosis, symptom source, treatment, or body-wide route |
| Measurement | Can boundary condition, wall geometry, excursion, pressure, flow, or neural behavior be acquired and interpreted for one use? | qualified local measure or rejected construct | causal direction, clinical importance, or decision value |
| Education | Can learners distinguish imposed demand, wall response, lumen, flow, symptom, and diagnosis? | comprehension or reasoning effect plus harms | truth of the pathway or better patient outcome |
| Therapeutic development | Does changing the boundary condition improve a patient-important outcome, and does the proposed intermediate change? | benefit, null, harm, or mechanism-discordant result | routine use or proof of historical origin |
| Implementation | Can a mature measure or intervention be delivered with reach, fidelity, access, and acceptable burden? | setting-specific delivery result | efficacy, mechanism, or system value |
| Health-system development | Does adding the mature component improve care enough to justify training, workflow, cost, and governance? | transparent institutional decision | validation of every maintained-deformation context or the wider series hypothesis |

This demonstration must retain Chapter 24's weakest arrow. A reliable geometry measure with low symptom specificity remains a measurement result. An intervention benefit with no change in the proposed intermediate retains the benefit and weakens mediation. A mechanistic null can retire one posture-to-deformation route while leaving other maintained-constraint contexts open.

## Required positive, negative, mixed, and inconclusive routing

| Result | Immediate classification | Next action | Claim that remains unavailable |
|---|---|---|---|
| Reproducible local mechanical relation under declared conditions | positive research result | replicate, test range and alternatives, determine whether a downstream question is worth asking | clinical importance or body-wide generalization |
| Predicted redistribution absent with adequate construct, range, and precision | negative for that proposition | narrow or retire the relation; document domain | default hidden redistribution |
| Several configurations produce similar selected outcomes | nonunique result | compare costs, states, endpoints, or accept degeneracy | one preferred route from the selected outcome |
| Signal is repeatable but poorly matches intended construct | measurement discordance | relabel, constrain to description, revise, or retire | validity, diagnosis, or treatment selection |
| Measure is valid for description but adds no decision value | no-added-value result | retain for research if useful; do not deploy clinically | clinical utility |
| Educational program is liked but accuracy does not improve | educational null | revise language, audience, comparator, or retire | successful reasoning education or truth of content |
| Education improves knowledge but increases fear or monitoring | mixed/adverse educational result | redesign and weigh benefit against burden | net benefit |
| Intervention improves outcome while proposed mediator does not change | mechanism-discordant benefit | retain benefit; test alternatives; narrow mechanism | proof of project mechanism |
| Mediator changes while patient-important outcome does not | mechanistic positive, therapeutic null | do not recommend; reassess target importance | therapeutic benefit |
| Intervention has no benefit or unacceptable harm | therapeutic negative | stop, revise, or abandon for domain | use because theory remains plausible |
| Effective intervention has poor reach or fidelity | implementation failure | alter strategy or setting; preserve efficacy estimate within its domain | routine effectiveness or equitable delivery |
| High adoption produces no added value over existing care | system negative | do not integrate or displace current practice | paradigm-change claim |
| Estimate is too imprecise or construct validity is inadequate | inconclusive | specify the next discriminating study or stop | positive, negative, or equivalence conclusion |

The result category itself must be justified. “Further research is needed” is incomplete unless the unresolved uncertainty and next discriminating design are named.
