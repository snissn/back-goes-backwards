## Editorial decision

Chapter 3 can be drafted as an explicit organizing hypothesis. The evidence base is sufficient to establish why the proposal cannot be reduced to a fixed hard-tissue/soft-tissue taxonomy and to supply several bounded examples of role exchange, direction dependence, active support, pressure-mediated support, ordinary deformation, and graded transfer. It does **not** independently validate the project's hard/soft vocabulary as a universal biological law.

The chapter's native statement remains:

> Load wants to travel through the hard body so that the soft body can remain soft.

For Book I, the controlled interpretation is relational:

- a **hard role** is relatively shape-preserving, path-establishing, constraining, or structurally resolving under stated conditions; it transmits demand, but force transmission alone does not assign the role;
- a **soft role** is relatively form-receiving and shape-conforming, yielding rather than imposing the gross geometry of the selected assembly while remaining available for task-relevant deformation, glide, containment, expansion, recoil, or recovery; it may carry and transmit substantial demand; and
- the assignment is incomplete until the system, scale, direction, task, state, phase, reference, and timescale have been named.

The terminology is **motivated by** traditional Chinese medicine's hard/soft and Yin/Yang concepts. That sentence records the model's authorial provenance. It does not claim an exact traditional translation, historical continuity, scientific validation by correspondence, or the later Six-Division and channel map.

## What the model is — and is not

The model must earn a distinction from conventional mechanical terms. It should not obtain apparent novelty by renaming them.

| Conventional description | What it measures or organizes | Why it is not automatically the hard/soft role | Null branch |
|---|---|---|---|
| Material class | Tissue composition or ordinary anatomical category | A nominal soft tissue can establish a tensile path; bone can deform and accommodate; an assembly may behave differently from each constituent | If role assignments merely reproduce tissue labels, use the tissue labels |
| Stiffness / compliance | A specified demand–displacement relation at a stated operating point | A role also asks what gross relation is being preserved, which freedom must remain available, and how the relation changes across phase or direction | If those additional questions add no observation or prediction, stiffness/compliance control |
| Strength / capacity | A specified failure or performance endpoint under stated conditions | A high-capacity structure can be form-receiving; a relatively shape-preserving route can be under-capacity | Do not infer role from a strength ranking |
| Impedance | Dynamic relation among force and motion, including inertial, damping, and elastic terms | It can operationalize response to perturbation but does not by itself identify the receiver's required form-changing function | Use impedance alone when it answers the selected question |
| Stability | Bounded response or return behavior under a stated perturbation and criterion | Stability is an outcome of an assembly; either role can contribute, and excessive constraint may preserve one relation while impairing another freedom | Do not equate shape preservation with global stability |
| Load share | Fraction of a stated resultant, contact load, or stress borne by a component or phase | A form-receiving structure may carry a large share; role is not a thresholded load fraction | If load share predicts the task without role information, the role label is nonessential |
| Prestress / tensegrity | Internal stress state or structural model in which tension and compression cooperate | These may produce shape preservation, but they do not determine the project's traditional provenance or the needed receiving freedom | Use the simpler prestress or structural account when sufficient |
| Anisotropy / viscoelasticity | Direction- and time-dependent constitutive behavior | These properties help explain role exchange but do not themselves define which assembly-level job matters | No role inference without a declared system and task |

The model's proposed added value is therefore narrow but real enough to test: it asks simultaneously **what relation must remain organized** and **what neighboring freedom must remain available**. It fails as a scientific explanation if it cannot make that pair more observable, comparative, or predictive than an ordinary account of stiffness, load share, contact, activation, and deformation.

## Terminology risks and controls

| Term or phrase | Principal risk | Chapter 3 control |
|---|---|---|
| “Load wants to travel” | Anthropomorphism or an unearned optimization claim | Preserve it once as the author's compact formulation, then translate it into a candidate division of labor under declared conditions |
| “Hard body” | A fixed anatomical compartment, bone-only path, or posterior identity | Use it as shorthand for the structures and relations performing a comparatively shape-preserving job in the selected system |
| “Soft body” / “remain soft” | Weakness, passivity, low stress, zero load, or unrestricted deformation | Define the role positively as form reception; state that substantial stress, force, pressure, or deformation can be ordinary and necessary |
| “Structural resolution” | A nonstandard phrase treated as a measured variable | Name the actual preserved relation, demand–displacement behavior, contact, pressure, force, or other observable in each use |
| “Load flow” or “handoff” | A substance or force packet moving along a unique conveyor | Identify carriers, interfaces, branches, time, and boundary; retain parallel and nonsequential paths |
| “Force line” | A drawn line mistaken for a measured anatomical cable | Use Chapter 2's external, inferred-internal, and embodied senses; name carrier and quantity before making a literal mechanical claim |
| “Motivated by TCM” | Exact historical translation, scientific proof by correspondence, or validation of later channel maps | Treat as disclosed authorial provenance; assign historical, textual, and correspondence claims to their own evidence and Book III |
| “Should” or “better” | Moralized anatomy or a universal preferred route | Attach a declared endpoint, comparator, task, capacity, and null; otherwise keep the statement explicitly hypothetical |

## Bounded mechanical and anatomical examples

### 1. The recurring object transfer — primary constructed example

Use `BGB-X-0001` only as a neutral event. Select one short phase and one system boundary. External contact, skeletal geometry, joint contact, active muscle, tendon, pressure, and deformable interfaces may contribute simultaneously. The teaching job is to ask which relation is comparatively shape-preserving and which structure must remain free to conform or move—not to prescribe a lifting technique or declare one internal route.

**Required null:** several internal organizations may satisfy the same external task. A clean-looking role diagram does not establish route dominance.

### 2. Tendon — high tensile demand with direction-dependent form reception

Tendon is anatomically soft tissue but can be a major tensile carrier. Its behavior is nonlinear, anisotropic, multiaxial, and region dependent. Wrap-around tendons can experience longitudinal tension together with transverse compression, and tendon fascicles or interfaces can permit sliding while the tissue transmits force. Tendon therefore blocks both “soft means unloaded” and “one tissue has one role.”

**Permitted Chapter 3 use:** along a declared tensile axis, tendon may participate in a comparatively shape-preserving path; at a bending or sliding interface, it may also receive form and change relation to its surroundings.

Interfascicular sliding is supported separately by equine comparative experiments, not by the wrap-around-tendon study. Thorpe and colleagues found greater sliding at the fascicular interface in the tested energy-storing tendon than in the tested positional tendon.[^thorpe2012]

**Limit:** neither experiment establishes ideal tendon strain, a generic human-tendon response, a whole-body chain, pathology, or treatment.

### 3. Active muscle — state-dependent structural contribution

Muscle can lengthen while active, generate force, and contribute to an assembly's apparent stiffness through activation and coactivation. The exact relation to stability is task- and model-dependent. This makes muscle a useful role-switch example without calling all contraction “hard.”

**Permitted Chapter 3 use:** compare the same joint assembly under two declared activation states and ask whether active force changes its demand–displacement relation or preserves a selected relation.

**Counterexample:** more activation or coactivation is not automatically better; it can change cost, movement, and other freedoms, and apparent stiffness is not a complete stability metric.

### 4. Ligament — recruitment and task-specific restraint

Ligaments have nonlinear, anisotropic, time-dependent behavior. Collagen recruitment changes the force–elongation relation, and ligament contribution to joint restraint varies by structure, direction, and joint configuration. A ligament may contribute little to one selected motion and substantially to another.

**Permitted Chapter 3 use:** a selected ligament or ligamentous assembly can move from comparatively slack or accommodating behavior toward a restraint role as configuration and demand change.

**Limit:** the generic example cannot stand in for every ligament, joint, or clinical instability.

### 5. Cartilage — deformation, contact, and pressure-mediated load sharing

Articular cartilage is deformable and fluid-saturated. In bounded experimental models, interstitial fluid pressurization can carry a large fraction of a contact load while solid-matrix deformation and tensile restraint evolve with time. This is a strong counterexample to both “soft means unimportant” and “support belongs to one solid carrier.”

**Permitted Chapter 3 use:** cartilage may receive local contact form while a fluid–solid assembly shares load and helps preserve a larger joint relation.

**Limit:** confined or unconfined compression findings do not become a universal account of every joint, task, or disease.

### 6. Pressure-supported structure — no bone required

Hydrostatic skeletons use internal pressure, an approximately volume-conserving fluid, and tensioned walls or muscle to support and transmit force. This provides a clean biological counterexample to any equation of hard role with bone or rigidity.

**Permitted Chapter 3 use:** a compliant, pressure-bearing assembly can perform a comparatively shape-preserving job.

**Limit:** an invertebrate hydrostatic skeleton is an existence proof about mechanical organization, not evidence that a particular human cavity performs the same job in the same way.

### 7. Enthesis — a graded interface rather than an abrupt handoff

Many tendon and ligament attachments—especially fibrocartilaginous entheses—use graded, region-specific structures that distribute stress and accommodate a transition between mechanically dissimilar tissues. Enthesis anatomy supports the reality of transfer into bone while correcting the archive's picture of a clean serial handoff.

**Permitted Chapter 3 use:** “soft-to-hard” can include a graded, distributed interface and neighboring tissues rather than a force packet passing across a sharp boundary.

**Limit:** enthesis anatomy does not prove the full fascia/muscle → tendon/ligament → bone/joint sequence or any body-wide route.

### 8. Fascia and myofascial continuity — boundary, not foundation

Anatomical continuities and some adjacent intermuscular tension transfer have empirical support, but functional significance, magnitude, task dependence, anatomical variation, and body-wide dominance remain unsettled. Fascia may be included as one candidate carrier or interface when the selected anatomy and quantity are named.

**Limit:** Chapter 3 must not use morphological continuity as proof of a single whole-body force chain, a TCM channel, or a preferred posterior route.

## Observability and discrimination program

The chapter should state the measurement problem without pretending that one scalar identifies a role.

### Minimum declaration

For every proposed assignment, record:

1. the selected system and boundary;
2. the gross relation proposed to be preserved;
3. the form-receiving structure's required freedom or function;
4. the imposed or endogenous demand and its direction;
5. task, phase, activation state, reference, and timescale;
6. candidate carriers and parallel paths;
7. the measured, calculated, inferred, modeled, hypothesized, or embodied status of each quantity; and
8. the conventional description against which the role model is being compared.

### Candidate observables

| Question | Candidate observables | Necessary qualification |
|---|---|---|
| Is a relation comparatively shape-preserving? | relative displacement, rotation, shape retention, tangent structural stiffness, contact migration, load share, active force, pressure, recovery after perturbation | Direction, operating range, reference, rate, boundary conditions, and error |
| Is a structure comparatively form-receiving? | local deformation, cross-sectional change, bending, excursion, relative glide, pressure–volume response, reversible accommodation | The needed freedom and why it matters to the declared task |
| Did roles exchange? | within-system change across direction, phase, activation, pressure, support, or task | Same or explicitly transformed coordinate frame; matched measurement method |
| Did the proposed division improve function? | task error, energetic measure, range, stability criterion, tissue-specific response, or another declared endpoint | A separately justified endpoint; no generic “efficiency” or “health” score |

### Discriminating tests

1. **Direction test:** the same structure is assigned different roles in longitudinal tension and transverse compression or bending; the model predicts a different observable in each direction before results are known.
2. **State test:** activation, pressure, or support is changed while geometry and task are controlled; the proposed shape-preserving relation changes in the predicted direction.
3. **Phase test:** the model predicts when a carrier becomes engaged or when form reception is required, rather than labeling the full movement afterward.
4. **Assembly test:** several individually deformable elements form a comparatively shape-preserving whole; component stiffness alone does not exhaust the explanation.
5. **Receiver-freedom test:** preserving the candidate route predicts retention of a separately measured glide, expansion, course change, or deformation without assuming that more is always better.
6. **Conventional-model comparison:** a stiffness, impedance, contact, activation, or load-sharing model is fit without the role vocabulary. If it predicts and explains the selected outcome equally well, the hard/soft terms remain heuristic rather than a distinct mechanical explanation.

### Falsifiers and anti-retrofit rules

The model is weakened for a selected system when:

- role assignments cannot be made before the outcome is known;
- the proposed hard carrier does not measurably participate in the selected demand;
- the proposed soft receiver does not require or exhibit the named freedom;
- roles remain unchanged when the model predicts a direction, phase, activation, pressure, or support switch;
- ordinary conventional variables explain the observations with equal clarity and fewer assumptions; or
- counterexamples are protected by redefining every loaded tissue as hard and every yielding tissue as soft after the fact.

## Candidate soft-to-hard handoff

The archive's tissue sequence should survive as a visible, first-order hypothesis:

```text
active or compliant gathering / actuation / accommodation
    -> tensile transfer or restraint
    -> skeletal contact and joint-geometry resolution
```

A bounded anatomical version may use muscle–tendon–bone and the enthesis. Ligament belongs only where the selected joint mechanics actually include it. Fascia belongs only where a named attachment, interface, or measured transmission is relevant. Pressure, parallel active structures, contact, and nonsequential routes remain available alternatives.

The first draft may call this a recurring pattern worth investigating. It may not call it the body's universal design, a mandatory serial route, or the established reason that organs, nerves, vessels, or health are protected.

## Traditional provenance boundary

The current source base establishes **authorial provenance**, not historical proof:

- the author reports that the vocabulary and functional polarity are motivated by TCM hard/soft and Yin/Yang concepts;
- the intended Yin sense includes form reception, yielding, and an internal connective or force-line intuition without passivity;
- Book I gives those motivations a transparent mechanical translation; and
- the translation stands or falls mechanically on its definitions, observables, alternatives, and results.

Before publication, a traditional-language reviewer must determine whether any historical or textual statement beyond this provenance disclosure is supportable. Until then, Chapter 3 should not claim that classical sources define hard and soft exactly this way, that Yin and Yang map one-to-one to the two roles, or that a mechanical result verifies TCM. The Six Divisions, channels, signed directions, gross limb projection, and exact correspondence program remain Book III work.

## First-draft authorization

The controlled reader draft may:

- state the native formulation prominently;
- define both roles positively and comparatively;
- say the distinction is motivated by TCM hard/soft and Yin/Yang concepts without claiming exact identity;
- use the object transfer, tendon, active muscle, ligament, cartilage, pressure-supported structure, and enthesis as bounded demonstrations;
- retain the candidate fascia/muscle → tendon/ligament → bone/joint pattern as a hypothesis nested inside the broader role model;
- state that substantial soft-tissue loading and deformation can be ordinary and useful;
- state that bone deforms and that pressure or active structures can contribute to shape preservation;
- show role exchange with direction, phase, activation, pressure, task, scale, state, and time; and
- include the strongest null: if ordinary variables do the same work, the hard/soft distinction remains a heuristic and provenance-bearing synthesis rather than a distinct scientific explanation.

The draft may **not** state that:

- hard equals bone, posterior, exterior, healthy, rigid, or Yang;
- soft equals anatomical soft tissue, anterior, interior, weak, passive, unloaded, harmful, or Yin;
- forces universally travel away from soft tissues or sequentially through fixed tissue classes;
- the body was “engineered” or “designed” to use one route;
- fascia constitutes a proven body-wide force chain;
- soft-to-hard routing prevents disease, protects organ function, diagnoses a mechanical failure, or prescribes a correction;
- posterior support, breath, all-Yang organization, or a TCM correspondence validates the general polarity; or
- traditional provenance supplies empirical evidence for the mechanical model.
