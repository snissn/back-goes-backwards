## Editorial decision

Chapter 26 should not solve an invented patient. It should expose the reasoning required before an attractive chronic mechanical story deserves priority.

The lead is a visibly hypothetical office-work/nonspecific-low-back-pain chronology. Its job is to preserve the archive's intended sequence—

> silent compensation → changed baseline → repeated demand relative to changing capacity and recovery → delayed or asymmetric consequence → findings that do not settle mechanism

—without presenting that sequence as observed, typical, or established. Population studies can support the plausibility of individual components. They do not establish the complete sequence, its frequency, its direction in one person, or a preferred anatomical route.

The packet therefore makes five decisions:

1. **Chronology precedes causal assignment.** The construction is represented in parallel rows for tasks, exposure, recovery, symptoms, function, context, findings, candidate mechanics, and missing information.
2. **The mechanical route is one branch.** Pain-led motor change, a shared contextual cause, useful adaptation, low mechanical priority, and unresolved plurality remain equally visible.
3. **Office work is context, not exposure.** Exposure requires a task, duration, frequency, force, support, variation, vibration, pace, recovery opportunity, and measurement status as applicable.
4. **No regional route is supplied.** The vignette has no measured carrier from a local workaround to a regional receiver. A published gait experiment is retained only as a methodological exemplar of how such a bridge could be operationalized.
5. **Ambiguous findings cut both ways.** Common findings in asymptomatic people and greater prevalence of selected findings in symptomatic groups can coexist. Neither an abnormal scan nor an inconclusive scan completes the causal history.

The chapter can succeed with a substantial, mixed, reverse-causal, adaptive, low-priority, or unresolved mechanical interpretation. A null result is not an editorial failure.

## Hypothetical provenance grammar

### Reader-visible evidence states

Every vignette element must be assigned one of the following states. These labels govern authority, not merely typography.

| Label | Meaning | Permitted use | Prohibited inference |
|---|---|---|---|
| **STIP-HYP** | A detail intentionally invented to construct the reasoning problem | Establish the hypothetical setting or event order | That the detail occurred in a patient, is prevalent, or is source-derived |
| **REP-HYP** | What the imagined person is stipulated to report | Distinguish report from direct observation | That the report is an objective exposure, movement, lesion, or causal history |
| **MEAS-HYP** | A fictional measurement with a named method, deliberately stipulated for the exercise | Test how a defined result would change competing interpretations | That the value was collected from a real person or validated for diagnosis |
| **POP** | A possibility or distribution supported in an external population | Establish that a component has empirical precedent | That the hypothetical person shares the result or that the full chronology is common |
| **REC** | A retrospective candidate reconstruction assembled from dated and undated elements | Compare temporal orders and identify missing records | That coherence converts the reconstruction into observation |
| **MOD** | A modeled exposure, force, moment, or state under explicit assumptions | Make assumptions and transfer limits inspectable | That the model directly measured tissue stress, injury, or individual cause |
| **PROJ-HYP** | A stronger hypothesis belonging to this project or later series | Preserve and define a future test | That Book I has established it |
| **ALT** | A competing chronology or explanation | Prevent one-route completion | That every alternative has equal prior probability |
| **UNK** | Missing, invalidly inferable, or deliberately unfilled information | Keep gaps visible | That absence supports the preferred route |

**MEAS-HYP is not patient evidence.** This packet populates no fictional numerical measurement. If reader drafting later uses a stipulated measurement, the method, quantity, uncertainty, and invented status must appear at first use and in the figure legend.

### Source authority does not cascade

The chapter must preserve this rule:

~~~text
population evidence for component x
    does not establish
component x in the hypothetical
    and does not establish
components y and z or the arrows among them
~~~

A study of immediate pain during prolonged sitting cannot establish years of cumulative office exposure. A prospective work-load association cannot establish an internal lumbar route. An experimental pain study can establish pain-before-movement-change under its protocol, not that every chronic movement pattern followed pain. Imaging prevalence cannot identify the pain generator or the history that produced a finding.

### Neutral timeline before interpretation

The teaching timeline should use intervals rather than a falsely precise date sequence. Empty cells stay unknown.

| Exposure dimensions | UNK | Duration, repetition, variation, support, pace, vibration, force, and recovery not yet measured | UNK | UNK | Define task-level measures |

| Sleep, health, mood, expectation, and social context | UNK | UNK | UNK | UNK | Measure rather than append as disclaimer |
| Findings and care | UNK | UNK | UNK | STIP-HYP: routine findings do not identify one sufficient cause | Record exact methods and interpretations |

No arrow is drawn across an unknown interval. “Initially preserved function” does not mean unchanged internal distribution, absence of effort, useful adaptation, or hidden cost. Each is a separate proposition.

### Demand and capacity remain multidimensional

The chapter must not draw one invented demand curve crossing one invented capacity curve. Instead it should track declared quantities or qualitative domains separately:

- task duration, uninterrupted duration, repetition, pace, peak demand, cumulative exposure, and variation;
- external support, task geometry, force, vibration, contact, movement, and recovery opportunity;
- tissue-specific or task-specific capacity where a valid measure exists;
- sleep opportunity, recent illness, medication, conditioning, prior episodes, and other state variables;
- symptoms, function, confidence, perceived effort, participation, and recovery; and
- measurement method, time of day, recall interval, uncertainty, and missingness.

Adaptation, stable tolerance, useful protection, fluctuation, recovery, increasing sensitivity, changed exposure, and adverse accumulation are parallel branches. No direction is built into the diagram.

## Evidence architecture

### Chronic and nonspecific are scope terms, not mechanical explanations

The WHO guideline addresses chronic primary low back pain in adult primary and community care and situates care in a person-centered, biopsychosocial context.[^who2023] Its category is not identical to every use of “nonspecific low-back pain,” and the guideline is not evidence for the hypothetical's cause or an authorization to provide treatment advice here.

For this packet, *nonspecific* means only that the construction supplies no single finding or recognized disease process sufficient to explain the presentation. It does not mean no structure matters, no diagnosis could later be made, or an unmeasured mechanical route is therefore likely.

### Exposure must be reconstructed at task and metric level

NIOSH occupational guidance separates force, repetition, posture, intensity, frequency, duration, vibration, work organization, and task or environment features.[^niosh2024] That supports the chapter's measurement grammar. It does not make the agency's hazard-identification framework an individual causal model.

The occupational source set gives positive but bounded examples. Norman and colleagues compared 104 automotive-industry cases with 130 controls using observation, physical measurements, and modeled peak and cumulative lumbar exposures; retained relations depended on the selected exposure quantity.[^norman1998] Coenen and colleagues prospectively analyzed 1,086 workers over three years after assigning sampled task observations and modeled cumulative L5–S1 moments to task groups; the highest cumulative category had greater odds of low-back pain than the lowest, but exposure sampling, extrapolation, baseline history, attrition, and model assumptions limit individual reconstruction.[^coenen2013] In 537 professional drivers followed for two years, Bovenzi found that vibration-dose and duration metrics related differently to low-back-pain outcomes and that associations were clearer for high pain and disability than for a binary twelve-month outcome.[^bovenzi2009]

These studies do not establish the lead office history. They show why “load,” “sitting,” and “years at a desk” are inadequate exposure variables.

Exposure recall also has method-specific limits. In eighteen workers performing three seedling-processing jobs, self-report was the least precise of self-report, video, and direct measures and tended to overestimate selected upper-extremity exposures; the best method depended on the quantity.[^spielholz2001] In d'Errico and colleagues' occupational cohort, one-year reproducibility among 507 same-job workers was fair to good for several self-reported ergonomic factors, while the five- to six-year comparisons among 163 and 162 matched workers were poor for most items and composites.[^derrico2007] The appropriate conclusion is not that history is useless. It is that recall interval, job change, variable, corroborating record, and measurement uncertainty must remain visible.

### Sitting and posture require positive and null evidence together

Roffey and colleagues screened 2,766 citations and included twenty-four occupational-sitting studies, five rated high quality. They found strong, consistent evidence against an independent association in the worker populations studied and moderate evidence against a dose-response relation.[^roffey2010] Swain and colleagues' umbrella review screened 4,285 publications and included forty-one reviews; positive and null associations coexisted, prospective evidence was inconsistent, and the reviews did not establish causal consensus for spinal posture or physical exposure and low-back pain.[^swain2020]

A different timescale gives a different result. De Carvalho and colleagues included ten studies that objectively matched sitting duration to immediate pain ratings; nine reported an immediate increase, and six reported clinically relevant pain levels among 330 participants. The review explicitly could not infer clinical low-back-pain episodes.[^decarvalho2020]

The controlled synthesis is:

~~~text
immediate symptom modulation during a sitting exposure
    can coexist with
failure to establish occupational sitting as an independent cause of clinical episodes
~~~

This distinction allows the chapter to take position- or task-related symptom change seriously without converting modulation into origin, diagnosis, posture morality, or an ergonomic prescription.

### Fluctuation and recurrence defeat the single-snapshot story

Kongsted and colleagues collected weekly pain intensity and pain-day reports for one year from 1,082 primary-care patients. Twelve latent-class models produced five to twelve subgroups, with several recurring patterns but model-dependent class counts.[^kongsted2015] The result supports heterogeneous and fluctuating courses, not biological subtypes or mechanisms.

Nim and colleagues obtained weekly ratings for fifty-two weeks from 719 chiropractic patients and then asked participants to select one of eight retrospective visual trajectories. The selected trajectory reflected intensity better than episodic, fluctuating, or persistent pattern, and considerable prospective variation remained within each retrospective class.[^nim2023] A coherent remembered history therefore cannot substitute for prospective measurement.

Da Silva and colleagues enrolled 250 people who had recovered from a recent episode and followed them monthly for twelve months. Recurrence depended on the outcome definition: 69% had an episode, 40% had activity-limiting recurrence, and 41% sought care; median time to any recurrence was 139 days.[^dasilva2019] These population rates establish that recovery and recurrence can coexist. They do not establish why a given person recurred or that the reported prognostic associations are mechanisms.

### Reverse causation is an evidenced branch

Three experiments show that pain or pain-related state can precede changed movement or recruitment.

Devecchi and colleagues induced movement-contingent lumbar pain in thirty healthy adults during repeated lifting. Participants reduced movement in the pain-provoking direction, and the flexion-session change persisted after pain resolution.[^devecchi2025] Trost and colleagues induced trunk-extensor soreness in fifty-one initially pain-free adults; pain-related fear predicted reduced lumbar flexion during a selected reaching condition after soreness but not at baseline.[^trost2012] Tucker and colleagues studied fourteen healthy adults during experimentally induced deep-tissue pain; matched low-force output was maintained while discharge of previously active motor units fell and the recruited motor-unit population changed.[^tucker2009]

These sources establish three controls:

1. movement change can follow pain;
2. expectation or fear can condition movement change after a painful state; and
3. output can remain stable while internal recruitment changes.

They do not establish the origin of chronic low-back pain, a chronic compensation history, long-term energetic or tissue cost, or the desirability of changing the observed strategy. Tucker is the packet's limited successful-output analogue, not proof that the hypothetical contains a beneficial or cost-free compensation.

### Sleep and work context belong inside the causal graph

Alsaadi and colleagues used seven days of diaries in eighty people with low-back pain, with objective sleep monitoring in a subgroup of fifty. Poorer sleep measures preceded higher next-day pain, and higher daytime pain preceded poorer subsequent sleep on measures that varied by assessment method.[^alsaadi2014] Gerhart and colleagues collected electronic diaries five times daily for fourteen days from 105 people with chronic low-back pain. Poorer prior-night sleep predicted next-day pain, function, and catastrophizing, especially earlier in the day; most reverse lagged paths were not significant in that study.[^gerhart2017]

Work evidence is likewise mixed. In cross-sectional 2015 US survey data from 17,464 employed adults, Shockey and colleagues reported adjusted prevalence relations for several psychosocial, organizational, and physical-exertion variables, while formal interactions with exertion were not statistically significant.[^shockey2024] Jahn and colleagues' 2024 review included twenty cohort or case-control articles, only one at low risk of bias; pooled odds ratios across six occupational psychosocial exposures ranged from 0.8 to 1.1, and the certainty was rated very low.[^jahn2024]

The chapter should not use “stress,” “poor sleep,” or “work control” as generic explanatory tokens. It should specify measure and temporal role. Any can be an antecedent, modifier, consequence, shared determinant, or irrelevant variable for the endpoint and interval being discussed.

### Imaging ambiguity does not point in one direction

Brinjikji and colleagues' review of thirty-three studies included 3,110 asymptomatic people and found that several degenerative imaging features were common and rose with age.[^brinjikji-asymptomatic2015] A separate meta-analysis by Brinjikji and colleagues included fourteen case-control studies and 3,097 adults aged fifty or younger. Several findings were more prevalent in symptomatic groups, while other comparisons were not statistically significant or were very imprecise.[^brinjikji-symptomatic2015]

Both results belong in the chapter:

~~~text
finding present
    does not by itself identify symptom source, route, or chronology

finding absent or nonspecific
    does not establish an invisible route

group association
    does not assign an individual cause
~~~

The hypothetical should not invent a scan result. It should state only that available routine findings have not supplied one sufficient explanation, then ask what exact findings and interpretations would be needed to alter the competing models.

### Material state and time of measurement can matter without proving damage

O'Connell and colleagues applied specified axial creep and unloaded-recovery protocols to cadaveric human lumbar discs. Loading and recovery followed different time courses, with recovery several times slower under the protocol.[^oconnell2011] Hamaguchi and colleagues imaged seventeen sedentary workers in the morning and evening of the same day and observed changes in selected composition-related MRI indices.[^hamaguchi2023]

These sources support time-dependent material state and time-of-measurement sensitivity. They do not establish ordinary sitting as damaging, supply a whole-person recovery interval, identify pain, or validate the hypothetical's cumulative-demand route.

### Hard and soft remain relational roles

The archive's hard/soft distinction is **motivated by** TCM hard/soft and Yin/Yang concepts without being definitionally identical to them. In Book I, a **shape-preserving role** maintains a boundary, span, contact, alignment, or relation against a declared demand. A **form-receiving role** yields, conforms, accommodates, contains, slides, cushions, or permits transfer under that demand.

These are task-relative mechanical roles, not permanent tissue classes. Bone, ligament, muscle, tendon, cartilage, fluid, fascia, external support, and contact surfaces can each participate differently depending on task, direction, scale, and state. The Chapter 26 question is whether sustained task preservation requires a measurable change in effort, deformation, contact, pressure, motion, moment, or recovery. It is not whether a “soft tissue” is inherently weak or whether the vignette proves a TCM correspondence.

## Regional redistribution decision

No local-to-regional route is supported for the hypothetical. The required figure should show the missing bridge rather than draw one.

Nakatsuji and colleagues provide a methodological exemplar, not the vignette route. Seventeen healthy adult men walked with unrestricted right-knee extension and with experimentally imposed 15-degree and 30-degree restrictions. Measured motion and ground-reaction data informed a musculoskeletal model of lumbar extension moment, muscle force, and L4–L5 joint-reaction force; selected modeled quantities increased with greater restriction.[^nakatsuji2022]

This study has the structure the archive's generic kinetic-chain language lacked:

| Required element | Nakatsuji exemplar | Chapter 26 hypothetical |
|---|---|---|
| Local manipulation or state | Experimentally restricted right-knee extension | Unspecified candidate task-preserving change |
| Named task | Ten-metre comfortable walking | Office work is too broad; task not yet selected |
| Boundary conditions | Brace-defined restriction conditions | Unknown |
| Carrier or intermediate | Measured gait kinematics and ground reaction forces used by a musculoskeletal model | Unknown |
| Regional receiver | Lumbar model at L4–L5 and named lumbar muscles | No receiver selected |
| Quantity | Modeled moment, muscle force, and joint-reaction force | No quantity measured |
| Timescale | Immediate experimental trials | Proposed chronic history, unmeasured |
| Stopping condition | No condition difference or model sensitivity can stop the proposed link | Any missing carrier, null task difference, reverse timing, or failure to track the course stops the route |

Nakatsuji does not study office work, spontaneous compensation, chronic low-back-pain origin, harm, tissue strain, or delayed consequence. It cannot be imported as evidence that the hypothetical route occurred. Its sole job here is to demonstrate what an operational cross-region proposition looks like.

The older route—

~~~text
forward head or anterior pelvic tilt
    -> minimal posterior-chain participation
    -> right lumbosacral load concentration
    -> thoracolumbar-fascial or contralateral-paraspinal compensation
    -> delayed or distal symptoms
~~~

—remains a project hypothesis family, not a Book I finding. It can re-enter only through a named task, local state, carrier, physical quantity, regional receiver, time relation, measurement or model, alternatives, and a result capable of stopping the chain.

## Competing chronology program

The chapter's central comparison should be made before the hypothetical receives any discriminating measurements.

| Route | Candidate order | Evidence that would raise its priority | Evidence that would lower or stop it | Present packet verdict |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **A — compensation precedes symptoms** | demand or state change → task-preserving strategy → changed later demand → symptoms or function change | dated pre-symptom change; repeated task-specific observation; named carrier; persistence across relevant exposure; later correspondence more specific than alternatives | strategy appears only after pain; no task-specific change; no carrier; same strategy without course; course changes while proposed quantity does not | Preserved as the archive-preferred hypothesis; not demonstrated by this source set or hypothetical |
| **B — symptoms or illness produce compensation** | pain, illness, sensitivity, fear, or expectation → changed movement, effort, support, or participation | state change precedes strategy; strategy tracks pain or health state; experimental or prospective support under a comparable task | strategy is well dated before symptoms; strategy persists independently; manipulation of pain or threat does not change it | Directly supported as a possible causal direction by experimental pain evidence; not assigned to the hypothetical |
| **C — shared context shapes both** | work or life change, sleep loss, illness, caregiving, constraint, or reduced activity → symptoms and task change | common antecedent precedes both; each outcome tracks context; apparent mechanical relation weakens after context is measured | mechanical change clearly precedes context and predicts a distinct outcome | Mandatory live alternative; evidence packet does not rank it |
| **D — useful adaptation without demonstrated cost** | perturbation or pain → changed strategy → preserved task or reduced threat, with no observed adverse consequence | output or participation preserved; relevant symptom or perturbation reduced; no measured cost over declared dose and time | strategy fails the task; receiver-specific exposure or recovery worsens; adverse outcome tracks the strategy | Must remain available; experimental force maintenance is only a narrow analogue, not chronic proof |
| **E — unresolved plurality** | several orders fit incomplete or conflicting observations | missing dates, weak specificity, incompatible measures, or several equal predictions | one route gains independent temporal and mechanistic support while alternatives fail | Default when discriminating evidence is absent |
| **F — low mechanical priority** | selected mechanical variables do not explain the declared endpoint or interval | exposure or strategy does not track course; another account explains chronology and function better; mechanical measure remains unchanged when outcome changes | repeatable task-specific modulation and measured route add explanatory or predictive information | A required successful outcome of the method, not rejection of biomechanics |

### Minimum discriminating record

Before Route A can be more than an attractive reconstruction, the record needs:

1. a defined transition in symptoms, function, participation, or recovery;
2. a dated task or context change before that transition;
3. a dated candidate strategy under the relevant task;
4. a measured or defensibly modeled physical quantity changed by that strategy;
5. a named local or regional receiver if downstream consequence is proposed;
6. repeated observation across the relevant exposure and recovery window;
7. an outcome that corresponds to the proposed quantity with useful specificity;
8. measurement of pain, sleep, health, expectation, work, and other plausible common or reverse causes; and
9. a prespecified null capable of lowering or stopping the route.

The chapter should not imply that ordinary clinical history-taking can always recover this record. Its educational value is partly to show what remains unknown.

### What counts as positive, null, reverse, and adaptive evidence

| Evidence role | Packet examples | What it can do | What it cannot do |
|---|---|---|---|
| Positive exposure association | Norman, Coenen, Bovenzi | Show that selected physical metrics can associate prospectively or cross-sectionally with selected low-back outcomes | Establish this office exposure, internal route, tissue state, or individual cause |
| Immediate positive modulation | De Carvalho | Show that pain can rise during objectively timed prolonged sitting in selected studies | Establish a clinical episode, chronic origin, or cumulative damage |
| Null or heterogeneous physical-exposure evidence | Roffey, Swain | Prevent sitting or posture from becoming independently causal by default | Establish that task mechanics never modulate symptoms |
| Reverse-direction evidence | Devecchi, Trost, Tucker | Establish that pain or pain-related state can precede motor change under experiments | Establish the chronic hypothetical's direction |
| Successful-output analogue | Tucker | Show unchanged task force with changed recruitment during pain | Establish benefit, optimality, zero cost, or long-term adaptation |
| Longitudinal fluctuation and recurrence | Kongsted, Nim, da Silva | Require prospective time structure and outcome definitions | Identify the mechanism of a trajectory or recurrence |
| Contextual positive and null evidence | Alsaadi, Gerhart, Shockey, Jahn | Keep sleep and work context inside competing causal graphs | Assign a universal direction or weight |
| Finding–symptom noncorrespondence and association | Both Brinjikji reviews | Keep asymptomatic prevalence and symptomatic group differences visible together | Diagnose the hypothetical or infer a hidden route |
| Time-dependent material or imaging state | O'Connell, Hamaguchi | Show that load history and time of measurement can matter for selected disc quantities | Prove pain, injury, harmful sitting, or a recovery prescription |
