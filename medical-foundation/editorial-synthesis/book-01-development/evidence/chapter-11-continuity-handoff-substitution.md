## Editorial decision

Chapter 11 should retain the project's compact intuition without presenting an invisible preferred route as measured fact:

> Load wants to travel through the hard body so that the soft body can remain soft.

In controlled mechanical language, this means:

> Gross structural demand is generally better resolved through relations capable of preserving shape, establishing a path, and transmitting the relevant force or moment, so that form-receiving structures retain the freedom to yield, conform, glide, contain, expand, and recover as the task requires. Which structures perform either role can change with direction, phase, activation, pressure, state, scale, and time.

The hard/soft distinction is **motivated by TCM hard/soft and Yin/Yang concepts but is not definitionally identical to them**. Book I owns the mechanical polarity. Book III owns detailed traditional interpretation.

This chapter's other project-native terms need the same treatment. *Continuity* names the availability of the relations needed to carry a task through a phase, not one anatomical cable. *Leakage* names a hypothesis that a changed distribution creates an unintended or poorly matched exposure elsewhere, not any deformation of a compliant structure. *Substitution* names preservation of an endpoint through another carrier or strategy, not damage. Each term becomes informative only after the task, system, carrier, quantity, phase, exposure, capacity, and evidence state are supplied.

## What mechanics establishes—and what remains underdetermined

At a declared system boundary and in a stated inertial frame, force and moment balance constrain the net mechanical account:

```text
sum of external forces = time rate of change of system linear momentum
sum of external moments about a fixed inertial point
    = time rate of change of system angular momentum about that point
```

Those balances do not by themselves identify how muscle force, tendon tension, joint contact, ligament restraint, pressure, passive tissue force, or external assistance shared the internal demand. Inverse dynamics can calculate net joint moments from measured motion and external forces, but many internal allocations may be compatible with the same net result. Direct measurements from an instrumented knee implant showed why the distinction matters: in one participant across four gait patterns, changes in external knee load and electromyography were generally weak indicators of changes in total, medial, and lateral contact force.[^meyer2013]

The chapter therefore needs two descriptions at once:

1. **balance:** what net force or moment the selected boundary must resolve; and
2. **allocation:** which candidate relations carry, transform, oppose, store, return, or dissipate parts of that demand.

A load path is a representation of the second description. It may be a line, network, field, or set of interacting carriers. It is not made unique by drawing one arrow.

## A route needs more than one state variable

The cleanest first-order representation is a task- and phase-specific graph. Nodes can represent bodies, segments, contacts, interfaces, or regions. Edges represent candidate interactions such as contact force, tensile transfer, active force, pressure, sliding restraint, or external support. The graph is explanatory, not an anatomical claim that every edge is a discrete cable.

For each candidate relation `e` at time `t`, keep at least four questions separate:

```text
A_e(t): is the relation mechanically available for the stated task and phase?
U_e(t): to what extent does the relation participate in transferring or resolving the selected quantity?
E_e(t): what exposure history reaches the relation or a named receiver?
C_e(t): what present, outcome-specific capacity does that same relation or receiver have for that exposure?
```

These symbols are labels for records, not a validated equation or score. Availability can be partial or uncertain. Participation can be distributed rather than binary. The `E` and `C` records inherit Chapter 10's exposure and capacity meanings: they must name the same selected system or receiver, quantity and direction, outcome, and time window. The state also needs an evidence label: observed, measured, calculated, modeled, inferred, hypothesized, or embodied.

This prevents several collapses:

- **available does not mean used;**
- **used does not mean dominant;**
- **dominant does not mean preferred;**
- **loaded does not mean overloaded;**
- **deformed does not mean harmed;** and
- **successful endpoint does not mean unchanged allocation.**

## Controlled transfer-state vocabulary

| Term | Controlled use | Minimum discriminating information | What it cannot mean by itself |
|---|---|---|---|
| Continuity | The task- and phase-specific availability of successive or cooperating relations needed to receive, transform, transfer, and resolve a defined demand | task, phase, boundary, incoming demand, candidate carriers, terminal support or opposition | one unbroken tissue line, visually smooth movement, or felt continuity alone |
| Handoff | A change in carrier, mechanical role, direction, scale, contact, or phase while the task remains mechanically resolved | what changes, across which interface or interval, and which quantity is transmitted, balanced, redirected, stored, returned, dissipated, or opposed | a literal pass between isolated parts or zero deformation at the interface |
| Interruption | A candidate relation required by the proposed route is unavailable during the stated phase | evidence of lost contact, excursion, geometry, force capacity, control, or other declared requirement | overload, avoidance, injury, or complete task failure |
| Bypass | A candidate route remains plausibly available but an alternative route carries the relevant demand | evidence that the original could participate, evidence of alternate allocation, and a matched comparison | an intrinsically inferior strategy |
| Overload | An engaged relation receives an exposure that exceeds a declared present capacity criterion | exposure history, capacity criterion, outcome, state, and time window | any high force, any fatigue, or an interrupted route |
| Substitution | Another structure, contact, carrier, or strategy performs enough of a role to preserve a declared endpoint | preserved endpoint plus evidence of changed allocation | pathology, hidden damage, or loss of the original route |
| Rerouting | Distribution among candidate relations changes over a stated interval or phase | before/after or phase-resolved allocation with a stable boundary definition | one permanent whole-body state |
| Leakage | Project-native hypothesis that altered allocation creates an unintended or poorly matched exposure in another receiver or role | receiver, quantity, dose, expected freedom, capacity mismatch, alternative explanation, and disfavoring observation | fluid escape, all compliant loading, or a visual deviation from an ideal line |
| Misrouting | Proposed allocation through a route less suited to the stated task, dose, and current capacity | comparison criterion and evidence that the alternative better meets it | departure from symmetry, posteriority, or one preferred configuration |

These states are analytical distinctions, not mutually exclusive diagnoses. A route can be partly available, used at a lower level, and overloaded. An external support can create a beneficial bypass. A substitution can be chosen before any interruption. A changed strategy can improve the endpoint.

## Continuity is availability across changing roles

An ordinary task is rarely carried by one material relation from beginning to end. During a lift, carry, turn, or placement, ground contact, skeletal geometry, muscle force, tendon transmission, joint contact, pressure-mediated support, object contact, and balance reactions can change their contributions. Continuity refers to the availability of a mechanically sufficient sequence or network across those changes.

The same idea applies within a muscle–tendon unit. In vivo measurements of the human tibialis-anterior tendon showed increasing tendon force, stress, displacement, and strain as stimulated contraction increased.[^maganaris1999] During one-legged hopping, the Achilles tendon in ten participants strained substantially and returned elastic energy to the task.[^lichtwark2005] These are bounded examples of active force being transmitted through a deforming tensile structure. They do not prove a body-wide chain, but they demonstrate that handoff can include deformation, storage, and return rather than a rigid transfer from “soft” to “hard.”

Continuity can also be distributed across joints. In twenty-five adults walking at self-selected speed, hip, knee, and ankle moment contributions to a support-moment description varied across stance and covaried with one another.[^fukui2016] The study's moment correlations do not reveal a unique internal route or tell the reader which joint should be preferred. They show why a whole-task account can remain continuous while the dominant regional contributor changes.

## Handoff is a change of mechanical job

A handoff can do several different things:

- transmit tension from active muscle through tendon to bone;
- redirect a force through a joint contact and change its moment arm;
- turn motion into stored elastic energy and later return it;
- exchange active force for passive restraint as a range changes;
- distribute contact across a surface;
- shift part of a trunk-support job into a pressurized assembly; or
- transfer demand to an external support.

The traction acting across an ideal bonded interface must be mechanically compatible on its two sides, but the surrounding biological account may still include distributed attachment, shear, pressure, friction, changing contact, and active regulation. “Handoff” is therefore a bookkeeping term for a change in carrier or role. It is not a claim that force exists as one conserved scalar packet moving down a line.

Pressure gives the hard/soft model an important counterexample to tissue classification. A finite-element and musculoskeletal model of static lifting estimated that intra-abdominal pressure could change spinal loading and stability depending on posture, coactivation, and the modeled pressure level.[^arjmand2006] The result is model-dependent and does not establish breath as a whole-body driver. It does show that an active, bounded, pressure-mediated assembly can perform a relatively shape-preserving or supporting role even though it is not bone.

## Normal deformation is the required null branch

The chapter must show substantial loading of form-receiving structures without calling it leakage.

Human knee cartilage imaged during cyclic compressive loading showed sliding displacement and regionally complex intratissue strain, including substantial shear, in healthy volunteers.[^chan2016] The Achilles example likewise shows normal tensile deformation and elastic recoil.[^lichtwark2005] Cartilage conforming under contact and tendon stretching under tension are not failures of skeletal routing. Their deformation is part of the mechanical job under the measured conditions.

A form-receiving role becomes a leakage candidate only when the account adds a mismatch:

```text
named receiver
    + named exposure history
    + freedom or function the receiver is expected to retain
    + evidence that the exposure is unintended or poorly matched
    + a credible route comparison
    + an outcome or disfavoring observation
```

Possible mechanical translations include sustained deformation, changed pressure, greater active tension, restricted excursion or glide, altered joint contact, a changed moment, or an exposure less well matched to current capacity and recovery. None is adverse merely by existing. The comparison must show why magnitude, direction, duration, timing, location, or consequence makes the exposure relevant.

## The same endpoint can conceal different allocations

The musculoskeletal system often has more elemental degrees of freedom than a task endpoint uniquely specifies. In detailed cat-hindlimb model instances matched to the postures of three cats, Sohn, McKay, and Ting found broad feasible activation ranges for producing prescribed endpoint forces; only a small fraction of the modeled muscles had nonzero lower bounds across tested conditions.[^sohn2013] The model is not a human whole-body measurement, but it supplies a precise existence proof that biomechanics may permit many internal solutions.

Human reaching experiments supply a complementary observation. Participants stabilized a pointer's endpoint and orientation through multijoint synergies while joint configurations varied across trials and target conditions.[^solnik2013] That study does not identify tissue forces or prove that every solution has the same cost. It shows that endpoint stability and internal configuration are different variables.

Assistance makes redistribution easier to observe. In a study published in 2026 involving fifteen healthy younger adults, moving robotic handles during standing and sitting reduced peak hip and knee extension moments relative to static handles while increasing vertical handle forces.[^ackermann2026] The endpoint remained a chair transition, but the person–device system carried more demand through the upper-body contact and less through selected lower-limb moments. This is a clean example of substitution or rerouting without an adverse verdict. It is not evidence of long-term benefit, target-population effectiveness, or universal superiority.

## Interruption, bypass, and overload require different comparisons

Use one matched task and one endpoint, then ask what changed:

| Candidate state | Availability | Participation | Exposure/capacity relation | Matched comparison that would help | Important null or alternative |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Interruption | proposed relation absent or insufficient for the phase | none or materially reduced | may be unknown | restore contact, excursion, control, or geometry while holding the task as constant as possible | the route was never required, or the observed marker did not index availability |
| Bypass | original relation plausibly available | reduced while alternate participation rises | either route may remain within capacity | remove or vary the alternate support and observe redistribution | the difference reflects ordinary variability or another uncontrolled task change |
| Overload | relation available and participating | present, possibly high or prolonged | declared capacity criterion is exceeded | alter dose or recovery while retaining the route | fatigue, discomfort, or high force does not necessarily meet the declared overload criterion |
| Substitution | original state may be interrupted, bypassed, reduced, or simply not chosen | alternate carrier preserves the endpoint | cost and consequence remain open | compare internal and external measures at equivalent endpoint | the new strategy may be equally viable or better suited |
| Unresolved | uncertain | distribution not identifiable | unknown | improve measurement or narrow the claim | uncertainty is a valid result |

The table prevents one observed change—such as less activation, a different posture, or use of a handrail—from being narrated simultaneously as interruption, overload, and compensation.

## Bounded continuity beyond direct series connections

Anatomical continuity can permit force transfer beyond the obvious muscle–tendon–bone series, but the scale and functional magnitude must be measured rather than assumed. In a human lower-limb experiment, changing hip and knee positions while holding the ankle apparatus constant altered ankle resting position and passive torque in ways the authors interpreted as evidence consistent with myofascial force transmission.[^marinho2017] The experiment concerns a bounded positional manipulation and indirect mechanical outcomes. It does not establish a continuous clinically dominant force line from foot to skull.

This is the appropriate Book I posture:

- direct anatomical attachment licenses a candidate carrier;
- measured displacement, force, pressure, torque, strain, or stiffness change can support a bounded interaction;
- adjacency or a felt line can motivate a hypothesis;
- only task- and scale-specific evidence can establish functional importance; and
- local evidence cannot be promoted automatically into a whole-body route.

The project may later find that internal force-line experience tracks useful multicarrier relations. Chapter 11 preserves that possibility while keeping embodied observation and measured allocation in separate columns.

## A route-account record

Every reader-facing routing account should be reconstructable from the following record:

| Field | Required entry | Example evidence state |
|---|---|---|
| Task and endpoint | what is being preserved or changed | observed |
| Phase and time window | when the account applies | measured or declared |
| System boundary | person, person–object, person–device, or selected region | declared |
| Incoming demand | external force, moment, acceleration, pressure, contact, or constraint | measured/calculated |
| Candidate carriers | skeletal contacts, active/tensile relations, pressure, compliant receiver, support | anatomical/model |
| Proposed route availability | which relations could perform the job | inferred/hypothesized unless directly tested |
| Observed allocation change | force, moment, motion, activation, pressure, strain, contact, or excursion | measured/calculated/model |
| Proposed alternate or substitute | what receives more or performs another role | inferred/model |
| Local exposure and capacity | quantity, direction, history, receiver, and criterion | measured/model/hypothesized |
| Alternatives | competing task, state, control, biological, behavioral, or measurement accounts | explicit |
| Disfavoring observation | result that narrows or rejects the proposed routing account | prespecified |

No row should be completed from prose rhythm alone. If the internal allocation is unknown, the table should say so.

## What would count against a routing account

A serious continuity or leakage hypothesis must be vulnerable to at least one of these outcomes:

- the proposed carrier is not anatomically or mechanically available at the stated phase;
- the named external balance cannot close under the proposed arrows;
- a controlled task variation changes the visible configuration but not the relevant internal exposure;
- the proposed substitute does not change when the presumed interruption is removed;
- the receiver deforms normally and recovers without the predicted loss of excursion or function;
- another model predicts the measured allocation with fewer assumptions;
- the embodied line does not correspond to the proposed measured carrier or quantity;
- repeated measurement cannot distinguish the proposed states; or
- the anterior–posterior or hard/soft description adds no prediction beyond ordinary geometry, contact, and capacity.

Failure at one scale need not reject the wider preferred-configuration program. It does require that the local account be narrowed, reformulated, or left unresolved.

## First-draft example program

### Opening callback without replaying the full recurring narrative

Chapter 10 retires the complete object-transfer narrative. Chapter 11 should recall only the final placement phase: the object reaches the support in two successful versions, once without added hand contact and once with a brief hand or forearm support. The endpoint remains visible while candidate contacts, moments, pressure strategies, and active contributions change. The prose must label this as a hypothetical comparison unless a measured analogue is supplied.

The robotic chair-transition study can then provide the measured analogue: added moving-handle support reduced selected leg moments and increased handle force.[^ackermann2026] It shows redistribution, not which route is globally preferred.

### Normal form-receiving counterexample

Use either tendon elastic recoil during hopping or healthy cartilage deformation under controlled knee loading. The point is explicit: a compliant structure can accept substantial load and change shape while performing its intended role.

### Inference-stop example

Use the instrumented-knee comparison to show that an external moment or electromyographic change may not reliably identify the internal contact-force change in a new gait strategy.[^meyer2013] The limitation is one participant and one implanted joint; its job is to stop over-inference, not deny all modeling.

## First-draft authorization

A controlled Chapter 11 draft is authorized if it:

- states the project-native hard/soft formulation positively and gives its exact mechanical translation;
- says the distinction is motivated by TCM hard/soft and Yin/Yang concepts without treating correspondence as scientific proof;
- distinguishes external balance from underdetermined internal allocation;
- defines continuity as task- and phase-specific availability across a sequence or network, not one cable;
- defines handoff as a change in carrier or role that may include normal deformation, storage, return, pressure, and sliding;
- separates availability, participation, exposure, capacity, and evidence state;
- translates leakage into a named poorly matched exposure and includes a normal-deformation null branch;
- keeps interruption, bypass, overload, substitution, rerouting, and unresolved allocation distinct;
- shows a beneficial or neutral substitution and permits a changed route to improve a task;
- uses a matched endpoint without inferring unchanged internal organization;
- requires alternatives and disfavoring observations for every routing claim;
- labels embodied force-line experience as experience unless a carrier and quantity are separately supported; and
- hands Chapter 12 a precise question about redistribution and local concentration.

The draft is not authorized to establish the preferred all-Yang route, equate posterior with hard, equate anterior with soft or adverse, infer an interruption from posture or palpation, claim that deformation is leakage, diagnose from a route diagram, prescribe a correction, or use local myofascial evidence as proof of one clinically dominant body-wide chain.
