## Editorial decision

Chapter 21 should be affirmative about the book's mechanical program while refusing a mechanical default. Its central question is not the vague proposition “does mechanics matter?” A physical body always has mechanics. The useful question is:

> For this declared question, boundary, population, timeframe, comparator, and outcome, how much explanatory or decision-relevant work does the proposed mechanical relation perform beyond the alternatives?

A coherent mechanical chain may identify a local exposure, explain a measured transition, or improve a prospective question without establishing origin, causal priority, symptom source, diagnosis, prognosis, or treatment. Another process may precede the mechanical observation. Both directions may interact. A common condition may alter mechanics and the outcome. The selected mechanical account may be accurate but already captured by ordinary care, irrelevant to the decision, or unable to discriminate histories.

These are not concessions that push mechanics to the edge. They are the means by which a stronger mechanical proposition can be distinguished from a merely possible one. The larger project survives by making a candidate relation precise enough to support, narrow, duplicate, or disfavor.

## The object under review is a scoped causal proposition

“Mechanics matters” is not yet a causal proposition. A reviewable proposition specifies at least:

```text
question: origin / current exposure / symptom modulation / function /
          recurrence / recovery / outcome / decision

population and setting
system boundary and scale
candidate mechanical relation
time zero and follow-up interval
comparator or counterfactual contrast
receiver and intermediate variables
endpoint
alternatives and common causes
permitted conclusion
```

Hernán and Robins organize causal inference around well-defined contrasts and the assumptions needed to identify them from observed data.[^hernan2020] Greenland, Pearl, and Robins show how a directed acyclic graph can make assumed causal paths, competing routes, confounding, and mediation visible.[^greenland1999] The graph begins from substantive assumptions; it does not discover or prove its own arrows, and an omitted or misdirected relation can change the interpretation. Hill's classic discussion treated strength, consistency, specificity, temporality, gradient, plausibility, coherence, experiment, and analogy as viewpoints for judging an association—not a mechanical checklist that produces causation.[^hill1965] Together these sources support a plain-language rule: specify which change is being compared, what outcome it is supposed to change, what paths are being assumed, and what would otherwise have happened before assigning explanatory weight.

Different questions about one person can yield different scopes. A support may alter a current task. That is not the same proposition as whether the unsupported task initiated a tissue state. A tissue state may alter mechanics even if mechanics contributes to later symptoms. A valid diagnosis may guide urgent care even while mechanical history remains unknown. The chapter should never demand one total rank for “mechanics” across all those questions.

## One observation can fit several causal structures

Use a neutral observation set rather than a disguised diagnosis:

```text
observed now:
    symptom or reduced task tolerance
    + altered movement or support strategy
    + common structural or tissue finding
```

At least five structures remain compatible:

```text
A. candidate mechanical exposure -> receiver response -> symptom/function

B. biological or tissue state -> symptom
                              -> changed mechanics

C. context or behavior -> changed task/exposure
                       -> symptom/function through another path

D. mechanical exposure <-> tissue/symptom state
   reciprocal changes across time

E. mechanical observation and finding are incidental to the declared outcome
```

A sixth common-cause form can sit behind several of these:

```text
development / injury / illness / work organization / care / resource condition
    |-> mechanical observation
    +-> receiver state, symptom, function, or measured outcome
```

The nodes can be observed or measured while the arrows remain inferred or hypothesized. A directed graph clarifies what must be distinguished and discloses the structure being assumed; it does not identify the correct graph by being drawn.[^greenland1999] Cross-sectional coexistence cannot establish order. Longitudinal observation improves temporal information but can leave common causes, measurement error, intervention, and selection unresolved.

The opening should not imply that all structures are equally likely in every context. A documented acute event, validated infection test, established inflammatory disease, or directly manipulated exposure can strongly change the comparison. The discipline is to show what makes one structure better supported rather than letting narrative detail substitute for discrimination.

## Mechanics can enter without a compulsory five-bin taxonomy

The archive repeatedly sought a distinction among mechanically led, mixed, downstream, and nonmechanical-primary contexts. Preserve that intent as flexible questions:

- Did a mechanical relation precede the receiver state under review?
- Does it explain a measured intermediate transition?
- Does repeated exposure change later dose or capacity?
- Does the relation alter present function or symptoms without identifying origin?
- Is the mechanical observation primarily a consequence of another state?
- Do both directions form a reciprocal loop?
- Does a common environment or process change both?
- Does the added mechanical account distinguish anything or change the declared result?

Terms such as *primary*, *contributory*, *modifying*, *consequential*, or *incidental* can summarize a supported account. They should not become a scorecard that creates false precision. One relation can occupy different positions at different times: an immune process can change tissue and movement first; the changed mechanics can then alter local exposure; pain and protection can then alter movement again. If evidence cannot distinguish those transitions, “underresolved” is more accurate than a polished category.

## Osteoarthritis and rheumatoid arthritis test the boundary without forming a dichotomy

The archive proposed osteoarthritis (OA) versus rheumatoid arthritis (RA) to distinguish mechanically led from immune-mediated disease. That contrast can survive only in a more exact form.

OA is not a single mechanical wear process. A major disease primer describes OA as a multifactorial whole-joint disease whose structural and clinical presentations reflect interacting local and systemic factors.[^martel2016] Felson's deliberately strong mechanical account argues that mechanically induced joint injury is central across much OA and that pathomechanics can dominate progression.[^felson2013] That paper is valuable because it articulates the strongest mechanical interpretation. Its thesis should be compared with, not substituted for, evidence on age, adiposity, injury, joint shape, inflammation, pain, muscle, behavior, metabolism, and heterogeneous phenotypes.

RA is a chronic inflammatory joint disease in which immune dysregulation, genetic susceptibility, environmental exposures, synovial inflammation, and downstream cartilage and bone damage occupy central causal positions.[^smolen2016] Calling RA immune-mediated does not make mechanics vanish. Inflamed or damaged joints still encounter task demand; pain, swelling, weakness, treatment, and structural change can alter movement and distribution. Those later mechanical relations may matter to function or local exposure without becoming the primary explanation of RA.

Prospective and experimental evidence makes the mixed account more concrete. In adults with established knee OA, baseline malalignment was prospectively associated with compartment-specific structural progression over eighteen months.[^sharma2001] Baseline MRI synovitis in knees without radiographic OA was also prospectively associated with incident disease after adjustment for measured structural and mechanical covariates.[^felson2016] Neither observational study proves initiation or eliminates residual common cause, but together they resist a choice between geometry and inflammatory tissue state. In experimental inflammatory arthritis, changing limb loading changed where and how severely disease appeared in mouse joints while the systemic immune process remained; the accompanying human RA study mapped erosion topography cross-sectionally in sixty-three patients.[^cambre2018] The causal loading manipulations were in animals, not people. The bounded inference is that mechanics can help localize or amplify an immune-driven process without being established as the upstream initiator of human RA.

The boundary exercise therefore produces a matrix, not two boxes:

| Question | OA example | RA example | Boundary lesson |
|---|---|---|---|
| What initiates or sustains the disease process? | mechanical, biological, metabolic, developmental, and contextual factors can interact | immune-inflammatory processes and their determinants carry central explanatory weight | disease families are heterogeneous; one label does not assign every arrow |
| What changes local exposure now? | geometry, task, pain, strength, tissue state, support, and behavior | inflammation, damage, pain, weakness, treatment, task, and support | mechanics can be current and important without being etiologically primary |
| What explains current symptom or function? | structure and load may contribute but do not map one-to-one to pain or participation | inflammatory activity, damage, pain processing, task, environment, and mechanics may all enter | the endpoint determines the relevant chain |
| What follows from a response to support or movement? | present modulation under tested conditions | present modulation under tested conditions | response does not retroactively select the origin graph |

The comparison is optional in reader prose pending rheumatology review. It must never become “OA is mechanical; RA is not” or a care recommendation.

## Clear other-primary contexts establish a real stopping point

Infection, immune-mediated disease, genetic conditions, malignancy, metabolic disease, toxicity, and acute trauma can carry the principal explanatory and care burden for a declared question. Chapter 21 does not need a long disease list or diagnostic teaching. The general reasoning point is enough:

```text
established or strongly suspected other-primary process
    -> tissue or system state
    -> possible changes in pain, capacity, control, task, and mechanics

mechanical background may remain
mechanical priority for origin or immediate decision may be low
```

The physical existence of load in these contexts does not establish incremental explanatory value. Conversely, low priority for the immediate medical question does not mean later mechanical exposure, assistance, comfort, participation, or recovery questions are unreal. The conclusion is scoped to the endpoint.

This is where the framework must allow itself to stop. If a mechanical history adds no discrimination, no measured intermediate, no change in interpretation, and no prospective research value beyond the established account, “no added value for this question” is a substantive finding.

## Reverse direction is experimentally observable

Pain, inflammation, injury, fatigue, fear, altered sensation, or another biological and experiential state can change movement and loading. That direction cannot be treated as a generic caveat after a mechanical-origin story.

Devecchi and colleagues induced direction-specific movement-evoked lumbar pain in thirty healthy adults during repetitive lifting. Participants reduced lumbar movement in the pain-provoking direction but not the non-provoking direction.[^devecchi2025] The study supplies a bounded experimental demonstration that pain can precede and shape a movement strategy. It does not reproduce chronic low-back-pain biology, show that every movement change is protective, or establish the long-term consequence of the adaptation.

Trost and colleagues induced delayed-onset trunk-extensor soreness in fifty-one initially pain-free participants. Pre-induction pain-related fear did not predict baseline lumbar flexion but did predict reduced lumbar flexion during selected reaching tasks after soreness was induced.[^trost2012] This shows an interaction: a psychological measure changed the motor response conditional on a new pain state. It does not make fear the origin of all pain or reduced motion.

The reverse direction is not limited to gross movement. Tucker and colleagues induced deep-tissue pain in fourteen healthy volunteers performing matched low-force tasks and recorded individual motor units. Recruitment changed while participants maintained the required external force.[^tucker2009] The result shows that a stable task output can conceal a pain-driven redistribution of how the task is produced. It remains a small acute experiment and does not identify the origin or long-term consequence of a chronic recruitment pattern.

Biological state can also precede movement change. In a double-blind, placebo-controlled crossover experiment with nineteen usable movement datasets, an experimentally induced inflammatory state changed walking speed, stride behavior, limb movement, head position, and timed mobility in healthy adults.[^lasselin2020] Because participants experienced the associated sickness state, the study cannot isolate inflammation from fatigue, nausea, pain, dizziness, mood, or awareness of condition. It nevertheless demonstrates under bounded conditions that biology and experience can lead into altered movement rather than merely follow it.

These experiments protect a strong reciprocal model:

```text
candidate exposure -> pain or tissue state -> changed strategy
       ^                                      |
       +---------- changed later exposure ----+
```

The first arrow, the motor response, and the later feedback require different evidence. A loop can become clinically or biologically meaningful even when its original entry point remains uncertain.

## Expectation and meaning can change the symptom node

In a controlled expectancy experiment, Shih and colleagues used cues associated with higher or lower pain in healthy participants and found expectation-related changes in pain ratings and brain responses, with individual certainty related to modulation magnitude.[^shih2019] The study supports a cognitive influence on the experience of a controlled nociceptive stimulus. It does not imply that pain is unreal, that tissue exposure is absent, or that expectations explain a disease.

A within-person episodic-migraine study supplies a complementary clinical example. Sixty-six participants treated attacks with rizatriptan or placebo labeled positively, neutrally, or negatively, alongside an untreated condition. The information attached to the pill changed reported pain response while the active drug retained an overall advantage.[^kamhansen2014] This does not mean expectation caused the migraine or that labeling replaced pharmacology. On the study's primary pain-score outcome, open-label placebo improved relative to no treatment; it did not significantly improve the secondary pain-free outcome, so those results must remain separate.

The exact node matters:

```text
same or controlled physical stimulus
    + learned cue / expectation / certainty
    -> changed reported pain and measured neural response
```

This entry need not compete with mechanics. Expectation can modify the symptom relation while a physical exposure remains. Fear and prior experience can change task selection or protective behavior, thereby changing later mechanical exposure. Meaning, culture, and care history can change goals, salience, trust, and participation without being reduced to “confounders” around a preferred mechanical chain.

## Whole-person context must enter at named nodes

Engel's biopsychosocial proposal was a direct challenge to a biomedical model that excluded social, psychological, and behavioral dimensions from its account of illness.[^engel1977] The World Health Organization's ICF describes functioning through interacting health conditions, body structures and functions, activities, participation, and environmental factors.[^whoicf] WHO's social-determinants framework further identifies the conditions in which people are born, grow, live, work, and age, and their access to power, money, and resources, as influences on health and inequity.[^whosdh]

Work context can enter before both exposure and recovery. NIOSH's Total Worker Health framework names work organization, schedules, compensation, physical environment, technology, and policy as conditions that can shape worker safety and health.[^niosh2026] It is a framework, not proof of a condition-specific arrow. In a cross-sectional analysis of 17,464 employed US adults, physical exertion, several psychosocial factors, and work-organization measures were associated with low-back-pain prevalence after adjustment.[^shockey2024] Self-report, cross-sectional timing, coarse exposure measurement, and residual or common-method confounding prevent causal assignment; formal interactions with physical exertion were not statistically significant. The study's value here is to keep work demand, organization, and psychosocial conditions inside competing causal graphs rather than relegating them to consequences of symptoms.

These frameworks establish scope, not case-level causal weight. Chapter 21 should operationalize them with a node-entry matrix:

| Domain | Candidate node or arrow | Example measurable object | Alternative or limit |
|---|---|---|---|
| Biological state | material properties, inflammatory response, healing, sensation, energy, capacity | imaging, assay, examination, performance, repeated response | marker may not identify causal direction or individual outcome |
| Cognition and expectation | task prediction, salience, symptom interpretation, choice | expectancy rating, task choice, reported experience, experimental manipulation | report is not tissue exposure; expectation need not explain origin |
| Emotion and learning | protection, avoidance, variability, persistence | behavior, movement, repeated task selection, physiological response | adaptive protection and excessive avoidance must be distinguished |
| Social and economic conditions | task demand, rest, resources, safety, access, recovery | schedule, job control, income/resource measure, care access, assistance | group association does not recover an individual's causal path |
| Occupational environment | external demand, tool, surface, repetition, duration, support | observed or measured task exposure and work organization | self-report, group estimate, and direct measurement retain different evidence states |
| Physical environment and equipment | boundary conditions and available paths | dimensions, contact, force, temperature, support, device state | a changed environment can affect several nodes simultaneously |
| Culture and meaning | goals, interpretation, acceptable strategies, participation | qualitative or mixed-method observation, stated values | not every meaningful influence is reducible to one scalar |
| Care history | tissue state, symptom, belief, task, assistance, measurement | medication, procedure, rehabilitation, advice, device, timing | response to care can create post-treatment confounding and new mechanics |
| Goals and values | which outcome and tradeoff matter | person-stated goals, participation, burden, risk tolerance | decision relevance is not an etiological variable |

The matrix should never imply that every row is active in every case. Each retained entry requires a candidate relation, timeframe, evidence state, comparator where possible, and outcome relevance.

## Mechanical modulation does not recover mechanical origin

A mechanical intervention asks a specific causal question: what did this intervention change relative to its comparator under the study conditions? It does not automatically answer what initiated the condition.

Hinman and colleagues randomized 164 people with medial knee OA to unloading shoes or conventional walking shoes. At six months, between-group changes in pain and function did not differ, even though both groups improved on several measures.[^hinman2016] The result does not show that knee mechanics are irrelevant. It shows that this unloading design did not add symptomatic benefit over the comparator in that trial.

Paterson and colleagues then explored biomechanical mediators and moderators in the same trial. No proposed mediator showed a significant mediating effect in the full cohort; subgroup signals were exploratory and underpowered.[^paterson2018] The mechanical intervention label therefore did not supply the mediator. Change in a proposed mechanical quantity, change in symptoms, and mediation of the symptom effect remained separate questions.

A positive response carries the same limits. In a six-week trial of 126 adults with painful patellofemoral OA, bracing reduced pain and the volume of targeted patellofemoral bone-marrow lesions, while the non-targeted tibiofemoral measure did not change detectably.[^callaghan2015] The response supports mechanical modifiability and regional specificity under the trial conditions. Pain change was not significantly associated with change in the MRI measure, so the trial does not establish mediation, disease origin, or a complete symptom mechanism.

The RESTORE trial compared cognitive functional therapy with or without movement-sensor biofeedback against usual care in 492 adults with chronic disabling low-back pain. Both cognitive-functional-therapy groups improved activity limitation relative to usual care, while adding sensor feedback produced no apparent additional benefit.[^kent2023] Because treatment was individualized, multicomponent, and unblinded, its response does not isolate cognition, emotion, behavior, movement, lifestyle, or another active component and does not recover etiology. The sensor result is equally important: a technically richer mechanical measurement layer does not automatically improve the declared outcome.

Even a clearly positive intervention result would need the same boundary:

```text
assigned intervention -> measured mechanical change
assigned intervention -> symptom or function change
measured mechanical change -> mediated outcome effect, if assumptions support it

none of these alone -> historical origin of the condition
```

VanderWeele's mediation guide makes the identification assumptions and sensitivity of direct and indirect effects explicit.[^vanderweele2016] Chapter 21 should not teach mediation analysis, but it can use the logic to prevent “it helped” from becoming “this was the original cause.”

## Structural findings can remain low priority for a symptom question

Chapter 19 already separates exposure, response, material finding, symptom, diagnosis, and outcome. One of its strongest null comparators is the prevalence of imaging findings in asymptomatic people. Brinjikji and colleagues synthesized imaging findings from 3,110 asymptomatic people across thirty-three studies and found several degenerative spine features increasingly common with age.[^brinjikji2015] The result does not make those findings meaningless. It prevents their presence alone from identifying a symptom source or past route.

The same finding can have different priority by question:

- high priority for describing anatomy or planning a procedure under relevant clinical conditions;
- moderate or uncertain priority for explaining a specific exposure or limitation;
- low priority for explaining a symptom when correspondence and alternatives are weak; or
- incidental for the declared endpoint.

Those are prose conclusions, not score bins. The evidence and question determine them.

## Evidence strength is arrow-specific, not one universal ladder

Different evidence forms answer different questions:

| Evidence form | What it can contribute | What it cannot establish by itself |
|---|---|---|
| observation or report | existence, chronology as recorded, experience, candidate relation | unmeasured internal quantity or causal direction |
| direct measurement | value of the measured variable under protocol | another scale, unmeasured receiver, or biological meaning |
| calculation or validated model | derived quantity under assumptions | direct observation or assumption-free tissue exposure |
| cross-sectional association | coexistence and pattern in a population | temporal direction or causal priority |
| longitudinal association | order and prospective relation under measured conditions | absence of residual common cause, selection, or measurement error |
| experimental manipulation | effect of assigned manipulation on measured outcomes in the study system | disease origin, unmeasured mediator, or population-wide clinical effect |
| mechanistic evidence | support for one intermediate relation | frequency or total causal importance in people |
| mediation analysis | estimated indirect/direct effects under explicit assumptions | mechanism merely because a variable was measured after treatment |
| condition-specific clinical outcome evidence | effect or prediction for the studied population and endpoint | universal mechanism or a different decision context |
| converging methods | robustness when biases differ and predictions converge | certainty if methods share bias, constructs drift, or alternatives remain |

Lawlor and colleagues define triangulation as integrating evidence from approaches with different and unrelated sources of potential bias, so that convergence can strengthen inference and divergence can expose assumptions.[^lawlor2016] This is especially useful for the book's research program: instrumented mechanics, prospective exposure history, tissue response, experimental perturbation, clinical outcomes, and qualitative experience should contribute different evidence rather than pretending to be interchangeable measures of one hidden route.

The stopping point is the weakest arrow necessary for the conclusion. A measured configuration-to-exposure relation may support a local mechanical statement even when the exposure-to-symptom link is weak. A symptom response may be established while the mediator and origin remain open. A predictive model may be accurate without showing that acting on it helps. Vickers and Elkin's decision-curve framework formalizes the distinction between prediction and decision consequences; the chapter needs only the principle, not a mandated method.[^vickers2006]

## Support, narrowing, and disfavor tests

The Chapter 21 framework gains value when it changes a comparison rather than guaranteeing mechanics a place.

### Results that support a stronger mechanical role for a declared question

- the proposed mechanical relation precedes the receiver or outcome change in prospective data;
- an exposure quantity is measured at the appropriate receiver and scale;
- manipulation changes the proposed intermediate and the endpoint with compatible timing;
- alternative graphs make different predictions and the mechanical graph performs better;
- results converge across methods with meaningfully different bias structures;
- the relation adds explanation, prediction, research coordination, or decision value beyond a named existing account; and
- the result survives population, task, and measurement-boundary checks.

### Results that narrow the role without erasing mechanics

- the relation changes present symptoms or function but origin remains unknown;
- the mechanical pattern follows a biological or experiential state;
- effect is receiver-, task-, phase-, subgroup-, or timeframe-specific;
- mechanics explains one intermediate but not the later clinical endpoint;
- a reciprocal loop is supported but its entry point is unresolved;
- the account improves research description but not prediction or decision; or
- another process carries the immediate diagnostic and care priority.

### Results that disfavor or stop the proposed use

- the proposed route cannot be measured or reconstructed reliably;
- temporal order contradicts the mechanical-origin account;
- the mechanical variable does not distinguish the candidate histories;
- manipulating the proposed relation does not change its own predicted intermediate;
- the endpoint changes while the proposed mediator does not, or vice versa;
- ordinary clinical, ergonomic, rehabilitation, or whole-person reasoning already captures the useful information;
- added variables do not improve the prespecified result or worsen burden and exclusion; or
- the mechanical observation is common, incidental, or irrelevant to the declared endpoint.

These are successful research outcomes. They make the program cumulative rather than self-sealing.

## First-draft authorization and limits

The reviewed source set can authorize a reader-facing chapter that:

- begins with one observation and multiple causal structures;
- defines causal scope before arguing priority;
- treats mechanical roles as flexible possibilities rather than mandatory bins;
- preserves OA/RA only as a nuanced and optional boundary exercise;
- gives experimental evidence that pain, transient inflammatory sickness, and fear can precede movement or recruitment change;
- places biological, psychological, social, occupational, environmental, care, and meaning factors at named nodes;
- distinguishes intervention effect, mechanical change, active component, mediation, and historical origin;
- calibrates each conclusion to the weakest necessary arrow;
- requires a comparator and incremental-value question; and
- ends with low or no mechanical priority as an affirmative possible result.

It does not authorize diagnosis, triage, referral, contraindication, prognosis, treatment, a disease taxonomy, a causal score, a preferred configuration, posterior necessity, TCM correspondence, breath-driven organization, assessment, or correction. General disease classes remain boundary examples, not teaching or care guidance.

## Figure and review obligations

1. **Same observation, several structures:** forward, reverse, reciprocal, common-cause, context-mediated, and incidental graphs; observed nodes and inferred arrows use different notation.
2. **Scope changes with the question:** origin, current exposure, symptom modulation, function, recovery, and decision questions applied to one observation set without assigning one total mechanical rank.
3. **Whole-person node-entry matrix:** domain, node or arrow, evidence state, timeframe, alternative, and outcome; blank cells remain blank.
4. **Evidence and stopping model:** arrow-specific evidence forms, permitted conclusions, and concrete stop conditions; no score or universal hierarchy.

Before publication, the packet and resulting chapter require causal-inference and evidence-method review; rheumatology review of OA/RA; pain-science and psychology review of experimental examples; social/occupational-health and ethics review of contextual relations; medical-coauthor review of every disease, diagnosis, symptom, and care boundary; patient-centered-language review; and figure, rights, accessibility, caption, and final-art review.
