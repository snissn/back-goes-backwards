# Bibliography

This first-draft bibliography consolidates the annotated source records documented in the chapter endnotes. Where the same record appears in more than one chapter, one complete entry is retained here. Composite records remain intact, so an individual source may appear again when its annotation or source grouping differs. The chapter notes preserve each proposition-specific use and limit. Publication editing will normalize house style and recheck metadata without changing the evidentiary job of a source.

- A. Singh et al., [“On the Morphological Relations of the Achilles Tendon and Plantar Fascia via the Calcaneus: A Cadaveric Study,”](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41598-021-85251-0) *Scientific Reports* 11 (2021): 5986. Nineteen cadaveric feet supported morphometric and superficial paratenon–periosteum–plantar-fascia relations without confirmed complete fiber continuity. The study did not measure living transfer magnitude or task consequence.
- Adam G. Culvenor et al., “Prevalence of Knee Osteoarthritis Features on Magnetic Resonance Imaging in Asymptomatic Uninjured Adults: A Systematic Review and Meta-Analysis,” *British Journal of Sports Medicine* 53, no. 20 (2019): 1268–1278, [doi:10.1136/bjsports-2018-099257](https://doi.org/10.1136/bjsports-2018-099257). Use for structural-finding and symptom non-equivalence and feature-specific age variation; bone-marrow-lesion prevalence was not significantly age-dependent, and pooled imaging findings remain heterogeneous.
- Adamantios Arampatzis et al., “Plasticity of Human Achilles Tendon Mechanical and Morphological Properties in Response to Cyclic Strain,” *Journal of Biomechanics* 43, no. 16 (2010): 3073–3079, [doi:10.1016/j.jbiomech.2010.08.014](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jbiomech.2010.08.014). Eleven young men trained each leg under different estimated strain magnitudes for fourteen weeks. Use for dose-specific material response and a null cross-sectional-area result; not a universal threshold or clinical rule.
- Ahmet Erdemir, Scott McLean, Walter Herzog, and Antonie J. van den Bogert, [“Model-Based Estimation of Muscle Forces Exerted During Movements,”](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.clinbiomech.2006.09.005) *Clinical Biomechanics* 22, no. 2 (2007): 131–154; Jennifer L. Hicks et al., [“Is My Model Good Enough? Best Practices for Verification and Validation of Musculoskeletal Models and Simulations of Movement,”](https://doi.org/10.1115/1.4029304) *Journal of Biomechanical Engineering* 137, no. 2 (2015): 020905. These reviews support the underdetermination and model dependence of internal musculoskeletal-force estimates and the need for verification, validation, and sensitivity analysis. They do not imply that internal forces are unknowable.
- Ahmet Erdemir, Scott McLean, Walter Herzog, and Antonie J. van den Bogert, “Model-Based Estimation of Muscle Forces Exerted during Movements,” *Clinical Biomechanics* 22, no. 2 (2007): 131–154, [doi:10.1016/j.clinbiomech.2006.09.005](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.clinbiomech.2006.09.005). Indeterminacy makes assumptions and validation consequential; it does not make every muscle-force estimate uninformative.
- Alejandro A. Espinoza Orías et al., [“Anatomic Variation in the Elastic Anisotropy of Cortical Bone Tissue in the Human Femur,”](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jmbbm.2008.08.005) *Journal of the Mechanical Behavior of Biomedical Materials* 2, no. 3 (2009): 255–263; Christopher C.-B. Wang et al., [“Optical Determination of Anisotropic Material Properties of Bovine Articular Cartilage in Compression,”](https://doi.org/10.1016/S0021-9290%2802%2900417-7) *Journal of Biomechanics* 36, no. 3 (2003): 339–353; Gerhard A. Holzapfel and Ray W. Ogden, [“Constitutive Modelling of Passive Myocardium: A Structurally Based Framework for Material Characterization,”](https://doi.org/10.1098/rsta.2009.0091) *Philosophical Transactions of the Royal Society A* 367, no. 1902 (2009): 3445–3475. These examples support local, tissue- and model-specific direction dependence. They do not supply a common whole-body material frame.
- Alessandra Bonfanti, Julia Duque, Alexandre Kabla, and Guillaume Charras, [“Fracture in Living Tissues,”](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.tcb.2022.01.005) *Trends in Cell Biology* 32, no. 6 (2022): 537–551. The review supports the bounded claims that living tissues experience internally and externally generated stresses and that tissue fracture is multiscale; it does not support a general health-outcome claim.
- Alexander Jahn, Johan Hviid Andersen, Andreas Seidler, David Høyrup Christiansen, and Annett Dalbøge, “Occupational Psychosocial Exposures and Chronic Low-Back Pain: A Systematic Review and Meta-Analysis,” *Scandinavian Journal of Work, Environment & Health* 50, no. 5 (2024): 329–340, [doi:10.5271/sjweh.4165](https://doi.org/10.5271/sjweh.4165). The small pooled estimates and very low certainty do not make work context universally causal or irrelevant.
- Ali Guermazi et al., “Prevalence of Abnormalities in Knees Detected by MRI in Adults without Knee Osteoarthritis: Population Based Observational Study (Framingham Osteoarthritis Study),” *BMJ* 345 (2012): e5339, [doi:10.1136/bmj.e5339](https://doi.org/10.1136/bmj.e5339). The study supports common magnetic-resonance findings among people with and without knee pain and finding–symptom non-equivalence. It does not dismiss all findings or establish mechanical irrelevance.
- Ali Kiapour et al., “Finite Element Model of the Knee for Investigation of Injury Mechanisms: Development and Validation,” *Journal of Biomechanical Engineering* 136, no. 1 (2014): 011002, [doi:10.1115/1.4025692](https://doi.org/10.1115/1.4025692). The model was compared with selected cadaveric kinematic, ligament, and contact data across declared tests. It supports multi-output validation within those tests, not universal credibility, subject-specific prediction, or clinical inference.
- Alice Kongsted, Peter Kent, Lise Hestbaek, and Werner Vach, “Patients with Low Back Pain Had Distinct Clinical Course Patterns That Were Typically Neither Complete Recovery nor Constant Pain: A Latent Class Analysis of Longitudinal Data,” *The Spine Journal* 15, no. 5 (2015): 885–894, [doi:10.1016/j.spinee.2015.02.012](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.spinee.2015.02.012). The cohort supplied weekly reports from 1,082 primary-care patients; model-dependent classes do not identify mechanisms, biological subtypes, or an individual's prognosis.
- Alix C. Deymier et al., [“Micro-Mechanical Properties of the Tendon-to-Bone Attachment,”](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.actbio.2017.01.037) *Acta Biomaterialia* 56 (2017): 25–35. Eleven micrometer-sized mouse enthesis beams from six mice were tested. The study supports a local high-compliance region and a small mineral-defect comparison in which strength, but not several other reported properties, differed significantly. Species, specimen, scale, and testing limits remain controlling.
- American Occupational Therapy Association, [“Domain & Process”](https://www.aota.org/practice/domain-and-process) and [“What Is Occupational Therapy?”](https://www.aota.org/about/what-is-ot), official professional resources accessed July 11, 2026. The sources support daily activity and participation, work, environmental and personal context, performance, goals, and adaptation. U.S. professional material does not establish universal legal scope, ownership of ergonomics, or the validity of this book’s routing object.
- American Physical Therapy Association, [“Movement System Management”](https://www.apta.org/patient-care/interventions/movement-system-management), official professional resource accessed July 11, 2026. The source supports movement as complex and contextual and documents existing movement-system development; it does not endorse a particular movement diagnosis or Book I terminology.
- American Physical Therapy Association, [“Documentation: Initial Examination and Evaluation”](https://www.apta.org/your-practice/documentation/defensible-documentation/elements-within-the-patientclient-management-model/initial-examination), official professional resource accessed July 11, 2026. The source supports the breadth of U.S. physical-therapy examination, including history, systems review, movement and function, cognition, affect, learning, collaboration, and scope. It is not a universal workflow.
- American Society of Mechanical Engineers, [*V&V 40-2018: Assessing Credibility of Computational Modeling through Verification and Validation—Application to Medical Devices*](https://www.asme.org/codes-standards/find-codes-standards/assessing-credibility-of-computational-modeling-through-verification-and-validation-application-to-medical-devices) (2018). The standard supplies an authoritative verification-and-validation framework. It is not biomechanical evidence for any particular anatomy or clinical claim.
- An-Wen Chan et al., “SPIRIT 2025 Statement: Updated Guideline for Protocols of Randomised Trials,” *BMJ* 389 (2025): e081477, [doi:10.1136/bmj-2024-081477](https://doi.org/10.1136/bmj-2024-081477). Protocol transparency does not substitute for a suitable design or ethical approval.
- Andrea G. Schwartz, Jill D. Pasteris, Guy M. Genin, Tyrone L. Daulton, and Stavros Thomopoulos, [“Mineral Distributions at the Developing Tendon Enthesis,”](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0048630) *PLOS ONE* 7, no. 11 (2012): e48630. The study supports a measured developmental mineral gradient at the murine supraspinatus enthesis, not direct human mechanics or an outcome claim.
- Andrea Monte, Francesca Nardello, Riccardo Magris, Paolo Tecchio, and Paola Zamparo, [“The Influence of In Vivo Mechanical Behaviour of the Achilles Tendon on the Mechanics, Energetics and Apparent Efficiency of Bouncing Gaits,”](https://doi.org/10.1242/jeb.242453) *Journal of Experimental Biology* 224, no. 16 (2021): jeb242453. The study supports task-bounded distinctions among tendon work, gearing, and apparent efficiency during hopping, not a generic efficiency or injury claim.
- Andreas Müller et al., [“Load Distribution in the Lumbar Spine during Modeled Compression Depends on Lordosis,”](https://doi.org/10.3389/fbioe.2021.661258) *Frontiers in Bioengineering and Biotechnology* 9 (2021): 661258. The study supports geometry-dependent, level- and quantity-specific modeled distribution across twenty-eight lumbar geometries derived from CT scans without visible degeneration. It does not establish an ideal curve or clinical outcome.
- Andreas Seidler et al., [“Cumulative Occupational Lumbar Load and Lumbar Disc Disease—Results of a German Multi-Center Case-Control Study (EPILIFT),”](https://doi.org/10.1186/1471-2474-10-48) *BMC Musculoskeletal Disorders* 10 (2009): 48. The study supports a substantial lifetime occupational-history reconstruction combining interview, expert task surveys, and modeled compression; it does not identify an individual's unique route.
- Andrew Dilley, Bruce Lynn, Jane Greening, and Nicola DeLeon, [“Quantitative In Vivo Studies of Median Nerve Sliding in Response to Wrist, Elbow, Shoulder and Neck Movements,”](https://doi.org/10.1016/S0268-0033(03)00176-1) *Clinical Biomechanics* 18, no. 10 (2003): 899–907. Ultrasound in healthy participants quantified movement-specific median nerve excursion and strain. The study describes ordinary nerve mechanics, not entrapment, impaired conduction, symptoms, or treatment.
- Andrew E. Anderson, Benjamin J. Ellis, Steve A. Maas, and Jeffrey A. Weiss, [“Effects of Idealized Joint Geometry on Finite Element Predictions of Cartilage Contact Stresses in the Hip,”](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jbiomech.2010.01.010) *Journal of Biomechanics* 43, no. 7 (2010): 1351–1357. The study compared subject-specific, spherical, conchoid, and smoothed geometry representations under matched loading cases and separately evaluated a rigid-bone material simplification. It supports sensitivity of modeled contact predictions to geometric representation, not an anatomical intervention, biological response, or ideal hip shape.
- Andrew E. Anderson, Benjamin J. Ellis, Steve A. Maas, Christopher L. Peters, and Jeffrey A. Weiss, [“Validation of Finite Element Predictions of Cartilage Contact Pressure in the Human Hip Joint,”](https://doi.org/10.1115/1.2953472) *Journal of Biomechanical Engineering* 130, no. 5 (2008): 051008. The one-specimen cadaveric study compared a subject-specific hip model with pressure-film measurements under prescribed experimental loading cases and tested sensitivity to material and boundary assumptions. Film range and coverage limit the experimental comparison; neither the model nor the film establishes living response.
- Andrew J. Meyer, Darryl D. D'Lima, Thor F. Besier, David G. Lloyd, Clifford W. Colwell Jr., and Benjamin J. Fregly, [“Are External Knee Load and EMG Measures Accurate Indicators of Internal Knee Contact Forces during Gait?”](https://doi.org/10.1002/jor.22304) *Journal of Orthopaedic Research* 31, no. 6 (2013): 921–929. In one participant with an instrumented implant across four gait patterns, external-load and electromyographic changes were generally weak indicators of measured compartmental and total contact-force changes. The result does not reject inverse dynamics or all internal-load models.
- Andrew J. Vickers and Elena B. Elkin, [“Decision Curve Analysis: A Novel Method for Evaluating Prediction Models,”](https://doi.org/10.1177/0272989X06295361) *Medical Decision Making* 26, no. 6 (2006): 565–574. The article supports the principle that model accuracy and decision consequences are different evaluations; its prostate-cancer example does not establish clinical utility for routing.
- Andréa Soucasse, Arthur Jourdan, Lauriane Edin, Jean-François Gillion, Catherine Masson, and Thierry Bège, “A Better Understanding of Daily Life Abdominal Wall Mechanical Solicitation: Investigation of Intra-Abdominal Pressure Variations by Intragastric Wireless Sensor in Humans,” *Medical Engineering & Physics* 104 (2022): 103813, [doi:10.1016/j.medengphy.2022.103813](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.medengphy.2022.103813). Twenty healthy volunteers completed supervised tasks and daily activity. The pressure proxy is not wall stress, injury, support, or an optimal pressure.
- Angelo d'Errico et al., [“Medium- and Long-Term Reproducibility of Self-Reported Exposure to Physical Ergonomics Factors at Work,”](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.apergo.2006.03.002) *Applied Ergonomics* 38, no. 2 (2007): 167–175. The cohort began with 1,283 workers; 507 same-job workers contributed the one-year comparison, while 163 and 162 matched-job records contributed the two long-term comparisons. The study supports variable- and interval-dependent reproducibility; it does not make every retrospective history unusable.
- Anirudh Saini, Devin Burns, Darian Emmett, and Yun Seong Song, [“Trunk Velocity-Dependent Light Touch Reduces Postural Sway during Standing,”](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0224943) *PLOS ONE* 14, no. 11 (2019): e0224943. Ten healthy adults stood with eyes closed while a robot supplied small velocity-dependent contact and, in one condition, additional vibration. The experiment did not isolate sensory information from the small mechanical contact.
- Anouk Suman et al., “Effectiveness and Cost-Utility of a Multifaceted eHealth Strategy to Improve Back Pain Beliefs of Patients with Non-Specific Low Back Pain: A Cluster Randomised Trial,” *BMJ Open* 9, no. 12 (2019): e030879, [doi:10.1136/bmjopen-2019-030879](https://doi.org/10.1136/bmjopen-2019-030879). The trial tested one eHealth strategy and comparator; it is not a universal test of education or of this chapter's vocabulary.
- AO Foundation, [“Plating,”](https://surgeryreference.aofoundation.org/orthopedic-trauma/adult-trauma/basic-technique/basic-principles-of-plating) *AO Surgery Reference*, accessed July 11, 2026. The reference supports bounded language about compression, absolute and relative stability, construct-mediated force transfer, load sharing, alignment, callus, and preservation of the fracture zone. It is not used to prescribe a construct.
- Arnold Y. L. Wong et al., [“Do Different Sitting Postures Affect Spinal Biomechanics of Asymptomatic Individuals?”](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.gaitpost.2018.10.028) *Gait & Posture* 67 (2019): 230–235. The twenty-minute experiments support a posture-dependent muscle-activity comparison and a bounded null result for selected short-term outcomes, not conclusions about long exposure or symptomatic populations.
- Austin Bradford Hill, “The Environment and Disease: Association or Causation?,” *Proceedings of the Royal Society of Medicine* 58, no. 5 (1965): 295–300, [doi:10.1177/003591576505800503](https://doi.org/10.1177/003591576505800503). Hill presented viewpoints for considering causation, not a checklist that mechanically proves it.
- Ben Darlow et al., “The Fear Reduction Exercised Early (FREE) Approach to Management of Low Back Pain in General Practice: A Pragmatic Cluster-Randomised Controlled Trial,” *PLoS Medicine* 16, no. 9 (2019): e1002897, [doi:10.1371/journal.pmed.1002897](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pmed.1002897). The multicomponent program is not a pure test of education and does not validate mechanical literacy.
- Ben Darlow, Anthony Dowell, G. David Baxter, Fiona Mathieson, Melanie Perry, and S. John Dean, “The Enduring Impact of What Clinicians Say to People with Low Back Pain,” *Annals of Family Medicine* 11, no. 6 (2013): 527–534, [doi:10.1370/afm.1518](https://doi.org/10.1370/afm.1518). This qualitative study reports participants' interpretations and experiences; it does not estimate population frequency or establish the causal effect of a phrase.
- Benjamin F. Healthy young men were sampled six to seventy-two hours after one-legged exercise. Use for the acute tendon collagen-synthesis time course; local strain, net matrix change, and tendon injury were not measured.
- Benoît Beyer et al., [“In Vivo Thorax 3D Modelling from Costovertebral Joint Complex Kinematics,”](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.clinbiomech.2014.01.007) *Clinical Biomechanics* 29, no. 4 (2014): 434–438; T. - Bo Rydevik, Göran Lundborg, and Ulf Bagge, “Effects of Graded Compression on Intraneural Blood Flow: An In Vivo Study on Rabbit Tibial Nerve,” *The Journal of Hand Surgery* 6, no. 1 (1981): 3–12, [doi:10.1016/S0363-5023(81)80003-2](https://doi.org/10.1016/S0363-5023%2881%2980003-2). This exposed-rabbit-nerve experiment directly related applied local compression to intraneural microcirculation. It supplies no human postural threshold or clinical inference.
- Bodo Kurz et al., “Biosynthetic Response and Mechanical Properties of Articular Cartilage after Injurious Compression,” *Journal of Orthopaedic Research* 19, no. 6 (2001): 1140–1146, [doi:10.1016/S0736-0266(01)00033-X](https://doi.org/10.1016/S0736-0266%2801%2900033-X). Cartilage explants were compressed to the same final strain at different rates. Use for a rate-dependent injury branch and loss of later anabolic response; not a human threshold.
- Brandon A. The association examined is post-treatment and selected; noncorrespondence between venographic class and symptom relief does not make geometry irrelevant.
- Brandon Michael Henry et al., “Cervical Rib Prevalence and Its Association with Thoracic Outlet Syndrome: A Meta-Analysis of 141 Studies with Surgical Considerations,” *World Neurosurgery* 110 (2018): e965–e978, [doi:10.1016/j.wneu.2017.11.148](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.wneu.2017.11.148). The pooled comparison reported cervical ribs in 1.1 percent of healthy participants and 29.5 percent of participants with thoracic outlet syndrome. It supports association plus nonsufficiency of the broad variant; it does not test the composite's exact first-rib fusion, arterial wall state, or causal history.
- Brett D. Steineman et al., “In Pursuit of Quantifying Patient Knee Contact Mechanics: Finite Element Model Validation of Cadaveric Knees in Axially Loaded MRI Scans,” *Journal of Orthopaedic Research* 43, no. 6 (2025): 1132–1143, [doi:10.1002/jor.26077](https://doi.org/10.1002/jor.26077). Five cadaver-knee models were compared with electronic pressure-sensor measurements during static axial loading. Agreement and discrepancy were output- and specimen-specific; the study does not establish gait, in-vivo biology, or clinical outcome.
- Brian A. Nosek, Charles R. Ebersole, Alexander C. DeHaven, and David T. Mellor, “The Preregistration Revolution,” *Proceedings of the National Academy of Sciences* 115, no. 11 (2018): 2600–2606, [doi:10.1073/pnas.1708274114](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.1708274114). Preregistration helps distinguish planned confirmation from exploration; it does not make a construct, design, or execution valid.
- Brian P. Selgrade, Montakan Thajchayapong, Gloria E. Lee, Megan E. Toney, and Young-Hui Chang, [“Changes in Mechanical Work During Neural Adaptation to Asymmetric Locomotion,”](https://doi.org/10.1242/jeb.149450) *Journal of Experimental Biology* 220 (2017): 2993–3000. The Methods and figures report thirteen healthy participants in a ten-minute split-belt task, although the abstract reports fourteen. The study supports calculated, time-varying leg work during adaptation and changing work distribution, not tissue exposure or a universal optimization law.
- Bureau International des Poids et Mesures, [*The International System of Units (SI)*, 9th ed., version 4.01](https://doi.org/10.59161/AUEZ1291) (Sèvres: BIPM, updated June 2026). - Bálint Petró, Judit T. Nagy, and Rita M.
