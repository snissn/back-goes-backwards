## Part III synthesis — A taxonomy of questions, not defects

Part III has replaced the vague idea of bad loading with a set of more exact possibilities.

A system can remain within a viable range or exceed a current capacity. A demand can pass through a selected carrier, branch, fail to cross an interface, or recruit a substitute. Global geometry can change the available paths while the clinically relevant question remains a local contact, pressure, stress, strain, or excursion. A deformation can persist because an external, intrinsic, repeated, active, or mixed relation continues to maintain it. Stability can depend on timing, feedback, support, and recovery rather than stillness. A strategy can preserve immediate function while changing later exposure. A regional change can remain regional or enter a wider relation only through identifiable intermediate links.

None of these families is automatically pathological. Concentration can be ordinary. Constraint can be temporary. Variability can be useful or inadequate. Bracing can protect. Compensation can be successful. A visible difference can be irrelevant to the outcome under study. The language earns its place by making those distinctions possible.

The Part also establishes a temporal order. Present state and capacity shape routing. Routing and handoff shape local exposure. A response can alter the next configuration. Compensation feeds back into later distribution. What looks like an upstream cause in one time window can be a downstream consequence in another. A later observation cannot reconstruct the whole sequence by itself.

This is the limit of a purely mechanical taxonomy. Medicine requires more: tissue biology, symptoms, function, diagnosis, prognosis, care context, and the whole person. Part IV begins from mechanics already embedded in medicine and asks what longitudinal load routing adds. It must preserve every intermediate link developed here while remaining prepared to find that mechanics is secondary, mixed, or uninformative in a particular problem.
