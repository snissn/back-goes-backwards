## Part V synthesis — What a complete narrative changes

The four narratives have not produced one mechanical diagnosis. They have produced a more demanding form of explanation.

The geometry narrative showed that orientation and line of action can alter moments, contacts, and modeled fields while leaving internal allocation and clinical consequence open. The thoracic-outlet narrative distinguished a changed lumen or excursion from the relation that maintains deformation and from the condition-specific evidence needed for medical interpretation. The instability narrative separated motion, control, repeated demand, capacity, response, and symptom rather than treating instability as a visible defect. The mixed chronic narrative preserved several live accounts at once and showed why a strategy must be understood for what it protects before compensation is blamed for cost.

Across all four, the same result appeared in different forms: a coherent chain is not the same as an established chain. Boundaries, carriers, quantities, chronology, receiver state, biological response, and alternatives each require their own support. A negative or nonunique result is not a failure of the method. It prevents the framework from becoming a story generator that confirms itself.

The cases also clarify the current practical limit. Observation can motivate a comparison. A valid measurement can characterize one relation. Neither automatically creates a diagnostic threshold, a treatment target, or an individualized correction. Those applications require their own evidence, purpose, reliability, benefit, harm, and decision-value work.

Part VI gathers what can responsibly follow now. It begins with a portable set of questions, then separates education, environmental design, prevention, measurement, intervention development, implementation, and health-system change. The final task is equally important: distinguish the mechanical foundation completed in this volume from the stronger body-wide hypothesis that gives the series its title.
