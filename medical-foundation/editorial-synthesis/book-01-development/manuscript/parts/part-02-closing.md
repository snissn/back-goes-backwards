## Part II synthesis — Cooperation before priority

The architecture of support is plural. Skeletal geometry, joint contact, active force, tensile transfer, restraint, glide, containment, pressure, and control solve different parts of a task. Their contributions overlap and change through time. No tissue label supplies its role in advance.

This cooperative account strengthens rather than weakens the posterior case. The posterior body contains a large, layered set of muscles, tendons, fasciae, joints, skeletal relations, and control demands. Posterior structures participate materially in stance, locomotion, propulsion, lifting, deceleration, balance, transfer, and reaching. The exact contribution depends on speed, phase, environment, strategy, state, and the quantity being studied. Morphological continuity does not establish one cable, and task contribution does not establish universal dominance.

Three statements therefore remain separate. The posterior chain exists as a useful scope-declared anatomical system. Posterior structures are mechanically important in many tasks. Posterior participation may also prove necessary within the preferred organization investigated later in the series. Part II supports the first two at their proper scale. It does not use them to smuggle in the third.

The same boundary applies to hard and soft roles. A posterior structure can receive form; an anterior or pressure-mediated relation can preserve shape. A visible extension can use an internal distribution that differs from another visually similar movement. Good support is not the same as rigid support, maximum posterior activity, or one ideal alignment.

Part II has described successful and variable cooperation. That makes the next problem possible to state. What happens when demand exceeds current capacity, a carrier fails to engage, a compliant structure must preserve geometry it cannot readily release, or a strategy that protects function alters later exposure? Part III develops a vocabulary for those adverse organizations without turning them into diagnoses.
