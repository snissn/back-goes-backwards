# Book I drafting style and citation guide

**Status:** Wave 7 working decision record; governs controlled first drafts and will be frozen or amended after the Chapter 2 → 13 → 24 cross-slice audit
**Scope:** *The Back Goes Backwards*, Book I: *Medical Foundations*
**Applies to:** reader-facing chapter prose, source notes, text-native figure placeholders, captions, part transitions, and drafting-stage case or composite disclosures
**Does not do:** promote an evidence state, close an external review gate, approve final art, or replace a chapter brief, evidence pack, salvage sheet, or publication-gate inventory

## 1. Authority and purpose

This guide translates the [Book I contract](../book-contract.md), accepted [detailed outline](../../series-synthesis/books/01-medical-foundations.md), accepted [chapter briefs](../chapter-briefs/), shared [causal and evidence-state grammar](../planning/causal-chain-and-evidence-state-grammar.md), [later-book seams](../planning/later-book-seam-table.md), and [Wave 1 pilot closure](../decisions/pilot-closure-record.md) into one prose standard.

When sources conflict, use this order:

1. the Book I contract and series boundary;
2. the accepted detailed outline and the chapter's current brief;
3. current claim, model, example, figure, evidence, decision, and salvage records;
4. this guide for expression and manuscript form; and
5. legacy or generated material only within its recorded salvage disposition.

A style decision cannot repair a claim problem. If a sentence requires stronger evidence, a different model, a new medical inference, or a wider book boundary, reopen the governing record rather than making the sentence smoother.

## 2. Decisions at a glance

## 3. Reader and voice

### Reader altitude

Write first for a professionally trained, evidence-literate reader who may know medicine, rehabilitation, anatomy, biomechanics, movement science, engineering, or physics but not all of them. A serious nonspecialist should be able to follow the argument through cumulative definition, concrete examples, and diagrams.

Do not write as an anatomy textbook, mechanics survey, clinical manual, self-help guide, manifesto against medicine, or defense brief for the wider series.

### Governing voice

The voice is:

- **direct:** state the mechanical question and the proposed answer early;
- **physical:** use concrete bodies, contacts, boundaries, directions, phases, and times rather than abstract systems language alone;
- **technically exact:** name the actual force, moment, pressure, stress, strain, deformation, excursion, response, or endpoint when it matters;
- **intellectually generous:** represent medicine, engineering, traditional systems, movement disciplines, alternatives, and null findings fairly;
- **constructive:** show what a routing lens adds without claiming that existing fields ignore mechanics;
- **non-moralizing:** anterior, posterior, hard, soft, stable, variable, symmetrical, and asymmetrical are descriptions, not virtues; and
- **comfortable with partial results:** a bounded mechanism, mixed account, negative result, or no-added-value outcome can advance the argument.

Retain the author's applied-engineering directness. Translate informal language into inspectable terms at the point where it bears weight; do not sterilize every intuition before the reader can understand it.

### Person and address

- Use **we** for an explicit authorial proposal, a declared analytic choice, or a reasoning step the text is undertaking: “We use *load path* as a model of distribution.”
- Do not use **we** to imply scientific consensus, a universal body, or a shared clinical judgment.
- Prefer neutral declarative prose to frequent second-person address. Use “consider” or “imagine” only for a bounded thought experiment, never as a self-test or bodily instruction.
- Describe people as people, not as postures, diagnoses, impairments, or exemplary bodies. Name disability, assistance, pregnancy, aging, illness, training, and environmental constraint as legitimate contexts rather than departures from a default body.

### Sentence and paragraph practice

- Put the object and relationship in the sentence: “External contact altered the measured pressure distribution,” not “mechanics changed.”
- Prefer one causal move per sentence when evidence states differ.
- Lead a paragraph with its new work, develop it, then end with the conclusion or question that licenses the next paragraph.
- Use short sentences for controlling distinctions and longer sentences for qualified synthesis.
- Use an analogy only after naming its job; state its mismatch before the reader can transfer it too far.
- Avoid strings of *may*, *might*, *could*, and *potentially*. Use a precise verb and then state what is unknown.
- Do not turn every paragraph into a disclaimer. Put the limit beside the claim that needs it.

## 4. Reader-facing chapter architecture

The chapter brief controls exact content and order. The manuscript should not reproduce the brief's labels, metadata, or acceptance criteria.

### Standard movement

Most chapters should make six moves:

1. **Concrete opening.** Begin with an ordinary task, anatomical relation, measured observation, bounded analogy, documented case, or disclosed composite. Establish the live question without implying the answer.
2. **First distinction.** Define only the terms needed to prevent the opening from being misread.
3. **Mechanism or anatomy.** Develop the chapter's unique model, relationship, or structure in a sequence the reader can inspect.
4. **Application.** Use one primary example to make the model do work; do not accumulate examples as rhetorical votes.
5. **Countercase and boundary.** Place ordinary variation, an alternative path, a null, a noncorresponding observation, or a failed arrow inside the argument.
6. **Synthesis and handoff.** Reconstruct the answer, state what remains conditional, and give the next chapter one exact inherited question.

Five to eight substantive subsections are normally enough. Subsection titles should name reader-facing content, not internal functions such as *Job*, *Inputs*, *Core move*, *Allowed examples*, *Acceptance criteria*, or *Review gate*.

### Opening rule

The opening should occupy roughly 5–10 percent of the body draft. It must disclose whether its central object is:

- an ordinary observation;
- a thought experiment or hypothetical vignette;
- a bounded engineering analogy;
- a documented case; or
- a source-grounded composite.

The opening should create a question, not smuggle in a diagnosis, preferred technique, universal route, or later-book solution.

### Section rule

Each subsection owns one new move. It may recall an inherited definition in a sentence, but should not reteach the earlier chapter. A subsection ends when the reader has the conclusion needed for the next move; it should not continue merely to exhaust related material.

### Conclusion rule

The conclusion should normally occupy 5–8 percent of the chapter. It must:

- answer the central question at the chapter's permitted strength;
- distinguish what was established, proposed, reconstructed, or left open;
- avoid introducing a new example or citation-dependent claim;
- state the chapter's addition to the Part; and
- hand the next chapter a specific unresolved question.

Do not recap every heading. Do not end with a generic statement that “more research is needed.”

### Part architecture

Each Part requires a short reader-facing introduction that states its question, why it follows, and what capability the reader will gain. Its conclusion should synthesize the Part's cumulative result and make the next Part necessary. A Part is not complete if its chapters are individually sound but can be reordered without changing the argument.

The book introduction and final conclusion are written or finalized only after the body and Chapter 30 handoff are stable.

## 5. Evidence-state expression in clean prose

Control IDs and development labels never appear in reader prose. Preserve their distinctions with ordinary language.

| Internal state or source type | Preferred reader-facing construction | Avoid |
|---|---|---|
| Foundational mechanics or anatomy | “For a defined system, force balance requires…” / “The structure attaches…” with an authoritative source at first load-bearing use | “It is obvious,” or uncited specialist detail |
| Direct observation | “The image shows…” / “The investigators observed…” with modality, task, and time | Treating appearance as force, exposure, or cause |
| Measurement | “Ultrasound measured excursion under this protocol…” with unit and uncertainty when material | “The tissue experienced…” when the study measured only motion or geometry |
| Calculation | “Using the stated inputs, the calculation gives…” | Presenting a derived value as directly measured |
| Model | “In this model, under these boundary conditions…” | “The body does…” when the result belongs only to the model |
| Inference or synthesis | “Taken together, these findings support the interpretation…” followed by the live alternative | Blending several citations into a directly observed mechanism |
| Authorial hypothesis | “We propose…” / “The framework asks whether…” plus a discriminating observation or failure condition | Passive phrasing that makes a project proposal sound established |
| Engineering analogy | “The hose illustrates causal order…” followed by the deliberate mismatch | Using the analogy as biological evidence |
| Embodied observation | “The reported or embodied experience is…” and “this motivates the hypothesis…” | Turning felt continuity into a measured force or anatomical cable |
| Traditional provenance | “The distinction is motivated by…” with historical scope stated | Exact equivalence, scientific validation by correspondence, or dismissive caricature |
| Reconstructed history | “The present findings and dated events support a reconstruction in which…” followed by the unobserved interval | “Repeated exposure caused…” when the transition was never observed |
| Unresolved link | “The available evidence does not determine whether…” and name the missing observation | Treating absence of evidence as support or refutation by default |

`OBS`, `MEAS`, `CALC`, `MOD`, `INF`, `HYP`, and `EMB` may appear in a figure legend or technical table after they have been defined. They should not become prose decorations or substitutes for complete sentences.

### Causal sentence rule

When a paragraph crosses from task to configuration, distribution, local exposure, tissue response, function, symptom, or clinical interpretation, every crossing must be readable as a separate proposition. Do not let the fluency of a paragraph supply a missing arrow.

A later observation does not retrospectively prove an earlier state. A treatment response does not by itself prove origin. A mechanically coherent chain does not become a diagnosis through completeness of narration.

## 6. Citation and source-note system

### Reader-facing form

Book I uses **numbered chapter endnotes** and a **consolidated bibliography**. Draft Markdown uses footnote syntax as the transport format; production renders the notes at the end of each chapter unless a publisher's house style requires a mechanically equivalent placement.

Example source form:

```markdown
A measured change in geometry does not by itself identify the local stress field.[^02-01]

[^02-01]: Author(s), “Article title,” *Journal* volume, no. issue (year): pages, DOI or other persistent locator. The study measured [specific object]; it did not measure [materially confusable object].
```

The source label is chapter-scoped and stable in manuscript source. Only the rendered note number is reader-facing. Do not expose claim, model, example, figure, salvage, or decision IDs in the note marker or note text.

### What receives a citation

Cite:

- a technical definition at its first load-bearing introduction;
- anatomy or biomechanics that a nonspecialist could not verify from common knowledge;
- every numerical value, prevalence, cohort description, measured relation, and quoted phrase;
- every biological, medical, historical, or traditional proposition;
- claims about the state, limits, consensus, or heterogeneity of a field;
- facts in a documented case or composite; and
- the source of any adapted data or visual relationship.

The project's own declared vocabulary or hypothesis needs provenance disclosure, not a scientific citation that appears to validate it. Internal project documents do not enter the reader bibliography as evidence.

### Source selection

- Use authoritative texts, standards, or consensus sources for settled definitions.
- Use primary studies for exact measurements, cohorts, cases, and empirical arrows.
- Use systematic reviews for the state and heterogeneity of a field, without treating a broad construct as a test of a narrower one unless they actually match.
- Use current guidelines for clinical terminology, practice boundaries, and care context—not as proof of a proposed mechanism.
- Cite sources appropriate to the exact tissue, population, task, scale, direction, measurement, and time described.
- Generated prose, generated references, search snippets, legacy placeholders, and unsourced outline language are never evidence.

### Placement and scope

- Put the note marker after the sentence or clause it supports and after punctuation.
- A paragraph-final note may support the whole paragraph only when every sentence clearly derives from the same source and scope.
- When sources support different arrows, cite each arrow separately.
- When sources disagree, cite representative support and opposition where the disagreement enters; do not hide conflict in a late omnibus note.
- A single note may contain several sources when their jobs are stated or unmistakable. Order the most direct support first, then contextual or review sources.
- Use author names in prose only when authorship, chronology, or disagreement matters. Avoid routine parenthetical author-year clutter.

### Note content

Source notes primarily identify support. They may also carry brief method, population, or scope information needed to prevent misreading. A limitation essential to the argument belongs in the body, not only in a note.

At first use within a chapter, record a complete provisional citation and persistent locator. Later notes may use a short author-title form. The publication pass must normalize every entry against the consolidated bibliography and remove duplicate, incomplete, placeholder, or inaccessible references.

Use quotation sparingly. Quote only when wording itself matters, reproduce it exactly, include a page or location, and complete permissions review where required. Prefer accurate paraphrase for scientific findings.

## 7. Terminology and notation

### Governing terms

| Term | Book I use | Required restraint |
|---|---|---|
| *load* | Conversational umbrella for mechanical demand | Replace with force, moment, pressure, stress, strain, deformation, excursion, work, energy, or exposure when the distinction carries the argument |
| *demand* | A deliberately broad task- or environment-level requirement when the internal quantity is not yet known | Do not make it sound like a measured local exposure |
| *load path* | A bounded model of distribution and transfer through named carriers and a stated system | Not a literal hidden pipe, cable, meridian, or unique resultant |
| *routing* | Distribution of a defined demand among available structures and paths | State quantity, carrier, scale, task, and time when the claim becomes technical |
| *force line* | External line of action, inferred multicarrier relation, or embodied continuity; always state which | Never treat the three senses as synonyms |
| *hard role* | Relatively shape-preserving, force-transmitting, constraining, or structurally resolving in a declared relation | Not a fixed hard-tissue class, always beneficial state, or synonym for posterior or Yang |
| *soft role* | Relatively form-receiving or shape-conforming while retaining relevant yield, glide, containment, expansion, pressure response, or local change | Not weak, passive, unloaded, inferior, or synonymous with anterior anatomy |
| *configuration* | Geometry plus contact, active state, pressure, material state, and available movement at a phase | Not interchangeable with visible posture |
| *mechanical homeostasis* | Provisional term for maintaining relevant variables within viable dynamic ranges | Not a fixed set point, one posture, or universal health score |
| *capacity* | Current, context-dependent ability to tolerate and recover from a defined exposure | Not a permanent scalar property of a person or tissue |
| *constraint-maintained deformation* | A deformation or loss of excursion whose persistence requires a specified extrinsic, intrinsic, repeated, active, or mixed relation | Narrowing alone is not the mechanism; restoration of shape is not automatically recovery |
| *posterior chain* | A scope-declared anatomical and task-participating system | Anatomy, size, or task contribution does not prove universal priority |
| *adaptation* | A specified change through time | Do not use as the automatic, beneficial, or inevitable response to load; *response*, *accommodation*, *remodeling*, or another named change may be more accurate |
| *preferred* or *efficient* | Later hypothesis requiring a comparison class and defined objective or proxy | Not comfort, symmetry, frequency, visual straightness, or low effort by implication |
| *recovery* | A specified return or improvement in state, capacity, function, or outcome over a stated time | Kinematic reversal or release of a constraint is not sufficient by itself |

The canonical provenance wording is:

> The hard/soft distinction is motivated by traditional Chinese medicine's hard/soft and Yin/Yang concepts. Book I develops a flexible mechanical translation of that motivation; it does not claim that the traditional and mechanical categories are definitionally identical.

Use the wording once in full where the distinction is introduced. Later callbacks can say “the TCM-motivated hard/soft polarity” when provenance is material; they should not repeat a disclaimer in every chapter.

### Technical style

- Use SI units and a space between value and unit: `20 N`, `3 mm`, `5 kPa`.
- Define every symbol at first use in a chapter or explicitly recall its earlier definition.
- State system boundary, frame, reference configuration, sign convention, and time or phase wherever they affect meaning.
- Use *moment* as the default technical term; introduce *torque* parenthetically where it aids recognition.
- Put derivations in the body only when the steps carry the reasoning. Move supporting algebra to a note or technical appendix; do not hide a load-bearing assumption there.
- Prefer a small number of reused symbols to notation created for one paragraph.
- Spell out an acronym at first use in each chapter unless it is both ubiquitous and unambiguous.
- Use *asymptomatic*, *reference group*, *observed range*, or the actual comparison rather than *normal* when normative meaning is not established.

### House mechanics

Use U.S. English, the serial comma, sentence-case headings, and em dashes without surrounding spaces. Italicize a project term at its defining introduction and use roman type thereafter. Capitalize Yin and Yang; use `TCM` only after spelling out *traditional Chinese medicine*. Avoid scare quotes once a term has been defined.

## 8. Uncertainty, alternatives, and negative evidence

Uncertainty is part of the argument, not a haze around it. Name the kind:

- **unknown:** the proposition has not been determined;
- **unmeasured:** the required quantity was not acquired;
- **unobserved interval:** endpoints exist but the transition was not watched;
- **model-dependent:** the result changes with assumptions or boundary conditions;
- **variable:** results differ across tasks, people, states, methods, or times;
- **mixed:** evidence supports more than one causal contribution or temporal order;
- **low-specificity:** the finding occurs with and without the proposed outcome;
- **not supported:** the available evidence fails to support the stated proposition; or
- **outside scope:** the present chapter or evidence pack does not decide it.

Do not substitute “unclear” for these distinctions.

Alternatives enter at the node or arrow they change. Name what observation could distinguish them. Include ordinary variation, successful adaptation, recovery, reverse causality, common cause, measurement error, and a low- or no-mechanical-priority result where applicable.

Do not treat a broad null as a refutation of a meaningfully different narrow construct. Do not preserve a hypothesis by changing its definition after every contrary result. State the construct, comparator, and disfavoring result before interpretation.

## 9. Cases and source-grounded composites

### Reader disclosure

A documented case is presented as one documented case. A hypothetical vignette is called hypothetical. A composite must be disclosed at its first appearance in language such as:

> The following account is a source-grounded teaching composite. Its anatomy, findings, and chronology are drawn from the cited records and are combined to make the reasoning sequence inspectable; it is not the history of one person and should not be read as a typical presentation.

Adapt that sentence to the actual construction. Do not call a composite a “case” without the modifier once the distinction could be forgotten.

### Internal provenance requirements

Before composite prose is drafted, its non-rendered provenance record must map every consequential detail to:

- a specific published source or an explicitly hypothetical element;
- source type, population, date, and measurement;
- whether the detail is observed, measured, calculated, inferred, reconstructed, or supplied only for exposition;
- any change of wording, sequence, or context;
- the reason the sources can be combined without implying a single patient or phenotype; and
- the alternatives, missing intervals, privacy issues, and clinical gates that remain.

Never invent a decisive exposure, chronology, negative test, treatment response, or outcome to make a chain complete. Do not combine mutually incompatible demographic, anatomical, disease, treatment, or temporal details. Do not borrow arterial evidence for a venous or neural arrow, or transfer a conduit finding to a sliding interface by analogy.

### Reconstruction language

Separate:

1. what is observed now;
2. what dated event or prior state is documented;
3. what interval is reconstructed;
4. which alternative histories remain possible; and
5. what the reconstruction cannot establish.

Clinical and vascular review, case provenance, privacy, causal wording, and source freshness remain publication gates even when a bounded first draft is authorized.

## 10. Text-native figure placeholders

Figures in the first draft are arguments, not decoration and not provisional medical art. Use plain Markdown tables, fenced text diagrams, or simple original vector-ready schematics. Do not copy a published image merely because it is available for reference.

### Placeholder form

Use this reader-visible draft form:

```markdown
> **Figure 2.1 — One task, several system boundaries [text-native first-draft figure]**
>
> [Plain-text, table, or schematic representation. Every load-bearing node and arrow names its evidence state or unresolved status.]
>
> **Caption.** State the teaching claim, the relevant system and conditions, and what the figure does not establish.
>
> **Text alternative.** Describe the sequence, branches, comparisons, evidence states, and missing links rather than only the visible objects.
```

The reader number and title are provisional until the Part is assembled. The associated figure registry retains the stable internal identity outside reader prose.

### Figure rules

- Every figure has one teaching job.
- Use redundant text, shape, and line style; never rely on color alone.
- Distinguish observed anatomy, measured quantity, calculation, model output, inference, hypothesis, embodied report, and open link.
- Show alternatives beside the arrow they affect.
- Distinguish current state from reconstructed history.
- Do not use a red pinch, glowing line, posture silhouette, or smooth body-wide cable as a complete mechanism.
- Put units, frame, phase, assumptions, and source near the represented quantity.
- Caption the figure's principal limit.
- Write a meaningful text alternative during first drafting, not after final art.
- Keep published source images out of the draft unless license, attribution, adaptation, and clinical-review decisions are already recorded.

A placeholder may advance to final art only after its technical or anatomical content, evidence-state encoding, provenance, rights, and accessibility have passed their required publication gates.

## 11. Later-book seams

Book I should be candid about the wider program and logically independent of it.

### Permitted movement

At an assigned seam, Book I may:

1. name the motivation or provenance;
2. state the later hypothesis directly;
3. say what it would add to the Book I foundation; and
4. identify what the later book must define, measure, compare, or risk losing.

### Prohibited movement

Book I may not use preferred configuration, posterior necessity, signed generalized modes, the all-Yang state, Six-Division or channel mapping, breath geometry, torus dynamics, bandhas, or practice as:

- an unstated premise of foundational mechanics;
- evidence for hard/soft, posterior function, a tissue response, or a named condition;
- the missing arrow in a composite or worked narrative;
- a diagnostic observation, bodily test, correction, protocol, or outcome; or
- retrospective proof that the title is true.

Chapter 3 owns the early hard/soft provenance. Chapter 4 may preview generalized modes only to prevent category errors. Chapters 7–9 establish posterior anatomy and task participation without preferred necessity. Chapter 16 may name wider system questions without completing them. Chapter 29 routes the research program. Chapter 30 owns the direct series disclosure and Book II handoff.

Use one meaningful signpost at the first necessary point and a controlled callback at the handoff. Do not preview the entire series in every chapter.

## 12. Word count, density, and continuity

### Count convention

The accepted chapter brief supplies the target range. Across the 30 current briefs, the ranges total **128,000–158,500 body words**. This is a planning envelope, not a mandate to fill space.

Count chapter body prose, displayed equations explained in prose, and reader-facing tables. Exclude:

- chapter title and running metadata;
- numbered source notes and bibliography;
- figure captions and text alternatives;
- figure-placeholder internals scheduled for replacement; and
- non-rendered control, provenance, or publication-gate records.

Draft inside the chapter's range when possible. If a chapter is more than roughly 10 percent outside its range, record whether the cause is missing argument, duplicated inheritance, an overgrown example, necessary evidence exposition, or a brief that should be revised. Do not pad a short chapter or compress an indispensable causal bridge merely to hit a number.

### Density

- Ordinary language precedes notation.
- One primary model or worked relation should carry a technical section; do not stack equations for atmosphere.
- Define only terms that do work later.
- A note may hold supporting detail, not a premise required to understand the conclusion.
- Tables compress repeated comparisons; prose explains why the comparison matters.
- Figures carry spatial or causal relationships that prose would otherwise repeat.

### Continuity rules

1. Start from the prior chapter's exit state, not from zero.
2. Recall an inherited definition in one sentence or a compact table; do not reteach it.
3. Each chapter contributes one unique job, one primary example, and one exact handoff.
4. Reuse the canonical causal chain in the same order unless a chapter explicitly analyzes feedback or reverse direction.
5. Keep the six adverse-organization families named consistently after Part III introduces them.
6. Parts I–II use the recurring object transfer cumulatively. Chapter 8 completes its main technical use; Chapter 9 widens and ends it. Chapters 10–12 may use one compact callback, and Chapter 27 may retrieve it once for synthesis.
7. A recurring figure adds only the layer owned by the current chapter.
8. Part introductions promise; Part conclusions show the payoff and hand the next Part its question.
9. Chapter 30 summarizes argumentative gains, not all chapter contents.
10. During the continuity audit, delete a passage if its only job is to remind the reader that the book has a thesis.

### Terminology owners

| Material | Primary owner | Later use |
|---|---|---|
| Mechanical quantities, boundaries, evidence notation, and force-line senses | Chapter 2 | Recall and apply |
| Hard/soft roles and traditional motivation | Chapter 3 | State relative roles; do not redefine |
| Configuration, frames, and load paths | Chapter 4 | Name the active frame and path assumptions |
| Mechanical homeostasis, capacity, dose, and time | Chapter 10 | Apply to histories and examples |
| Continuity, handoff, interruption, and substitution | Chapter 11 | Use stable route-state language |
| Constraint-maintained deformation | Chapter 13 | Apply by system without pooling evidence |
| Exposure-to-response bridges | Chapter 19 | Inherit in medically exposed chapters |
| Causal scope, alternatives, and whole-person placement | Chapter 21 | Apply rather than repeat disclaimers |
| Wider-series hypothesis and direct Book II handoff | Chapter 30 | Earlier chapters only signpost assigned components |

## 13. Controlled first draft versus publication

### Gate matrix

| Area | Controlled first draft | Publication-ready manuscript |
|---|---|---|
| Structure | Accepted chapter job, sequence, boundaries, and handoff | Whole-Part and whole-book continuity accepted |
| Claims | Only material authorized by the current packet, at its recorded posture and exclusions | Load-bearing wording matched to completed evidence and review decisions |
| Sources | Traceable source notes and persistent links for every factual or empirical proposition | Bibliography normalized; citations complete, current, accurate, accessible, and audited |
| Mechanics and models | Assumptions and limits visible; unresolved publication review recorded | Required formal, dimensional, biomechanics, and model review complete |
| Anatomy and measurement | Bounded sourced prose within the packet; no invented precision | Anatomy, method, measurement, and population review complete |
| Medical material | Non-diagnostic, non-advisory, evidence-bounded drafting where specifically authorized | Medical-coauthor and condition-specialist review complete; safety and care boundaries accepted |
| Cases and composites | Form disclosed; every consequential detail traceable; reconstructed intervals visible | Provenance, privacy, clinical framing, causal wording, and source freshness approved |
| Figures | Original text-native placeholder, draft caption, and text alternative | Final art technically reviewed; provenance, rights, accessibility, and caption audit complete |
| Traditional and later-book material | Canonical provenance and non-load-bearing signpost only | Historical/translation, authorial-intent, and series-seam review complete where required |
| Editorial residue | No prompts, control IDs, migration notes, generated language, or visible drafting instructions | No unresolved placeholder, stale note, hidden scaffold, or production marker remains |

No external specialist review is implied by a polished first draft. The draft should be readable enough to review and bounded enough that a later correction can narrow or replace a passage without collapsing the chapter's valid job.

### First-draft hard stops

Stop and reopen the relevant control before drafting a passage when:

- it introduces a new load-bearing numerical, anatomical, biological, medical, diagnostic, prognostic, treatment, prevention, or later-book claim outside the current packet;
- its source cannot be verified or belongs only to generated or placeholder material;
- an equation is dimensionally inconsistent or lacks a declared system, variables, assumptions, or boundary conditions;
- a clinical detail cannot be assigned to a source or clearly hypothetical/composite element;
- an analogy or another tissue system is being used as evidence;
- the prose completes an unobserved causal interval without reconstruction language;
- the passage becomes a self-test, corrective cue, protocol, care substitution, or individualized recommendation; or
- it contradicts the contract, accepted chapter job, or series boundary.

An open publication gate is not itself a first-draft hard stop when the controlling packet has authorized bounded use and the unreviewed material is visibly limited or excluded.

## 14. Per-chapter drafting packet and exit check

Before writing, the drafter reads:

1. the chapter brief;
2. its controlling definitions and models;
3. claim, example, and figure records that bear weight;
4. the current evidence pack or source table;
5. the locator-level salvage sheet;
6. relevant later-book seam and continuity controls; and
7. the explicit first-draft authorization and remaining publication gates.

Before a chapter leaves first draft, verify:

- [ ] The opening's status—ordinary, hypothetical, analogy, documented case, or composite—is clear.
- [ ] Every subsection advances the central question and has a necessary successor.
- [ ] Reader prose contains no control IDs, editorial prompts, acceptance criteria, migration notes, or generated scaffolding.
- [ ] Every factual or empirical proposition has a scoped note; no raw URL or placeholder reference remains.
- [ ] Standard relation, observation, measurement, calculation, model, inference, authorial hypothesis, analogy, embodied report, traditional provenance, and reconstruction remain distinguishable.
- [ ] Task, configuration, demand, distribution, local exposure, response, function, finding, symptom, diagnosis, and treatment have not collapsed.
- [ ] Alternatives, nulls, reverse direction, and whole-person modifiers enter where they change the chain.
- [ ] Any composite is disclosed and fact-level provenance remains complete outside reader prose.
- [ ] Figures are text-native, original, captioned, accessible in text, and no more certain than the claims they represent.
- [ ] Hard/soft retains the shape-preserving/form-receiving meaning and its TCM motivation without exact identity.
- [ ] Later-book material is direct but non-load-bearing and appears only at its assigned seam.
- [ ] The conclusion answers the chapter and hands forward one exact question.
- [ ] Length and technical density match the brief or the variance has an editorial reason.
- [ ] The post-draft salvage audit finds no promoted generated sentence, invalid equation, invented citation, or quarantined certainty.
