# Chapter 27 — Mechanical questions and observations in clinical reasoning

## Questions do not become a test by being ordered

Return once more to the ordinary transfer used throughout this book. A person lifts a familiar object from one support, carries it several steps, and places it on another support. The task is simple enough to recognize and rich enough to expose a recurring error: a visible action does not reveal one internal mechanical story.

The first difference is the system boundary. For the combined person-and-object system, the hand–object interaction is internal. For the person alone, the object acts across the boundary. For the object alone, the hands act across the boundary in the other direction. A boundary around a forearm, joint, or tissue changes the accounting again. Moving inward is not merely zooming in on the same force diagram. It creates a different system with different boundary actions.[^27-01]

The second difference is the level of the claim. A nearly steady carry can have little acceleration while substantial forces remain present. A calculated net joint force or moment can be compatible with several combinations of muscle, ligament, contact, and pressure. An observed trunk shape can coexist with different active strategies, supports, histories, and receiver states. Each description may be useful, but none licenses the next description automatically.

This chapter condenses the method of Book I into seven questions. Their order matters because it makes missing links visible. Their answers do not produce a score, a mechanical diagnosis, or a treatment choice. An answer can support one proposed link, weaken it, redirect attention to another chronology, leave several accounts unresolved, or stop the route entirely. The sequence is therefore a reasoning scaffold: a way to ask what is known, what follows under stated assumptions, and what has not yet been established.

Confidence belongs to propositions, not to the narrative as a whole. A task history can be well documented while its internal distribution remains unknown. A local exposure can be measured while its role in symptoms remains weak. A symptom can change reproducibly with support while the initiating history remains unresolved. The account should therefore say which arrow changed and why. It should not award points for the number of mechanically interesting observations or average unlike evidence into one confidence rating.

The questions are also recursive. A proposed path may require a smaller boundary, which creates a new demand question at that scale. A changed strategy may lead back to the history to ask when it appeared. A measurement limit may require a coarser conclusion rather than another inference. Recursion is useful only while each pass names a more precise object or a consequential uncertainty. When it merely adds anatomical detail without discriminating among accounts, the scaffold has reached a stop.

## 1. What demand entered the system?

Begin with the event rather than the presumed fault. What task occurred? What was supported, accelerated, resisted, contacted, carried, or stabilized? Over what phase and interval? Which boundary is under study? What environment, assistance, equipment, pace, repetition, and recovery opportunity belonged to the event?

These questions do not require a complete force record. They require the description to remain at its actual resolution. *Lifted a box from the floor* names an event but not the object's mass, acceleration, hand forces, foot contacts, or segment resultants. *Worked at a computer for six hours* names a broad context but not an uninterrupted exposure, a particular configuration, or a local tissue dose. *Pain began after work* orders two reports coarsely but does not establish what demand preceded the change.

The boundary must be stated before forces and paths are named. During the object transfer, the external demand on the person includes the object's action, ground contact, and any other support. Within the person, force and moment resultants depend on how the body is divided for analysis. A stationary or slowly moving phase does not mean zero mechanical demand; it means that the net force and moment balances have a particular relation to acceleration. Conversely, visible motion does not tell us which internal structure bore the demand.

History belongs here because one event can meet a receiver changed by previous events. Task frequency, duration, variation, prior injury or illness, activity, sleep, care, equipment, and time to recover can change the boundary conditions or the state that receives the next demand. They should not be compressed into a generic *mechanical history*. Each item enters at a named link: task opportunity, present capacity, response, behavior, or another condition that may shape both the task and the outcome.

The first question can stop the inquiry. If the task cannot be identified, if the relevant interval is unknown, or if the proposed exposure is inferred only from a symptom or later finding, the account may remain a plausible research question but cannot advance as an observed demand history. A more detailed story is not necessarily a more accurate one.

## 2. What configuration and paths were available?

Configuration describes the relation among structures, contacts, supports, and active states during a declared part of the task. It is more specific than a posture label and less specific than an internal stress field. The useful question is not merely *what shape was visible?* It is *what mechanical options did that relation make available under these boundary conditions?*

Joint geometry can change moment arms and contact. Foot placement can change the external line of action. A handrail can add a support path. Active stiffness can preserve one relation while permitting motion elsewhere. Posterior structures may participate substantially in one task and minimally in another, but their visible prominence or presumed absence is not itself a finding of deficient routing. Anterior, posterior, lateral, rotational, pressure-mediated, and externally supported contributions remain possible until the task and quantities distinguish them.

Ordinary observation can describe configuration, but it must name the object observed. Agreement for video-based posture observations varies with the parameter and the categorization scheme; broadening a category may improve agreement while reducing resolution.[^27-02] Thus *rounded*, *shifted*, or *asymmetric* is not yet an operational observation. A description needs a segment, reference, direction or plane, task phase, observer or instrument, and uncertainty.

Touch does not bypass this requirement. A systematic review of low-back palpation found poor reliability for bony and joint-mobility palpation, inconsistent reliability for soft-tissue procedures, and limited validity evidence across the included methods.[^27-03] That evidence is region- and procedure-specific, not a ban on touch. It does block a stronger shortcut: palpation cannot be treated as direct access to an otherwise hidden whole-body force path without a validated method for that use.

Several internal paths can produce a similar external action, and one path can serve several external actions. Configuration narrows a possibility space; it rarely selects a unique solution. The route should stop here when the proposed path is only drawn onto anatomy, when no carrier or physical quantity is named, or when the visible relation does not distinguish the proposed route from ordinary alternatives.

## 3. How were hard and soft roles divided?

The hard-and-soft distinction asks how mechanical labor was divided within the declared interaction. A relatively hard role is shape-preserving or structurally resolving at the selected scale, direction, and time. A relatively soft role is form-receiving: yielding, conforming, accommodating, containing, or permitting glide while remaining mechanically active and consequential.

These are roles, not tissue bins. Bone deforms. Muscle can create a shape-preserving path through active tension. A ligament can preserve a relation while elongating. Cartilage can receive form while distributing pressure. A pressurized vessel can receive surrounding shape while its wall sustains stress and its contents affect its behavior. External support can take part of a shape-preserving role that would otherwise be distributed within the person.

The project's compact intuition—load wants to travel through the hard body so that the soft body can remain soft—becomes useful only after it is translated into a local question. Which demand? Which relatively shape-preserving assembly? Which form-receiving structure? Which direction, quantity, phase, and duration? *Remain soft* does not mean unloaded. It means retaining the deformation, glide, containment, or pressure-bearing behavior required for that structure's function without presuming a harmful retained state.

The division can change during one task. During the object transfer, the ground, skeleton, active muscle, joint geometry, connective tissue, and object can share shape-preserving work in different phases. Muscle, tendon, cartilage, vessel, skin, abdominal contents, and other interfaces can receive and redistribute form. The vocabulary does not determine that one division is ideal, that posterior participation is always required, or that a form-receiving role is a failure.

This question earns its place when it identifies a relation that can be examined. It loses force when *hard* simply means good and *soft* means burdened, or when a tissue name substitutes for a measured quantity. If the proposed role cannot be tied to the task and receiver, the route should remain at the level of configuration rather than advance through an assumed role failure.

## 4. Where, if anywhere, might local exposure have accumulated?

External demand is not local exposure. Between them lie configuration, contact, active contribution, material properties, and internal sharing. A segment-level force or moment is a resultant: it balances the declared free body without revealing the unique forces that produced it. Estimating individual muscle forces, for example, requires assumptions about recruitment, anatomy, optimization, and model validity because several internal solutions can satisfy the same net demand.[^27-04]

The receiver and quantity therefore have to be named. A joint surface may receive contact pressure and shear. A tendon may receive tensile stress and strain. A vessel wall may deform under pressure-derived and surrounding actions while lumen geometry and flow remain separate objects. A nerve may experience deformation, excursion, pressure, or altered interface behavior. *Load* can remain useful in general prose, but a consequential local claim must say whether it means force, moment, pressure, stress, strain, deformation, contact, work, or another quantity.

Accumulation adds time. A large brief exposure, a smaller sustained exposure, repeated cycles, variable loading with recovery, and a retained material state are not interchangeable. The relevant dose may include magnitude, direction, rate, repetition, duration, sequence, and recovery, while capacity may change through adaptation, illness, injury, fatigue, learning, care, or ordinary variation. No scalar *load burden* should be invented merely because the full vector or history is difficult to measure.

The phrase *if anywhere* is load-bearing. A changed configuration can redistribute demand without creating an adverse concentration. A local field can be heterogeneous and ordinary. A tissue can deform and recover. A proposed exposure can be too small, too transient, poorly measured, or unrelated to the selected outcome. Another biological or contextual process can dominate the receiver's response even when a mechanical exposure is real.

This question may support a bounded mechanical proposition while stopping before medical consequence. It may establish that geometry plausibly changed an internal distribution, yet leave tissue response unknown. It may reveal that a calculated surrogate failed to track a measured internal quantity. It may also find no receiver-specific exposure at all. None of these endings licenses a diagnosis or correction.

## 5. What, if anything, did the changed strategy preserve?

A difference becomes a compensation only when there is something it helped preserve under a changed condition. The preserved object might be task completion, balance, comfort, tissue tolerance, vision, breathing room, confidence, participation, or another valued function. Without a prior state, a perturbation or constraint, and an outcome that remained possible, *compensation* can become a retrospective label for any difference.

Protection comes before cost. A person who uses a handrail, reduces excursion, changes pace, recruits another region, or avoids one task may be preserving safety, capacity, participation, or comfort. That strategy may be temporary or durable. It may consume more effort, narrow options, redistribute exposure, or have no demonstrated cost. Successful preservation and adverse redistribution are separate propositions.

Chronology matters. If pain, illness, fatigue, fear, medication, or environmental constraint preceded the changed strategy, the strategy may be a response rather than the origin of the selected outcome. If a change preceded symptoms, it still does not become causal unless a relevant carrier, exposure, response, and time relation are supported. A shared change in work, sleep, resources, or activity may produce both the strategy and the outcome. The mechanically coherent story must compete with these orders rather than absorb them as minor modifiers.

This is where confirmation pressure is most dangerous. In one bounded experiment, confirmatory information search was associated with persistence in an incorrect preliminary diagnosis among psychiatrists and medical students.[^27-05] In another vignette experiment, family physicians' evaluation of incoming evidence could be distorted toward a leading hypothesis.[^27-06] Neither study estimates bias in biomechanical reasoning. Together they justify a simple discipline: record what changed and what remained possible before naming the change compensation, and state an observation that would favor a reverse, adaptive, shared-cause, or incidental account.

The question can end affirmatively with *useful adaptation*, *no demonstrated cost*, or *preserved function with unresolved tradeoffs*. It can also end with *nothing has yet established that this difference preserved anything*. Compensation is earned by the relation among state, task, change, and preserved outcome; it is not presumed from asymmetry, muscle activity, stiffness, or continued task performance alone.

## 6. What changes with task, support, time, or state?

Variation can help distinguish a relation, but modulation is not origin. If a symptom, movement, pressure, or task tolerance changes when support changes, the result may show that the present system is mechanically modifiable. It does not by itself establish why the problem began, which internal variable changed, or whether repeating the condition would be beneficial.

Compare only what the question can support. A change across tasks may identify task specificity. A change with an external support may show that boundary conditions matter. Repeated observations may estimate within-person variability. A time series may place a change before or after another event. Recovery after rest may reveal state dependence. None of those comparisons becomes a tissue mechanism without the intermediate measurements.

The relevant state is wider than local anatomy. Pain, attention, expectation, confidence, sleep, illness, medication, training, previous care, work control, assistance, and opportunity for exposure can change movement, force, participation, report, or recovery. They can be causes, consequences, shared determinants, or modifiers at different links. Calling the account whole-person does not mean adding them as a checklist; it means locating each one where it can change the proposed sequence.

Negative and noncorresponding results refine the next question. If a configuration changes while the selected internal measure does not, the path from configuration to that quantity weakens. If a report changes while a selected kinematic record does not, the two observations should remain distinct. If a local mechanical state changes without the predicted function or symptom, the later arrow may stop. If the same outcome occurs across materially different histories, a proposed unique chronology loses priority.

Task modulation can also reveal capable variation. More than one strategy may meet the same demand. A stable strategy may be effective under one support and costly under another; a variable strategy may be adaptable or simply noisy. The book's vocabulary does not assign value from variability, symmetry, stiffness, posterior participation, or range alone. The declared task, receiver, outcome, and timescale decide what the variation means.

## 7. What can actually be measured, and what remains uncertain?

The final question applies to every preceding one. An ordinary observation, an embodied report, a research instrument, a validated clinical measure, a calculation, a model output, an inference, a reconstruction, and a hypothesis are different evidence objects. They can inform one another without becoming interchangeable.

Measurement begins by defining the measurand or construct, method, conditions, result, and uncertainty. Metrology distinguishes measurement from its result and separates repeatability from reproducibility; many clinical and embodied constructs are not physical quantities, but they still require an operational definition appropriate to their use.[^27-07] The FDA–NIH BEST resource likewise separates biomarkers from clinical outcome assessments and distinguishes patient-, clinician-, observer-, and performance-based outcomes while making validation dependent on context of use.[^27-08] A sensor output, image, report, and performance therefore belong in separate columns unless evidence justifies a relation among them.

Measurement properties answer different questions. Reliability concerns discrimination relative to variation. Agreement and measurement error concern how close repeated results are in their units. Validity concerns whether the interpretation represents the intended construct or quantity. Responsiveness concerns detection of change. Interpretability concerns what a value means. A consensus taxonomy separates these domains rather than allowing one favorable coefficient to validate an instrument wholesale.[^27-09] Agreement and reliability can even lead to different impressions because reliability depends partly on heterogeneity in the sampled population.[^27-10]

A detectable change is not automatically important. A small difference may exceed technical error yet add no meaningful information; a large reported change may remain nonspecific for mechanism. Foundational work on minimally important difference made the person's perspective and consequences of change central, but it did not create one universal threshold for individual decisions.[^27-11]

The distance from observation to clinical use is longer still. Screening is an organized population activity with a target condition, follow-up, quality, benefit, burden, and harm obligations; seven conceptual questions do not meet that definition.[^27-12] Diagnosis is an iterative, collaborative process embedded in a health-care work system, not the output of one mechanical measure or narrative.[^27-13] Even statistical discrimination or accuracy does not encode the consequences of acting on a result.[^27-14]

Uncertainty should be attached to the link it affects. Unknown task history limits the demand claim. Nonunique internal sharing limits the path claim. Measurement error limits change. Poor construct validity limits interpretation. Missing chronology limits origin. Nonspecific modulation limits mechanism. Lack of incremental value limits use even when the measurement itself is sound.

The scaffold should stop when the proposed observation is not operational, the internal distribution is nonunique and unmeasured, the receiver or quantity cannot be named, response chronology is missing, error is too large for the intended distinction, modulation is equally compatible with alternatives, or the result adds nothing consequential to an existing account. It should also yield when another concern has higher immediate priority, when the question lies outside the user's role, or when a validated domain-specific method should replace a generic mechanical scaffold.

## Four narratives, four different endings

The worked narratives in Part V did not demonstrate the same proposition four times. They tested different portions of the sequence and ended at different evidentiary boundaries.

Chapter 23 established a strong, bounded relation among task, geometry, internal distribution, and local fields in selected knee and lumbar contexts. It also showed that an external surrogate can fail to track a measured internal force and that an imposed interface change can have no detected effect on selected contact measures. The route became less complete when it approached living tissue history, symptoms, and clinical meaning.

Chapter 24 began with focal anatomy, current arterial findings, distal occlusion, collaterals, and a pressure difference observed together in a source-grounded teaching composite. It therefore carried a clinically consequential chain farther than a generic corridor example could. Yet repeated deformation, wall transition, and the full untreated chronology remained reconstructed, while healthy anatomy and waveform comparisons prevented anatomy or positional change from becoming sufficient.

Chapter 25 treated repeated demand as demand applied to a changing receiver. Passive restraint, task-level relation maintenance, and perceived instability could correspond or diverge. Prospective studies supplied selected temporal order without a complete mechanism, and proximal kinematic observations did not automatically become proximal tissue exposure. Useful adaptation, retained change, new event, mixed state, and unresolved history all remained available.

Chapter 26 used a transparent hypothetical rather than an empirical case or teaching composite. Its office-work and nonspecific-low-back-pain chronology was constructed to compare possible orders, not to establish that one route occurred in a person or population. The mechanical account could end as substantial, mixed, reverse-causal, adaptive, low priority, or unresolved, and no regional carrier was established merely because the story was mechanically coherent.

## A scaffold that is allowed to stop

The seven questions improve reasoning only if they remain vulnerable to answers the project did not prefer. They ask for the task before the path, the configuration before the local exposure, the preserved outcome before the presumed cost, and the evidence state before the clinical interpretation. They make a mechanical account easier to examine because its arrows are exposed.

They do not establish that every clinical history needs a longer mechanical explanation. A concise existing account may already contain the relevant information. The proposed carrier may be unmeasurable at the required scale. A mechanical relation may be real but secondary for the selected outcome. Further inquiry may add burden, anxiety, inequity, opportunity cost, or false precision without improving understanding or consequence. Stopping under those conditions is not failure; it is part of the method.

If the sequence is ever developed beyond a conceptual scaffold, attractive wording will not be enough. Its constructs, intended users, contexts, comparators, measurement properties, alternatives, harms, access, incremental value, and consequences will need staged development and evaluation. Guidance for complex interventions emphasizes iterative work across context, theory, stakeholders, uncertainty, implementation, and evaluation; it does not validate this sequence or dictate that it become an intervention.[^27-15]

For now, the outcome is narrower and useful. A reader can describe a demand, declare a boundary, separate visible configuration from internal distribution, ask how shape-preserving and form-receiving roles were divided, name a possible receiver and quantity, test whether a changed strategy preserved anything, compare task and state, and stop at the strongest supported link. Chapter 28 carries that discipline into education, environments, and prevention claims, where clearer language can be valuable before individualized correction or medical effect has been established.
