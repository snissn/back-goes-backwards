# Chapter 29 — Building the research and development program

## One pathway, six different questions

Chapter 24 followed a focal relation in which surrounding anatomy and a later arterial state could be observed together, while the repeated deformation proposed to connect them remained reconstructed. That missing interval is not merely an imperfection in one narrative. It is a productive object of work. The question is what kind.

A mechanics study could ask whether a declared boundary condition deforms the wall during a specified task. A measurement study could ask whether that deformation can be acquired reliably and whether the signal represents the intended construct. An education study could ask whether learners distinguish surrounding demand, wall response, lumen, flow, symptom, and diagnosis. Therapeutic development could ask whether changing a declared boundary condition improves an outcome that matters to patients and whether the proposed intermediate changes with it. Implementation research could ask whether a sufficiently mature method can be delivered with fidelity, access, and acceptable burden. Health-system development could ask whether the accumulated component adds enough value to justify changes in training, workflow, financing, or policy.

Those are six different questions. Their answers can inform one another, but none contains the others. A reproducible deformation does not create a clinical measure. A reliable measure does not identify a diagnosis. A well-understood explanation does not improve health by comprehension alone. A beneficial intervention does not prove the mechanism proposed for it. Successful delivery does not establish benefit, and widespread adoption does not establish system value.

The framework developed in this book therefore needs more than a list of promising studies. It needs a development architecture in which every project earns a declared output and in which the limit of that output travels with it. Existing research frameworks already reject a simple one-way march from idea to implementation. Updated Medical Research Council guidance treats development, feasibility, evaluation, and implementation as iterative work shaped throughout by context, program theory, stakeholders, uncertainty, refinement, and economic considerations.[^29-01] The National Institutes of Health Stage Model likewise permits recursive movement among intervention generation, mechanism work, efficacy, effectiveness, and implementation rather than prescribing one irreversible sequence.[^29-02] Neither framework supplies the exact architecture used here. They establish the more general point: different kinds of maturity can develop, fail, and return upstream without rising together.

This chapter organizes the project into exactly six tracks: research, measurement, education, therapeutic development, implementation, and health-system development. The tracks form a network, not a staircase. The aim is not to reduce ambition. It is to let the program become more ambitious without allowing one attractive result to carry claims it never tested.

## A portfolio, not a staircase

Each track has a distinct immediate question, output, and stopping condition. A project can occupy more than one track, but only when its aims and results remain separable. A study might measure both patient outcomes and delivery fidelity, for example. That combination does not turn one outcome into evidence for the other.

The matrix makes two kinds of dependency visible. Some are necessary. Implementation requires an object mature enough to deliver, and health-system development requires components whose benefit, burden, and intended use have been characterized well enough to compare with existing practice. Other dependencies are merely useful. Education can teach established distinctions before a new sensor exists. Measurement can qualify a quantity for research even if no intervention follows. Research can disfavor a proposed mechanism while an independently supported educational tool remains useful.

For that reason, the program has no single maturity score. Readiness always completes a sentence: ready to describe under one protocol, ready to compare groups, ready to test a mediator, ready to teach to one audience, ready to evaluate for benefit, ready to deliver in one setting, or ready to inform a system decision. The same object can be mature for one use and immature for another. A motion estimate might be adequate for comparing two laboratory tasks while remaining too reactive or nonspecific for clinical interpretation. A plain-language explanation might be ready for a comprehension study while the underlying authorial hypothesis remains untested. Recording those unequal states is more informative than placing the project on one ladder.

Multi-track projects need the same discipline. Their aims should be declared separately, their methods assigned to the outputs they can support, and their stopping conditions applied independently. If one component fails, the others do not inherit failure automatically; if one succeeds, the others do not inherit success. A combined design is useful when shared recruitment, timing, or context improves the questions. It becomes dangerous when logistical proximity is mistaken for causal or evidentiary unity.

An output should be judged by the decision it was designed to inform. A precise instrument can fail as a clinical tool and succeed as a laboratory measure. A curriculum can improve causal-language accuracy without changing behavior. A beneficial intervention can be worth preserving after its proposed mediator fails. This use-specific account of success is the organizing principle for all six tracks.

## Track 1: Research

Here, the research track has the narrower job of testing proposed physical and causal relations; every other track also requires research methods appropriate to its own question. This track begins with one declared relation along the book's causal chain: task and environment, configuration and available paths, external demand and internal distribution, local exposure, material or biological response, and a later finding, symptom, function, participation, or outcome. A study should state which link it observes, which it measures or calculates, which it represents with a model, and which it leaves open.

The six adverse-organization families developed in Part III generate concrete questions. Does a change in geometry redistribute a defined force or moment? Does an interface concentrate stress or strain under a stated condition? Does a segment fail to maintain a relation during a task? Does timing alter a handoff between named carriers? Does an imposed boundary maintain deformation or reduce excursion? Does a compensation shift exposure, preserve performance, or do both at different times? These are candidate relations, not generic searches for proof that biomechanics matters.

Design follows the relation. Anatomical and material studies can establish that a structure or property exists without establishing clinical importance. Perturbation and within-person studies can compare distribution under declared tasks without recovering a complete internal route from surface motion. Longitudinal observations can place exposure before response while leaving causal alternatives. Simultaneous multiregion measurement can test a proposed carrier; adjacency or shared timing alone cannot. A prospective comparison can ask whether mechanical history adds information beyond an existing model, but association and added information remain different from decision value.

Exploratory work belongs in this program. So does confirmatory work. The distinction should be recorded before interpretation rather than reconstructed after an appealing result. Preregistration can help separate planned tests from exploratory analyses, although it cannot repair an invalid construct or poor execution.[^29-03] The aim is not procedural purity. It is to prevent an observation that generated a hypothesis from reappearing as if it had independently confirmed it.

A good research question also says what would lower its priority. If predicted redistribution is absent across an adequate range, the relation should narrow or retire within that domain. If several configurations produce equivalent selected outcomes, the result is nonunique; it does not select one route. If a local change is observed but no regional carrier is measured, the result stops locally. If compensation lowers rather than raises exposure, the model must retain that protective result. An unmeasured hidden whole-body mechanism cannot become the default destination for every null.

Nonsignificance alone is not evidence of equivalence. Equivalence testing requires a substantively justified range of effects small enough to treat as unimportant and a design precise enough to test that range.[^29-04] The program should therefore distinguish a negative result within a prespecified meaningful range from an inconclusive result caused by poor precision, inadequate exposure, missing data, or a weak construct. “Further research” becomes useful only when the unresolved quantity and the next discriminating design can be named.

The research track succeeds when it makes the causal chain more specific. That success can be positive, negative, mixed, nonunique, or local. Its product is a better-bounded relation—not an automatic measure, diagnosis, intervention, or institutional claim.

## Track 2: Measurement

Measurement turns a proposed quantity or construct into an inspectable acquisition and interpretation problem. The first question is not whether a device produces a number. It is what that number is meant to represent, within which system, at what scale and phase, under which protocol, and for what use.

At least seven claims should remain separate. The signal can be acquired. Its disagreement and error can be estimated. It represents the intended construct. It distinguishes relevant states or changes when the construct changes. The change is interpretable relative to error and a meaningful reference. Using it improves a declared decision. Its burden and reactivity are acceptable. A project may support one claim and fail the next.

Measurement science offers established distinctions without supplying a ready-made load-routing instrument. The COSMIN taxonomy separates reliability, measurement error, validity, and responsiveness while also treating interpretability and feasibility as distinct concerns.[^29-05] Reporting guidance for reliability and agreement studies requires the sample, observers, repetitions, measurement process, and estimates to be explicit; repeatability is not truth.[^29-06] The living FDA–NIH BEST glossary makes context of use central and distinguishes biomarkers, clinical outcome assessments, endpoints, validation, and clinical utility.[^29-07] These frameworks arose in particular measurement domains. Their distinctions transfer more readily than their thresholds.

A minimum record for a mechanical measure names the construct, unit, direction, boundary, scale, phase, and time. It declares whether the intended use is description, comparison, state tracking, mechanism testing, prognosis, diagnosis, decision support, or outcome measurement. It specifies acquisition, calibration, preprocessing, missingness, reference, known confounds, error, generalizability, and decision consequence. It also asks whose bodies, tasks, devices, assistance, and settings were represented.

Burden is part of validity for use. A sensor can alter the movement it intends to observe. Repeated imaging can impose cost and access barriers. A monitoring system can change attention, behavior, privacy, and power. A composite score can conceal incompatible quantities behind an apparently simple result. Digital precision should never substitute for a defined construct.

This chapter does not create a whole-body efficiency score, a misrouting score, a diagnostic threshold, or a treatment-selection instrument. Those objects first require an operational state, a comparison class, transformation rules, measurement properties, and disfavoring evidence. A measure can mature for one narrow research use without becoming a proxy for the framework or the person.

## Track 3: Education

Chapter 28 proposed mechanical literacy as a way to describe task, support, duration, repetition, variability, recovery, control, options, and uncertainty. The education track asks whether a selected audience actually learns or communicates something better through that language. It does not use assent to the framework as an endpoint.

The first record names what is taught, to whom, compared with what, for which direct outcome, over what interval, and with which possible harms. Public audiences, patients, clinicians, movement professionals, engineers, researchers, and educators cannot be treated as interchangeable. Health literacy is shaped by organizational resources and social conditions as well as individual knowledge and competencies; responsibility for intelligibility and access therefore belongs partly to the system providing information.[^29-08]

Educational outcomes form no automatic chain. Satisfaction, recall, comprehension, reasoning accuracy, communication, behavior, clinical outcome, and system outcome are separate. Medical-education research has usefully distinguished studies that describe what occurred, compare whether an approach worked, and investigate how or why it worked.[^29-09] A memorable phrase can fail an accuracy test. Accurate classification can fail to change communication. Changed communication can fail to affect care. Each result remains valuable at the level it measured.

For this project, candidate direct outcomes include whether learners distinguish task from configuration, external demand from internal distribution, and local exposure from symptom or diagnosis. Another study could test whether they identify a missing carrier in a body-wide account, ask a better load-history question, calibrate uncertainty, or stop a mechanical explanation when it adds little. These are educational questions about the framework, not evidence that the framework's untested mechanisms are true.

Adverse outcomes belong beside benefit. Teaching can increase fear, pain vigilance, shame, body surveillance, jargon burden, professional overconfidence, or the belief that one body shape is correct. It can exclude disabled or assisted movement, erase useful discipline-specific language, or shift responsibility from an institution to an individual. A well-liked program that does not improve accuracy is an educational null. A program that improves knowledge while increasing fear is mixed or adverse. Either result changes the curriculum, audience, or claimed use; neither decides the underlying mechanics.

Education can therefore move early and independently. Established mechanics, evidence-state distinctions, and person–task–environment reasoning can be taught without waiting for a novel measure or therapy. What cannot move with them is an unearned promise of behavior change, clinical competence, health benefit, or prevention.

## Track 4: Therapeutic development

The wider project contains a range of assessment and practice ideas. Preserving them as development objects is different from presenting them for use. Therapeutic development begins only after a candidate has a declared population, purpose, comparator, provider, outcome, time horizon, and safety burden. Book I supplies no examination sequence, corrective cue, exercise, breathing practice, movement system, protocol, diagnosis, or treatment recommendation.

The first task is to make the candidate reproducible enough to evaluate. TIDieR asks authors to describe why an intervention is proposed, what it contains, who provides it, how and where it is delivered, when and how much is given, how it is tailored or modified, and what fidelity is achieved.[^29-10] Complete description does not establish benefit. It prevents an intervention name from hiding several materially different objects.

Prospective trial protocols and complete reports have their own obligations. SPIRIT requires the intended questions, methods, outcomes, analyses, oversight, and registration of randomized trials to be specified transparently.[^29-11] CONSORT requires corresponding clarity in reports, including participant flow, outcomes, analyses, protocol information, and deviations.[^29-12] Neither standard can turn an unsuitable design into good evidence, but both make it harder for the tested object or endpoint to change silently after results appear.

Benefit and harm must be sought with comparable seriousness. Updated CONSORT harms guidance emphasizes planned ascertainment, analysis, and reporting, including how recurrent and serious events were handled.[^29-13] “No harm observed” is interpretable only in relation to how harm was elicited, how many people were observed, and for how long. Access, cost, disability, language, cultural fit, professional scope, co-interventions, adherence, and the burden of repeated attention also belong to the candidate's record.

An outcome effect and a mechanism effect are different objects. Process evaluation separates delivery, mechanisms of impact, and context.[^29-14] Causal mediation analysis adds assumptions about how a proposed intermediate and outcome are related; measuring both before and after an intervention is not sufficient by itself.[^29-15] If function improves while the proposed mechanical mediator does not, the benefit should be retained and the mechanism claim weakened. If the mediator changes while a patient-important outcome does not, the mechanical result remains and therapeutic use remains unsupported.

Study purpose also affects what can be inferred. PRECIS-2 asks designers to align eligibility, recruitment, setting, organization, delivery, adherence, follow-up, outcome, and analysis with a more explanatory or pragmatic aim.[^29-16] A tightly controlled study can ask whether a candidate produces a change under selected conditions without establishing ordinary-care effectiveness. A pragmatic study can estimate performance in a real setting without isolating a mechanism. The useful design is the one fitted to the immediate question.

Therapeutic development is therefore not the track where a plausible mechanical idea becomes advice. It is where the proposed object becomes specific enough to fail. Feasibility, safety, efficacy, effectiveness, mechanism, comparative value, and scope can then mature independently.

## Track 5: Implementation

Implementation begins with a sufficiently mature object and asks what happens when it enters a real setting. The object might be an educational module, a research or clinical measurement procedure, an environmental design, or an intervention. The implementation strategy is separate: training, facilitation, documentation, reminders, financing, staffing, workflow redesign, or another means of delivery must be named rather than folded into the object.

Implementation outcomes are not clinical outcomes. A widely used taxonomy distinguishes acceptability, adoption, appropriateness, feasibility, fidelity, implementation cost, penetration, and sustainability from service and patient outcomes.[^29-17] RE-AIM separately draws attention to reach, effectiveness, adoption, implementation, maintenance, and the representativeness of both participants and settings.[^29-18] CFIR organizes possible determinants across the intervention, outer setting, inner setting, people, and implementation process.[^29-19] These frameworks help organize questions; completing one does not prove successful or equitable delivery.

The implementation record asks who is offered the object, who is excluded, which settings adopt it, what core function is preserved, what form changes, who controls adaptation, what resources are required, whose workload increases, and whether effects persist. It compares the proposal with the actual existing workflow, not with an imagined system that contains no mechanical reasoning. It also examines unintended effects: surveillance, stigma, data extraction, professional boundary conflict, low-value follow-up, displacement of effective care, and unequal access.

Effectiveness and implementation questions can be examined in one prospectively designed study. Hybrid designs formalize that possibility while retaining separate aims and outcomes.[^29-20] If an effective object has poor reach or fidelity, implementation can return it for redesign without erasing the efficacy estimate within its tested domain. If delivery is strong but patient outcomes are null, adoption cannot rescue effectiveness. If benefit appears despite low measured fidelity, the definition of fidelity, the theory of the object, and alternative contributors all require review.

Equity is not a late subgroup table. The Health Equity Implementation Framework places recipients, providers, the encounter, organizational context, and wider social conditions inside the implementation problem.[^29-21] For this project, that means asking who defined the target, whose movement was treated as the reference, who can refuse monitoring, who bears time and financial costs, who has access to alternatives, and who controls the resulting data and decisions.

An implementation failure is information about a relation among object, strategy, setting, and people. It does not automatically disprove the underlying mechanism. Successful implementation is equally bounded. It shows that something was delivered under stated conditions, not that it should be adopted everywhere or that it produced system value.

## Track 6: Health-system development

The project's institutional ambition is direct: medicine may eventually benefit from a more coherent account of demand, routing, exposure, capacity, recovery, and person–task–environment relations across existing disciplines. That horizon is not permanently limited to a minor adjunct. Its present posture is additive and comparative because durable change must show what it adds, duplicates, displaces, or worsens.

Health-system development begins with mature components rather than with allegiance to a doctrine. Possible objects include a shared vocabulary that improves a declared reasoning outcome, a measure qualified for a particular decision, an intervention with favorable benefit and harm relative to alternatives, or an implementation strategy that reaches its intended population without disproportionate burden. No single specialty, device, assessment, therapy, traditional system, or preferred-state model needs to own the field.

Decision frameworks make the additional burden visible. GRADE Evidence-to-Decision work for health-system and public-health questions considers effects, resources, equity, acceptability, feasibility, and implementation while making the basis of judgments explicit.[^29-22] The World Health Organization describes health-technology assessment as a multidisciplinary evaluation of direct and indirect consequences for policy decisions.[^29-23] Such frameworks structure judgment. They do not fill missing evidence, choose society's values, or favor this project in advance.

A system proposal must therefore address opportunity cost. Training consumes professional time. Documentation can displace attention. Measurement infrastructure creates financial, privacy, and maintenance burdens. A new label can trigger unnecessary follow-up. A service can fragment care or duplicate work already performed well. An apparently equitable program can distribute benefit, inconvenience, risk, and decision power unevenly.

*Overdiagnosis* has a narrow meaning: a condition is identified correctly but would not have caused symptoms or harm during the person's lifetime. It should not be used as an umbrella for every project risk.[^29-24] False positives, misdiagnosis, detection of inconsequential variation, overtreatment, surveillance burden, and low-value care should be named separately when those are the actual concerns.

System development can begin incrementally. Shared language, research infrastructure, data standards, or interdisciplinary questions may warrant evaluation before disease-specific prevention or reimbursement claims. Later milestones might include improved histories, validated measures, effective and equitable interventions, better coordination, justified design changes, and cumulative evidence strong enough to revise broader medical models. Each milestone is a new empirical and normative question, not a reward granted for completing the previous track.

The overhaul ambition survives precisely because it has stopping conditions. A component that adds no value over existing care should not displace it. A system that expands low-value measurement, worsens access, or imposes more burden than benefit should narrow or stop. Institutional scale must be earned by system outcomes.

## Provenance must travel

The six tracks prevent one kind of result from becoming another. A second control prevents a result from losing its history during a legitimate handoff. Every claim, model, measure, curriculum, candidate intervention, implementation strategy, and institutional proposal needs a compact provenance record.

Origin matters without fixing destiny. An embodied observation can generate a task or construct for measurement. If a later experiment finds a reproducible relation, the measured relation gains external support; the original experience does not become a direct measurement of anatomy or force. A traditional correspondence can motivate a comparison without serving as proof of mechanics. A theoretical extrapolation can be corrected, formalized, and tested, but the initial extrapolation does not become a scientific source. Favorable evidence upgrades the tested proposition, not everything upstream of it.

A handoff should therefore read like a compact contract between tracks. It names the result being transferred, the domain in which it was obtained, the uncertainty that remains, the stronger inference still prohibited, and the new question the receiving track will ask. Measurement might receive a task-specific mechanical relation from research and ask whether a proposed quantity represents it. Therapeutic development might receive a qualified measure and use it only as a mediator, not as a diagnostic label. Implementation might receive evidence of benefit for a named population while retaining uncertainty about another setting. Health-system development might receive a mature component while reopening cost, access, opportunity cost, and governance. At each crossing, the receiving track adds a burden; it does not erase the burden already carried.

Scientific-data stewardship is one part of this record. National Institutes of Health policy requires prospective planning for data management and appropriate sharing, including standards, repositories, and justified limits.[^29-25] The wider provenance record also needs versions, materials, code, analysis plans, deviations, conflicts, consent, cultural and community governance where applicable, and a route for negative findings. Data availability alone does not guarantee interpretability or responsible reuse.

Retirement also requires a record. A candidate can re-enter after new evidence, a materially revised construct, or a demonstrated method failure. It cannot re-enter merely because the same null has been redescribed at a more abstract level. Preserving an idea in the program keeps its history and possible route forward; it does not endorse it.

Suppose research demonstrates a reproducible task-specific change in local geometry. That result supports the local relation under its tested conditions. Suppose the measurement is reliable but poorly specific to symptoms. It can remain useful for description or mechanism research without becoming diagnostic. Suppose an educational module improves learners' separation of geometry from diagnosis. That is an educational success, not confirmation of the pathway. Suppose an intervention improves function but the proposed geometry does not change. The benefit remains; the mediation claim weakens. Suppose the intervention is effective in a selected study but cannot be delivered with adequate reach or equity. The efficacy estimate remains bounded while implementation returns the object upstream. Suppose a mature component adds no value over existing care. The system track stops even if earlier results remain sound.

The reverse is also possible. A careful research study might find no declared deformation across the relevant task range. That result can retire one route without erasing the general concept of constraint-maintained deformation in other structures and conditions. A local mechanism can fail while the wider load-routing framework remains useful. The project does not need every hypothesis to survive in order to develop.

## Negative results are program results

A disciplined program does not treat every unfavorable result as the same kind of failure. The destination depends on which claim was tested and why the result was negative, mixed, or inconclusive.

Mixed results deserve the same specificity. An education program can improve accuracy and increase fear. An intervention can improve one outcome and worsen another. An implementation strategy can increase adoption while reducing access. A system change can help one group and transfer cost or surveillance to another. The correct response is not to average these into an overall impression but to retain each outcome, its importance, and its distribution.

An inconclusive result also needs a cause. The method may lack precision. The construct may not have been valid. The exposure range may have been too narrow. Attrition or missingness may have changed the comparison. Outcomes may conflict. Two models may be observationally equivalent. The next project should target that uncertainty specifically—or the program should stop spending effort on an object that cannot yet be made discriminating.

This result grammar protects the project's breadth. A null does not have to destroy the entire series, so it can be accepted without rhetorical rescue. A positive result does not validate the entire series, so it can be used without inflation. The program advances by changing the exact proposition tested.

## Ambition through division

Book I has produced more than a general request for further study. It has defined relations worth testing: how task and configuration alter distribution; how local exposure depends on carrier, geometry, direction, dose, state, and time; how maintained deformation, instability, handoff, and compensation can be observed without becoming diagnoses; how tissue response and whole-person context modify clinical meaning; and how education and environmental design can be evaluated without promising a universal correction.

The six tracks turn those questions into a durable program. Research earns bounded relations. Measurement earns qualified quantities for declared uses. Education earns audience- and outcome-specific learning results. Therapeutic development earns separate feasibility, safety, benefit, harm, mechanism, and comparative results. Implementation earns setting-specific delivery evidence. Health-system development earns institutional decisions based on mature components, added value, opportunity cost, equity, governance, and burden. Each output can move only through an explicit handoff, carrying its provenance and limits.

This architecture also gives stronger future hypotheses somewhere honest to live. Preferred configuration, signed whole-body modes, traditional correspondences, breath and pressure geometry, embodied observation, and practice can enter research, measurement, education, therapeutic-development, implementation, or system programs appropriate to their actual claims. Inclusion does not validate them. It prevents their preservation from being confused with recommendation and their ambition from being confused with readiness.

The result is a program capable of genuine revision. A pathway can narrow. A measure can remain descriptive. A curriculum can be withdrawn. A beneficial intervention can survive a failed mechanism. An implementation strategy can return upstream. An institutional proposal can stop for lack of added value. None of those outcomes requires denying the mechanical dimension of life or forcing unrelated parts of the project to compensate.

The final chapter can now state the wider series hypothesis directly. Its task is not to borrow authority from this development architecture. It is to distinguish what Book I has already earned from what the preferred-configuration, coordinate, breath, and practice programs must still define, compare, measure, and risk losing.
