## Hard/soft terminology audit

The chapter states the direct formulation once:

> Load wants to travel through the hard body so that the soft body can remain soft.

Its immediately adjacent controls are sufficient for this first draft:

- the sentence is called an organizing proposition rather than a law of motion;
- *load* is identified as umbrella language that must become a named mechanical quantity in technical use;
- the distinction is explicitly **motivated by** traditional Chinese medicine's hard/soft and Yin/Yang concepts;
- the mechanical roles are declared a flexible translation rather than a definitionally exact identity;
- hard is relative shape preservation, force transmission, constraint, or structural resolution in a stated relation;
- soft is relative form reception and shape conformation while retaining task-relevant freedom;
- softness is not weakness, passivity, anterior location, zero stress, or zero load;
- bone, active muscle, tendon, ligament, fascia, cartilage, pressure-supported boundaries, and coordinated assemblies can perform shape-preserving roles;
- one structure can change roles with direction, activation, pressure, task, phase, scale, and time; and
- the observability paragraph requires a candidate assembly, a measured receiving freedom, an altered or compared surrounding relation, method/variation control, restoring behavior, and separately measured function.

The tendon–SSCT interpretation is especially bounded. Tendon is proposed as a tensile, relatively shape-preserving carrier along the declared direction; the adjacent interface is proposed as form-receiving and available for relative movement. Both carry demand and both move. The cited studies support their measured motion, not the role vocabulary, TCM correspondence, or a body-wide law.

## Scope and term audit

The required term search covered *load*, *hard*, *soft*, *stress*, *strain*, *pressure*, *deformation*, *constraint*, *excursion*, *narrowing*, *compression*, *impingement*, *glide*, *friction*, *recovery*, *diagnosis*, *treatment*, *exercise*, *protocol*, *posterior*, and *breath*.

- *Load* is umbrella language only in the direct authorial formulation and nearby explanation; technical passages name the actual quantity or say *demand*.
- *Hard* and *soft* appear only as relational roles or inside the disclosed traditional motivation.
- *Stress* and *strain* name material quantities or explicit non-equivalences; neither becomes an intensifier, symptom, or fluid-flow synonym.
- *Pressure* distinguishes wall, internal/external, contact, fluid, and boundary roles; it is never equated with flow.
- *Constraint* always refers to a current or candidate limiting relation, not all contact or every smaller range.
- *Excursion* remains task- and reference-specific; *glide* does not silently replace displacement, friction, shear stress, or health.
- *Narrowing* and *compression* are not used as complete mechanisms or guaranteed harms.
- *Impingement* does not appear in reader prose.
- *Recovery* occurs only to distinguish a presently unmeasured endpoint from kinematic return.
- *Diagnosis* and *treatment* occur only in boundary statements saying the chapter does not provide them.
- *Exercise*, *self-test*, and *protocol* do not appear as reader action.
- *Posterior*, preferred configuration, all-Yang organization, Six-Division mapping, breath-as-driver, bandha, and torus dynamics are absent.
- The ordinary verb *breathes* appears once to illustrate time-varying human boundary conditions; no breath model or technique is implied.

No term is used to conceal a jump from geometry to pathology or from an engineering model to clinical consequence.
