## Demand, exposure, and capacity audit

The draft preserves the audited sequence in both prose and Figure 10.3:

```text
task-level demand
    -> configuration and boundary conditions
    -> measured or modeled selected-system exposure
    -> exposure relative to capacity for the same system and outcome
    -> separately observed response or outcome
```

The distinction appears before any longitudinal interpretation. *Demand* remains a task- or environment-level requirement at the chosen boundary. *Exposure* remains the resulting mechanical history at a selected tissue, interface, segment, or modeled subsystem. Capacity is then related to that exposure, not compared directly with an object label or whole-task description.

The body adds the required scale ladder. External contacts and motion can be measured; segment forces and moments can be calculated; internal sharing and local exposure generally require a declared model; biological or functional response requires separate evidence. The same object mass can enter different moment arms and support relations. The same net joint moment can be shared through different active, tensile, skeletal, pressure-mediated, passive, and external contributors. The prose never presents a modeled local quantity as directly observed.

Capacity is qualified by system, exposure, outcome, state, and time. The examples distinguish:

- tendon-specimen integrity from whole-task completion;
- maximal force from repeated submaximal performance;
- joint excursion from symptom tolerance;
- preservation of output from restoration after the task; and
- person-only boundaries from systems that include assistance or environmental support.

The threshold passage keeps the endpoints separate. A material failure criterion can be sharp and commensurate within a bounded test. A performance limit may be gradual or multidimensional. A symptom-tolerance threshold concerns a different observation. The text does not infer that these thresholds coincide.

The word *reserve* survives as the authorial systems intuition but is controlled. It means a currently available margin to preserve a declared function under perturbation or added demand. The reader is immediately required to ask: reserve of which system, relative to which exposure, for which outcome, state, and time? The draft provides no scalar subtraction and no invisible reserve inferred from visual dislike.

## Mechanical-homeostasis audit

The chapter begins with bounded literature rather than silently promoting the project term. The tissue-equivalent review supports establishment, maintenance, and restoration questions in cell-seeded constructs and records uncertainty about sensed variables and targets. The tissue-and-organ review supports the importance and incompleteness of mechanical-homeostasis modeling. The reader-facing extension to a task system is explicitly introduced as Book I's synthesis.

The draft prevents five common collapses:

1. viable range does not mean a fixed pose;
2. movement, asymmetry, temporary deformation, fatigue, and assistance can occur inside a viable range;
3. a tissue-scale homeostatic question does not establish whole-person homeostasis;
4. function at one scale does not show that every local variable stayed in range; and
5. a changed workable state need not be a return to the earliest measured state.

The model has a failure condition. It must identify variables and a comparison, support an observable or modelable distinction, and risk narrowing or replacement by established stability, tolerance, or control constructs. The draft does not preserve the proposal by redefining every result as hidden compensation.

The wider preferred-organization program appears only once in the conclusion. It is named directly as a later question about whether some whole-body organizations manage demand more efficiently. The paragraph states that viable-range mechanics neither needs nor supports that hypothesis. No posterior, directional, TCM-coordinate, breathing, torus, bandha, or practice claim carries Chapter 10.

## Time, performance, and recovery audit

The exposure record retains magnitude, direction, rate, duration, repetition, variability, sequence, task phase, rest, recovery interval, starting state, and uncertainty. It is explicitly a record whose components may have different units and predictive value. The cumulative passage is especially important for the author's longitudinal program: it keeps chronology available while refusing to equate cycle count, work, impulse, time above a level, and fatigue-damage estimates.

The source-grounded task examples remain bounded:

- the ten-participant handgrip protocol supports sequence dependence and partial recovery of one voluntary-force measure during a lower-intensity phase;
- the fatigue review supports task specificity and continued submaximal performance despite reduced maximal capacity; and
- the five-participant repetitive-lifting study supports stable task performance and modeled lumbosacral torque alongside changed fatigue-related muscle characteristics.

The prose extracts only the shared distinction: task output and internal state can diverge. It does not pool the protocols, generalize the samples, identify hidden tissue damage, or derive a whole-body dose law.

Recovery is variable-specific. The cadaveric disc study supports creep and slower unloaded recovery under one axial protocol. The living magnetic-resonance study supports morning-to-evening variation in selected indices and sensitivity to measurement time. The draft does not claim that the latter study measured prior daily exposure, that ordinary loading caused harm, or that either study supplies a rest interval.

Kinematic return, material state, capacity, physiology, comfort, and future function remain distinct. Recovery can mean return toward an earlier value, return into a task-acceptable range, restoration of performance, or establishment of a changed baseline. The criterion follows the question and is not presumed to be the earliest observed state.

## Outcome and load-mismanagement audit

The chapter retains the negative side of the book's thesis without making it the baseline. Tendon-fatigue models, controlled animal bone loading, and cartilage mechanobiology make adverse and divergent tissue responses mechanically intelligible. Human tendon intervention evidence establishes that longer-term property changes can occur under repeated loading. Each source remains tied to its tissue, species, protocol, and endpoint.

Figure 10.4 gives equal visibility to:

- no material observed change;
- successful short-term accommodation;
- practice or altered coordination;
- longer-term adaptation or remodeling;
- transient fatigue with recovery;
- sensitization or symptom change;
- accumulated material change or injury; and
- immediate task or material failure.

The prose then defines the categories narrowly enough to keep them from becoming one continuum. Accommodation is current task success, not necessarily a durable change. Practice concerns performance or coordination. Adaptation and remodeling require specified change through time and are not automatically beneficial. Fatigue is measure- and task-specific, not damage by definition. Injury requires separate structural or biological evidence. Symptoms require their own mechanism and context. Material and task failure are different endpoints.

The archive's stronger concerns are protected forward. The text states that later chapters will test whether modest, repeated, or poorly resolved demands can accumulate, shift among carriers, precede symptoms, or contribute to later consequences. It assigns interrupted handoff, concentration, maintained constraint, instability, and compensation to their own chapters and assigns tissue-specific biology and condition-specific meaning to later bridges. The concerns are neither deleted nor used as conclusions before those bridges exist.

Nulls and alternatives remain inside the model rather than in a closing disclaimer. Different strategies can support the same task without adverse change in the observed period. Practice can alter coordination. Support can change exposure without changing symptoms. The selected mechanical measure can change little while symptoms change. A chronological observation does not identify the local exposure or causal bridge. These branches prevent the framework from making every exposure confirm load mismanagement.

## Environment, assistance, and hard/soft audit

Environment is represented through concrete mechanical entries: contact, constraint, support, available motion, timing, sequence, repetition, pace, floor, handle, seat, backrest, shelf, tool, visual target, vibration, sport rule, device, and another person. The NIOSH source is used as occupational guidance for multidimensional assessment, not as a local tissue model or individual risk calculator.

The prose gives environmental factors no universal sign. A surface can improve precision and increase local contact. Variability can distribute one exposure and add control demand elsewhere. Consistency can preserve accuracy or maintain the same exposure. Work organization can alter chronology even when workstation geometry is unchanged. Nonmechanical effects of social or institutional conditions are acknowledged rather than reduced to the mechanical variables they may change.

Assistance is treated as part of the system boundary. A cane, orthosis, exoskeleton, cart, handrail, chair, or second person can change contacts and available routes, lower one exposure, add another, alter performance, or make participation possible. The text does not use assistance as evidence of a deficient body or imply that an unassisted version is the proper comparator.

The hard/soft vocabulary receives one compact application rather than a redefinition. Shape-preserving and form-receiving roles can each have task- and direction-specific capacity; both can carry demand, and their roles can change. Soft is not zero load. Hard is not zero deformation. No fixed tissue class, posterior identity, traditional correspondence, or moral rank enters Chapter 10. Because the traditional provenance was fully introduced in Chapter 3 and is not material to this chapter's new claim, the canonical wording is not repeated.

- living structure meets demand from a history-dependent state;
- magnitude, direction, rate, duration, repetition, variability, sequence, support, and recovery can distinguish exposures;
- the same task can produce different internal exposure;
- task completion and internal state can diverge;
- adaptation and adverse accumulation remain possible rather than automatic;

- assistance and external support are real carriers;
- compensation before visible failure remains a question for later chapters;

The reconstruction supplies missing intermediate steps rather than ablating ambition. “Cumulative load” becomes an ordered exposure record whose candidate summaries must be justified. “Capacity exceeded” receives a system and outcome. “Silent weakening” becomes an unresolved trajectory requiring exposure, response, and chronology. “Modern life” becomes measurable contact, support, timing, repetition, and work organization. “Reserve depletion” becomes a qualified change in ability to preserve a named function. The stronger versions remain visible at their assigned later homes.

## Internal decision and next action

Chapter 10 passes its controlled first-draft gate. It opens Part III with a neutral but consequential dynamic baseline: the same nominal task can enter a different history; task demand becomes selected-system exposure only through configuration and boundary conditions; capacity is current and outcome-specific; recovery is variable-specific; environment and assistance are part of the system; and repeated exposure can precede null, beneficial, mixed, or adverse trajectories.

The draft preserves the author's concern with load mismanagement and delayed consequence without making harm the default. It also preserves the wider preferred-organization program as a direct but non-load-bearing later question. No claim depends on a universal score, fixed pose, hidden reserve, or medical shortcut.

The draft is ready for Chapter 11's continuity-and-handoff work and for later Part III integration. It is not publication-approved. Chapter 11 may now ask how demand is handed among available carriers and what evidence distinguishes successful transfer, ordinary redistribution, interruption, bypass, and reassignment.
