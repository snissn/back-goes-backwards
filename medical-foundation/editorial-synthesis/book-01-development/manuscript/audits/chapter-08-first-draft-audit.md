# Chapter 8 first-draft audit — the physics and ordinary function of posterior support

**Unit:** `BGB-U-0008`

**Draft audited:** [Chapter 8 — The physics and ordinary function of posterior support](../chapters/08-physics-ordinary-function-posterior-support.md)

**Status:** complete controlled first draft as of 2026-07-11; internally validated against the accepted brief, evidence packet, salvage sheet, recurring-task specification, Book I contract, and current manuscript standard; independent biomechanics, anatomy, evolutionary, medical, accessibility, source, figure, and authorial review remain publication gates

## Editorial result

The draft takes the intended strong position:

> Posterior structures make large and sometimes decisive contributions to defined phases of upright support, locomotion, object transfer, braking, energy management, and balance recovery.

It does not neutralize that statement into the observation that all structures participate. The chapter names gluteal, hamstring, spinal-extensor, plantar-flexor, Achilles-tendon, and posterior shoulder-girdle functions across ordinary tasks. It also preserves the exact limit that makes the claim defensible: every contribution belongs to a task, phase, boundary, quantity, geometry, state, evidence class, and model.

The chapter does not establish posterior exclusivity, one lifting or sitting technique, injury prevention, a universal energy advantage, or the preferred posterior organization investigated in Book II. It directly distinguishes posterior anatomical location from force direction, moment sign, tissue role, extension motion, and later Yang sign.

## Narrative and seam audit

The opening explicitly identifies its scene as the book's recurring hypothetical object transfer rather than beginning with an anatomical catalogue or implying an observed case. Two viable unassisted transfers and one cart-assisted transfer reach the same endpoint through different geometry, contacts, rates, and carriers. This makes the chapter's central distinction visible before technical language appears: matching task success does not imply matching mechanics.

The sequence then follows the accepted Chapter 8 arc:

1. a bounded free-body and moment account;
2. separate energy terms;
3. standing, sitting, and sit-to-stand as support histories;
4. walking as the strongest positive posterior case;
5. perturbation recovery as a three-dimensional repertoire;
6. completion and retirement of the recurring object transfer;
7. separation of performance, fatigue, stability, exposure, injury, and health;
8. evolution as a bounded research program; and
9. a whole-body handoff to Chapter 9.

The Chapter 7 seam assumes the posterior atlas and adds function rather than repeating attachments or scope definitions. The conclusion hands Chapter 9 a positive but conditional result and previews its three distinct propositions: posterior-system existence, a bounded anterior–posterior comparison, and the later preferred-organization hypothesis. Chapters 10–19 retain ownership of dose, capacity, interruption, concentration, constraint, compensation, tissue response, and biological consequence.

The full object-transfer narrative visibly ends here. Later chapters may recall a phase or variant, but the draft explicitly retires the complete sequence.

## Force, moment, and routing audit

The free-body account declares that system boundary changes which interactions are external and which become internal unknowns. Gravity, contact, acceleration, distance, and three-dimensional geometry remain distinct. The text states that inverse dynamics can constrain net resultants and moments without solving individual-muscle sharing.

The draft includes a concrete coactivation counterexample: two opposing muscle contributions can rise while the same net moment remains. That prevents a net extension moment from being read as an erector-spinae force. It also keeps passive restraint, pressure-mediated support, skeletal geometry, and environmental contact available as candidate contributors.

The hard/soft program appears only through its controlled mechanical kernel. Organized skeletal geometry can resolve substantial gross demand while compliant structures retain freedom for their own functions. The sentence identifies this as one meaning of routing through the hard body and immediately states that it is a first-order model, not a claim that bone carries every component or soft tissues should be unloaded. No tissue is assigned a permanent hard or soft class, and no traditional mapping is imported into this chapter.

The cart and exoskeleton discussion treats assistance as a change in the mechanical system. Reduced modeled loading at one site is not promoted into whole-body benefit, injury prevention, or device superiority. Movement restriction, added mass, other contact, and user experience remain possible tradeoffs.

## Energy audit

The energy section distinguishes:

- positive active work;
- negative active work;
- recoverable elastic storage;
- return;
- dissipation;
- redirection; and
- energy crossing a declared system boundary.

The Achilles and hopping examples remain task-, speed-, participant-, tendon-, and method-bounded. Tendon recoil does not become a whole-posterior spring claim. Controlled lowering supplies the required counterexample: muscle can perform negative work while tendon deforms, and placement can transfer energy into the destination rather than reverse departure.

The efficiency paragraph blocks an unregistered global metric. It requires a denominator and declared objective, separates mechanical work from metabolic cost, and notes that speed, repeatability, precision, heat management, and recovery can rank strategies differently. Book II retains ownership of any formal efficiency functional.

No energy term points directly to health, injury prevention, Yang organization, breath, or a practice instruction.

## Ordinary-function audit

Standing and sitting are represented as time-varying contact systems. Feet, floor, seat, backrest, arms, work surface, and optional hand support can all enter the boundary. The sitting source is used narrowly: activity differed across twenty-minute conditions in asymptomatic adults while selected immediate and short-term outcomes did not differ. The text does not infer long-duration, symptomatic, or universal null effects.

Duration and sequence are made explicit. The draft neither moralizes slouching nor declares variability universally beneficial. Sit-to-stand makes posterior contribution visible while retaining several viable combinations of momentum, foot placement, hand support, speed, and joint sharing.

Walking is the strongest positive case. The chapter states that model-estimated gluteal, hamstring, gastrocnemius, soleus, and related contributions in eight children with unimpaired gait change with speed and phase and coexist with vasti, lateral balance, arm and trunk relations, sensory control, skeletal alignment, and foot pressure. The ground-to-body paragraph uses linked contacts and active–tensile relations without describing one invariant packet of ground force traveling through a posterior cable.

The distinction among motion, moment, work, and region is explicit: a posterior muscle may contribute an extension moment during flexion, extension, or little motion; an observed extension motion does not identify its source.

Balance recovery includes foot placement, step timing, ankle and foot action, hip and posterior contribution, trunk and arm angular momentum, and external support. Stabilization is defined by a declared task relation and perturbation rather than by motionlessness, symmetry, or maximal coactivation. Both prose and the Figure 8.4 alternative state that sensory information changes the selection and timing of mechanical relations but is not another carrier of force.

## Outcome and evolutionary audit

The chapter separates five outcome objects:

1. performance;
2. perceived fatigue and performance fatigability;
3. stability;
4. mechanical exposure; and
5. injury or health outcome.

The table gives each an example measure and blocks substitution across columns. The following prose states what evidence would be required to connect capacity to performance, modeled exposure to injury, or symptom change to mechanism. This preserves the archive's performance, fatigue, stability, injury, and health program without treating it as one automatic ladder.

Evolution is retained as a substantive rationale for studying posterior specialization. The draft uses a trait → candidate function → evidence class → alternatives and tradeoffs → bounded modern prediction sequence. Endurance-running and gluteus-maximus studies remain meaningful evidence without becoming proof of one natural posture, mandatory behavior, universal route, or current preferred configuration.

The section adds a falsifiability control: a productive evolutionary account should make comparative or task-specific predictions; an explanation that accommodates every feature after the fact has not added evidence.

## Controlled-salvage and series-boundary audit

The draft preserves the `new` outline's positive center:

- posterior structures are among the body's largest and most mechanically consequential systems;
- levers, moments, tension, compression, contact, stabilization, and force pathways remain central;
- absorption, redirection, dissipation, deceleration, storage, and return remain visible and become mechanically distinct;
- standing, sitting, walking, lifting, transitions, and balance all receive explicit homes;
- performance, fatigue, stability, exposure, injury, and health remain active research objects;
- evolutionary specialization remains a live line of inquiry; and
- preferred posterior organization remains a named later hypothesis rather than being deleted.

The reconstruction adds intermediate work rather than removing intent. “Primary pathway” becomes a substantial contribution under specified conditions. “Shock absorption” becomes an energy account. “Healthy posture” becomes a support-history comparison. “Posterior stabilization” becomes phase- and perturbation-specific coordination. “Evolutionary design” becomes a testable trait/function/evidence program.

The reader draft contains no Yang direction, Six-Division or meridian mapping, breath driver, bandha, torus, diagnosis, treatment, home test, correction, activation cue, lifting instruction, exercise, prevention claim, or individualized practice. Book II owns preferred configuration and posterior necessity; Books III–IV own traditional and breath mappings; Book V owns practice.
