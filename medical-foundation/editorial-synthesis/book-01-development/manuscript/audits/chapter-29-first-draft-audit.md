## Measurement and evidence-state audit

The measurement track separates at least seven claims: acquisition, disagreement or error, representation of the construct, state distinction or change, interpretability relative to error and reference, decision value, and acceptable burden or reactivity. It requires system, unit, direction, boundary, scale, phase, time, intended use, acquisition, calibration, preprocessing, missingness, comparison, generalizability, and consequence where material.

No whole-body efficiency score, misrouting score, clinical cutoff, diagnostic threshold, prognosis, or treatment-selection instrument is created. Precision from a sensor or model output is not treated as construct validity. A measure may mature for one laboratory or research use while remaining inappropriate for clinical or system use.

The provenance figure carries the established evidence-state distinctions explicitly: observed (`OBS`), measured (`MEAS`), calculated (`CALC`), modeled (`MOD`), inferred (`INF`), and hypothesized (`HYP`). Manipulated, taught, and delivered material remains separately named. The abbreviations occur in a technical figure rather than as prose decoration. Favorable findings update only the tested object and evidence field.

Unknown, negative, mixed, nonunique, method-limited, inconclusive, not-tested, and retired states remain distinguishable. A nonsignificant result is not called equivalence. Re-entry after retirement requires new evidence, a materially revised construct, or a demonstrated method failure; it cannot consist only of redescribing the same result more abstractly.

## Implementation, equity, and institutional audit

The implementation section distinguishes the object being delivered from the strategy used to deliver it. It keeps implementation, service, and clinical outcomes separate and asks about reach, adoption, appropriateness, feasibility, fidelity, adaptation, cost, equity, maintenance, and unintended effects. Framework use is not treated as proof of valid measures or successful delivery.

Equity is part of the central implementation boundary. The draft asks who defined the target, whose movement is the reference, who can refuse monitoring, who bears time and financial burden, who has access to alternatives, and who controls data and decisions. Surveillance, stigma, data extraction, professional boundary conflict, low-value follow-up, displacement of effective care, and unequal access appear as possible outcomes, not late disclaimers.

The health-system section preserves eventual medical overhaul as an explicit horizon rather than permanently restricting the project to an adjunct. Its present posture remains additive and comparative: mature components must show what they add, duplicate, displace, or worsen. Existing disciplines and care are active comparators, not an imagined mechanics-free baseline.

Institutional development remains incremental and conditional. No specialty, device, assessment, therapy, traditional system, or preferred-state model is assigned ownership. Opportunity costs include professional attention, documentation, infrastructure, privacy, maintenance, follow-up, fragmented care, and unequal distribution of burden or decision power. The narrow definition of overdiagnosis is separated from false positives, misdiagnosis, detection of inconsequential variation, overtreatment, surveillance burden, and low-value care.

The chapter does not recommend a curriculum, workflow, data standard, reimbursement model, policy, or system redesign. A component with no added value stops at the system boundary even if earlier results remain favorable.

## Result-routing audit

Figure 29.4 gives distinct program destinations to ten result types:

- absent predicted redistribution with adequate discrimination;
- several routes producing the selected outcome;
- a local finding without a regional carrier;
- a repeatable but nonspecific signal;
- an educational program liked without improved accuracy;
- benefit without mediator change;
- mediator change without patient-important benefit;
- an effective object with poor reach, fidelity, equity, or sustainability;
- a mature component with no added value; and
- inadequate precision or construct validity.

The chapter additionally handles mixed results: improved accuracy with increased fear, improved outcome with another outcome worsened, adoption with reduced access, or benefit to one group with transferred burden to another. It does not average these into a favorable or unfavorable global impression.

Inconclusive results are named by cause—precision, construct validity, exposure range, attrition, missingness, conflicting outcomes, or observational equivalence. “Further research is needed” appears only as an insufficient response; the draft requires a next discriminating question or stopping decision.

Negative findings can retire one declared pathway without deciding the entire Book I framework or later series. Positive findings likewise do not validate unrelated tracks. This symmetry is the chapter's main falsifiability and preservation control.
