The chapter answers its central question at the authorized strength. Medicine already measures and changes mechanical conditions across several systems. These endpoints can be necessary and clinically meaningful while remaining incomplete accounts of origin. The remaining question is not whether mechanics belongs in medicine, but whether a provenance-labeled longitudinal routing record adds a defined result beyond established endpoint, exposure, biomechanical, rehabilitation, or ergonomic reasoning.

## Mechanical-variable and boundary audit

The draft preserves the principal non-equivalences:

- pressure is not flow or perfusion;
- compression depth is not coronary perfusion pressure or survival;
- ventilator pressure or volume is not regional tissue strain;
- construct stiffness and alignment are not one local interfragmentary field or biological healing;
- canal pressure is not nerve cross-sectional area, electrodiagnostic severity, symptom, or prognosis;
- surface pressure is not complete interface traction, deeper-tissue strain, comfort, or tolerance; and
- immediate endpoint change is not persistent response or prior causal history.

Each domain receives a declared system boundary. Redrawing that boundary is treated as a different question rather than discovery of a universally correct scale. Measurement verbs retain provenance: pressure is measured, support is applied, a mechanical state is intentionally altered, strain is construct-generated in the animal experiment, repair distribution is imaged, and internal histories remain inferred or reconstructed.

No domain's evidence is transferred to another. Resuscitation does not validate fracture routing. Fixation does not validate whole-body compensation. Carpal release does not validate decompression elsewhere. Socket distribution does not prove a universal tissue response. Their shared result is a reasoning pattern, not a shared constitutive law.

## Substitution and affirmative-support audit

Mechanical substitution is defined by the supported function. The draft does not present external support as an inferior detour from a true unsupported body. Brief resuscitative support, ventilatory assistance, orthotic constraint, prosthetic function, and other person–environment assemblies can all be legitimate within their declared task and purpose.

The affirmative center remains intact: an intervention or device can preserve a necessary or valued function. The exposure question follows rather than negates that success. Support can reduce one demand, create another, remain useful, become unnecessary, or form a continuing capable assembly. No general benefit, harm, device superiority, or restoration target follows from the word *substitution*.

## Fixation, decompression, and interface audit

The fixation section distinguishes absolute and relative stability, construct-mediated transfer, alignment, interfragmentary motion, repair biology, and functional outcome. The sheep study's sample, loading timing, construct-generated strain gradient, computed-tomography outcome, and nonhuman boundary remain in the body and note. No numeric human threshold or operative instruction is derived.

The decompression section distinguishes an intervention's local target from the cause of a condition. Position, pressure, nerve cross-sectional area, electrodiagnostic result, symptom, and recovery remain different objects. A response may support relevance to a selected endpoint without uniquely identifying origin. The chapter does not teach diagnostic criteria or a release indication.

The interface section treats assistance as part of the working system. Pressure and shear are separate, location and phase are retained, and peak, duration, and integral are non-equivalent. Sparse sensor maps are allowed to be useful without becoming complete fields. Comfort, confidence, participation, security, and device value remain person-level outcomes rather than noise or automatic consequences of a mechanical map.

## Endpoint-versus-history audit

The draft makes underdetermination bidirectional:

1. several prior histories can converge on one present endpoint; and
2. one documented history can lead to several mechanical, biological, symptomatic, and functional endpoints.

This is not used to claim that causation is unknowable. Chronology, prospective observation, manipulation, comparison, measurement, modeling, and explicit assumptions can narrow a history. The chapter instead blocks retrospective certainty supplied by a coherent story.

Immediate care without history is retained as a positive counterexample. CPR may be necessary before upstream history is known. A local endpoint can also be sufficient for a device-fit, stability, or pressure question. A longitudinal record must therefore outperform an explicit comparator for a declared purpose; comprehensiveness alone does not earn relevance.

## Internal decision and next action

Chapter 17 passes its controlled first-draft gate. It opens Part IV from common ground: medicine already measures and changes physical conditions and can preserve a necessary function through mechanical substitution. The chapter gives fixation, decompression, and living interfaces exact mechanical jobs without allowing any of them to validate the project's later whole-body hypotheses.

Its distinct contribution is the endpoint/history boundary. A local endpoint may be real, well measured, and sufficient for an immediate question without uniquely encoding its past. A longitudinal history can be more expansive while remaining less reliable or less useful. Chapter 18 must now define the proposed routing record inside the established landscape and show what would support, narrow, duplicate, or disfavor it.

The chapter is ready for controlled Part IV integration. It is not publication-approved.
