## Audit boundary

This audit covers Chapter 16's affirmative, bidirectional account of transition among local, regional, and whole-task levels; its operational requirements for crossing levels of description; environment and assistance inside the task boundary; whole-body configuration transition as a visible but non-load-bearing research program; independent sensory-and-control, pressure–flow–excursion, tissue-response, and energetic-or-regulatory branches; two text-native figures; thirteen provisional source notes; and the Chapter 15 → 16 → 17 seam.

It does not validate a universal kinetic chain, one carrier of interregional coupling, a preferred whole-body configuration, visual symmetry, a general configuration-transition class, a fascia-wide mechanism, a sensory–bracing cascade, vascular or lymphatic benefit, nerve entrapment, tissue injury, autonomic dysfunction, fatigue causation, diagnosis, prognosis, treatment, or practice. It does not complete external biomechanics, motor-control, neuroscience, vascular, lymphatic, peripheral-nerve, mechanobiology, energetic, regulatory, medical-coauthor, causal, accessibility, source-freshness, final-art, bibliography, or whole-book review.

The draft is a clean synthesis from the accepted controls. It preserves the project's larger whole-body question without allowing that question to carry the chapter's established mechanical result. The load-bearing claim is narrower and affirmative: a local condition can change regional participation and whole-task organization; a task and its environment can redistribute demand toward a local receiver; and every transition requires a declared quantity, intermediate bridge, chronology, alternatives, and null. Physiological consequence begins only after that mechanical account is complete.

## Operational scale audit

The draft keeps four levels distinct without turning them into a rigid hierarchy.

1. **Local** refers to a declared receiver or relation, such as strain in a tissue volume, pressure at an interface, nerve excursion relative to a bed, or contact stress over a surface.
2. **Regional** refers to a declared distribution among segments, contacts, forces, moments, activations, pressure relations, timing, or support.
3. **Whole-task** refers to a repeatable relation among regions, environment, contacts, timing, and outcomes such as speed, endpoint control, external work, or angular momentum.
4. **Whole-person context** includes goal, pain, fatigue, attention, assistance, environment, and other circumstances that can modify the task. It is not treated as one more mechanical scalar.

The transition grammar requires an object and quantity at both ends. Motion cannot silently become force; a net moment cannot become one muscle's force; aggregate momentum cannot recover a unique segmental organization; external loading cannot become internal tissue stress; and a local measure cannot establish a global organization. Geometry, contacts, forces, moments, activation, pressure, material behavior, model assumptions, and time are inserted according to the question.

The chapter also prevents *kinetic chain* from functioning as a conclusion. The phrase remains available as a prompt to investigate beyond one joint. A defensible account must still name the intermediate task and regional quantities. A selected null is retained as evidence rather than reinterpreted as an invisible route that happened to escape measurement.

## Bidirectional organization audit

The regional-to-task argument is built from three bounded experimental families. Arm-swing manipulation supports task-specific interlimb coupling. Split-belt walking with and without a handrail supports segmental contributions to whole-body angular momentum and environment-dependent early adaptation. Multiple arm-swing amplitudes show that selected task-level mechanical measures and metabolic cost do not remain locked together.

The reverse direction is not asserted as a mirror image. The draft gives an explicit sequence from task and environment through configuration, contacts, active state, regional resultants, interface sharing, local boundary conditions, and finally a local exposure. Each step has its own measurement or model burden. A task change may leave the selected receiver similar, alter a different receiver, or share a cause with the local observation.

The bidirectional map therefore permits feedback history without claiming a closed loop. A local restriction can precede altered task organization; the resulting organization can change a different modeled local quantity. Environment, instruction, pain, speed, active redistribution, and assistance can enter between those observations. No force is described as traveling intact through every level.

## Whole-body reorganization audit

The stronger project-native possibility is preserved directly. Repeated relations among contacts, timing, active support, and regional distribution may reorganize together so that a later task begins from a meaningfully different multiregion state. The draft does not reduce that possibility to a list of local changes, but it also does not treat a silhouette or anatomical continuity as its proof.

A configuration-transition research program must declare:

- the represented object, such as resultants, local fields, contact-and-tension networks, or a defined combination;
- a reference state, task family, and timescale;
- criteria distinguishing ordinary movement within a repertoire from change in the repertoire;
- a longitudinal record before, during, and after the proposed transition;
- transfer, retention, perturbation-response, route, gradient, or coupling predictions; and
- outcomes that would disfavor the proposal.

The hypothetical stepping study makes those obligations concrete without simulating evidence. A retained pose is insufficient. The proposal would have to predict a retained or transferable cross-region relation and a corresponding change at a declared local receiver. Failure of retention, transfer, coupling, or local consequence counts against the particular proposal. A positive result would remain bounded to the tested people and tasks and would not establish a universal preferred configuration or a health outcome.

This hypothesis is explicitly non-load-bearing. The chapter's multiscale conclusion survives if no general class of configuration transitions is ever demonstrated. That architecture preserves future authorial development while protecting Book I from depending on it.

## Branch-separation audit

The draft uses four independent downstream gates.

| Branch | Required object or intermediate | Preserved positive possibility | Required null, reverse, or alternative |
|---|---|---|---|
| Sensory and control | Named sensory measure, activation, perceived effort, timed response, or action | Small contact, pain state, or support may alter a selected control result | Another measure is unchanged; control changes first; attention, expectation, or task affects both |
| Pressure, flow, and excursion | System-specific lumen, gradient, waveform, velocity, volume, pressure, excursion, strain, shear, conduction, glide, or cavity behavior | Geometry and maintained mechanical relations may change a defined vessel, nerve, interface, or cavity quantity | Ordinary positional change; no functional consequence; compensation; different system or common cause |
| Tissue response | Named receiver and exposure history followed by material, cellular, signaling, repair, or remodeling measurement | A bounded exposure may produce a time-resolved response | No response, useful response, recovery, biology-first change, or misestimated exposure |
| Energetic or regulatory | Defined task and strategy followed by work, activation, metabolism, effort, recovery, or named physiological variable | Reorganization or assistance may change a defined cost or reserve measure | Assistance helps; more assistance reverses benefit; selected cost is unchanged; physiology changes first |

Within pressure, flow, and excursion, the draft separately names artery, vein, lymphatic vessel, peripheral nerve, sliding interface, and pressure-bearing cavity. These systems do not share one generic result called circulation or flow. The arterial, venous, and nerve sources retain vessel- or interface-specific jobs. The lymphatic, sliding-interface, and cavity rows remain research obligations rather than conclusions imported from neighboring evidence.

The maintained deformation point is handled carefully. External contact, surrounding geometry, wall tension, pressure difference, and active tissue can maintain deformation of a compliant structure. Lumen geometry can participate in flow, but geometry, stress and strain, gradient, flow, tissue outcome, symptom, and benefit remain different quantities. The hose analogy is used only to expose the causal sequence it cannot finish; it does not supply active vessel biology, valves, branching, or clinical meaning.

Separation does not mean biological isolation. The draft permits interactions among sensation, muscle activity, pressure, and a vessel-specific measure. Such interaction is treated as the larger proposition: both branches must be measured on compatible timescales, temporal order must be established, and a shared task change must be compared. One branch can change while another remains similar, and that disagreement remains informative rather than being smoothed into a systemic narrative.

## Energetic and regulatory audit

Cost always retains a numerator, denominator, task, and interval. Muscle activation, segmental work, net metabolic power, oxygen cost per distance, perceived effort, attention, recovery time, and loss of options do not substitute for one another. The arm-swing result prevents improvement in a selected mechanical aggregate from becoming automatic metabolic benefit.

The two assistance studies provide positive and reversal branches. Individualized powered assistance lowered metabolic cost during a bounded loaded-walking task. Moderate energy-removing assistance lowered cost under one tested condition, while the higher tested magnitude reversed the benefit. Neither study establishes a universal optimum, health effect, long-term adaptation, intrinsic bodily efficiency, or preferred route.

Reserve remains an operational possibility rather than a hidden store. It can refer to measured ability to repeat, vary, recover, or answer perturbation under declared conditions. Fatigue, low resilience, or diffuse symptoms can motivate those measurements but cannot establish a mechanical cause.

Regulatory language is explicitly deferred to named variables. Heart-rate variability, blood pressure, ventilatory work, endocrine response, temperature control, attention, and other candidate measures require separate mechanical and physiological bridges. Posture, activation, or effort does not become autonomic dysfunction by relabeling it regulatory.

## Internal decision and next action

Chapter 16 passes its controlled first-draft gate. It closes Part III with a positive mechanical thesis: local, regional, and whole-task organization can participate in a bidirectional history, but transitions across scale must be measured or modeled rather than inferred from anatomy or appearance. It preserves the stronger whole-body configuration program as an explicit, generous, and falsifiable research question while making clear that Book I does not depend on it.

The draft also prevents the multiscale result from becoming a medical cascade. Sensory/control, pressure–flow–excursion, tissue response, and energetic/regulatory consequences each retain their own systems, intermediates, chronology, alternatives, and nulls. Interaction remains possible, but it must be demonstrated rather than supplied by the word *systemic*.

The chapter is ready for controlled Part III integration and the Chapter 17 seam. It is not publication-approved. Chapter 17 may now begin from the fact that medicine already measures and changes mechanical conditions, then distinguish what established endpoint reasoning can support from what a longitudinal routing account may add.
