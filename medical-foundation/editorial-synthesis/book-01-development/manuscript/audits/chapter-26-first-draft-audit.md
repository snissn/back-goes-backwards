## Executive result

The draft performs Chapter 26's plural longitudinal job. It uses a deliberately sparse office-work/nonspecific-low-back-pain hypothetical to show what a mechanical history would require when there is no decisive initiating injury, no single sufficient finding, no measured local-to-regional route, and several plausible causal orders. The construction is disclosed as neither a patient nor a composite at the opening, at the first causal reconstruction, and in the conclusion.

The chapter preserves the archive's silent-compensation sequence as Route A without completing it. Pain-led change, shared context, successful adaptation, unresolved plurality, and low mechanical priority receive equal standing as Routes B–F. The final comparison allows substantial, mixed, reverse-causal, adaptive, low-priority, and unresolved interpretations. No branch is presented as the hidden correct answer.

Chronology, external exposure, internal strategy, local response, symptoms, function, findings, whole-person context, and evidence state remain separate. Office work is treated as a context that must be decomposed into tasks, metrics, boundary conditions, and recovery opportunities. A present posture, symptom, scan, or report is never projected backward into an observed history.

The regional section reaches an intentionally negative result: no carrier or receiver quantity supports a local-to-regional route in the hypothetical. One experimental gait model is used only to demonstrate the fields an operational regional proposition would need. The figure leaves the hypothetical fields blank rather than drawing a force line through missing evidence.

The chapter contains no diagnosis, prognosis, screening rule, workstation rule, self-test, corrective cue, exercise, breathing practice, treatment choice, or preferred-configuration conclusion. Its conclusion closes Part V by showing that the framework can survive an outcome other than mechanical confirmation and hands Chapter 27 a portable reasoning problem rather than a clinical verdict.

Feedback and alternative ordering remain visible. Pain, illness, fear, expectation, sleep disruption, medication, or reduced confidence can precede a changed strategy. Local response is treated as a necessary intermediate rather than an automatic consequence of exposure.

## Silent-compensation and route audit

The chapter defines silent compensation conservatively: a task-preserving change that was not reported, recognized, or measured at the time. “Silent” describes the record, not occult pathology. A movement difference earns the term *compensation* only when a preserved function or relation and a changed strategy have both been specified.

Protection precedes cost. Increased activation, reduced variation, altered support, or retained movement is not converted automatically into mechanical work, metabolic cost, tissue damage, fatigue, or lower future capacity. A cost claim requires a receiver, physical or physiological quantity, denominator, dose, and timescale. The chapter also allows a strategy's role to change through time without making its initial protective role a retrospective error.

Routes A–F are all present in prose and Figure 26.2:

- **Route A:** compensation precedes symptoms and alters later demand;
- **Route B:** symptoms, illness, or threat precede the changed strategy;
- **Route C:** a shared context shapes both histories;
- **Route D:** the changed strategy is useful with no demonstrated adverse cost;
- **Route E:** the available record cannot rank several causal orders; and
- **Route F:** the selected mechanical variables add little for the declared endpoint and interval.

Each route has observations that can raise its priority and observations that can lower or stop it. Route A is identified as the archive's preferred hypothesis but is not visually or rhetorically privileged as the case answer.

## Exposure, whole-person, and medical-boundary audit

Office work remains a context rather than a measured exposure. The prose separates duration, uninterrupted duration, repetition, force, support, variation, pace, vibration, task organization, environment, and recovery opportunity. It also distinguishes candidate exposure metrics such as summed time, peak force, force–time integral, modeled moment, transition count, and opportunity to recover. External exposure is not treated as local tissue exposure without configuration, active state, contact, support, and path assumptions.

Sleep, health, mood, expectation, work control, economic constraint, caregiving, medication, activity, and care appear as variables that can alter exposure, capacity, response, behavior, reporting, and causal order. They are not placed in a late biopsychosocial disclaimer. Positive, null, and very-low-certainty work-context findings remain bounded by their study designs.

The chapter does not diagnose low-back pain, infer a pain source, forecast recurrence, or turn modulation into origin. It supplies no self-assessment, ergonomic setup, posture rule, movement cue, exercise, breathing practice, referral instruction, or treatment recommendation. References to care, medication, workstation change, activity change, or environmental supports occur only as possible dated changes in a causal history; the chapter does not evaluate or prescribe them.

## Positive, null, reverse, and adaptive balance

The draft preserves the packet's paired evidence rather than using citations as confirming votes:

- occupational exposure studies show metric-specific associations, while sitting and posture reviews constrain independent causal claims;
- immediate symptom increase under prolonged sitting can coexist with lack of evidence for occupational sitting as an independent cause of clinical episodes;
- pain and fear can precede movement change, preventing present movement from being projected backward as origin;
- external task output can remain stable while recruitment changes, preserving successful or neutral compensation as a live interpretation;
- sleep and pain can show lagged relations in more than one direction, while work-context evidence remains mixed or very uncertain;
- imaging features can be common without symptoms and selected features can still be more prevalent in symptomatic groups; and
- time-dependent disc state can be real without establishing damage, symptom cause, or a whole-person recovery rule.

The final six-ending matrix preserves these distinctions. A substantial mechanical conclusion requires chronology, task specificity, a named carrier and receiver, outcome correspondence, and measured alternatives. A mixed result allows interaction. Reverse causal priority keeps mechanics as response or mediator. Successful adaptation prevents speculative cost from overruling observed function. Low mechanical priority is a local success of the method. Unresolved plurality identifies missing discriminators rather than ending with a generic call for more research.
