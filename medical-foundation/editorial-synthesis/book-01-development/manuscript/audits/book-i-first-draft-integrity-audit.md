## Executive result

Book I now exists as one reproducible reader manuscript rather than a collection of outlines, generated development material, or disconnected chapter drafts. The canonical assembly contains the title page, introduction, six Parts, Chapters 1–30, all required Part transitions, chapter notes, and a consolidated first-draft bibliography in the order declared by the [assembly manifest](../assembly-manifest.md). Chapter 30 closes Part VI and the volume; no redundant Part VI synthesis follows it.

The volume delivers a cumulative argument. Living bodies are treated as mechanical, biological, sensing, acting systems; force, moment, pressure, stress, strain, deformation, work, energy, dose, response, and outcome remain distinct; hard and soft name changing relational roles; support is cooperative; posterior anatomy and ordinary posterior contribution receive an affirmative treatment; adverse organization is described through six non-diagnostic families; mechanical exposure is connected to tissue response without collapsing either into symptoms or diagnosis; four worked narratives end at different evidentiary strengths; and the final Part separates reasoning usable now from education, development, system change, and the stronger later-series hypothesis.

The manuscript is source-grounded at first-draft level. Its 464 chapter-note definitions document proposition-specific uses and limits, and every chapter has a corresponding evidence and audit trail. This whole-book pass verifies integration and integrity; it does not replace full-text source verification, specialist review, medical-coauthor review, or publication approval.

## Hard, soft, posterior, and title boundaries

The manuscript consistently treats hard and soft as task-, direction-, scale-, state-, phase-, and time-dependent mechanical roles rather than tissue classes. The polarity is **motivated by** traditional Chinese medicine's hard/soft and Yin/Yang concepts. It is not described as inspired by, derived from, or definitionally identical to those concepts. Bone is the clearest hard-role example, but active muscle, tendon, ligament, fascia, cartilage, pressure, external support, or a coordinated assembly can participate in a shape-preserving relation. A vessel, nerve, sliding interface, cavity, muscle, or other tissue can receive form while remaining mechanically active and substantially loaded.

The title has three explicitly ranked meanings:

1. posterior anatomy exists and includes major muscles, tendons, fascial and aponeurotic relations, joints, and neural control;
2. posterior support is a productive organizing lens for ordinary tasks and life history; and
3. the later series proposes a preferred-configuration hypothesis in which posterior participation may be necessary but insufficient.

Only the first two do work in Book I. Chapter 30 names the stronger proposition, its current signed modes, its TCM correspondence program, and a genuine rejection condition without using any of them to prove earlier chapters. Book II assumes Book I has been read and must introduce bilateral reference symmetry, skeletal-primary routing, preferred-organization criteria, signed generalized modes, transformations, coupling, phase, contact, alternatives, and tests as additional modeling work.

## Reader integrity and claim boundaries

The combined manuscript contains no project IDs, TODOs, prompts, acceptance criteria, migration notes, generator instructions, outline scaffolding, conflict markers, or rejected TCM provenance wording. The one ordinary use of *archive* concerns study data, not manuscript development.

Book I does not teach a home test, corrective posture, exercise or breathing protocol, diagnostic score, treatment selector, force-line practice, bandha, individualized route assessment, or a method for finding the later preferred configuration. Its object-transfer and moment-arm prompts are neutral thought experiments, not self-assessment or corrective instructions.

The volume is mechanically affirmative without claiming mechanical exclusivity. It separates task and environment, configuration and available paths, external demand and internal distribution, local exposure, material or biological response, findings, symptoms, function, participation, and interpretation. Biological, psychological, social, developmental, and environmental factors remain inside the causal account. Mechanics may initiate, contribute, localize, amplify, maintain, modify, follow from, interact with, or remain incidental to a selected outcome without being forced into one universal contribution label.

The current clinical posture is additive and comparative: a mechanical-history or routing account must show what it adds to established reasoning for a declared use. That is an evidence-stage boundary, not a permanent ceiling on the author's longer medical-overhaul ambition. Chapter 29 preserves that ambition through separately gated research, measurement, education, therapeutic development, implementation, and health-system work.
