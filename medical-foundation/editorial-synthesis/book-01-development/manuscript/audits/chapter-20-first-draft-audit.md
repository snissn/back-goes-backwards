The draft is a clean synthesis from the accepted brief, audited evidence packet, and inclusive salvage sheet. No archive locator, internal control ID, planning key, generated mechanism, prompt language, or quarantined medical certainty enters reader prose. The authorial formulation remains direct: load wants to travel through the hard body so that the soft body can remain soft. ## Hard/soft and maintaining-relation audit

The draft uses the required provenance verb, “motivated by.” It spells out traditional Chinese medicine before the later TCM callback. It states that the mechanical terms are not definitionally identical to the traditional categories. The provenance is neither concealed nor asked to validate the biomedical model.

Shape-preserving and form-receiving are consistently relational. The draft does not equate hard with mineralized, rigid, posterior, Yang, always beneficial, or always force-bearing. It does not equate soft with weak, passive, anterior, Yin, unloaded, inferior, or biologically secondary. Bone, tendon, muscle, fascia, fluid, pressure, vessel wall, and cavity boundary are used to demonstrate role dependence rather than material bins.

The authorial phrase appears twice: at the opening translation and in the conclusion. Both uses state its bounded mechanical content. For a declared task, enough demand may be carried or distributed by shape-organizing relations that a form-receiving receiver retains the freedom its function requires. The text equally states that vessels, nerves, interfaces, and cavity walls continue to bear stress, strain, pressure traction, contact, or shear. The proposal concerns exposure distribution, not unloaded soft tissue.

The extrinsic sequence is explicit:

1. a surrounding geometry, contact, pressure, tether, or movement relation applies or maintains a boundary condition;
2. stress, strain, deformation, or lost excursion occurs in the named receiver;
3. a lumen, course, contact, or other geometry may change;
4. the receiver-specific output may change;
5. tissue, function, finding, or symptom requires additional support.

The garden hose is assigned one job: ordering the external maintaining relation before lumen and flow. Its biological mismatches appear immediately. The draft never uses the hose as evidence for a vessel, lymphatic, nerve, airway, condition, or clinical consequence.

## Persistence and restoration audit

Figure 20.2 and its surrounding prose distinguish four histories plus unknown. Transient/restored deformation is affirmative function. Extrinsic persistence requires an effective external relation. Intrinsic retention belongs to a receiver-specific wall, material, tone, swelling, lesion, thrombotic, scar, adhesion, or related state. Mixed persistence permits external exposure to change the receiver and intrinsic state to alter later surrounding mechanics.

Restoration is operationalized as an observation after a specified perturbation, not inferred from generic elasticity. The draft names immediate, delayed, partial, overshooting, and undetected return as different possible histories. It also notes that repetition can recreate an extrinsic relation faster than restoration, while a brief static sample can make ordinary variability look fixed.

Response to repositioning supports present modulation only. It does not establish original cause, medical significance, or treatment. Persistence after repositioning does not prove intrinsic disease because the relevant boundary may not have changed adequately, a different maintaining relation may remain, the interval may be wrong, or the measurement may be insensitive.

## Quantity and causal-chain audit

The following distinctions remain intact:

- a surrounding relation is not the stress or strain it produces;
- stress or strain is not visible geometry;
- geometry is not lumen;
- lumen is not pressure gradient, resistance, waveform, or flow;
- local flow is not perfusion, venous return, network transport, or clinical effect;
- lymphatic diameter is not pump work, clearance, fluid balance, edema, or immune function;
- neural excursion is not strain, contact pressure, circulation, axonal transport, conduction, sensation, symptom, or diagnosis;
- relative tendon–SSCT displacement is not shear, frictional work, adhesion, tissue response, function, or pain;
- airway caliber or critical closing pressure is not airflow, gas exchange, sleep-disorder cause, or treatment target;
- an intragastric pressure proxy is not boundary stress, distributed support, organ deformation, injury, or an optimal pressure; and
- mechanical plausibility is not whole-person causal weight.

The draft uses separate sentences or explicit arrows when it crosses these quantities. No disease list, universal soft-tissue cascade, or physiologic omnibus noun substitutes for the intermediate links.

## Internal decision and next action

Chapter 20 passes its controlled first-draft gate. It preserves the project’s central intuition generously and gives it enough mechanical structure to be tested. Shape-preserving relations can matter because form-receiving structures perform work by yielding, gliding, changing caliber, containing, accommodating pressure, and restoring—not because soft tissues are weak or should avoid demand.

The chapter’s strongest addition is its nontransfer rule. Vessels, lymphatics, nerves, sliding interfaces, airways, and cavities can be compared through needed freedom, maintaining relation, output, measurement, alternative, and null. Their equations, thresholds, evidence, and clinical consequences remain receiver-specific.

The chapter is ready for controlled Part IV integration. It is not publication-approved. Chapter 21 may now ask how much causal and decision-making weight each partial mechanical account should receive alongside tissue state, biology, behavior, development, psychology, social conditions, environment, symptoms, diagnosis, and ordinary alternatives.
