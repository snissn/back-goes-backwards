## Executive result

The draft performs Chapter 25's state-transition job. It uses recurrent lateral ankle instability as an explicitly disclosed multistudy reconstruction rather than an implied patient history, and it transfers only the resulting relational questions to lumbar segmental control. Passive or material restraint, task-level relation maintenance, perceived instability, pain, confidence, recurrence, exposure, and clinical meaning remain distinct.

The chapter states its positive thesis early: repeated demand acts on a changing receiver. It then makes that thesis branch. Earlier demand, response, recovery, adaptation, learning, care, behavior, support, and exposure can leave lower capacity, increased capacity, useful protection, an unresolved state, a new boundary condition, or no important retained change. Recurrence is not presented as a deterministic decline.

The evidence-led nulls remain load-bearing rather than appearing as late caveats. Selected passive inversion did not distinguish chronic-instability participants from copers; selected laxity measures did not differ where a reflex measure did; selected talar-position and static-balance measures did not carry the same information as reports; whole-curve landing kinematics did not differ despite greater reported instability; and a proximal knee-angle difference did not correspond to a group difference in peak calculated anterior tibial shear force. Prospective models supply time order and incomplete prediction, not mechanism, screening, or individual prognosis.

Four original text-native figures make the three-domain distinction, time-resolved task record, recurrent-state timeline, and scale-transition stopping rules visible. No published visual is copied. No diagnostic test, self-assessment, prognosis, prevention promise, brace selection, exercise, return-to-activity rule, movement cue, treatment, or preferred-configuration result appears.

## Multistudy and provenance audit

The ankle narrative visibly separates four evidence families:

- cohort definitions and theoretical organization;
- cross-sectional correspondence and noncorrespondence among passive, task, and experiential measures;
- prospective observations that order selected states in time; and
- task studies that compare motion, force, calculated quantities, report, and proximal observations under declared conditions.

The prose does not splice cohort sizes, tasks, or outcomes into one synthetic patient. It states that coper definitions vary, that landing is not walking or recurrence, and that no source continuously observed the whole injury–recovery–recurrence route. The paired opening is labeled as a reconstruction. No invented lesion, exposure, chronology, care response, tissue load, recurrence, or outcome is attributed to an individual.

The lumbar material remains a transfer, not a second composite. It carries questions about passive relation, active recovery, experience, task, chronology, and evidence state while leaving ankle anatomy, inversion tests, cohort labels, thresholds, recurrence rates, and causal arrows behind.

## Positive and null finding audit

The chapter preserves the evidence packet's paired positive and null structure:

- Brown: greater passive inversion in the chronic-instability group than controls; no detected chronic-instability–coper inversion difference and no group stiffness difference.
- Bowker: a selected soleus-reflex difference; no detected group difference in the selected instrumented laxity measures.
- Wikstrom: self-report distinguished groups more strongly; the selected talar-position measure had poor utility.
- de Vries: functional-score differences; no detected selected static-balance difference or correlation with those functional scores.
- De Ridder: greater reported instability and selected task difficulty; no detected group difference in whole-curve lower-limb landing kinematics.
- Watanabe: selected waveform-summary associations with perceived instability; peak summaries did not identify the same relations.
- Doherty prospective cohort: selected early and intermediate observations preceded a later research classification; performance remained imperfect and did not complete the mechanism.
- Pourkazemi: reported multivariable recurrence result; only ten events, many candidate variables, and no task-exposure denominator constrain inference.
- Mohamadi: a chronic-instability group difference with a capable coper/healthy comparison; compensation was not observed longitudinally.
- Gribble–Robinson and Doherty landing comparison: positive proximal motion or energetic observations; no claim of tissue exposure, causal direction, or later proximal injury.
- Terada: less knee flexion at the instant of peak calculated anterior tibial shear; no detected group difference in that calculated peak force or the other selected variables.
- Iguchi: study-specific radiographic/symptom correspondence with a translation-versus-angulation contrast; no causal or universal-threshold claim.
- Mok–Hodges: imposed restriction changed selected recovery measures; no transfer to chronic diagnosis or treatment.

The prose explicitly says that a failure to detect a difference is not proof of equivalence and that a null stops the arrow tested rather than proving mechanics absent at every scale.

## Evidence-state and causal-chain audit

The draft distinguishes:

```text
documented event
    -> measured passive, task, experiential, contextual, or exposure state
    -> calculated quantity where applicable
    -> reconstructed between-event transition
    -> hypothesized local-to-proximal carrier
    -> separately measured receiver or endpoint
```

An observed or reported event is not treated as a continuous mechanism. Cross-sectional difference is not narrated as a preinjury cause. Principal-component covariance is not made into an anatomical mode. Prospective time order is not promoted into unique causal direction. Calculated anterior tibial shear is not tissue strain. Radiographic relation is not task-level instability or pain origin.

The state-transition model includes recovery, adaptation, increased capacity, successful protection, changed exposure, new acute event, retained change, unresolved state, mixed history, and no-added-value outcomes. It does not draw recurrence as an inevitable staircase. Exposure opportunity appears as a necessary denominator, with task class, time at risk, frequency, intensity, variability, environment, support, assistance, and participation named at the level that can actually be observed.

The scale-transition figure and prose require a shared task, local quantity, proposed carrier, proximal quantity, chronology or manipulation, receiver-specific exposure when harm is proposed, and a stopping result. A positive proximal kinematic observation cannot advance through a null selected-load result. The route may stop at the ankle without weakening the supported local account.

## Hard/soft and later-book boundary audit

Hard and soft are described as relational shape-preserving and form-receiving roles. Their vocabulary is explicitly said to be **motivated by** traditional Chinese medicine concepts of hard/soft and Yin/Yang, not definitionally identical to them. Bone, joint geometry, ligament, muscle-tendon stiffness, ground, footwear, and external support can share shape-preserving work; compliant tissues can receive, yield, conform, glide, contain, or accommodate while remaining loaded and mechanically consequential. A ligament can preserve while deforming, and muscle can stiffen in one direction while yielding in another.

The chapter contains no fixed tissue class, moral polarity, anterior/posterior ranking, posterior-chain explanation, all-Yang interpretation, signed-coordinate model, channel mapping, breath mechanism, force-line claim, skeletal-primary-routing conclusion, or preferred-configuration proof. The later program is neither used covertly nor argued in miniature.

## Chapter 24 and Chapter 26 seam audit

Chapter 24 ends by contrasting constraint-maintained deformation with repeated failure to hold and correct a relation under changing capacity. Chapter 25 opens directly on that inverse problem. It does not reteach the thoracic-outlet example or import vascular evidence into the ankle.

Chapter 25 then adds the temporal state model Chapter 26 requires. Its conclusion states that the next chapter keeps changing capacity and compensation but removes the clear first event. The handoff leaves Chapter 26 free to find a substantial, mixed, low-priority, or unresolved mechanical contribution rather than forcing a hidden route into an ambiguous chronic history.

## No-protocol, no-diagnosis, and person-centered audit

The chapter does not provide or imply:

- a self-test, diagnostic cutoff, questionnaire score, balance test, or screening battery;
- a recurrence probability, prognosis, return-to-activity rule, or prevention promise;
- exercise, bracing, footwear, landing, posture, spinal-motion, rehabilitation, or treatment advice;
- a cue to increase or reduce stiffness, excursion, posterior participation, or lumbar motion;
- a claim that perceived instability proves a lesion or that radiographic motion proves pain origin; or
- a claim that a local ankle history necessarily creates proximal, contralateral, whole-body, energetic, or systemic harm.

Care, support, assistance, changed participation, confidence, pain, and environment appear as state variables rather than noise. Coper status is not romanticized as mechanical optimality. Reduced activity is not equated with recovery, and valued task adaptation is not classified automatically as failure.
