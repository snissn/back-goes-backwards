## Formal mechanics and quantity audit

The distal-segment equations preserve force and moment dimensions:

```text
sum of external forces + knee-boundary force = mass × center-of-mass acceleration

sum of external moments about O
    + knee-force moment about O
    + knee-boundary free moment
    = angular-momentum rate about O
```

The text declares the frame and sign-convention requirement and explains that changing the boundary changes the bookkeeping. A second relation decomposes a separately declared joint-cut resultant into medial contact, lateral contact, muscle, ligament, and other contributions. It is called a bookkeeping relation and explicitly not a solvable decomposition. The draft also prevents the resultants from two different boundaries from being assumed numerically identical.

The segment resultant and internal distribution remain conceptually separate. The chapter explains that a resultant deliberately omits how the actions producing it were distributed; a compartment force can likewise omit the spatial field beneath it.

At the interface scale, the draft distinguishes average normal pressure from traction, stress, and strain. The traction relation `t = sigma n` is dimensionally and conceptually appropriate. The text says that `F_n/A` is only an average normal action over a declared area and cannot recover the stress tensor, tangential traction, through-depth variation, or material-dependent strain.

The rotation-matrix control is explicit and accurate at the intended level: a matrix can represent relative orientation or transform already defined vector or tensor components. It cannot establish a force, contact, muscle solution, pressure, stress, strain, or decompression without the physical model and boundary conditions. No unit-inconsistent angular-velocity equation, decorative rotation proof, universal amplification factor, or unsupported threshold enters the chapter.

Formal mechanics, coordinate-frame, sign-convention, notation, and equation review remain publication gates. This audit verifies internal dimensional coherence, not external specialist approval.

The principal nulls are inside the mechanism rather than appended as disclaimers. Reduced calculated adduction moment did not guarantee reduced measured medial contact force. A created radial meniscal tear did not significantly change the selected contact measures. Ordinary healthy walking produced localized deformation without an injury conclusion. Neutral alignment was not universal among asymptomatic young adults. Magnetic-resonance findings occurred with and without knee pain. One moment measure associated with one cartilage outcome while another moment measure and other outcomes did not.

The meniscectomy and tear experiments are not presented as one pooled quantitative comparison. Figure 23.3 and its caption identify their different study conditions and prevent either result from becoming a posture, living-adaptation, symptom, osteoarthritis, or treatment claim.
