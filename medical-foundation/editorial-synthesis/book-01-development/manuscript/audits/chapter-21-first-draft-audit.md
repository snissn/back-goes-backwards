## Causal-structure audit

Figure 21.1 and the surrounding prose preserve six genuinely different structures:

1. mechanical exposure preceding receiver response and symptom or function;
2. tissue or biological state preceding both symptom and changed mechanics;
3. work, resource, or behavioral context changing task exposure and outcome through different paths;
4. reciprocal mechanical, tissue, symptom, and movement change through time;
5. a common prior condition producing both a mechanical observation and the selected outcome; and
6. an incidental mechanical observation or structural finding for the declared endpoint.

Observed-now nodes and inferred or hypothesized arrows use different words and line styles. The figure does not privilege the forward structure. The prose states that an acute event, established disease process, or experimental manipulation can alter the relative support for candidate structures; causal pluralism is not treated as equal probability.

The draft also keeps these objects distinct:

- task observation from internal distribution;
- visible configuration from receiver-level exposure;
- exposure from tissue or system response;
- response from finding, function, or symptom;
- present modulation from historical origin;
- current association from temporal order;
- prospective order from freedom from residual common cause;
- intervention assignment from mechanical change;
- mechanical change from mediator;
- mediator from active component and outcome;
- prediction from decision value; and
- a complete narrative from a complete cause.

No later observation is used as retrospective proof of an earlier state. The low-priority branch is substantive: even a precise mechanical description can be true yet fail to distinguish outcomes, precede change, improve prediction, or add value beyond existing information.

## OA/RA boundary audit

The boundary exercise retains both strong and limiting evidence. Felson's strong mechanical account of OA is stated directly, while the disease remains heterogeneous and multifactorial. Sharma's prospective result is described as an association in adults with established disease, not disease initiation. The MOST synovitis result remains observational and cannot establish an independent causal effect.

RA remains immune-inflammatory in its central disease account without becoming mechanics-free. Cambré's loading and unloading manipulations are explicitly confined to mouse inflammatory-arthritis models. The accompanying human RA evidence remains cross-sectional. The note preserves the source discrepancy between the Methods cohort of sixty-three and the Figure 4 caption's *n* = 64 without spending either number in body prose.

The chapter uses the contrast only to distinguish disease initiation, present exposure, symptom or function, and current modulation. It gives no disease teaching, diagnosis, prognosis, management, rehabilitation, or treatment recommendation. Rheumatology and medical-coauthor review remain publication gates. If that review finds the comparison more reductive than clarifying, it can be narrowed or replaced without collapsing the chapter's causal job.

## Intervention, null, and incremental-value audit

The unloading-shoe trial is reported as no added pain or function benefit over the conventional-shoe comparator, not as evidence that knee mechanics is irrelevant. Paterson's analysis preserves the lack of a significant mediator in the full cohort and the exploratory, underpowered status of subgroup findings.

The patellofemoral-brace trial supports regional modifiability under the studied conditions. The draft explicitly states that pain change was not significantly associated with change in the magnetic-resonance measure. It therefore does not infer mediation, disease origin, or a complete symptom mechanism.

RESTORE is reported as improvement in activity limitation for both cognitive-functional-therapy arms relative to usual care and **no apparent added benefit** from sensor feedback. The draft does not call the sensor arms equivalent. It does not isolate cognition, emotion, behavior, movement, lifestyle, or another component from an individualized, multicomponent, unblinded intervention.

VanderWeele supports the separation of intervention, mediator, and outcome under explicit assumptions; the chapter does not teach mediation analysis. Brinjikji supports non-equivalence of common imaging findings and symptoms; the chapter does not dismiss imaging or all structural relevance. Lawlor supports triangulation across methods with different bias structures; convergence is not treated as certainty. Vickers supports the prediction-versus-decision distinction; no decision rule or calculation is imposed.

## Internal decision and next action

Chapter 21 passes its controlled first-draft gate. It gives the book a causal contract strong enough to support its ambition: a mechanical relation earns authority by answering a declared question, surviving alternatives, and adding value—not by being physically possible or narratively complete.

The draft's strongest contribution is its successful null. Mechanics can be important, mixed, downstream, incidental, or low priority without those results threatening the project. This allows a narrow mechanical link to remain strong, another process to retain priority where warranted, and the broader longitudinal-routing program to become more ambitious only through accumulated discrimination.

Chapter 22 may now inherit this discipline for controlled workflow drafting. Chapter 21 is not publication-approved, and no open specialist, medical, ethical, source, figure, accessibility, or continuity gate is represented as complete.
