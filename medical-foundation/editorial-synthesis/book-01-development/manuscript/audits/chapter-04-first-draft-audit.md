## Frame, configuration, and observability audit

The draft separates three changes that are often conflated:

1. changing the coordinate basis used to express one physical quantity;
2. changing the boundary that determines which interactions are external; and
3. changing the physical configuration or task phase itself.

This distinction is carried through the object transfer and Figure 4.1. The room, object, segment, surface, and curved-centerline frames are presented as question-dependent constructions. A local tangent does not generate unique transverse directions, and cylindrical notation is restricted to geometries with a declared axis. The reader is not asked to infer a whole-body ontology from a convenient basis.

The configuration record is deliberately expandable rather than universal. Geometry, orientation, contacts, constraints, active forces, preload, pressure, material history, fields, available movement, phase, method, and provenance enter only when the claim requires them. Figure 4.2 places observed geometry and external balance upstream of several candidate internal models. Additional force, motion, pressure, deformation, activation, or sensitivity evidence is required to discriminate among them.

The chapter therefore preserves three viable cases:

- similar visible postures with materially different internal configurations;
- different visible postures that solve the same external task; and
- one visible transfer described adequately at one scale by a planar model while requiring three dimensions for another claim.

No posture, direction, or configuration is labeled healthy, harmful, efficient, optimal, or preferred. The later efficiency hypothesis remains available to Book II but does not carry the foundational argument.

## Load-path and force-line audit

The reader-facing distinction is explicit: a line of action belongs to a defined force, while an internal load path is a model of how a declared quantity is carried through named structures, contacts, or fields between selected boundaries. External balance constrains candidate internal routes but usually does not identify them uniquely.

The chapter allows skeletal contact, muscle, tendon, ligament, fascia, pressure, sliding or frictional interfaces, and external support to enter a path when named and relevant. Segment inertia remains in the configuration and dynamic balance rather than being mislabeled as an anatomical carrier. “Multicarrier” means that parallel or serial contributions are permitted, not that every carrier is always important. Line of action, force-and-moment resultant, graph, field, and time-varying network representations are differentiated by modeling job rather than ranked as levels of truth.

The project's internal **force line** language is preserved in two senses. A technical line of action is mechanically defined. An embodied or anatomical force line can remain a proposed internal connection, but must be translated into candidate carriers, boundaries, directions, and observables before it can support a mechanical inference. This retains the author's Yin-adjacent internal-connection intuition for later development without silently converting experience into a measured vector.

The adverse-load preview also remains bounded. Maintained geometry or constraint may impose deformation or pressure on a form-receiving structure, and the resulting mechanical state may matter more than a bare description of “less space.” The chapter supplies an investigational narrative but makes no diagnosis, disease-causality claim, treatment implication, or universal hard-to-soft rule.

## Four-description crosswalk audit

| Description | Preserved job | Required later formalization | Prohibited shortcut |
|---|---|---|---|
| Spatial frame | Express components of vectors and tensors relative to a declared basis and origin | Local/global transformations and moving-frame construction | Three components do not derive three bodily modes |
| Generalized coordinate and load | Describe a selected change and its work-conjugate load | Variable, units, sign, constraint, coupling, transformation, and observation | A spatial tangent is not an opening coordinate or moment |
| Material-symmetry frame | Approximate direction-dependent constitutive behavior in a selected material or region | Tissue, region, scale, state, axes, parameters, purpose, and validation range | Local orthotropy is not whole-body orthotropy |
| Principal-stress frame | Describe directions of the current stress state at a point | Stress tensor, frame, state, method, uncertainty, and degeneracy treatment | Principal directions are not permanent anatomy, channels, or force lines |

Rib rotation is permitted to contribute to thoracic expansion while remaining distinct from width, area, perimeter, or volume. The pelvic scissors analogy is retained only as a possible relative-angle or composite-mode representation; the prose explicitly rejects large literal sacroiliac opening and states that the hinge, segments, coordinate, magnitude, and observation must be supplied.

The crosswalk preserves the series' research runway. Book II can define axial, opening or rotational, and expansive generalized modes; relate them to local and global spatial frames; choose constitutive assumptions where needed; and predict measurable changes. Book I does not require any proposed correspondence to survive.

## Controlled-salvage and scope audit

The draft preserves the archive's useful structure without inheriting its categorical overreach:

- the body remains a concurrent three-dimensional mechanical event rather than three isolated planes;
- local and global frames, curvature, contact, moment arms, and rotational inertia remain available;
- configuration remains a richer state than visible posture;
- load paths remain branching, multicarrier, phase-dependent, and model-relative;
- hard/soft roles remain spatially locatable within paths and are explicitly **motivated by** TCM hard/soft and Yin/Yang concepts without definitional identity;
- the axial, opening or rotational, and expansive research program remains visible for Book II;
- rib and pelvic analogies remain recoverable after coordinate formalization;
- local orthotropy remains available when its constitutive assumptions fit a selected tissue and question;
- force-line language remains available both technically and as a later embodied hypothesis; and

It does not rank the object-transfer configurations or turn a coordinate description into a health recommendation.
