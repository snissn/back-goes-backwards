This audit covers Chapter 15's reader-facing definition of compensation through protected function; separation of endpoint, external mechanics, calculated regional mechanics, modeled internal sharing, and person-valued outcomes; narrow use of silent function and new baseline; repeated-strategy chronology; conditional cumulative demand; delayed, distal, and contralateral reconstruction; causal-role separation; three text-native figures; ten provisional source notes; and the Chapter 14 → 15 → 16 seam.

It does not validate a preferred route, original route, universal compensation score, long-term cost of assistance, tissue-damage mechanism, contralateral disease cause, chronic-pain explanation, diagnostic inference, prognosis, restoration target, or corrective practice. It does not complete external biomechanics, motor-control, longitudinal-causal, medical-coauthor, disability/accessibility, source-freshness, final-art, rights, bibliography, or whole-Part review.

The draft is a clean synthesis from the accepted evidence and salvage controls. No generated or legacy sentence, invented clinical chronology, deterministic disease cascade, protocol, archive locator, or internal control label enters reader prose. The archive's stronger intention remains recognizable: an initially successful and symptomatically quiet strategy can recur, change the next starting state, and alter later demand. The controlled manuscript preserves that negative possibility without making compensation itself a defect or a later finding evidence of its own origin.

The chapter answers its central question at the authorized strength. A successful strategy can alter later demand because repeated contact, timing, work, motion, support, or another named variable can change the starting state and exposure of subsequent tasks. That possibility becomes a credible delayed account only through a recorded chronology and route. Compensation itself remains neutral until a defined outcome and comparison show otherwise.

## Protected-function and disability-framing audit

The draft begins with continuation rather than deviation. Cane use, hand support, prosthetic or orthotic strategies, altered speed, changed stance, environmental support, assistance from another person, and task-specific preference all enter as legitimate ways to preserve function. The prose never implies that an unsupported, visually symmetric, or historically prior solution is the proper endpoint.

Compensation is described as an optional analytical term, particularly in disability contexts. The draft directs reader-facing description toward the person's known goals and language. It does not use a strategy as an identity or treat assistance as evidence of lesser embodiment. Persistent successful assistance is a complete outcome rather than a temporary stage awaiting restoration.

The three affirmative empirical examples have different jobs:

- Tazawa and colleagues support an added hand–cane–ground contact, reduced selected calculated affected-side hip-moment measures, and reduced reported walking pain in an acute fixed-order comparison;
- Kawase and colleagues show that greater instructed cane support changes calculated upper-limb joint work nonuniformly, without turning that work into injury or disability experience; and
- Lee and colleagues provide a task-specific counterexample in which powered individualized exosuit assistance reduced measured metabolic cost, while the small experienced-user sample and trial-order and tuning limits remain visible.

The chapter uses metabolic cost only as an immediate task-level outcome. It does not infer regulatory burden or whole-person energetic organization. Chapter 16 retains that scale-transition question.

## Allocation and evidence-state audit

The draft preserves the hierarchy needed for a route comparison:

1. the task, endpoint, and person–environment boundary;
2. observed or measured contact, motion, force, and timing;
3. calculated moment, impulse, power, and work;
4. modeled muscle force, joint reaction force, or local distribution under stated assumptions;
5. reported comfort, effort, safety, preference, or participation; and
6. any later tissue, symptom, or clinical outcome, which remains unknown unless separately followed.

No level silently becomes another. The Vial experiment supports acute step- and limb-specific hip-to-knee work redistribution with maintained acceleration; it does not establish chronic exposure or injury. The McCain study supplies a model-dependent null in which increased selected external measures did not produce increased modeled lower-limb joint reaction forces under the stated simulations. The draft retains the omitted brace moments and does not generalize induced restriction to clinical gait. The Bakker study supplies a group-level interlimb null and heterogeneity warning rather than a universal conclusion about pain or unmeasured internal exposure.

The manuscript also recognizes when endpoint matching is impossible or incomplete. Assistance can make a previously unavailable task possible, change speed, or alter the system boundary so substantially that an unsupported trial is not equivalent. This limits the comparative mechanical claim without diminishing the value of the assisted task.

Figure 15.1 makes the evidence-state stack inspectable. Its final row leaves later consequence unknown rather than allowing a successful endpoint or route difference to supply a tissue response.

## Silent, retained-state, and cumulative-history audit

Silent function has three explicit comparison states: exposure unmeasured, exposure measured with no meaningful difference, and exposure changed while remaining within the selected capacity and recovery comparison. The draft therefore distinguishes unknown, mechanical null, and successful adaptation. None is relabeled as concealed dysfunction.

New baseline is bounded to a repeatedly encountered starting organization for a defined task, condition, parameter, and interval. Vasudevan and Bastian support speed-specific aftereffects and partial retention; Selgrade and colleagues support time-varying calculated work during adaptation; Reisman and colleagues support partial and parameter-specific transfer from treadmill to overground walking. These findings are not made to establish permanence, structural change, whole-body reorganization, or durable treatment benefit.

The route-history table records protected function, immediate state, strategy, repetition, recovery, retention or transfer, later receiver exposure, and outcome. It keeps an unobserved interval visible and permits persistent useful strategy, resolution, replacement, and null outcomes.

Cumulative demand remains a history of named quantities rather than one scalar. Force-time, impulse, mechanical work, activation time, excursion, contact, and pressure are not treated as interchangeable. The workday hand-support example requires a receiver-specific measure and separates support count, hand-force impulse, shoulder work, adaptability, and recovery. Exposure and capacity can both change through time; the draft does not model an unchanging burden acting on an unchanging body.

The possible adverse branch is nevertheless explicit. Recurrent strategy can change a named receiver's force, moment, pressure, contact, active tension, deformation, or excursion. If that exposure is poorly matched to present capacity and recovery, a material or biological response becomes a legitimate later question. The manuscript stops at that question. Chapter 19 owns the tissue-response bridge, and Chapter 21 owns the full causal and clinical interpretation.

## Distal reconstruction and causal-role audit

The draft grants delayed, distal, and contralateral hypotheses full conceptual standing while requiring an inspectable chain. An initial state must precede a measured strategy change; external contacts and regional resultants must connect to a calculated or modeled distribution; a named receiver must acquire a repeated exposure; recovery and the unobserved interval must be recorded; and tissue response must be separately evidenced before a later finding can enter.

Aljehani and colleagues supply the required long-term disfavoring result. The selected biomechanical variables measured six to twenty-four months after unilateral primary total knee arthroplasty did not distinguish or predict contralateral radiographic progressors at mean 8.8-year follow-up. The draft does not overgeneralize this null to every mechanical quantity or history. It uses the result for the narrower conclusion that chronology and contralateral progression do not validate predictors that fail in the tested account.

Alternatives enter at the links they alter: common biology or environment, pain or disease preceding the strategy, task selection, treatment, recall, measurement error, a different unmeasured mechanical quantity, and incidental co-occurrence. Manipulation can strengthen a nearby arrow without filling years of unobserved history. The prose also states direct disfavoring conditions: absent route change, absent covariation, failed transfer, reverse temporal order, unchanged receiver exposure under support change, or a better account with fewer unsupported links.

Origin, adaptation, maintenance, consequence, and incidental context remain distinct. Pain can initiate a gait change, follow it, maintain it, be modified by it, or coexist with it. A device can be an adaptation and environmental support without being the origin of a diagnosis. A strategy can change roles through time. The first available clinical observation is explicitly treated as a potentially late state rather than a transparent record of origin.

## Internal decision and next action

Chapter 15 passes its controlled first-draft gate. It preserves the archive's full temporal ambition while making its branches inspectable. Compensation begins as a successful way of continuing. Repetition can change the next starting state and create a named exposure history. A delayed or distant consequence remains possible, but it becomes harder to support as links accumulate. Persistent assistance, recovery, learning, replacement, adequate capacity, and null results retain equal standing.

The chapter is ready for Chapter 16's scale-transition work and later Part III integration. It is not publication-approved. Chapter 16 may now ask whether a local or regional strategy changes neighboring participation, whole-task organization, pressure or excursion, sensory control, tissue response, or energetic demand—but it must keep those branches independent and cannot borrow a medical conclusion from Chapter 15's chronology.
