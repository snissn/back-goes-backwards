## Nested-scope and terminology audit

The opening presents three equally defensible maps before naming them. The narrow scope is consistently muscular and myotendinous. The broad scope adds only a named attachment, fascial or aponeurotic relation, ligament, retinaculum, periosteal relation, capsule, or sliding interface. The whole-support scope adds what is needed to close a selected mechanical question: skeleton, articulations, pressure, control, external contact, and anterior, lateral, or deep partners.

Later uses of *posterior chain* either name the narrow or broad scope directly or refer to the controlled family of scopes established in the opening and conclusion. Hyphenated overlay labels retain the same meaning. *Posterior system* is used for the wider anatomical synthesis, and *whole-posterior support system* is reserved for the mechanically complete boundary. No sentence silently moves a functional result from the whole-support system back into the narrow muscular map.

The draft does not make an exhaustive absence claim from anatomical nomenclature. It uses the official FIPAT/IFAA portal for current component naming and orientation, then declares *posterior chain* to be the book's analytic grouping rather than a replacement nomenclature entry. Final component-level terminology review remains open.

Posterior remains a regional descriptor. The Chapter 3 callback uses the required phrase **motivated by traditional Chinese medicine** and keeps hard/soft relational. A posterior tendon can be form-receiving while carrying tension; an anterior or lateral structure can be comparatively shape-preserving. Posterior is not equated with exterior, hard, healthy, Yang, or preferred. No traditional correspondence is used to establish anatomy or mechanics.

## Metric, anatomy, and regional-atlas audit

The manuscript controls every load-bearing size statement by naming a measure or anatomical basis. The Riem dataset is tied to muscle volume in 102 active healthy adults aged 19–50. The Ward comparison is tied to physiological cross-sectional area across 27 muscles from 21 cadaveric limbs and deliberately retains vastus lateralis as a nonposterior comparator. The Achilles description remains a conventional gross-size and strength ranking from an anatomical review rather than a living task-force estimate. Latissimus and trapezius are called broad or region-spanning on attachment grounds rather than assigned an unsupported absolute size rank.

The distal map separates three relations that a cable illustration would merge:

1. gastrocnemius and soleus contribute directly to the Achilles tendon;
2. the tendon attaches to the calcaneal system; and
3. the calcaneus participates in a shared skeletal and superficial paratenon–periosteum–plantar-fascia relation without confirmed complete fiber continuity in the cited 19-foot cadaveric study.

Ground contact, the rest of the foot skeleton, arches, intrinsic muscles, deeper posterior-compartment structures, anterior and lateral muscles, retinacula, and sensory control appear only as whole-support additions. The text makes no push-off, elastic-return, or progression claim.

The pelvic map preserves the archive's full posterior emphasis while replacing a passive bridge with a branching junction. Hamstrings, gluteus maximus, and proximal tendons enter the narrow map. The 97-hemipelvis anatomical source supports retinacular tissue, layered sacrotuberous relations, direct superficial-layer continuity with long-head biceps femoris, and neighboring semimembranosus and adductor-magnus relations. Femur, ischial region, sacrum, hip and sacroiliac relations, ligaments, deep and lateral hip muscles, abdominal structures, pressure, neural interfaces, and contralateral support prevent one posterior pelvic handoff from being assumed.

The axial map disaggregates *spinal extensors* into long erector-spinae spans, shorter multifidus and transversospinal spans, posterior skeletal elements, ligaments, aponeuroses, compartments, and sliding planes. The eight-cadaver multifidus study and ten-cadaver paraspinal-compartment study remain attached to their preservation methods and anatomical endpoints. *Extensor* stays a conventional anatomical or moment-tendency label rather than a claim of continuous activation, shortening, decompression, or preferred extension.

The reference-frame paragraph prevents a ground-to-cranium sweep from turning every local fiber into a cranio-caudal vector. Local fascicles and aponeurotic directions may turn, fan, cross levels, or attach obliquely even while the regional atlas has a longitudinal organization.

The thoracolumbar map remains girdling rather than posterior-only. It includes abdominal aponeuroses, transversus-abdominis and oblique relations, paraspinal sheath, ribs, vertebrae, pressure boundaries, and lateral raphe logic alongside posterior layers. The scapular map includes serratus anterior, clavicle, humerus, ribs, thoracic contact, and control rather than treating trapezius and rhomboids as an isolated support system.

The cranial endpoint preserves the foot-to-cranium ambition and specimen variation. Vertebral-aponeurosis relations are tied to the 40-cadaver source. The direct upper-trapezius cranial attachment appears as six of 20 cadavers in one small, older, two-population sample, not as a prevalence estimate. Nuchal ligament, deep cervical fascia, posterior cervical muscles, entheses, craniovertebral joints, skull, anterior and lateral partners, and sensory control keep the endpoint anatomically complete without one universal trapezial line.

## Relation and transfer audit

The draft uses seven explicit relation types:

- named component and attachment;
- direct tissue continuity;
- connective continuity or shared interface;
- shared skeletal junction;
- coordinated task participation;
- measured regional transfer; and
- inferred long-range transfer.

Each type names an observation and a stopping point. Direct continuity does not provide magnitude. A connective layer or shared bone does not become one fiber population. Coordinated motion or activation does not identify a tensile carrier. A measured regional endpoint does not establish ordinary whole-body magnitude. A long-range model does not become evidence until its distinct predictions are tested.

The Barker result remains bounded to eight unembalmed cadavers, 10-N traction at named muscular attachments, and measured tension and marker displacement in posterior and middle lumbar fasciae. It establishes regional transmission under imposed cadaveric conditions without exporting magnitude to living tasks.

The Carvalhais result remains bounded to 37 living participants and contralateral hip resting-position and passive-stiffness endpoints. The prose explicitly says that the experiment did not measure fascial force and retains joint mechanics, active control, skeletal coupling, and other carriers. The Procópio comparison remains a 48-participant illustration of population, endpoint, distance, effect size, and measurement error. Its diagnostic grouping is not used to infer origin, pathology, symptoms, treatment, or disease mechanism.

The two 2016 reviews occupy separate levels: one supports morphological continuity across examined proposed-chain transitions; the other supports some adjacent transfer under heterogeneous conditions. The 2026 remote-stiffness review remains a 15-study healthy-adult synthesis with very-low certainty for lengthening, moderate certainty for contraction, suspected reporting bias, varied protocols, and electromyographic monitoring in only eight studies. Remote stiffness remains a measured endpoint, not a transported force or unique carrier.

The five-level ladder permits evidence to stop. Embodied continuity is retained as hypothesis-generating provenance beside the ladder rather than being erased or promoted to measurement. *Force line* is explicitly separated into external line of action, inferred internal multicarrier relation, or embodied continuity. No body sentence presents it as a literal internal cable.

The null model is genuine: posterior-chain language may remain a useful anatomical index without adding predictive value beyond linked regional models. The stronger model must specify quantity, carrier, boundary, task, direction, state, and expected magnitude before the result and outperform active-control, skeletal, pressure, external-contact, or linked-segment alternatives. A failed whole-route prediction narrows that claim without erasing the documented regional anatomy.

## Controlled-salvage and series-boundary audit

The draft preserves the pre-synthesis Section 2 program generously:

- the posterior body remains a major anatomical system rather than an arbitrary muscle list;
- the atlas retains the full ground-to-cranium sweep;
- calf–Achilles, hamstrings, gluteals, spinal layers, thoracolumbar structures, latissimus, trapezius, scapular relations, neck, and cranium receive explicit treatment;
- fasciae, aponeuroses, ligaments, retinacula, periosteal relations, bones, joints, and sliding interfaces remain substantive carriers and junctions;
- the system remains extensive under volume, architecture, tendon, and attachment-span measures;
- bounded regional transfer remains a positive empirical result where measured;
- internal force-line and consequential whole-posterior hypotheses remain active research programs; and
- posterior importance is the chapter's conclusion rather than an apologetic aside.

The reconstruction is relation and evidence discipline, not ablation. “One continuous series” becomes a complete atlas in which every transition has a relation type. “Structural powerhouse” becomes a system containing several large-by-measure or region-spanning structures. “Force passes through the chain” becomes a staged question about carrier, quantity, direction, task, state, boundary, and measurement. “Primary pathway” remains a later necessity or preference hypothesis rather than an anatomical finding.

No task-performance, fatigue, evolutionary, injury-prevention, health, disease, diagnosis, prognosis, treatment, breath, posture-correction, activation, exercise, self-test, or protocol claim entered. No all-Yang state, Six-Division or channel mapping, bandha, torus, or practice model entered. Those programs remain preserved in their assigned chapters and books rather than being rejected or smuggled into anatomy.

## Internal decision and next action

Chapter 7 passes its controlled first-draft gate. It replaces an ambiguous chain with three usable scopes, preserves the original posterior confidence, maps the complete ground-to-cranium anatomy, treats each junction as a real but relation-specific handoff, and gives anatomical continuity, coordination, regional transfer, and whole-system hypothesis separate evidentiary objects.

The chapter ends at the correct positive conclusion: posterior anatomy is a major, layered support and transfer system worthy of focused analysis. It does not pre-decide what the system contributes in a given task or whether a preferred whole-body organization exists. The manuscript is ready for the Chapter 7 → Chapter 8 seam and later Part II integration. It is not publication-approved.
