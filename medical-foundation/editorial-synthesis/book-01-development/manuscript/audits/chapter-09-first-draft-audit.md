## Editorial result

The draft closes Part II around three independently gated posterior propositions:

1. posterior structures form a major anatomical and mechanical system — a Book I conclusion;
2. anterior–posterior distribution is a useful conditional lens within whole-body load sharing — a Book I organizing synthesis; and
3. posterior participation is expected to be necessary but insufficient within the preferred organization investigated in Book II — a direct authorial hypothesis that remains nonderived and non-load-bearing in Book I.

The chapter does not soften the first proposition into a generic statement that every body part participates. It keeps posterior anatomy and task contribution mechanically significant, then locates that significance inside unequal cooperation with anterior, lateral, deep, skeletal, tensile, form-receiving, pressure-bearing, sensory, environmental, and assistive relations.

The third proposition is stated rather than hidden. The draft names the later all-Yang program of downward axial resolution, external or opening rotation, and outward expansion. It also states that Chapters 1–9 do not derive it, that posterior participation alone is insufficient, and that Book II must supply the definitions, comparison or efficiency criterion, scale, countercases, and discriminating measurements. Neither of the Book I propositions borrows its authority.

No pregnancy, pain, injury, illness, connective-tissue, disability, assistance, aging, or reserve discussion becomes diagnosis, prognosis, treatment, prevention, correction, or technique. No young, unassisted, symmetrical, nondisabled body is installed as the healthy reference.

## Whole-body and hard/soft audit

The draft distinguishes anatomical significance, available capacity, and participation in a selected output. It also distinguishes motion, moment, work, load receipt, shape support, and environmental contact. A large posterior system can contribute little in one phase; a smaller structure, pressure change, or external contact can be decisive under another geometry. This keeps the positive posterior center measurable without making it universal.

Anterior, lateral, deep, pressure, skeletal, sensory, and environmental relations are not a disclaimer list. They enter the reasoning as possible brakes, guides, containers, shape-preserving boundaries, segmental relations, carriers, contacts, and sources of task information. Sensory information affects selection and timing but is not described as an anatomical carrier of force.

The hard/soft distinction uses the exact controlled provenance phrase **motivated by traditional Chinese medicine**. In Book I it remains a relational distinction between comparatively shape-preserving and form-receiving roles under stated conditions. A posterior muscle may receive form while active, a tendon may transmit tension while deforming, an anterior pressure-supported assembly may preserve a larger shape, and skeletal contact may cease to carry a dominant route when the boundary changes.

Region, relative domain, role, and sign remain separate throughout. Posterior is not equated with exterior, hard, Yang, healthy, correct, or preferred. Exterior and interior depend on a selected boundary. Yin and Yang remain proposed later relations rather than validated Book I variables. The draft explicitly notes that preserving these columns gives later formal work counterexamples instead of deciding the mapping by definition.

## Lifespan, state, disability, and variation audit

Development is represented as changing geometry, proportions, contact possibilities, sensory calibration, confidence, experience, environmental opportunity, and control—not as a deficient version of adult movement. The infant evidence remains small-sample and study-specific. Naturalistic step and fall averages are not converted into milestones or practice instructions. Childhood, adolescence, and adulthood are treated as continuing histories without asserting a universal sequence.

Training is separated into force, power, endurance, dimensions, stiffness, timing, skill, confidence, perception, strategy, measured outcome, and transfer. The one six-week comparison remains small and short. It demonstrates outcome specificity without selecting a program or implying that increased posterior engagement is universally restorative.

Pregnancy receives its own section. The prose changes anthropometric and person–environment assumptions rather than assigning one pregnancy posture. The two fifteen-participant studies remain bounded to their gait tasks, models, measurement periods, and individual variation. No symptom, fall, treatment, or correction claim is inherited.

Pain, injury, illness, and connective-tissue variation are not collapsed. Experimentally induced direction-linked pain supports adaptation to that protocol, not chronic-pain origin or treatment. Injury changes the starting state but does not identify a failed route. Illness requires condition, variable, task, time, treatment context, and comparison before a directional claim. The joint-hypermobility comparison establishes that a passive-range classification does not uniquely determine active strategy; it does not establish causal direction.

Disability is not modeled by subtraction from an unmarked reference body. The WHO ICF supplies an interactional functioning and context frame, not a tissue constitutive model. Device and helper relations enter through contact, fit, stiffness, friction, timing, control, environment, tradeoffs, and participation goal. The Saffuri device comparison is explicitly not treated as disabled lived experience or long-term clinical effectiveness. Assistance can be ordinary architecture, and an asymmetric strategy is not judged against undeclared symmetry.

Aging remains heterogeneous. Population trajectories are not individual destinies, and gait speed, strength, power, daily activity, recovery, environment, and posterior capacity are not substitutes. Reserve is provisional and conditional: a family of margins between a declared demand and whole-system capability over a specified timescale. Assistance, pacing, familiarity, and task redesign can alter practical margin without being contaminants or proof of health.

## Participation, capacity, compensation, and reserve audit

The four candidate-observation questions remain independent:

- **participation:** did a named structure or system contribute during a declared task and phase?
- **capacity:** what demand could a selected system meet under stated conditions?
- **compensation:** what changed to preserve something valuable?
- **reserve:** what task-specific margin remained or how did capability change with demand, support, fatigue, perturbation, or recovery?

Compensation begins with preservation rather than failure. Comfort, current tissue protection, task completion, balance, participation, or energy may be the preserved outcome. Efficiency, cost, durability, necessity, and later consequence remain separate questions with separate timescales.

The sit-to-stand example distinguishes observed movement, measured external forces, calculated joint moments, selected activation signals, graded task performance, and changed support. None becomes a tissue-health score, diagnosis, correction, or explanation of personal choice. The observation table retains a visibly empty validation column so candidate markers cannot be read as a test battery.

## Three-proposition and Book I/II seam audit

Level 1 is stated at authorized Book I strength: posterior structures form a major, layered anatomical and mechanical system with substantial task-specific contributions. *Major* does not become exclusive, sufficient, or dominant in every task.

Level 2 is also a Book I result: anterior–posterior distribution can organize a comparison when it changes a prediction, measurement, or interpretation. It remains conditional and sits beside lateral, deep, pressure, temporal, sensory, and environmental lenses. A null result is permitted.

Level 3 uses the author's firmer intended wording: posterior participation is **expected to be necessary but insufficient** within the preferred organization investigated in Book II. The draft does not reduce this to a vague possibility. It names the later directional program, then identifies the work still missing: definition of participation and preferred state, relevant scale, comparison or efficiency criterion, derivation, countercases, and disfavoring observations.

The figure blocks both inference directions. Levels 1–2 do not prove Level 3; Level 3 cannot validate Levels 1–2. A reader may accept the foundation, test the lens, and remain unconvinced by the preferred-organization hypothesis without contradiction.
