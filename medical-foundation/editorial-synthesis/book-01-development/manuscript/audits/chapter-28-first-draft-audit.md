## Scope and authority audit

The draft answers the chapter's authorized central question: what can responsibly be taught or redesigned now, and which stronger claims still require research and development? Its answer has three layers:

1. a public and professional language can describe task demand, options, and uncertainty without diagnosing or correcting a person;
2. environments and organizations can alter boundary conditions and realistic option space, but each change requires direct evaluation of its selected outcomes and tradeoffs; and
3. prevention is meaningful only when its outcome, population, comparison, timeframe, measure, implementation, and harms are named.

The chapter does not treat this stance as evidence that education or environmental design improves health. It explicitly allows the proposed vocabulary to fail through poor comprehension, lack of added value, adverse vigilance, inequitable access, scope crossing, or better existing language. It allows a design to redistribute demand, exclude a user, fail in implementation, improve one outcome while worsening another, or produce no important effect.

The draft remains subordinate to Chapters 21, 22, and 27 for whole-person, professional, measurement, and clinical boundaries. It does not reproduce Chapter 27's seven-question scaffold. It inherits the observation–measurement–inference discipline and applies it to education and design. It supplies Chapter 29 with six separate development tracks rather than implying a ready-to-scale intervention.

## Causal-chain and evidence-state audit

The reader draft preserves the Book I causal separation:

```text
technology, work, care, transport, sport, assistance, or environment
    -> task duration, pace, repetition, support, variation, and control
    -> external demand and available strategies
    -> exposure relative to changing capacity and recovery
    -> adaptation, specialization, stable tolerance, fatigue,
       symptom modulation, injury, participation change, or no important effect
    -> later behavior, care, environment, resources, and bodily state
```

The sequence branches rather than degenerates by default. Work, screen use, sitting, standing, manual tasks, caregiving, sport, transport, and assistance remain heterogeneous activity categories rather than diagnoses. Repetition, variability, support, and specialization can be capable, costly, mixed, or inconsequential depending on the declared task, receiver, state, interval, and outcome.

Observation, report, measurement, model, and inference remain nonidentical. The opening explicitly separates visible configuration and reported experience from internal load paths and tissue fields. Tactile, visual, and embodied teaching modes are permitted as representations and reports, not direct measurements of force, stress, strain, pressure, or a force line. A symptom that appears later does not recover the initial internal route; absence of symptoms does not prove zero exposure.

The modern-exposure studies retain their divergent endpoints. Reduced sitting does not become disease prevention. Improved decision control does not conceal increased awkward-posture exposure. Low-back-pain recovery does not become incidence prevention or neck recovery. Implemented changes do not become efficacy. A favorable attendance result does not erase pain nulls or worse self-efficacy.

## Environmental, hard/soft, and access audit

The chapter's environment model contains the person and changing state, task and performance requirement, assistance/tools/support, organization/schedule/resources/control, and built/social/information environment. No element is presumed to be the problem or correction target.

External support is represented as a boundary condition that may enable participation, redistribute demand, add requirements, or have no important effect. It is not coded as weakness or failed independence. The draft does not need to restate Chapter 3's full hard/soft provenance because it does not use hard/soft terminology as a load-bearing distinction. Nothing conflicts with the relational, TCM-motivated shape-preserving/form-receiving model.

Accessibility, disability, pregnancy, aging, chronic illness, pain, fatigue, body diversity, language, culture, job security, caregiving, resources, digital access, and safety alter the core option-space model. The draft distinguishes universal design, individual accommodation, and assistance. It does not imply that a universal feature eliminates every need, or that accommodation and assistance are lesser approximations of unsupported movement.

Responsibility follows control. Individual education does not replace staffing, equipment, scheduling, access, or source-level redesign. More theoretical options are not presumed better; usable options can be constrained by cost, safety, time, cognitive burden, or institutional authority. Participation remains an outcome in its own right rather than a proxy for symptoms, tissue state, unsupported independence, or conformity.

Monitoring is treated as a possible hazard-recognition tool with simultaneous risks involving surveillance, privacy, ownership, discrimination, algorithmic management, false alerts, unequal performance, and burden shifting. The draft requires governance questions without declaring every sensor harmful. It similarly treats vigilance, fear, guilt, shame, and blame as early adverse educational outcomes rather than assuming awareness is benign.
