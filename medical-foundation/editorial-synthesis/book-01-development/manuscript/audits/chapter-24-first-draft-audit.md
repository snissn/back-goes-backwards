## Length and structure

The canonical body count is **4,501 whitespace-delimited words**, inside the brief's 4,500–5,500-word target. The pre-notes manuscript contains 5,508 words when figure blocks are included; the complete Markdown file, including notes, contains 6,482.

The canonical count used here:

1. excludes the chapter-title line;
2. stops before `## Notes`;
3. removes each complete blockquoted figure beginning with `> **Figure` and continuing through the end of that blockquote; and
4. retains body headings and nonfigure displayed chains.

This definition is the proposed repeatable method for later chapter audits. It avoids the earlier ambiguity in which headings and displayed chains were excluded but figure-adjacent prose could be counted inconsistently.

7. synthesize the chapter's contribution and hand off to instability.

The count is within the style guide's five-to-eight range. The chapter no longer reads as a sequence of technical defenses: the necessary qualifications now enter at the causal links they govern.

## Causal and mechanics audit

The chapter maintains the following levels:

```text
surrounding anatomy and task
    -> candidate boundary conditions and imposed exposure
    -> wall stress, strain, and deformation
    -> reconstructed retained wall-state change
    -> observed wall and lumen findings
    -> local thrombus / distal occlusion
    -> collateral, pressure, and partly unmeasured perfusion state
    -> findings and presentation
    -> clinical interpretation
```

Support for one level is not used as automatic support for the next. In particular:

- narrowing is not load;
- pressure is not wall stress;
- wall stress is not strain;
- deformation is not force;
- position is not cumulative dose;
- lumen geometry is not flow or perfusion;
- collaterals are compatible with prior reorganization but do not date it or prove adequate perfusion;
- coldness does not identify a tissue or substitute for measured perfusion;
- symptoms do not establish diagnosis; and
- later or post-treatment geometry does not recover untreated history.

The artery is described as containing pressurized blood and conveying pulsatile flow while the wall sustains pressure-derived stress. Patient-specific stress or strain is not inferred from the image. The chapter names the reference geometry, wall properties, axial constraint, luminal pressure, external contact conditions, and task state that a serious estimate would require.

Intrinsic, extrinsic, mixed, reverse-direction, non-target, and measurement alternatives enter at the arrows they would change. The chapter does not force every possible mechanical contribution into one universal rank scheme.

## Hard/soft and terminology audit

Hard and soft remain relative roles. The focal bony assembly is comparatively shape-preserving; the artery is comparatively form-receiving while remaining active, pressurized, loaded, and capable of restoring behavior. Hard does not mean bone-only or universally good. Soft does not mean passive, weak, unloaded, anterior, or biologically inferior.

The phrase “load wants to travel through the hard body so that the soft body can remain soft” is preserved as inherited project shorthand that generates a measurement question, not as a physical law or established clinical conclusion. Chapter 24 does not reteach the full Chapter 13 definition or its traditional Chinese medicine motivation. Cross-slice review should confirm that Chapter 13 establishes those terms before this callback.

The revision keeps corridor/structure, wall/lumen, observed finding/reconstructed history, pressure/flow, load/deformation, transient/retained, kinematic return/recovery, relative displacement/frictional work, and presentation/diagnosis distinct. *Compression* is not permitted to pool them into one mechanism.
