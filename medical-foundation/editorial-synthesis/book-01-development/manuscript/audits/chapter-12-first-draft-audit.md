## Mechanics and formal audit

### Free body and regional account

The object-distance example declares a quasi-static planar boundary, a selected reference point, object weight, and perpendicular moment arm. The relation \(M=Wd\) is used only to compare external moments. The prose explicitly leaves muscle sharing, passive tension, pressure support, joint reaction, contact distribution, and local fields modeled or unknown.

The draft does not infer a local spinal exposure from the external moment. The Dolan lifting study is used only for a bounded task comparison involving twenty-one men and eighteen women and varying mass, bulk, distance, asymmetry, speed, and style. Its electromyographic and normalization methods do not become direct local measurements or thresholds.

### Frames, curvature, and transformations

Curvature is treated as one model input rather than a verdict. The draft distinguishes global angle, segment orientation, local curvature, contact position, and full configuration. It states that force components can change when resolved in rotated local axes while the force in the laboratory frame remains the same. Rotation matrices are limited to coordinate transformation; no transformed component becomes evidence of decompression or benefit.

The two spinal-model sources remain distinct. The twenty-eight CT-derived lumbar geometries support level- and quantity-specific model dependence under standardized compression. The eighty-five non-pathological radiographs support observed sagittal variation and model-estimated load variation. Neither supplies symptoms, an optimal curve, or a local capacity comparison.

### Resultant and field

The surface-traction relation is used as a standard mechanical identity. The rectangular patch then gives two exactly integrable fields over the same width and length: a uniform pressure and a linearly varying pressure with the same total normal force and average but twice the uniform value at one edge. Geometry, area, coordinates, and the construction's idealized status are explicit.

The draft also names the conditions that make an anatomical contact problem richer: separation, changing contact area, normal and tangential traction, nonlinear or time-dependent material behavior, active support, and boundary conditions. It never treats force divided by visible area as a measured peak field.

### Finite sensitivity

The sensitivity representation is:

```text
S(q,g) = (Delta q / q_ref) / (Delta g / g_ref)
```

Its controls are complete for a first draft:

- \(g\) is one named scalar geometric input;
- \(q\) is one named scalar summary derived from a local field;
- numerator and denominator each normalize like units by nonzero references;
- the resulting comparison is dimensionless;
- the two states, interval, task, material/contact model, and boundary conditions must be declared;
- signed and magnitude-only conventions are distinguished;
- the quantity is finite rather than a derivative;
- no whole-body or conserved meaning is assigned; and
- near-zero references, nonlinearity, contact-regime changes, resolution, uncertainty, and omitted-term dominance are explicit failure conditions.

The draft further makes the scalarization visible. Peak, percentile, area-above-threshold, principal strain, and regional average are different choices for \(q\); thickness, angle, radius, pointwise surface error, and fit error are different choices for \(g\). This prevents “small geometry” and “large local result” from concealing incompatible measures.

Formal, dimensional, contact-mechanics, and numerical review remain publication gates. The audit establishes internal coherence, not independent validation.

## Enthesis counterexample audit

The enthesis sequence preserves three different source jobs:

1. Schwartz and colleagues support a measured micrometer-scale developmental mineral gradient at the murine supraspinatus enthesis.
2. Deymier and colleagues support micromechanical testing of eleven beams from six mice, local image correlation, a high-compliance region, and lower strength in the small mineral-defect group; modulus, toughness, resilience, and failure strain are correctly reported as not significantly different.
3. Liu and colleagues support an idealized model in which spatially varied anisotropic grading changed selected stress concentrations and the optimized grading was not a simple linear interpolation.

Measured anatomy, measured micromechanics, and modeled stress effects remain distinct. The draft does not transfer the mouse findings to human injury or use the idealized model as a living field measurement.

The counterexample performs its required conceptual job. A normal material transition can localize deformation and also help transfer demand. Local compliance is not weakness by definition. Conversely, a gradient is not automatically protective across directions and boundary conditions. Stress and strain fields, observation scales, constitutive relations, and receiver-specific consequences remain separate.

## Null, alternative, and chronology audit

The following required branches appear in ordinary argument rather than in a disclaimer appendix:

- negligible regional change despite visible geometry change;
- regional redistribution without a material local peak;
- localization at the proposed site;
- localization at another site;
- active, pressure-mediated, or environmental redistribution before the interface;
- a model result too sensitive or uncertain to interpret;
- geometry difference without a material exposure difference;
- exposure difference with no demonstrated adverse response and a separately required tolerance basis;
- a later contribution through a defined exposure history and receiver capacity, deferred to Chapter 19; and
- another state changing material properties, activation, movement, geometry, and exposure, leaving reverse direction plausible.

The asymptomatic CT study is used narrowly. The sample comprises 1,111 hips from 590 asymptomatic people, and several measurements crossed commonly used morphology thresholds. The draft does not claim that shape never matters, that asymptomatic means mechanically identical, or that the sample establishes lifelong tolerance. It uses the study to prevent an imaging label from substituting for local field, function, symptoms, or disease.

The healthy patellofemoral MRI study is also bounded: fifteen healthy men, static loads of zero, 200, and 400 newtons, changing contact area, and cartilage-thickness reduction. It supports ordinary deformation and contact change under load, not a complete pressure field, injury threshold, or universal area rule.

## Controlled-salvage and scope audit

The draft preserves the archive's intended positive program:

- whole-body and segment geometry can alter path, leverage, support, contact, and regional demand;
- a subtle global difference can produce a materially different local result under bounded conditions;
- local stress, strain, pressure, traction, and deformation can be strongly nonuniform;
- hard-body organization and form-receiving tissue freedom can be investigated through named quantities and receivers;
- contact, curvature, congruence, thickness, stiffness contrast, and material boundaries remain first-class mechanical questions;
- concentration, cumulative exposure, compensation, and delayed consequence remain available to later chapters; and
- nulls and alternatives make the program testable rather than deleting it.

The reconstruction supplies the intermediate mechanics that the archive compressed. “Misalignment creates focal stress” becomes geometry → regional balance → interface → local field. “Small deviations cause large consequences” becomes a bounded, normalized input–output sensitivity plus alternative branches. “Joint stress” becomes a named spatial quantity over a stated surface. “Stiffness mismatch” receives the normal graded enthesis counterexample. “Repeated concentration causes degeneration” remains protected for a later exposure–response bridge.

The reader draft contains no universal alignment claim, posterior correction, signed preferred mode, all-Yang configuration, Six-Division mapping, breath geometry, torus dynamics, bandha, TCM channel claim, diagnostic rule, disease reversal, ergonomic instruction, self-test, movement cue, corrective drill, or practice protocol. It contains no legacy angular-velocity coupling and no rotation-matrix-to-decompression inference.

## Internal decision and next action

Chapter 12 passes its controlled first-draft gate. It makes the strongest authorized mechanical claim plainly: geometry can materially redistribute regional resultants and local fields. It distinguishes regional redistribution from local concentration inside the longer task → regional → interface → field → response chain; uses the validated hip example without converting prescribed, measured, and modeled quantities into one evidence state; uses the enthesis as a normal graded-boundary counterexample; formalizes local sensitivity without creating a universal law; and includes null, compensated, asymptomatic, tolerated-only-if-evidenced, uncertain, and reverse-direction branches.

The draft is suitable for continuity review with Chapters 11 and 13 and for later Chapter 19 and Chapter 23 dependency work. It is not publication-approved and does not authorize any closed extension above. The next controlled step is seam and repetition review, followed by the listed external specialist, medical-coauthor, source, and figure gates rather than rhetorical strengthening of the present claims.
