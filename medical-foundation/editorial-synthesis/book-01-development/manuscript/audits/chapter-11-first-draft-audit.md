The chapter answers its central question at the authorized strength. A successful handoff is not one unchanged tissue path; it is a mechanically closed task relation that can change carriers and roles. ## Balance, allocation, and route-model audit

The draft keeps the external and internal accounts in separate layers. The external layer names the selected boundary, contacts, force and moment requirements, and task phase. The internal layer contains candidate active, tensile, skeletal, joint-contact, pressure-mediated, passive, compliant, and external-support contributions. Figure 11.1 uses observed or measured, calculated, and modeled or hypothesized descriptions rather than drawing a unique internal line.

The prose preserves four non-equivalences:

1. external balance does not identify internal allocation;
2. a net joint moment does not identify unique muscle, tendon, or contact forces;
3. task success does not establish that a proposed internal route was available or used; and
4. visible configuration or embodied continuity does not measure a carrier or quantity.

Continuity is allowed to be sequential, parallel, distributed, partial, phase dependent, or unresolved. A route must terminate in declared support, opposition, momentum change, or a smaller-system boundary account. This closes the mechanical bookkeeping without claiming that every internal force can be identified.

The route representation is explicitly replaceable. A single edge is not retained when a network or field better represents distributed contact, pressure, or state-dependent participation. The chapter therefore uses graph language as a first-order record rather than an anatomical ontology.

## Hard/soft and handoff audit

The exact governing sentence appears once and unaltered:

> Load wants to travel through the hard body so that the soft body can remain soft.

Its adjacent controls satisfy the brief:

- “wants” is identified as a heuristic about geometry, relative stiffness, contact, active control, pressure, and available paths;
- *load* remains umbrella language until a technical passage names the quantity;
- the distinction is stated as **motivated by traditional Chinese medicine's hard/soft and Yin/Yang concepts**;
- traditional and mechanical categories are explicitly not definitionally identical;
- hard and soft are changing relational roles assigned for direction, phase, state, scale, and time;
- hard includes relative shape preservation, path establishment, force transmission, constraint, or structural resolution;
- soft includes relative form reception and sufficient freedom to conform, glide, contain, expand, change pressure, or recover;
- anatomically soft tissues, pressure-supported assemblies, and external supports can perform hard roles;
- soft does not mean weak, passive, unloaded, anterior, or mechanically unimportant; and
- a successful handoff can include ordinary deformation, storage, return, pressure, sliding, and changing contact.

The pressure-model example prevents the hard role from becoming bone-only. Its model dependence, posture and coactivation dependence, and exclusion from breath-driver or technique claims are visible in body and note. The tendon and cartilage nulls prevent soft loading from becoming leakage by definition.

The chapter does not repeat Chapter 3's complete defense of the polarity. It applies the inherited model to transfer and adds the unique question of whether a form-receiving freedom is preserved across a change in carrier or role.

## Leakage, state distinctions, and null audit

Leakage is positively preserved as a project-native routing hypothesis rather than removed. It is translated into a possible poorly matched exposure in a named receiver. The minimum account includes receiver, quantity and direction, exposure history, required freedom, proposed route change, mismatch criterion, alternative, and disfavoring result.

The draft prevents these collapses:

- deformation → leakage;
- soft-tissue force → leakage;
- increased activation → leakage;
- anterior, lateral, medial, superficial, or deep location → misrouting;
- less participation → interruption;
- high force or fatigue → overload;
- alternate support → harmful compensation; and
- changed allocation → hidden pathology.

The forearm-contact comparison supplies the normal-deformation null: local tissues can conform while the broader relation transmits force without demonstrated loss of freedom. A stronger adverse account would need a maintained or repeated state, task-relevant lost excursion, matched capacity comparison, and separately observed consequence.

The endpoint-equivalence null is supported at several levels. The cat-hindlimb model demonstrates broad feasible activation solutions for prescribed endpoint force. Human reaching demonstrates stabilized endpoint variables alongside joint-configuration variance. The moving-handle study demonstrates measured redistribution from selected hip and knee moments toward handle force. None establishes equal cost, global preference, long-term benefit, or hidden harm.

The matched transfer-state comparison gives each label a different observation burden. It permits mixed and unresolved states rather than forcing one category after the fact. Substitution remains neutral and can be chosen, beneficial, enabling, or better suited to anatomy, disability, current state, device, or environment.

## Route-record and evidence-state audit

The four route variables retain exactly the packet's status:

```text
A_e(t) = availability record
U_e(t) = participation or use record
E_e(t) = exposure record
C_e(t) = outcome-specific present capacity record
```

They are expressly not a validated equation, route score, additive vector, or force-packet law. The draft does not subtract exposure from capacity or multiply availability by use. Exposure and capacity must match receiver, quantity, direction, outcome, and time window before comparison.

Figure 11.4 separates direct observation, instrument measurement, calculation, model output, inference, hypothesis, and embodied report. Blank internal-allocation fields are permitted. The task and boundary, and an alternative/disfavoring result for a serious hypothesis, are not permitted to remain unspecified.

The embodied force-line boundary is retained. Felt continuity can be reported by task, side, and phase and can motivate a candidate measured correlate. It does not become a measured force, anatomical cable, or proof of TCM correspondence.

Disfavoring observations are local and consequential. They include anatomical unavailability, failure of external balance, unchanged internal exposure under the proposed variation, unchanged substitute when the presumed interruption is altered, normal receiver deformation and recovery, a simpler predictive model, inability to distinguish route states, and no added prediction from hard/soft language. Failure of a local account narrows that account without being used to decide the whole later-series hypothesis.

## Controlled-salvage and scope audit

The draft preserves the archive's intended escalation without using its unsupported endpoint claims:

```text
integrated multicarrier architecture
    -> changing hard/soft division of labor
    -> task-specific continuity and handoff
    -> candidate interruption, bypass, overload, or substitution
    -> named receiver and local exposure
    -> later concentration, constraint, timing, compensation, biological,
       and medical bridges
```

Recovered directly or reconstructed:

- soft-to-hard transfer survives as a bounded candidate handoff rather than a universal tissue ladder;
- continuity survives as task-specific availability rather than one foot-to-skull cable;
- leakage survives as a measurable exposure hypothesis rather than a generic warning;
- glide, pressure, contact, active force, tensile transfer, and external support remain candidate mechanical jobs;
- compensation survives here as neutral endpoint-preserving substitution, with chronology left to Chapter 15;
- local myofascial evidence survives with scale limits; and
- embodied force-line experience survives as hypothesis-generating observation.

Protected forward rather than promoted:

- posterior necessity and preferred organization;
- all-Yang routing and signed generalized modes;
- exact hard/soft, Yin/Yang, channel, and Six-Division correspondence;
- breath, pressure phase, bandha, and torus dynamics as route drivers;
- diagnostic interpretation of interruption or substitution;
- biological, symptomatic, disease-specific, or systemic consequences; and
- tests, cues, corrections, training, or practice.

Reader-prose searches found no control ID, archive locator, prompt instruction, acceptance criterion, migration note, generated equation, diagnosis, named disorder, treatment, self-test, exercise, protocol, preferred all-Yang route, channel mapping, bandha, torus, or corrective breath claim. Posterior and ventral do not appear as moralized route categories.

## Internal decision and next action

Chapter 11 passes its bounded first-draft gate. It preserves the exact authorial hard/soft formulation and agreed **motivated by** provenance, distinguishes external balance from internal allocation, defines continuity without a hidden cable, makes handoff multicarrier and phase dependent, translates leakage into a vulnerable exposure hypothesis, separates all required transfer states, gives normal deformation and endpoint equivalence full null status, and keeps the route records as nonadditive prompts rather than a score.
