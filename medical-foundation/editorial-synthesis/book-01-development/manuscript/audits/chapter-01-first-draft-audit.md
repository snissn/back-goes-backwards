## Source-note audit

The source set remains deliberately small and matches the evidence pack:

1. Bonfanti and colleagues support internally and externally generated tissue stresses and the multiscale character of living-tissue fracture. The draft does not use the review as a general health source.
2. Humphrey, Dufresne, and Schwartz support dynamic cell–extracellular-matrix reciprocity in soft connective tissue. The draft excludes a universal body-wide set point or preferred state.
3. Leung, Glagov, and Mathews provide the bounded mechanical-to-biological example. The body preserves rabbit aortic medial cells, elastin membranes, repeated elongation and relaxation, matrix-component synthesis, in-vitro status, and the lack of intact-vessel or whole-person transfer.
4. Thompson and colleagues provide the bounded biological-to-mechanical comparison. The body preserves bovine cartilage explants, interleukin-1 beta exposure, matrix catabolism, changed stiffness and stress-relaxation behavior, in-vitro status, and the absence of a human clinical or treatment inference.
5. Guo, Mofrad, and Tepole support the methodological need for explicit multiscale modeling bridges. The review is not used to identify an internal field from the object transfer.
6. WHO ICF and its beginner’s guide support contextual description of body structure and function, activity, participation, and environment. The chapter explicitly says that this framework does not settle etiology, equal causal weighting, or any specific factor-to-node pathway.

The chapter's concrete environmental relations are either part of the stipulated task geometry or presented as candidate comparisons. Its illness, fatigue, pain, expectation, access, work, and caregiving pathways are questions to observe or source, not conclusions attributed to the ICF. A later chapter that makes one of these arrows load-bearing must add evidence for that exact job.

All five DOI identities were checked against live Crossref records, and both WHO links returned current official pages on 2026-07-11. Bibliographic normalization and source-freshness review remain publication gates; this check does not substitute for them.

## Terminology, scope, and safety audit

The required post-draft search covered *load*, *stress*, *strain*, *pressure*, *path*, *support*, *adaptation*, *alignment*, *balance*, *integrity*, *efficiency*, *resilience*, and the later-book vocabulary.

- *Load* appears as ordinary language or as the unresolved umbrella handed to Chapter 2. It is not used as a measured local quantity.
- *Stress*, *strain*, and *pressure* name distinct candidate mechanical objects and never substitute for symptoms, disease, or generic burden.
- *Path* is used only for the object’s route through the room, available strategies, causal paths, or the explicitly unclaimed internal route.
- *Support* describes object contact, assistance, surfaces, or material resolution; it does not encode a preferred posture or health status.
- *Adaptation* appears only in the sentence that denies it as a universal response. *Integrity* and *efficiency* do not carry an argument. *Alignment*, *resilience*, and the project’s later-book technical vocabulary are absent.
- Breathing and circulation appear only as ordinary ongoing physiology in the opening; neither acts as organizer, intervention, or health mechanism.

The draft contains no diagnosis-by-position, self-test, prevention claim, corrective cue, exercise, protocol, treatment recommendation, care substitution, named chronic-disease mechanism, or individualized advice. The acute-illness branch is hypothetical and used only to demonstrate low mechanical priority for a selected question.
