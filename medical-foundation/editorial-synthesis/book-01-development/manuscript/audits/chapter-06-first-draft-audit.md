## Muscle, tendon, and role audit

The draft does not use muscle as a generic actuator. It describes active muscle as state- and history-dependent and ties each job to the recurring transfer: positioning during contact, changing force during acceptance, ongoing force during a steady-looking hold, and braking or redirection during placement. The boundary paragraph preserves a key scale distinction: muscle forces remain internal at the person-plus-object boundary, become explicit unknowns at selected segment or joint boundaries, and require additional material and architectural variables at tissue scale.

Surface electromyography, activation, muscle force, joint moment, apparent stiffness, and task stability remain separate. Coactivation is allowed to change a measured or modeled force–displacement response without becoming a recommendation for maximal bracing or a synonym for stability. The prose neither says individual-muscle force is unknowable nor infers it from visible motion.

The tensile account keeps the authorial handoff in a positive form. Active tissue can generate or redirect force; tendon, ligament, fascia, capsule, or a pressure boundary can transmit or constrain it; a graded attachment, contact, or interface changes its local expression; and skeletal geometry plus external support participate in resolution. The prose adds three required corrections without qualifying the handoff out of existence:

1. several carriers can operate in parallel;
2. energy, force components, contact, and recruitment can change direction or timing; and
3. the division of labor changes by phase.

Tendon is neither passive nor lossless. The nonhuman evidence is named by species and task, and the porcine wrap-around-tendon result remains ex vivo and direction-specific. Ligamentous and capsular statements recall Chapter 5's established configuration dependence rather than adding an unsupported universal joint rule. The attachment is presented as a distributed change in local expression, not a dimensionless transfer point or proof of one complete sequence.

The hard/soft callback uses the required wording: the vocabulary is **motivated by traditional Chinese medicine**. It applies Chapter 3's relative shape-preserving/form-receiving distinction rather than redefining it. Tendon can be comparatively shape-preserving longitudinally while form-receiving in another mode; cartilage can receive form while carrying substantial contact demand; and a pressure-bounded assembly can become comparatively firm. Soft never means weak, unloaded, passive, anterior, or inferior.

## Deformation, interface, and pressure audit

The draft establishes ordinary deformation before mentioning adverse constraint. Cartilage is presented as a fluid-saturated solid in a bounded experimental and theoretical context. Gross task motion, relative excursion or glide, structural deformation, and local contact or material quantities remain distinct. The paragraph on magnitude prevents the positive account from becoming a rule that more mobility is always better.

Chapter 13 receives an exact downstream dependency: a form-receiving structure becomes a candidate adverse case only when a specified relation maintains a consequential state, exceeds a capacity, or prevents task-relevant movement. Chapter 6 makes no injury, degeneration, symptom, diagnosis, or treatment inference.

Every structural use of pressure names a boundary and scale:

- articular cartilage is a fluid-saturated material under declared specimen contact and compression;
- a hydrostatic skeleton uses a muscular and connective boundary around approximately volume-conserving contents; and
- the human lumbar result belongs to three prone participants under phrenic-nerve stimulation.

The displayed relation

```text
F_p = integral over A of -p n dA
```

declares the selected surface, pressure, and normal and is used only to prevent pressure from becoming boundaryless. The text distinguishes cavity pressure, contact pressure, boundary traction, wall stress, and reported internal effort. It states separately that uniform pressure on a complete closed surface yields no net translational resultant and that a pressure difference across a deformable wall can generate wall stress and alter or resist deformation. Final mechanics review remains required for sign convention, surface orientation, and reader interpretation.

The three-participant Hodges experiment remains explicit in the body and note. Its 8–31 percent stiffness change is tied to the study's prone stimulation setup and endpoint. The draft does not generalize it to voluntary breathing, upright function, posture, pain, health, treatment, or whole-body organization. Ordinary breathing is named once only to preserve the later Book IV question; it performs no load-bearing work in Chapter 6.

## Fascia and cross-scale audit

The chapter gives fascia a substantive role without turning it into one undifferentiated cable. It names septa, aponeuroses, retinacula, sheaths, compartment walls, and thoracolumbar fascia, then separates connecting, separating, containing, attaching, gliding, sensory, and tensile jobs. The text makes a positive point often lost in network language: preserving separation can be as mechanically important as making a connection.

The Barker cadaveric experiment remains bounded to eight unembalmed cadavers, 10 N traction at named muscle attachments, and measured response in posterior and middle lumbar fasciae. It is used to establish regional tensile transmission under that protocol, not living task dominance, neural control, clinical effect, or a traditional channel.

The continuity ladder preserves five separate propositions:

1. named anatomy is connected;
2. perturbation produces a measured regional mechanical response;
3. its magnitude matters in a specified living task;
4. it changes an endpoint after alternatives are considered; and
5. a whole-body model predicts better than competing representations.

The systematic-review and meta-analytic sources are used at their actual rungs. Anatomical continuity and some adjacent transfer remain supported; the 2026 remote-stiffness result is correctly described as measured during local lengthening or contraction and remains compatible with several carriers and controls; living-task magnitude, clinical consequence, route dominance, and whole-body comparative advantage remain open. The draft neither dismisses the larger tension-network program nor lets a lower rung prove a higher one.

## Initial state, network, and tensegrity audit

The initial-state section is plural by construction. It distinguishes local activation, passive resistance, length and contraction history, pressure, and contact from regional coactivation or prestress and from a global set-point hypothesis. External support and environment are included so that a proposed global tone does not absorb boundary differences. The same visible configuration can therefore enter a different subsequent mechanical problem without becoming evidence of one hidden scalar.

*Prestress* retains its mechanics meaning: stress present before an added demand in a declared model. It is not used as health, readiness, optimality, or tensegrity by definition. The global-set-point idea remains visible as a legitimate later hypothesis that would require simultaneous multiscale variables, formal coupling, and a discriminating prediction.

The model table prevents integration from becoming coronation. The six families differ by natural object, inputs, output, and stopping condition. In particular:

- a broad tension-network model may include active members, multiaxial tissues, articular contact, pressure boundaries, external support, and changing topology;
- formal tensegrity requires declared nodes, discrete compression members in a continuous or distributed tensile relation, connectivity, member behavior, prestress, constraints, equilibrium, and contact rules; and
- either model must add a prediction or clarification beyond linked-segment, continuum, muscle-control, or pressure accounts.

The stop rule is explicit. The network claim narrows when it cannot be specified before the result, when a predicted carrier does not participate, when active control or external contact explains a remote change equally well, or when a simpler model predicts the endpoint with fewer assumptions. Tensegrity is retained as a legitimate research-generating model; visual resemblance, anatomical continuity, or holistic appeal does not select it.
