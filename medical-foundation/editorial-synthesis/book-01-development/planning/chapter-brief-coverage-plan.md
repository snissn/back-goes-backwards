# Book I Chapter-Brief Coverage Plan

**Status:** working expansion plan

**Scope:** all 30 Book I units in the accepted six-Part architecture

**Current coverage:** 3 drafting-depth pilot briefs; 27 briefs to create

## Purpose

This plan scales the method established by Chapters 2, 13, and 24 without turning every chapter into an evidence pack before its argument is stable. It records a dependency-aware development order, the recurring material each brief must control, and the review burden expected from the current outline metadata.

All 27 missing briefs can be developed provisionally without waiting for literature completion or medical-coauthor review. Those later gates control evidence state and publication readiness. The only near-term authorial choices likely to change brief architecture are the primary worked-case selections in Chapters 23, 25, and 26; their briefs can still be built to a case-selection gate in advance.

## Current authorial controls

- **Hard and soft:** use the relational distinction between relatively shape-preserving and relatively form-receiving roles. Chapter 3 should identify the vocabulary early as a flexible mechanical translation motivated by TCM hard/soft and Yin/Yang concepts, without claiming exact definitional identity. A tissue's role can change with direction, task, scale, state, and time.
- **Chapter 13 neutral transfer example:** flexor tendon–subsynovial connective tissue relative glide remains the primary non-lumen example; median-nerve excursion remains available as a reserve or later comparison.
- **Chapter 24 thoracic-outlet example:** phenotype distinctions are useful only where arterial, venous, or neural tissues require materially different variables and causal chains. The chapter need not make the taxonomy of thoracic-outlet syndromes its subject. Its controlling job is to test maintained deformation, lost excursion, chronology, and alternatives in a dynamic corridor.
- **Series boundary:** Book I stands without proving preferred configuration, the signed generalized-mode model, Six-Division or channel correspondence, breath as a whole-system driver, or a corrective practice system. It may name those programs openly and hand them to Books II–V.

## Cross-batch dependencies that should become shared assets

The following work should be created once and referenced across briefs rather than duplicated:

1. **Book-wide claim ladder and evidence-state legend:** extend the pilot conventions only when a new load-bearing claim family appears.
2. **Recurring-object specification:** one bounded object-transfer task, phases, system boundaries, and permitted variants for Chapters 1–8.
3. **Hard/soft control record:** one authorially aligned definition that includes TCM motivation, relational roles, observability questions, and the no-exact-identity boundary.
4. **Failure-family matrix:** definitions, discriminating observations, overlaps, counterexamples, and chapter ownership for all six families.
5. **Common causal-chain figure grammar:** shared notation for observed, measured, calculated, modeled, inferred, reconstructed, and hypothesized links.
6. **Whole-person alternatives matrix:** recurring biological, psychological, social, developmental, and environmental alternatives without boilerplate repetition.
7. **Worked-case selection rubric:** teaching job, evidence accessibility, case provenance, negative comparison, medical exposure, figure feasibility, and transfer value.
8. **Later-book seam table:** exact Book I signposts and destinations in Books II–V so deferred material remains visible without carrying the foundation.
