# Worked-Narrative Selection and Comparison

**Status:** shared Book I development control

**Primary owners:** Chapters 23–26

**Required use:** case selection, evidence-pack design, drafting, figures, and the Chapter 27 synthesis

## Purpose

Part V should make the Book I framework do real work without turning four examples into four confirmations of the same story. Each chapter therefore tests a different part of the model, admits outcomes that would narrow or disfavor it, and earns only the level of conclusion supported by its evidence.

The cases are selected for teaching structure rather than dramatic diagnosis. They must expose intermediate variables, alternative histories, and missing links. A case that ends with a partial, mixed, low-mechanical-priority, or unresolved account can be more valuable than a smooth narrative that hides uncertainty.

This control coordinates:

- the [causal-chain and evidence-state grammar](causal-chain-and-evidence-state-grammar.md);
- the [adverse-organization family matrix](adverse-organization-family-matrix.md);
- the chapter briefs for [geometry and localized strain](../chapter-briefs/BGB-U-0023-geometry-localized-strain.md), [thoracic outlet](../chapter-briefs/BGB-U-0024-thoracic-outlet.md), [instability and repeated demand](../chapter-briefs/BGB-U-0025-instability-repeated-demand.md), and [silent compensation](../chapter-briefs/BGB-U-0026-silent-compensation-mixed-chronic.md); and

The sequence deliberately becomes less bounded. Chapter 23 offers the cleanest mechanical reconstruction. Chapter 24 adds tissue-specific physiology and retained state. Chapter 25 adds recurrence and changing control. Chapter 26 asks the framework to remain useful under causal plurality and incomplete history.

## Case-source forms

Every narrative must declare which source form it uses. Forms may be combined only when the reader can tell which fact comes from which source.

| Source form | Legitimate use | Minimum disclosure | Cannot establish by itself |
|---|---|---|---|
| Idealized mechanical construction | Teach a force, moment, contact, deformation, or scale relation | Assumptions, boundary, units or normalization, model state, and failure conditions | Prevalence, actual patient exposure, symptoms, diagnosis, or clinical importance |
| Transparent hypothetical vignette | Hold variables constant, compare causal orders, and expose missing observations | State that no patient is represented; identify which features are pedagogically assigned | Empirical frequency, prognosis, natural history, or that the proposed sequence occurs |
| Published case | Preserve an unusually well-documented chronology or mechanism | Source, selection context, contemporaneous facts, retrospective facts, and missing intervals | Typicality, general prevalence, or population-level effect |
| Source-grounded composite | Integrate recurring evidence features without implying one complete patient | Provenance for every feature; explicit composite label; no invented decisive link | That all features commonly coexist or form one continuous observed history |
| Cohort or comparison group | Establish correspondence, noncorrespondence, association, or distribution | Population, definition, comparator, measurement, effect and uncertainty, and selection limits | Individual causal history or a unique mechanism |
| Within-person or experimental comparison | Test task modulation or a bounded mechanical link | Intervention or task change, timing, measurement validity, carryover, and alternatives | Original cause, diagnosis, durable benefit, or generalizability |
| Clinical definition or guideline | Bound a presentation and represent current care | Version, population, intended use, and evidentiary scope | A project-specific mechanism or a complete causal explanation |

## Entry gate for a lead case

A candidate may become the lead only when it can support all of the following at a level appropriate to its teaching job:

1. **Bounded event and system.** The task, position or episode, environment, contacts, anatomy, phase, and scale can be stated without a generic body-wide story.
2. **Visible provenance.** Observed, reported, measured, calculated, modeled, inferred, hypothesized, embodied, and reconstructed material can be distinguished.
3. **Intermediate variables.** The case contains more than an endpoint and an explanation. At least one consequential mechanical bridge can be measured, calculated, or modeled with stated limits.
4. **Time structure.** Exposure, current state, response, recovery, recurrence, retained change, and missing intervals are represented as applicable.
5. **Tissue or system specificity.** Force, pressure, stress, strain, excursion, wall state, contact, activation, conduction, flow, or another variable is selected for the actual structure.
6. **Noncorrespondence.** The evidence program can include people, tasks, states, or observations in which geometry, mechanics, findings, symptoms, and outcomes do not align neatly.
7. **Alternatives at the right link.** Reverse causality, common cause, ordinary variation, biological primary processes, behavior, context, measurement error, and no important mechanical contribution enter where they affect the chain.
8. **A real null ending.** The case can conclude that the routing framework adds little, not merely that more research is needed before accepting the preferred story.
9. **No hidden protocol.** Selection does not depend on teaching a provocative maneuver, self-test, correction, exercise, or treatment.
10. **Review feasibility.** The required biomechanics, anatomy, mechanobiology, clinical, causal-method, accessibility, and figure reviews can actually be obtained.

Failure at one gate does not automatically delete the underlying concept. The candidate may become a shorter comparison, an idealized model, a research obligation, or a quarantined medical claim.

## Shared narrative sequence

Every chapter should remain reducible to the same sequence while emphasizing a different segment:

1. **Declare the case form and boundary.** State whether the case is idealized, hypothetical, published, composite, comparative, or mixed.
2. **Record the initial observations.** Do not begin with the project's causal interpretation.
3. **Define the selected task, history, and system.** State contacts, frames, relevant structures, environment, and time.
4. **Reconstruct external demand and internal distribution.** Mark all unmeasured internal terms.
5. **Identify the proposed local exposure or lost freedom.** Use a tissue- or interface-specific quantity.
6. **Add dose, current capacity, and state.** Keep a high exposure distinct from an adverse response.
7. **Trace the biological or functional bridge only as far as evidence allows.** Separate tissue response, finding, function, symptom, diagnosis, and outcome.
8. **Build the chronology.** Permit recovery, retention, recurrence, substitution, mixed state, and reverse direction.
9. **Insert alternatives and disfavoring observations at each contested arrow.** Do not save them for a final disclaimer.
10. **Compare at least two final interpretations.** One must be nonconfirming, low-mechanical-priority, or unresolved.
11. **State what the framework added.** Name a sharper variable, comparison, chronology, or research question rather than claiming value by comprehensiveness.
12. **State what remains unavailable.** End before diagnosis, prognosis, treatment, or later-book preferred-configuration inference unless separately supported and in scope.

| Recurrent object | First full owner | Permitted later use |

| Maintained extrinsic, intrinsic, and mixed state | Chapter 24 | Chapters 25–26 may use the time-state distinction without reusing the corridor narrative |
| Material, functional, and experienced instability | Chapter 25 | Chapter 26 may mention control uncertainty but does not repeat the three-way taxonomy |
| Compensation begins by preserving function | Chapter 25 as worked example; Chapter 15 as conceptual owner | Chapter 26 extends it into an ambiguous long history |
| Whole-person causal plurality | Chapter 26 | Earlier cases include specific alternatives but remain mechanically bounded |
| Evidence-state chain and legitimate null | Shared control | All four cases must instantiate them with case-specific records |

The same anatomy should not become the primary example for two distinct chapters merely because it can illustrate both. A callback earns space only when changing the model exposes a limit or transfer, as tendon–SSCT relative glide does for Chapter 24.

## Comparative outcome set

Each chapter should finish with an outcome set appropriate to its evidence rather than a binary verdict.

| Outcome posture | Meaning | Required language discipline |
|---|---|---|
| Mechanically substantial | The supported mechanical chain explains a material part of the selected exposure, function, finding, or outcome | Specify the endpoint and population; do not convert contribution into total cause |
| Mechanically contributory or modifying | Mechanical variables alter probability, severity, timing, performance, or experience alongside other processes | State which variable changed and how contribution was distinguished from correlation |
| Mixed or reciprocal | Mechanical and nonmechanical variables influence one another or causal direction changes through time | Show chronology and feedback; do not choose an arbitrary first cause |
| Low mechanical priority | The proposed routing account adds little relative to another explanation for the selected question | Treat as a successful discriminating result, not a caveat |
| Unresolved plurality | Several histories remain compatible with the available evidence | Preserve missing observations and the next discriminating study; do not reward the most coherent story |
| Disfavored proposed route | A required intermediate link fails, the model predicts poorly, or a stronger alternative accounts for the evidence | Narrow or remove that route while preserving other independently supported parts of the framework |

These are descriptive outcome postures, not a mandatory clinical classification and not a score. A case may support different postures for different links or times.

## Evidence-pack architecture

Each lead candidate needs a case-specific pack with the same top-level sections:

1. presentation and population definition;
2. anatomy and system boundary;
3. task, exposure, and chronology;
4. kinematics, kinetics, contact, pressure, stress, strain, excursion, activation, or other relevant mechanics;
5. tissue or system response;
6. function, finding, symptom, diagnosis, or outcome, kept separate;
7. noncorrespondence and asymptomatic comparison;
8. alternative and reverse-causal explanations;
9. measurement validity and model limitations;
10. clinical and specialist review notes;
11. claim-by-claim synthesis with evidence posture; and
12. explicit reasons to retain, narrow, replace, or quarantine the candidate.

The Chapter 23 candidates should receive parallel short packs before one is promoted. Chapter 24 already has a pilot evidence structure and needs clinical completion. Chapter 25 needs separate local-instability and proximal-routing judgments. Chapter 26 needs a provenance design before literature is assembled so that a composite cannot be reverse-engineered to fit the theory.

## Figure comparison program

The Part V figure set should look like a progressive stress test rather than four colored anatomy panels:

- **Chapter 23:** nested scales and a local field;
- **Chapter 24:** maintaining relation, tissue-specific variable, and state through time;
- **Chapter 25:** recurrent-state loop with noncorresponding instability measures;
- **Chapter 26:** branching chronology with competing causal orders and unresolved cells; and
- **Chapter 27 callback:** the same evidence-state and question grammar compressed without becoming a checklist score.

Every figure should show what is missing as carefully as what is present. Alternatives belong beside the affected arrow. A caption must state the highest inference the figure supports and at least one inference it cannot support.

## Decision record required at promotion

When a candidate is promoted, record:

- the selected lead and source form;
- the chapter job it can uniquely perform;
- the strongest supported mechanical and medical endpoints;
- the weakest required arrow;
- the principal negative or noncorresponding comparison;
- the outcome postures the case genuinely permits;
- why the candidate was preferred to its alternatives;
- which preserved concepts moved to a comparison, research agenda, later book, or quarantine;
- all required specialty reviews; and
- explicit replacement triggers discovered during drafting.

Promotion is provisional through research and review. It is not permission for prose to outrun the evidence pack.

## Acceptance criteria

The Part V case program is ready for drafting when:

- each chapter has a unique mechanical and evidentiary job;
- every lead declares its source form and provenance limits;
- Chapter 23 has a documented lumbar-versus-limb selection result;
- Chapter 24's arterial anchor and composite form have passed condition-specific review;
- Chapter 25's ankle candidate can distinguish material, functional, and experienced instability and bound any proximal claim;
- Chapter 26 has a case-provenance design that permits low-priority and unresolved outcomes;
- each evidence pack includes noncorrespondence, reverse causality, and a real null ending;
- no case borrows a tissue variable, clinical mechanism, or evidence base from another system;
- the four endings can differ without threatening the book's coherence;
- the figure set makes increasing causal complexity visible; and
- Chapter 27 can synthesize the questions without turning the cases into diagnostic categories or a self-help protocol.
