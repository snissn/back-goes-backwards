# Part IV — From mechanical organization to medical meaning

## Part proposition

Medicine already measures and changes mechanical conditions. Book I adds a longitudinal account of how demand is routed, received, transformed biologically, and interpreted within whole-person causality and clinical workflow.

## Reader movement

Chapter 17 establishes common ground in medical mechanics. Chapter 18 defines the longitudinal routing record. Chapter 19 follows receiver-specific exposure into tissue response. Chapter 20 compares controlled freedom across several form-receiving systems. Chapter 21 assigns causal role within a whole person. Chapter 22 turns the resulting model into a selective professional workflow.

## Part opening — Mechanical reasoning is already medical

- **Proposition:** Pressure, flow, stability, contact, deformation, excursion, support, and distribution are established medical objects.
- **Develop:** Use a compact range of medical examples; state the additional longitudinal question; preview the receiver, response, causality, and workflow sequence.
- **Evidence/example:** A montage or table of established mechanical endpoints.
- **Continuity:** Inherits Part III's candidate pathways and hands Chapter 17 their medical common ground.
- **Target:** 300–450 words.
