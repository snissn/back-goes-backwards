# Later-Book Seam Table

**Status:** development control

**Purpose:** Control how Book I preserves, names, and hands off the stronger series programs without either making them carry the foundational argument or erasing them from the project.

This table reads the [series outline](../../series-synthesis/series-outline.md), [Book I outline](../../series-synthesis/books/01-medical-foundations.md), [Book I contract](../book-contract.md), [project-model memo](../../project-model-memo.md), and [Book II outline](../../series-synthesis/books/02-the-back-goes-backwards.md) together. It is a placement and inheritance control, not a judgment that deferred material is unimportant or unlikely to mature.

## Governing rule

Book I may do three things with a later-book hypothesis:

1. name its intellectual motivation or provenance;
2. state the hypothesis directly at the point where the reader can understand what it would add; and
3. identify the definitions, evidence, alternatives, and measurements that the later book must supply.

Book I may not use that hypothesis as an unstated premise, as evidence for an earlier claim, as the explanation of a medical outcome, or as authorization for a test, correction, or practice. “Non-load-bearing” therefore describes the hypothesis's logical job in Book I, not its value to the project.

Traditional Chinese medicine should be signposted by provenance rather than hidden or treated as an exact translation. The canonical early wording is:

> The hard/soft distinction is motivated by traditional Chinese medicine's hard/soft and Yin/Yang concepts. Book I develops a flexible mechanical translation of that motivation; it does not claim that the traditional and mechanical categories are definitionally identical.

Later books may inherit definitions and findings actually established earlier. They may not retroactively upgrade an earlier signpost into a premise that Book I secretly proved.

## Program-specific handoff wording

The following formulations are approved patterns, not mandatory final prose. They state the later program generously while keeping its Book I role explicit.

### Preferred configuration and posterior necessity

**Book I signpost**

> Posterior structures form a substantial anatomical and mechanical system, and anterior–posterior distribution is one useful lens within three-dimensional load sharing. The broader project asks a stronger question: whether human anatomy has a preferred body-wide routing organization and whether mechanically meaningful posterior participation is necessary, though not sufficient, within it. Book II defines and tests that proposition; the argument of this book does not depend on its answer.

**Obligations transferred:** Define *preferred*, the comparison class, the efficiency relation, the applicable task and scale, posterior participation, necessity and sufficiency, the signed-mode relation, counterexamples, and null predictions.

### Generalized modes and all-Yang organization

**Book I signpost**

> A later reduced model describes body-wide organization through axial, opening/rotational, and expansive/lateral modes. Its current preferred-sign hypothesis combines downward axial resolution, external or opening organization, and outward expansion. These are generalized modes rather than Cartesian force components, and Book II must determine whether their signs describe configuration, movement, conjugate load, routing, phase, bias, or return tendency.

**Obligations transferred:** Define regional coordinates and conjugate loads; distinguish opening from expansion even where they couple; specify local frames, sidedness, transformations, and composition; and show that the model predicts more than ordinary three-dimensional motion or task-dependent variability.

### Force lines and embodied connection

**Book I signpost**

> In this project, a force line may mean an external line of action, an inferred internal connection carried through several mechanical media, or an embodied experience of continuity. Only the first has a conventional mechanical meaning without further project-specific definition. The other senses preserve observations and hypotheses whose carrier, scale, sidedness, quantity, and measurement remain to be developed.

**Obligations transferred:** Retain the observation's provenance, including direct embodied reports; distinguish felt connection from inferred route and measured load; compare cable, field, pressure, contact, active-control, and multicarrier accounts; and specify what evidence would disfavor each.

### TCM, Six Divisions, and hard/soft correspondence

**Early Book I provenance statement**

> The hard/soft distinction is motivated by traditional Chinese medicine's hard/soft and Yin/Yang concepts. Book I uses a flexible mechanical translation—shape-preserving and structurally resolving in relation to form-receiving and shape-conforming—without claiming exact identity between the vocabularies.

**Closing Book I signpost**

> Book III asks whether the three signed mechanical modes can be related meaningfully to Yin/Yang, the Six Divisions, paired channels, gross limb roles, bandhas, and embodied point relationships. It treats traditional observation, historical continuity, authorial interpretation, anatomical correspondence, and mechanical validation as related but independent questions.

**Obligations transferred:** Preserve the exact Shaoyin–Taiyang axial, Taiyin–Yangming opening/rotational, and Jueyin–Shaoyang expansive map; keep local channels, gross limb roles, bandha maps, cross-tradition routes, and embodied anchors at their proper scales; and support historical and mechanical claims independently.

### Breath, pressure, bandhas, and whole-system geometry

**Book I signpost**

> Breathing already belongs to the ordinary mechanics of a living pressure-bearing body. A later program asks a more specific question: whether respiratory phase and pressure drive, organize, modulate, or merely accompany changes in whole-body routing. Hyoid, bandha, tensegrity, cube, torus, paired-loop, and other geometries remain candidate models for comparing those possibilities, not premises of the present foundation.

**Obligations transferred:** Separate ordinary respiratory fact from whole-system hypothesis; define phase, route, pressure, force, tissue motion, attention, and outcome independently; compare candidate geometries against simpler models; and specify measurements, alternatives, and experiments that can discriminate among driver, organizer, modulator, and companion roles.

### Observation and testing

**Book I signpost**

> The questions and observations developed here organize inquiry; they are not yet a diagnostic or corrective instrument. Later books must test the reliability, validity, specificity, alternatives, and practical consequences of any observation before it becomes a score, threshold, personalized interpretation, or guide to action.

**Obligations transferred:** Match validation to intended use. A research measure, teaching observation, clinical assessment, self-observation, and treatment-selection tool require different comparators, error costs, safety review, and evidence.

### Practice

**Book I signpost**

> The project preserves movement, breathing, bandha, cueing, teaching, and self-observation practices as a later applied program. Their originating observations may inform the research questions now; their publication as instruction must wait for the relevant definitions, outcomes, contraindications, adverse-event rules, accessibility work, and professional boundaries.

**Obligations transferred:** Keep exploratory practice available without converting it into universal advice. Book V must identify which upstream claims actually survived testing and tailor each practice artifact to audience, purpose, risk, and revision conditions.

## Book I/II inheritance contract

Book II is sequential. It should assume Book I was read and begin its own argument rather than reconstructing the entire foundation.

### What Book II may assume without re-teaching

| Inherited foundation | Exact inheritance |
|---|---|
| Living mechanics | The body receives, distributes, transforms, transmits, and dissipates mechanical demand while managing pressure and deformation over time. |
| Working quantities | Force, moment, pressure, stress, strain, deformation, stiffness, compliance, dose, capacity, recovery, and mechanical exposure are distinct quantities or relations. |
| Configuration and path | Geometry, contact, active state, pressure, and boundary conditions influence how demand is partitioned among multiple carriers. A load path is a bounded model, not necessarily one cable. |
| Description control | A spatial frame, generalized mode, material-symmetry frame, and principal-stress frame are different descriptions and cannot validate one another merely by having three directions. |
| Hard/soft roles | Hard is relatively shape-preserving and structurally resolving; soft is relatively form-receiving and shape-conforming. Roles vary by direction, scale, phase, task, state, and time. The vocabulary is motivated by TCM but not definitionally identical to it. |
| Support architecture | Skeletal, joint, active, tensile, compliant, pressure-bearing, and distributed systems share support. Book I establishes their ordinary anatomy and cooperation, not a preferred partition among them. |
| Posterior foundation | Posterior structures form a substantial anatomical and task-relevant system, and anterior–posterior distribution can be a useful conditional lens. Neither proposition establishes preferred organization. |
| Adverse-organization language | Concentration, interruption, substitution, constraint-maintained deformation, instability, discoordination, compensation, rerouting, and scale transition are available descriptive families. |
| Medical and epistemic boundary | Mechanical exposure, tissue response, symptoms, diagnosis, and intervention are not interchangeable. Whole-person alternatives and modifiers remain active, and every causal arrow retains its own evidence burden. |

Book II may recap these inheritances compactly in its introduction and Chapter 1.1, then use cross-references when a technical dependency matters. It should redefine an inherited term only when Book II gives it a more specialized meaning.

### What Book II must introduce as new

| New Book II proposition or assumption | Required posture at introduction |
|---|---|
| A preferred body-wide organization exists | Direct authorial hypothesis, not an implication of Book I |
| Preferred corresponds to a real whole-system efficiency relation | Provisional model requiring a domain, comparison class, terms, tradeoffs, and observables |
| Bilateral reference symmetry | Deliberate first-order modeling assumption with known departures |
| Skeletal-primary routing | Deliberate first-order routing assumption, not Book I's conclusion that other carriers are secondary or unimportant |
| Optional regional orthotropy or other constitutive approximations | Model choices justified by region, tissue, scale, and data; never a derivation of the modes |
| Axial, opening/rotational, and expansive/lateral generalized modes | Defined reduced variables with conjugate loads, coupling, signs, and transformations |
| The all-Yang combination is preferred | Hypothesis requiring clarity about whether signs qualify configuration, motion, load, routing, phase, bias, or attractor behavior |
| Posterior participation is necessary but insufficient | Current authorial expectation requiring derivation, scope, measurements, counterexamples, and disfavoring results |
| Preferred configurations can persist or undergo routing-regime transitions | Model requiring operational state variables and discrimination from ordinary movement or compensation |

### What Book II may not claim to have inherited

Book I does not establish:

- that one preferred global organization exists;
- that one posture, route, or sign combination is universally efficient or healthy;
- that skeletal-primary routing is already proven at whole-body scale;
- that the generalized modes are established anatomical coordinates;
- that `(+,+,+)` is preferred, or what its signs qualify;
- that posterior participation is necessary;
- that Yin/Yang or the Six Divisions validate the mechanical model;
- that breath is required to generate or maintain the configuration; or
- that any observation is a validated test or any practice a correction.

### Canonical Book I closing handoff

> Book I has established a working language of mechanical demand, configuration-dependent routing, changing hard and soft roles, posterior support, adverse exposure, tissue response, compensation, and whole-person interpretation. Book II inherits that foundation without inheriting a preferred route. It asks the stronger question directly: whether human anatomy has a preferred body-wide routing organization, whether three signed generalized modes can describe it, and whether posterior participation is necessary but insufficient within it.

### Canonical Book II opening seam

> A load path can be described without declaring it optimal. Book I developed that neutral account and showed why posterior anatomy deserves serious mechanical attention. Book II now introduces, rather than inherits, the stronger hypothesis that some whole-body routing organizations are preferred under stated conditions. The chapters that follow must define that preference, derive its proposed relation to the three signed modes and posterior participation, and expose the hypothesis to alternatives and failure.

## Acceptance checks

The seam is functioning when:

- Book I remains coherent if every later-book hypothesis is rejected;
- the TCM motivation of hard/soft is visible when the terms first become important;
- later hypotheses are stated in their strongest coherent provisional form rather than reduced to vague “future work”;
- every signpost names its destination and the work still required there;
- posterior anatomy, hard/soft roles, Yang signs, and preferred organization remain related but nonidentical;
- spatial directions, generalized modes, material axes, and principal-stress directions remain distinct;
- ordinary breath and pressure mechanics do not silently establish a breath-driven whole-system model;
- observation does not become testing, and testing does not become practice, without the intervening validation and safety work;
- Book II can begin with preference instead of repeating Book I; and
- no later book retroactively strengthens the evidence state of an earlier Book I statement.
