## Verdict

Book I now has a coherent development system rather than a pilot surrounded by an outline. All 30 units have one drafting-depth brief, a unique chapter job, reader entry and exit states, an argument sequence, claims and model obligations, examples, figures, evidence and review needs, boundaries, salvage instructions, and acceptance criteria. The six Parts form a cumulative argument:

1. establish the mechanical language;
2. build the architecture of support and the bounded posterior proposition;
3. define adverse load organization and compensation;
4. connect mechanical exposure to medical meaning without creating a parallel diagnostic system;
5. test the framework in four unlike worked narratives; and
6. convert the result into reasoning, education, research, and a non-load-bearing handoff to the wider series.

The workspace is ready to move from briefing into the [research and controlled-salvage execution sequence](research-and-salvage-execution-sequence.md). It is **not** evidence-complete or publication-ready. The central claim, model, example, and figure registries remain intentionally concentrated on the Chapters 2, 13, and 24 vertical slice; most other briefs identify records “to create” rather than pretending that editorial coherence has already earned a stable scientific or clinical record.

No missing chapter, unresolved book-boundary contradiction, broken local link, unknown referenced control ID, placeholder chapter, or prohibited TCM-provenance wording blocks the next phase. The remaining work consists of explicit acceptance, control promotion, source retrieval, formalization, case selection, specialist review, controlled passage salvage, and staged drafting.

## Mechanical and authorial controls now preserved

The development system consistently preserves the following directions:

- **Hard and soft:** relatively shape-preserving or structurally resolving roles in relation to relatively form-receiving or shape-conforming roles. Roles can change with direction, scale, phase, task, state, and time.
- **Traditional provenance:** the distinction is **motivated by** traditional Chinese medicine's hard/soft and Yin/Yang concepts. Book I does not claim definitional identity, one-to-one tissue classification, historical equivalence, or scientific validation by correspondence.
- **Load-routing intuition:** “Load wants to travel through the hard body so that the soft body can remain soft” is retained as compact authorial shorthand and unpacked into task-specific mechanical relations rather than erased or published as a universal law.
- **Posterior proposition:** posterior anatomy exists, is substantial, and has important task-specific roles. Anterior–posterior distribution is a useful conditional lens. The stronger expectation that posterior participation is necessary but insufficient within preferred organization remains a Book II hypothesis.
- **Maintained constraint:** an extrinsic account begins with the relation that sustains deformation or lost excursion, not with “loss of space” as a complete cause. Intrinsic, extrinsic, and mixed persistence remain distinct.
- **Chapter 13 comparison:** flexor tendon–subsynovial connective tissue relative glide is the primary non-lumen calibration; median-nerve excursion remains reserve material.
- **Chapter 24 framing:** objective arterial thoracic-outlet material is the current evidence anchor. Venous and neural distinctions are used only when their structures, quantities, chronology, or evidence limits improve the causal test; subtype taxonomy is not the chapter's reader-facing architecture.
- **Medical boundary:** exposure, response, finding, symptom, diagnosis, causal priority, and treatment remain different propositions. A low- or no-mechanical-priority result is legitimate.
- **Later-book boundary:** preferred configuration, signed generalized modes, posterior necessity, Six-Division and channel correspondence, breath geometry, bandhas, torus and related whole-system models, testing, and practice are preserved at explicit seams but do no hidden work for Book I.

## Part-by-Part coverage

### Part I — Mechanical foundations of living structure

**Units:** Chapters 1–4

**Coverage present:** material embodiment without mechanical reductionism; exact quantity and system-boundary language; hard/soft roles and their early TCM-motivation signpost; configuration, local and global frames, multicarrier load paths, and the separation of spatial frames, generalized modes, material directions, and principal-stress directions.

**Primary sources and controls:** Chapter 2 definitions pack; `BGB-C-0001`–`0007` and `0009`; `BGB-M-0001`–`0005`; recurring object; precise `new`-archive source locations in each brief.

**Remaining work:** accept notation and force-line senses; formal and dimensional review; establish reusable sources for living mechanics and mechanics–biology reciprocity; test hard/soft observability against ordinary mechanics; resolve the exact foundation figures; recover passages only after those controls stabilize.

### Part II — The architecture of support

**Units:** Chapters 5–9

**Coverage present:** skeletal and joint contributions; active, tensile, compliant, pressure-bearing, sliding, and bounded tension-network systems; narrow, broad, and whole-system meanings of posterior chain; task-specific posterior function; whole-body, lifespan, disability, pregnancy, development, aging, and reserve contexts; the three-level posterior proposition.

**Primary sources and controls:** recurring object; hard/soft model; Chapter 4 frame/path distinctions; Chapter 7–9 claim and source obligations; later-book seam table.

**Remaining work:** anatomy atlas and boundary review; task-biomechanics source tables; representative rather than exhaustive posterior examples; variability and shared-support counterexamples; lifespan and accessibility review; exact proposition-ladder figure; no promotion from muscle size or anatomical continuity to preferred configuration.

### Part III — Load management, failure, and compensation

**Units:** Chapters 10–16

**Coverage present:** dynamic mechanical viability through demand, dose, variability, capacity, recovery, and environment; continuity, handoff, interruption, substitution, and leakage as controlled language; redistribution and localization; maintained constraint; instability and discoordination; compensation and delayed consequence; scale-transition branches with explicit off-ramps.

**Primary sources and controls:** adverse-organization matrix; causal grammar; Chapters 13 pilot ledger and models; whole-person matrix; chapter-specific archive locators.

**Remaining work:** decide whether *mechanical homeostasis* survives as the principal reader term; build the adverse-organization evidence atlas; define candidate observables and counterexamples for every family; separate instability from timing and control; review compensation chronology; keep neural, pressure/flow, tissue, and energetic branches independently gated.

### Part IV — From mechanical organization to medical meaning

**Units:** Chapters 17–22

**Coverage present:** established mechanical reasoning in medicine; the incremental longitudinal-routing proposal and a no-added-value comparator; tissue-specific exposure–response branching; comparison of conduits, nerves, sliding interfaces, airways, and pressure systems without evidence pooling; flexible causal scope and whole-person alternatives; additive clinical workflow with provenance and stopping rules.

**Primary sources and controls:** causal grammar; whole-person matrix; Chapters 13 and 24 focused packs; Chapter 17–22 evidence obligations; Chapter 22's minimum worked-narrative record.

**Remaining work:** representative cross-specialty common-ground evidence; comparison with existing biomechanics, rehabilitation, physical therapy, ergonomics, and exposure science; tissue-specific mechanobiology; receiver-variable source tables; condition-specific clinical review; decide whether the osteoarthritis/rheumatoid-arthritis boundary exercise survives evidence review; validate no workflow instrument or clinical utility by implication.

## Source and salvage coverage

### What is covered now

- Every chapter brief identifies precise archive locations, current source-coverage records, conceptual kernels to preserve, defective moves to avoid, and later-book destinations.
- The source reconciliation accounts for the six `new` JSON outlines and prevents editorial planning keys, migration notes, prompts, protocols, and generated extrapolations from entering prose.

- The pilot source-salvage map provides controlled archive topology, recovery dispositions, and locator-level targets for Chapters 2, 13, and 24.
- The execution sequence specifies proposition-level source-table fields, retrieval order, literature-refresh records, passage dispositions, and stopping rules.

### What remains incomplete

- No full-book locator-level passage-salvage ledger exists yet; chapter briefs are the recovery contracts from which those sheets should be made.
- The central reference apparatus remains unusable as claim support until exact works are retrieved, assessed, and linked to exact propositions.
- Generated prose and old combined manuscripts remain conceptual and wording leads only; none is a scientific source.
- Most non-pilot chapters lack proposition-level evidence tables and specialist dispositions.
- Figure provenance, rights, and accessibility remain open beyond early specifications.
- Literature freshness must be checked at retrieval and again before publication; the briefs themselves do not complete that work.

The correct next source action is therefore passage- and proposition-level recovery in the execution waves, not promotion of an old outline, generated run, or combined manuscript.
