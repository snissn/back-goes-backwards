The finished brief should govern chapter development, archive salvage, and eventual drafting without becoming an evidence pack or reader-facing prose.

## What a brief must accomplish

A chapter brief turns the authoritative detailed outline into a controlled argument. It should be specific enough that a later drafter can tell what the chapter does, what it does not do, why each section follows, which claims and models bear weight, what source work remains, and what the reader must carry into the next chapter.

The brief does **not**:

- decide that a scientific or medical proposition is true by editorial declaration;
- contain a source-by-source literature assessment better suited to an evidence pack;
- promote legacy or generated prose merely because it is already written;
- expand Book I into the preferred-configuration, TCM-coordinate, breath-geometry, or practice arguments assigned to later books; or
- hide unresolved choices behind polished prose.

Use the [Book I contract](../book-contract.md), the [detailed Book I outline](../../series-synthesis/books/01-medical-foundations.md), and the [series boundary](../../series-synthesis/series-outline.md) as controlling sources. Use the three existing pilot briefs to calibrate depth and tone.

## Source-salvage instructions

1. Read this brief and its control records before opening legacy prose.
2. Recover **concepts, questions, anatomy, metaphors, and direct authorial formulations** generously.
3. Classify candidate material as `reuse with editing`, `conceptual source`, `provenance lead`, `incompatible wording`, or `pipeline contamination`.
4. Preserve the raw formulation and its provenance when normalization changes its apparent strength or ontology.
5. Rebuild equations, causal chains, and citations from valid sources; do not repair them by copy-editing generated text.
6. Route later-book material to its existing series unit or quarantine record rather than deleting it.

## Acceptance criteria

The brief is suitable for controlled salvage and eventual drafting when:

- the chapter's unique job, entry state, exit state, and adjacent handoffs are unambiguous;
- the central question and working answer match the chapter's evidence posture and medical exposure;
- every subsection advances one cumulative argument rather than collecting related material;
- load-bearing claims, models, examples, and figures have control records or clearly named registry actions;
- external demand, internal mechanics, tissue exposure, biological response, findings, symptoms, and diagnosis remain distinct where applicable;
- alternatives, noncorrespondence, and null possibilities appear inside the argument;
- the source and review obligations are specific enough to create an evidence pack without repeating the brief;
- author-direct, traditional, generated, editorial, and external-evidence provenance cannot be silently fused;
- later-book material is signposted or routed without becoming necessary to Book I's argument;
- archive salvage can proceed additively without promoting an old manuscript wholesale; and
- any authorial, formal, anatomical, medical, ethical, safety, audience, or figure-rights blocker is named explicitly.
