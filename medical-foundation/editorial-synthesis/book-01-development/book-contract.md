# *The Back Goes Backwards*, Book I: *Medical Foundations*

## Book contract

**Status:** working governing contract

**Series role:** independent foundation for Books II–V

## 1. Purpose

Book I establishes a coherent mechanical dimension of medical reasoning.

Its central task is to show how a living body receives, distributes, transforms, transmits, and dissipates mechanical demand; how configuration and available paths translate that demand into local force, moment, pressure, stress, strain, and excursion; and how the resulting exposure can interact with tissue state, capacity, recovery, behavior, development, environment, and the rest of the person over time.

The book does not offer mechanics as the explanation for every health problem. It argues that clinical and biological accounts can be more complete when load history, load distribution, and body-wide load routing are examined alongside biological, psychological, social, developmental, and environmental factors.

Book I must be worthwhile if every higher-specificity hypothesis in the rest of the series is later revised or rejected.

## 2. Reader and audience

### Primary audience

The center of gravity is a professionally trained, evidence-literate reader who can engage with anatomy, medicine, rehabilitation, movement science, biomechanics, or applied engineering. This includes physicians, physical therapists, rehabilitation specialists, biomechanics and movement researchers, anatomists, and medically literate engineers or physicists.

The book should respect this reader's knowledge without assuming specialist command of every neighboring field. Mechanical terms receive precise introductions; medical examples retain diagnostic uncertainty; anatomical claims do not assume an engineering background; and engineering analogies do not replace biological evidence.

### Secondary audience

The book should remain intelligible to serious movement professionals, coaches, martial artists, yoga teachers, somatic educators, and motivated general readers. Accessibility comes through a cumulative argument, concrete examples, clear diagrams, and plain language—not through removing the distinctions that make the model responsible.

The volume is not an entry-level anatomy text, a professional clinical manual, or a self-help guide.

## 3. Reader promise

By the end of Book I, the reader should be able to:

1. describe bodily demand without treating “load” as one undifferentiated quantity;
2. follow an explicit chain from task and environment through configuration, paths, local exposure, tissue response, compensation, and possible clinical meaning;
3. understand hard and soft as complementary, changing mechanical roles rather than permanent tissue classes;
4. recognize the skeleton, joints, active tissues, tensile structures, compliant structures, sliding interfaces, and pressure-bearing systems as a coordinated architecture of support;
5. understand the posterior chain as a substantial anatomical and functional system without being asked to accept universal posterior priority;
6. distinguish recurring families of load mismanagement from diagnoses or inevitable damage;
7. ask better questions about load history, capacity, recovery, compensation, local exposure, and alternatives within whole-person clinical reasoning; and
8. distinguish the foundation established here from the stronger preferred-configuration program introduced for the later series.

## 4. Governing thesis

> The human body is a living biomechanical system. Its tissues and structures continuously receive, distribute, transform, transmit, and dissipate forces and moments while managing pressure and deformation over time. Clinical reasoning can improve when load history, load distribution, and body-wide load routing are considered alongside biological, psychological, social, developmental, and environmental factors.

The project's compact intuition is:

> Load wants to travel through the hard body so that the soft body can remain soft.

This sentence is an organizing proposal about a division of mechanical labor. It does not classify bone as always hard or other tissues as always soft. It does not mean that compliant structures should bear no load. It proposes that gross structural demand is generally best resolved through capable, mechanically continuous, relatively shape-preserving and force-transmitting routes, leaving form-receiving structures sufficiently free to yield, conform, glide, contain, expand, regulate, and recover within their functional ranges.

The appropriate scale, direction, task, time horizon, and measure of “sufficiently free” must be stated whenever the proposal becomes a technical or medical claim.

## 5. The argument Book I must earn

The manuscript develops the following connected propositions rather than presenting them as axioms:

1. **Embodied life has a mechanical dimension.** Material structures cannot avoid forces, moments, pressures, contact, deformation, and motion, although mechanics is not always the dominant explanation for a clinical problem.
2. **External demand becomes internal exposure through organization.** System boundary, configuration, geometry, contact, active control, material state, and available paths determine how an external task becomes local mechanical conditions.
3. **Living structure divides mechanical labor.** Shape-preserving, active, tensile, form-receiving, sliding, and pressure-bearing roles cooperate and change with scale, direction, state, and task.
4. **The posterior chain is a major support and transfer system.** Its anatomy and ordinary participation in stance, locomotion, lifting, carrying, propulsion, deceleration, and balance can be described without deriving one universal preferred route.
5. **Poorly resolved demand has recognizable mechanical forms.** Geometric redistribution, local concentration, instability, discoordination or handoff failure, constraint-maintained deformation or lost excursion, and compensatory rerouting provide a useful pattern language.
6. **Mechanical exposure acquires medical meaning through biology and context.** Magnitude, direction, rate, repetition, variability, duration, tissue state, reserve, recovery, adaptation, sensitivity, and the whole person's circumstances mediate the path from exposure to response, findings, symptoms, or outcomes.
7. **A routing lens can improve inquiry before it licenses intervention.** The immediate contribution is a clearer set of questions, models, measurements, comparisons, and research programs—not a universal diagnosis or correction system.

The manuscript should make every necessary intermediate step visible whenever it moves from ordinary mechanics toward a biological or clinical consequence.

## 6. Recurring explanatory structure

The book's principal reasoning chain is:

> task and environment → body state and configuration → available paths and load distribution → local force, stress, strain, pressure, contact, or excursion → tissue response over time → function, findings, and possible symptoms

Capacity, recovery, development, biology, behavior, expectations, and social conditions may modify every link. Feedback also runs in the opposite direction: pain, inflammation, injury, fear, fatigue, disease, disability, and prior compensation can change configuration, control, exposure, and future capacity.

No worked narrative may jump directly from visible posture or alignment to a diagnosis or health outcome. A useful narrative identifies its system boundary, quantities, time course, proposed carriers, intervening biological links, competing explanations, and observations that would favor or disfavor it.

## 7. Controlled concepts

### Load and routing

“Load” is a conversational umbrella. Technical passages should distinguish external force, moment, pressure, internal resultant, stress, strain, deformation, contact, excursion, work, energy, and exposure dose as needed.

A load path is a model of how a defined demand is carried and ultimately resolved through contact, support, inertia, active action, pressure, or internal opposition. It is not presumed to be a literal pipe, cable, or single resultant hidden inside the body.

Routing is the distribution of demand among available structures and paths. Continuity, handoff, interruption, bypass, overload, substitution, concentration, and rerouting are hypotheses about that distribution and must identify what quantity, carrier, scale, and task are meant.

“Force line” may name an external line of action, an inferred internal multicarrier relation, or an embodied experience of continuity. The latter meanings may be preserved as project-native descriptions, but a formal or reader-facing claim must state the proposed carrier and measured quantity or remain explicitly relational.

### Hard and soft roles

A hard role is relatively shape-preserving, force-transmitting, constraining, or structurally resolving at the scale under discussion. Bone is the clearest example, but active muscle, tendon, ligament, fascia, cartilage, a pressurized cavity, or a coordinated assembly can perform a hard role in a particular direction and task.

A soft role is relatively form-receiving and shape-conforming. It yields rather than imposing geometry and thereby remains available for functions such as glide, containment, expansion, buffering, flow, pressure response, and local shape change. Softness is not weakness, passivity, absence of load, or a synonym for anterior anatomy.

One tissue can perform hard and soft roles simultaneously in different directions or exchange roles as activation, pressure, geometry, timescale, or task changes. The terms retain their TCM-derived project history, but Book I uses them as an explicit mechanical polarity. It does not require the reader to accept a TCM channel model.

### Mechanical homeostasis and capacity

Mechanical homeostasis names the proposed maintenance of relevant mechanical variables within viable dynamic ranges, not one fixed posture or a universal set point. Capacity is a current, context-dependent ability to tolerate and recover from a defined exposure, not a permanent scalar property of a tissue or person.

Living tissues may respond, accommodate, adapt, remodel, recover, sensitize, deteriorate, or fail depending on exposure and state. The manuscript should not use “adaptation” as an automatic or uniformly beneficial consequence of load.

### Adverse mechanical organization

Load mismanagement means that demand is resolved through tissues, interfaces, or deformation modes poorly matched to its magnitude, direction, rate, duration, repetition, variability, recovery context, or current capacity.

Book I develops six overlapping families:

1. global or regional geometric redistribution;
2. local stress or strain concentration;
3. segmental or task-specific instability;
4. timing, coordination, or handoff failure;
5. constraint-maintained deformation or loss of excursion; and
6. compensatory rerouting.

These are organizing patterns, not diagnoses. They may coexist, be protective, remain asymptomatic, reverse, or prove incidental in a particular case.

Constraint-maintained deformation receives special attention. When a compliant conduit, nerve, sliding interface, cavity, or other structure remains narrowed, displaced, tethered, or unable to move freely, the narrative should investigate the maintained mechanical relation that holds it there—not only the secondary loss of space. External constraint, restoring tendency, wall stress and strain, contact geometry, internal pressure, flow, excursion, conduction, and symptoms remain separate links.

### Posterior anatomy

The posterior chain is an anatomical and functional example before it is a directional doctrine. Book I may establish that large and mechanically consequential posterior structures participate in ordinary support, transfer, propulsion, braking, elastic behavior, balance, and movement. It must also show their cooperation with anterior, lateral, deep, active, tensile, compliant, and pressure-mediated systems.

Book I does not need to prove that posterior loading is always superior, visually apparent, necessary in every task, or sufficient for preferred organization. The stronger expectation that posterior participation may be necessary but insufficient within the eventual domain of a preferred whole-system organization belongs to Book II's research program.

## 8. Scope and architecture

The current six-Part architecture is the governing structural proposal. Chapter placement remains revisable through explicit structure review.

### Part I — Mechanical foundations of living structure

Establish embodied mechanics, a disciplined language of load, hard and soft mechanical roles, three-dimensional configuration, spatial frames, and load paths. The Part should teach the reader how to define a system and exposure before making a medical inference.

### Part II — The architecture of support

Show how skeleton, joints, muscle, tendon, ligament, cartilage, fascia, sliding interfaces, fluids, cavities, pressure, and baseline tension cooperate. Use posterior anatomy and ordinary tasks as the first extended whole-body example, including lifespan, disability, training, fatigue, and alternative strategies.

### Part III — Load management, failure, and compensation

Develop the book's main explanatory contribution: mechanical homeostasis and capacity; continuity and handoff; geometric concentration; constraint-maintained deformation; instability and discoordination; compensation and delayed consequence; and transitions among local, regional, and whole-body scales.

### Part IV — From mechanical organization to medical meaning

Begin from mechanical reasoning already used within medicine and propose longitudinal load routing as an additional organizing lens. Connect exposure to tissue response, compare compliant systems carefully, preserve causal alternatives, and show how the lens could enter clinical reasoning without becoming a diagnosis or treatment system.

### Part V — Worked mechanical narratives

Pressure-test the framework through four contrasting narratives: geometry and localized strain; a compliant structure maintained under constraint, led by the thoracic-outlet corridor; instability under repeated demand; and silent compensation in a mixed chronic presentation. Final example selection may change after the evidence review, but the set must span different failure families and include alternatives, null cases, and reverse causality.

### Part VI — Reasoning, prevention, development, and the wider hypothesis

Gather the framework into bounded questions for clinical reasoning, education, environmental design, research, measurement, and staged professional development. Conclude by distinguishing the durable Book I foundation from the preferred-configuration, coordinate, breath, and practice programs of Books II–V.

The recurring neutral task—moving an object from one support to another and carrying it several steps—should provide continuity through Parts I and II without prescribing one ideal technique. Other examples should be chosen for explanatory contrast rather than rhetorical accumulation.

Internal inventories remain development tools, not reader-facing chapters. The mechanism-by-region matrix, analogy inventory, posterior and lifespan inventory, and source-proposition ledger preserve the wider research program while the manuscript selects only material that serves its argument.

## 9. What belongs in Book I

Book I includes:

- necessary mechanics: system boundaries, free-body reasoning, force, moment, pressure, stress, strain, deformation, stiffness, compliance, work, energy, dose, and time;
- anatomy and biomechanics of skeletal, joint, active, tensile, compliant, sliding, and pressure-bearing support;
- the hard/soft mechanical division of labor in its general form;
- posterior-chain anatomy, ordinary function, lifespan variation, capacity, and limits;
- load-path continuity, handoff, interruption, substitution, concentration, constraint, instability, discoordination, compensation, and scale transition;
- mechanobiological response and reciprocal interaction between mechanics and tissue state;
- carefully bounded comparison of conduits, nerves, sliding interfaces, and pressure systems;
- a small set of worked mechanical narratives with explicit alternatives;
- implications for questions, measurement, education, research, environmental design, and possible future clinical integration; and
- a direct but non-load-bearing introduction to the stronger series hypothesis.

Historical movement or traditional material may appear only where it is necessary to disclose the origin of a project term or orient the later series. It should not become a parallel traditional-medicine argument inside Book I.

## 10. What Book I does not need to establish

The Book I argument must not depend on:

- one correct posture, one universal load path, or one static ideal body shape;
- universal posterior preference or the proposition that anterior loading is inherently harmful;
- the all-Yang preferred-configuration hypothesis;
- the formal axial, opening/rotational, and expansive sign system;
- the Six-Division or meridian correspondence;
- qi, prana, or another energetic substance;
- bandha mechanics, hyoid-centered fourthness, tall-torus, cube, looping-breath, or final tensegrity models;
- breath as a necessary driver of configuration;
- a global orthotropic body model;
- a complete disease taxonomy or disease-specific causal system;
- diagnostic tests, individualized corrections, therapeutic protocols, home tests, drills, or exercise prescriptions;
- a total theory of health, illness, consciousness, development, or human optimization; or
- the claim that medicine, biomechanics, rehabilitation, or movement science currently ignores mechanics.

These exclusions are book boundaries, not rejections of the wider research program. Book I may name the later questions accurately, preserve their provenance internally, and make the seam to the next volume explicit.

## 11. Series boundary

Book I stands independently. Books II–V may assume it has been read.

- **Book II, *The Back Goes Backwards*,** asks whether human anatomy has a preferred whole-system organization expressed through axial, opening/rotational, and expansive modes, and whether posterior participation is necessary but insufficient within its domain.
- **Book III, *The Body's Coordinates*,** develops the proposed Yin/Yang, Six-Division, channel, gross-limb, bandha, and embodied-anchor mappings without treating tradition as proof of mechanics.
- **Book IV, *The Breath-Structured Body*,** asks whether breath and pressure drive, organize, modulate, or accompany whole-system dynamics and compares candidate geometric models.
- **Book V, *Embodiment and Practice*,** develops observation, teaching, research, and practical applications only through their appropriate evidence, clinical, ethical, audience, and safety gates.

Book I's final chapter should disclose this program plainly. It should not make later hypotheses into hidden premises, attempt to prove them in miniature, or become a catalogue of material deferred to later volumes.

## 12. Claim posture and provenance

Every load-bearing proposition should be identifiable internally as one or more of:

- foundational mechanics or anatomy;
- organizing synthesis;
- authorial mechanical or biological hypothesis;
- traditional or historical observation;
- medical, health, performance, educational, or practice application candidate; or
- editorial or research-program design.

Reader-facing prose need not display metadata codes, but it must preserve the distinction in ordinary language. A standard mechanical relationship, a biological finding, a project-native interpretation, an engineering analogy, and a medical hypothesis cannot borrow authority from one another merely by appearing in the same paragraph.

Project authority and scientific confidence remain separate. Direct authorial intent determines what hypothesis the project is trying to study. It does not establish the hypothesis scientifically. Conversely, weak legacy wording, uncertain evidence, or generated overstatement may require normalization, deferral, relocation, or quarantine without erasing the protected idea.

Generated outlines and prose are never evidence. Traditional sources require historical, textual, terminological, and translation review appropriate to the claim being made. Visual and engineering analogies must state what they illuminate and where the correspondence stops.

## 13. Medical and causal boundaries

Mechanical relevance is case-, scale-, tissue-, task-, and time-dependent. It may be important without being exclusive, upstream without being original, or modulating without accounting for diagnosis. The manuscript should state the exact role it proposes when that distinction matters, but it should not force every exploratory observation into a rigid universal causal taxonomy.

Book I must:

- preserve biological, psychological, social, developmental, and environmental determinants as active parts of the explanation;
- distinguish exposure, tissue response, finding, symptom, diagnosis, prognosis, and outcome;
- distinguish intrinsic pathology from extrinsic or configuration-maintained deformation;
- include reverse causality, compensation, confounding, natural variation, measurement error, and plausible nonmechanical alternatives where relevant;
- treat asymptomatic structure and symptom modulation as evidence requiring interpretation, not automatic proof or disproof of a model;
- define population, task, outcome, timeframe, comparison, and causal strength for medical claims as the evidence phase demands; and
- integrate rather than compete reflexively with diagnosis, medication, surgery, rehabilitation, referral, and established care.

Informal internal health language should be read charitably at the weakest reasonable level consistent with its context. Before publication it must be translated into an explicit, supportable claim. A statement about comfort, ease, tolerance, or function should not be inflated into universal disease causality; a broad intuition should not remain vague merely to evade evaluation.

Named conditions may appear as worked contexts, boundary exercises, or research candidates. Their inclusion does not establish diagnosis, origin, prevention, prognosis, or treatment. Disease-specific causal or clinical statements carry condition-specific evidence and medical-coauthor review requirements.

Book I does not authorize a reader to diagnose a load-path problem, infer a hidden cause from posture, discontinue care, or perform a corrective protocol. Clinical observations in the book are questions and comparisons unless a separately reviewed claim establishes more.

## 14. Evidence and review standard

Evidence review proceeds claim by claim. It should ask what exact proposition is supported, in which population, tissue, task, scale, direction, dose, timeframe, and measurement context. Research should be capable of supporting, narrowing, revising, relocating, or disfavoring a formulation.

At minimum:

- foundational mechanical equations and definitions require dimensional consistency, stated system boundaries, and suitable authoritative sources;
- anatomical and biomechanical claims require sources appropriate to the structure, function, scale, and population described;
- mechanobiological bridges require evidence for each intermediate link rather than citation adjacency;
- organizing syntheses and authorial hypotheses require definitions, alternatives, counterexamples, observables, and possible disfavoring results;
- named-condition claims require condition-specific evidence and medical-coauthor review; and
- public, clinical, or individualized applications require a separate audience, scope, contraindication, adverse-event, referral, and safety review and ordinarily belong in Book V rather than Book I.

The metadata schema's review gates govern development:

- `M0–M1` material may remain exploratory when uncertainty and scope are visible, although factual statements still require support.
- `M2` assertive publication requires at least `DEF`, `EVID`, `LINK`, and `ALT`.
- `M3` material additionally requires `CLIN`; an exploratory item may survive only as a clearly bounded, non-advisory hypothesis or candidate example.
- `M4` material requires `SAFE`, `CLIN`, and `AUD` and is outside Book I's ordinary reader-facing scope.

`FORM`, `MEAS`, `PROV`, `HIST`, `ETH`, and `VIS` apply wherever the model, claim, source, audience, or figure requires them. Passing a gate means completing the work, not merely attaching its label.

Evidence selection should favor authoritative texts for settled definitions, primary studies for exact empirical propositions, appropriate systematic reviews for the state and heterogeneity of a field, and guidelines for current clinical practice boundaries. A review's broad null or mixed result neither automatically refutes a narrower project hypothesis nor supports evasion by redefining it after the fact. The exact tested construct and the project's exact construct must be compared explicitly.

## 15. Models, equations, analogies, and figures

Models should clarify a defined problem rather than provide scientific atmosphere.

- Choose the system boundary before drawing forces or inferring paths.
- Use free-body diagrams where external demand and support are central.
- Keep force, moment, pressure, stress, strain, energy, movement, and generalized variables dimensionally and conceptually distinct.
- State assumptions, scale, boundary conditions, mapped elements, deliberate mismatches, and failure conditions.
- Do not treat a rotation matrix, visual resemblance, tensegrity diagram, or anatomical continuity as proof of decompression, clinical benefit, or body-wide force transmission.
- Use a locally orthotropic constitutive model only where the tissue, region, direction-dependent behavior, and available measurements justify it. Orthotropy does not derive the later three-mode hypothesis.
- Label an analogy as an analogy and explain what would have to be measured to promote it into a model.

The figure program should privilege explanatory diagrams that let the reader inspect a claim: free-body diagrams, bounded anatomical routes, changing moment arms, graded role handoffs, compliant tubes under maintained constraint, exposure chains, and explicit comparison among alternatives.

## 16. Prose and tone

The manuscript should be clear, cumulative, technically serious, and intellectually generous. Its voice may retain the directness and physical intuition of an applied engineering conversation while translating informal language into terms a medical or scientific reader can inspect.

The book should:

- lead with ordinary mechanical and anatomical common ground before introducing the project's synthesis;
- define important terms once, use them consistently, and remind the reader when scale or meaning changes;
- prefer concrete verbs and complete causal chains to abstract declarations;
- distinguish confidence through sentence construction and context rather than repetitive defensive disclaimers;
- acknowledge uncertainty without draining every proposition of content;
- treat medicine, engineering, movement disciplines, and traditional systems accurately rather than using any as a rhetorical foil;
- introduce objections where they sharpen the model and state what evidence would resolve them;
- keep examples cumulative and transitions intentional so the book reads as an argument rather than a catalogue; and
- remove prompts, acceptance criteria, migration notes, metadata, and editorial scaffolding from reader-facing prose.

Avoid moralizing anterior and posterior, treating compliant as inferior, calling all load stress or all deformation damage, implying that visible symmetry equals mechanical efficiency, or using “topological,” “invariant,” “ground state,” “tensegrity,” or similar formal terms beyond the precision actually established.

Traditional origins should be credited without forcing either mystical endorsement or reductive dismissal. Book I may explain that hard/soft is a mechanical translation of a TCM-derived polarity; the full traditional correspondence belongs later.

## 17. Chapter-development standard

Each accepted chapter needs a brief containing:

- stable unit ID and unique editorial job;
- reader-entry and reader-exit state;
- opening question, observation, or scene;
- argument sequence and transition from the previous chapter;
- indispensable claims and their posture;
- terms and models introduced or inherited;
- anatomical, mechanical, and medical examples;
- figures or free-body models;
- evidence, formalization, measurement, provenance, clinical, and safety obligations;
- strongest objection, competing explanation, or null case;
- excluded and later-book material;
- chapter synthesis and transition forward; and
- legacy sources to inspect for salvage.

The chapter brief governs prose. Existing prose may supply language or ideas but cannot silently change the brief, contract, or claim record.

Parts also require an explicit introduction, cumulative arc, outcome, summary, and conclusion. The book introduction and final conclusion should be finalized after the body argument is stable.

## 18. Development order

Book I should proceed through these gates:

1. accept this contract;
2. accept, revise, merge, split, move, or defer every structural unit;
3. build a vertical slice for `BGB-U-0002`, `BGB-U-0013`, and `BGB-U-0024`;
4. validate the claim, model, example, figure, and evidence-map formats against that slice;
5. build accepted briefs by Part, beginning with Parts I–II, then III, IV, V, and VI;
6. salvage useful legacy material against each accepted brief;
7. draft and jointly review the three vertical-slice chapters to establish voice and density;
8. draft sequentially by Part;
9. run evidence, citation, model, medical-coauthor, repetition, accessibility, figure, and series-seam reviews; and
10. perform copyediting, layout, and PDF quality assurance only after substantive acceptance.

Research and briefing may overlap where dependencies are clear. Drafting should not outrun unresolved structural or load-bearing claim decisions simply because source prose is available.

## 19. Publication success criteria

Book I succeeds when a careful reader can retain its mechanical reasoning without being required to accept the later series hypothesis.

Before publication:

- every reader-facing chapter has an accepted, nonduplicative job and a complete argument arc;
- load-bearing claims have stable records, appropriate sources, explicit intermediate links, alternatives, and wording matched to support;
- factual anatomy and mechanics have passed technical review;
- medically exposed claims and worked narratives have passed the required medical-coauthor and clinical review;
- hard/soft is understandable as a functional polarity and cannot reasonably be read as a fixed hard-tissue/soft-tissue taxonomy;
- posterior anatomy is compellingly developed without anterior/posterior moralization;
- worked narratives clarify the model without masquerading as diagnoses or completed case histories;
- applications do not cross into unreviewed tests, protocols, or individualized advice;
- the manuscript acknowledges the wider hypothesis directly while remaining logically independent of it;
- references are complete, traceable, and free of placeholders;
- figures are technically reviewed and their analogical limits are visible;
- repetition and continuity audits confirm that each Part advances the argument; and
- the introduction, conclusion, title treatment, and Book II handoff accurately describe the book actually written.

The desired reception is not universal agreement. Success is a medically and mechanically serious reader concluding that the framework is coherent, bounded, testable, and worth using or investigating; a movement professional recognizing familiar observations without having been offered false scientific certainty; and a general reader gaining a more responsible way to think about mechanical history and health.

## 20. Governing commitments and revisable parameters

The following commitments govern Book I:

- the foundational argument remains independent of preferred configuration, TCM correspondence, breath geometry, and practice;
- mechanics remains integrated with whole-person biological and contextual reasoning;
- hard and soft remain complementary, changing mechanical roles;
- posterior anatomy receives substantial treatment without a universal directional conclusion;
- stronger authorial hypotheses are disclosed and preserved rather than hidden or prematurely deleted;
- claim strength, publication layer, and review burden increase with medical exposure and artifact maturity;
- no generated artifact becomes canonical without structural, provenance, and evidence review; and
- Book I is a conceptual and research foundation, not a diagnostic or corrective system.

The exact chapter count, chapter titles, balance of technical and general exposition, final worked examples, figure set, degree of formal mathematics, subtitle, and strength of the eventual institutional argument remain revisable. Changes are judged by the reader promise, evidence, narrative continuity, and series boundary rather than by fidelity to any one legacy outline.
