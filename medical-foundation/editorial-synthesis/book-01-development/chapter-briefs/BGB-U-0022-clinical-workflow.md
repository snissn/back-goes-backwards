## Chapter job

This chapter places Book I's mechanical lens inside an ordinary clinical-reasoning sequence without turning it into a parallel diagnosis, screening doctrine, validated checklist, or treatment system. Established clinical problems, safety concerns, diagnoses, current care, goals, and professional scope come first. Mechanical history and routing are then added only where they sharpen a defined question about task, exposure, response, function, recurrence, recovery, or context. The chapter must show what information the lens can organize, what it cannot decide, and when it should stop.

Its second job is architectural. Part V needs worked narratives that are comparable and auditable rather than persuasive stories. Chapter 22 therefore produces a minimum narrative record: the canonical causal chain, evidence states, provenance, alternatives, nulls, missing observations, clinical context, and a stated interpretation boundary. This is a drafting and reasoning scaffold for the book, not a patient self-test or a finished clinical instrument.

**This chapter must:**

- begin with the clinical problem, safety, current diagnosis and care, patient goals, and scope;
- show how task, load history, temporal pattern, compensation, recovery, and context can be added selectively;
- distinguish symptom or function modulation from proof of origin;
- place imaging and asymptomatic or noncorresponding findings in evidence-proportionate roles;
- show complementarity with diagnosis, medication, surgery, rehabilitation, physical therapy, and referral relationships;
- include an explicit stopping rule and low/no-added-value branch; and
- create the minimum worked-narrative handoff used by Chapters 23–26.

**This chapter must not:**

- present an unvalidated intake form, score, screening test, diagnostic category, or referral algorithm;
- imply that clinicians have overlooked all biomechanics or that physical therapy already does nothing similar;
- use posture, imaging, movement response, tenderness, or symptom change as proof of a body-wide history;
- prescribe correction, exercise, breath practice, positioning, medication change, surgery, or care sequence;
- tell readers to delay or replace established medical assessment; or
- use preferred configuration, TCM mapping, breath geometry, or practice claims to fill missing evidence.

## Reader entry and exit

**The reader enters** accepting that mechanics can sometimes organize clinically relevant context but unsure how to add that lens without competing with diagnosis, overreading findings, or drifting into treatment advice.

**The reader exits** able to place a bounded mechanical question within established care, record what is observed versus reconstructed, preserve whole-person alternatives and nulls, identify when the lens adds little, and audit a worked narrative before accepting its conclusion.

## Central question

How can load history and routing be added to clinical reasoning in a way that complements established evaluation and care, improves the structure of selected questions, and remains visibly non-diagnostic, non-prescriptive, and testable?

## Working answer

The lens should enter after the clinical problem, safety context, current diagnosis and care, goals, and scope are clear. It adds a structured account of task and environment, configuration and available paths, external demand and internal distribution, local exposure, response through time, and function or symptoms. Each element is labeled by evidence state and provenance; alternatives and nulls enter at the affected node. Mechanical modulation can support a current functional hypothesis without proving origin. The workflow succeeds when it clarifies what is known, missing, testable, or unnecessary—not only when it finds a positive mechanical story.

## Narrative arc

The chapter opens with an ordinary encounter in which the mechanical question is neither the first nor the only question. It establishes a clinical-first entry sequence and then adds load history, task, time, compensation, and recovery selectively. Observation, measurement, reconstruction, and provenance are separated before any causal interpretation. Imaging and modulation are then handled as evidence with bounded jobs. A care-interface map shows how the lens can complement diagnosis, medication, surgery, rehabilitation, physical therapy, and other disciplines without claiming ownership. The chapter closes by converting the encounter into the auditable record that every Part V narrative must follow, including a branch where the lens adds little.

## Argument sequence

### Opening — the mechanical question enters an ordinary encounter

Use a neutral, source-grounded composite encounter rather than a dramatic condition or a TOS case. The person has a defined concern, relevant medical context, current care, functional goal, and a task in which symptoms or capacity vary. The opening first shows the ordinary clinical questions: what is happening, what needs timely attention, what diagnoses or conditions are known or being considered, what care is already in place, and what matters to the person.

Only then ask whether load history or routing could clarify one bounded issue. Do not make the opening clinician discover a hidden global cause. Include a version in which the added lens ultimately produces no new decision-relevant information.

### 22.1 Begin with the clinical problem, safety, goals, and scope

**Job:** Establish that mechanical reasoning is additive to established evaluation and care.

Describe a general sequence without writing a new protocol: presenting concern and functional impact; safety and urgent-context considerations under established standards; known diagnoses, medications, procedures, rehabilitation, and referrals; relevant biological and whole-person context; patient goals and preferences; and the professional question within scope. Cite external standards rather than restating red-flag or referral rules.

The book must not suggest that a routing hypothesis changes emergency, diagnostic, medication, surgical, or referral obligations. A mechanical question can be deferred, limited, or unnecessary when another concern has priority.

**Exit:** With the clinical frame intact, the workflow can decide whether an added mechanical question has a defined job.

### 22.2 Add task, history, time, and recovery selectively

**Job:** Introduce the proposed information without turning the encounter into an exhaustive body map.

Ask which task or environment matters, how the concern changes across activities and time, what exposures repeat, what recovery opportunities exist, what changed before or after the concern, and what compensations or supports are observed. Separate current observation from remembered history and from modeled internal distribution. Use Chapter 18's longitudinal record only to the depth required by the declared question.

Include occupational, developmental, care, access, sleep, stress, behavior, and social context where they plausibly affect a named node. Do not make every field mandatory. A concise local history may be more useful than a speculative whole-body reconstruction.

**Exit:** The encounter now contains candidate data, but provenance must be explicit before the data can support a causal story.

### 22.3 Keep an observation, evidence-state, and provenance ledger

**Job:** Prevent direct findings, calculated variables, reconstructions, and embodied reports from blending together.

For each consequential entry, record what was observed, measured, calculated, modeled, inferred, hypothesized, or embodied; who or what produced it; at what time; under what task; and with what missing interval or uncertainty. “Reconstructed” remains a temporal qualifier: a reconstructed route may combine `OBS`, `MEAS`, `MOD`, and `INF`, but the synthesis is not itself a direct observation.

Patient report and embodied observation remain legitimate evidence forms with bounded jobs. They need not be dismissed because they are not instrument measurements, and they cannot be silently promoted into tissue stress, diagnosis, or causal history.

**Exit:** Once provenance is visible, modulation and imaging can be interpreted without carrying more than they show.

### 22.4 Modulation informs the present without proving the past

**Job:** Use changing symptoms or function as bounded information.

A task change, support, rest, movement, position, manual contact, environmental change, or treatment may alter symptoms, performance, or a measured variable. Such modulation can help identify a current relation worth measuring or managing within established care. It does not by itself reveal the initiating cause, validate a body-wide route, or prove that the modulating condition is universally preferable.

Include placebo/context effects, expectation, attention, learning, natural fluctuation, regression to the mean, measurement error, and concurrent care as appropriate alternatives. The response may be real and clinically useful without settling etiology.

**Exit:** Structural observations require the same discipline: correspondence is a question, not an assumption.

### 22.5 Imaging and findings occupy bounded places in the chain

**Job:** Integrate structural evidence without treating it as either destiny or irrelevant noise.

Place imaging, examination findings, laboratory results, and functional measures at their actual chain node. A structural finding may define anatomy or tissue state, support a diagnosis within established criteria, or constrain a model. It may be common in asymptomatic people, poorly aligned with current symptoms, downstream of another process, or unrelated to the selected route. Conversely, absence of a routine finding does not prove absence of a mechanical exposure.

Require finding- and condition-specific correspondence evidence. Avoid the symmetrical errors of making imaging the cause and dismissing all imaging because noncorrespondence exists.

**Exit:** The mechanical lens can now be placed alongside—not over—other forms of care and expertise.

### 22.6 Interface with diagnosis, medication, surgery, rehabilitation, and physical therapy

**Job:** Define complementarity and overlap without professional caricature or novelty overclaim.

Build a care-interface map. Medical evaluation can identify diagnoses, tissue states, urgent concerns, exclusions, and treatment options; medication and procedures can change biology, symptoms, or structure; surgery can change anatomy and routing; rehabilitation and physical therapy already address movement, load, capacity, function, adaptation, education, and graded return; occupational and environmental work can change tasks and constraints. The proposed routing object may connect time and scale across these domains, but its incremental value is not assumed.

Address the “this is just physical therapy” objection directly: overlap is substantial and desirable. The project must specify whether it contributes a clearer longitudinal representation, cross-disciplinary vocabulary, research question, or nothing new. It must not claim a new profession or supplant expert care.

**Exit:** A useful workflow must state what it outputs, when it stops, and how its account can be tested.

### 22.7 Produce a bounded interpretation and stopping rule

**Job:** Convert collected information into an evidence-proportionate question, not a diagnosis.

The output is a short structured statement: the declared clinical question; observed/measured context; proposed mechanical relation; evidence state of each link; biological and whole-person alternatives; missing observations; one or more discriminating measurements or research questions; current care boundary; and whether the added lens changes understanding or communication. It may conclude that mechanics has a narrow role, remains underdetermined, or adds little.

Stopping rules include: the clinical question is adequately addressed by established care; a nonmechanical concern has priority; the history is too nonunique; measurement cannot discriminate the hypothesis; the proposed relation does not change explanation or decision; or further reconstruction would create burden without value. These are successful uses of the framework.

**Exit:** The same output can become a standardized editorial handoff for the worked narratives.

### 22.8 The minimum worked-narrative record

**Job:** Supply Part V with a complete and auditable case architecture.

Create a table with the following rows: clinical or research question; safety and care context; task/environment; configuration and available paths; external demand/internal distribution; local exposure; tissue/system response through time; function/finding/symptom; interpretation/outcome; whole-person factors at named nodes; forward, reverse, reciprocal, and common-cause alternatives; null or low-mechanical-priority result; discriminating observations; missing cells; evidence state; provenance; and boundary statement.

Every arrow receives its own proposition. A worked narrative may remain incomplete; blank cells and weak links are results, not drafting defects. The table is for writing, evidence review, and model audit. It must not be released as a validated clinical checklist unless later research and governance independently justify that use.

**Exit:** Part V can now test the framework on distinct failure signatures without changing the rules between examples.

### Conclusion and handoff

Return to the opening encounter and compare three outputs: a bounded mechanical hypothesis that clarifies a current question, a partial account that remains worth measuring, and a no-added-value result. None is a hidden diagnosis or a treatment plan. The lens earns a place only by improving a defined question while preserving safety, established care, provenance, alternatives, and the person's goals.

Chapter 23 receives the minimum narrative record and applies it to geometry and localized strain. Chapters 24–26 then keep the same grammar while changing the failure signature, receiver, timescale, and evidence burden. Any narrative that cannot fill a consequential arrow must show the blank rather than smoothing it with rhetoric.

## Model obligations

### Additive encounter workflow

1. **Object represented:** Sequence from clinical problem and safety context to a selectively added mechanical question and bounded interpretation.
2. **System boundary and scale:** Clinical encounter and care ecosystem, with person–environment, regional, and tissue scales added only as needed.
3. **Variables and states:** Concern, diagnosis/care context, goals, task, history, routing nodes, alternatives, null, evidence states, and stopping condition.
4. **Assumptions:** Established clinical standards govern safety; professional roles vary; the workflow is conceptual and unvalidated.
5. **What the model explains:** Where mechanical information can enter without becoming a parallel diagnosis.
6. **What it cannot establish:** Safety rule, diagnosis, causal priority, referral, prognosis, treatment, or clinical efficacy.
7. **Failure or replacement condition:** Revise when it duplicates existing frameworks, burdens encounters, obscures care, or cannot produce reliable information.
8. **Registry action:** to create.

### Observation and provenance ledger

1. **Object represented:** Origin, time, task, evidence state, and uncertainty of each consequential item.
2. **System boundary and scale:** One encounter or worked narrative across all declared scales.
3. **Variables and states:** `OBS`, `MEAS`, `CALC`, `MOD`, `INF`, `HYP`, `EMB`; reconstructed as temporal qualifier; source and date.
4. **Assumptions:** Reports, measurements, and models have different validity conditions and may conflict.
5. **What the model explains:** What the narrative actually knows and where synthesis begins.
6. **What it cannot establish:** Truth merely by documentation, causal direction, or clinical importance.
7. **Failure or replacement condition:** Replace if the ledger becomes a burdensome checklist or hides rather than clarifies uncertainty.
8. **Registry action:** to create.

### Care-interface map

1. **Object represented:** Complementary and overlapping roles of medicine, medication, surgery, rehabilitation, physical therapy, occupational/environmental work, and the proposed routing inquiry.
2. **System boundary and scale:** Care and research relationships, not anatomical force distribution.
3. **Variables and states:** Information, expertise, intervention, handoff, overlap, gap, and outcome question.
4. **Assumptions:** Roles vary by jurisdiction, specialty, setting, and person.
5. **What the model explains:** Where the lens might integrate context and where it may duplicate existing work.
6. **What it cannot establish:** Professional authority, superiority, care pathway, or benefit.
7. **Failure or replacement condition:** Replace where existing integrated practice already captures the proposed information more clearly.
8. **Registry action:** to create.

### Minimum worked-narrative record

1. **Object represented:** Complete causal-chain, evidence-state, alternative, null, provenance, and boundary record for Chapters 23–26.
2. **System boundary and scale:** Declared separately in each worked narrative.
3. **Variables and states:** All canonical chain nodes, each arrow, whole-person entries, discriminators, missing cells, and evidence states.
4. **Assumptions:** It is an editorial/research scaffold, not a validated clinical form.
5. **What the model explains:** Whether a narrative is complete enough to assess and where its weakest links lie.
6. **What it cannot establish:** Clinical validity, diagnosis, screening result, treatment, or utility.
7. **Failure or replacement condition:** Revise if worked narratives can pass while hiding missing links or if fields encourage false certainty.
8. **Registry action:** to create.

## Example program

### Primary example

The mechanical lens should clarify one narrow question about exposure, compensation, or current modulation without yielding a diagnosis or prescription. - Imaging finding with weak symptom correspondence — bounded evidence role.

## Principal objections and responses

### “This is an unvalidated checklist masquerading as clinical guidance.”

The chapter explicitly presents a conceptual workflow and an editorial worked-narrative record. It supplies no score, threshold, diagnosis, referral rule, or treatment. Any future clinical instrument would require separate development, validation, governance, and scope review.

### “Physical therapists and biomechanists already do this.”

Substantial overlap is expected. The proposed contribution must be tested against those practices and may narrow to a cross-disciplinary representation, a longitudinal research object, or no novel contribution. The chapter cannot claim novelty through omission.

### “If changing position changes symptoms, the mechanical cause has been found.”

The observed modulation is real evidence about the present conditions. It does not alone identify historical cause, tissue state, diagnosis, or durable outcome, and it may reflect several concurrent mechanisms.

### “Safety and referral language becomes medical advice.”

The chapter points readers to established professional standards and says that the mechanical lens does not replace them. It does not reproduce a rule set or advise individual action.
