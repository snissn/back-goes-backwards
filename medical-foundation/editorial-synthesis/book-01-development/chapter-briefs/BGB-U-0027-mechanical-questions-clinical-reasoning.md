## Chapter job

This chapter compresses the whole Book I method into a portable sequence of non-diagnostic questions. Parts III–V have defined adverse organization, medical boundaries, and complete worked narratives. Chapter 27 makes that reasoning reproducible without turning it into a score, screening instrument, self-test, diagnosis, or treatment selector. Its distinctive contribution is disciplined question order: begin with task and history, move inward one evidenced level at a time, and let each answer increase, reduce, redirect, or leave unchanged confidence in a proposed mechanical account.

The chapter should feel like a usable intellectual tool rather than an appendix of caveats. It may briefly recall the familiar object-transfer task and contrast the four Part V narratives, but it must not restage a patient encounter already owned by Chapter 22 or reteach the cases. The reader should see how the same scaffold produces different answers—including a null or low-mechanical-priority result—depending on the evidence.

**This chapter must:**

- preserve the causal-chain order from task and environment through clinical interpretation;
- distinguish ordinary observation, validated clinical measure, research measure, calculation, model output, inference, reconstruction, hypothesis, and embodied report;
- carry hard/soft roles, posterior participation, compensation, capacity, and whole-person alternatives into the questions without turning any one of them into the expected answer;
- show how uncertainty and noncorrespondence change the next question rather than merely weaken a final conclusion;
- use one compact callback to the recurring object-transfer task and a concise Part V comparison; and
- include explicit stopping conditions when the proposed mechanical route is unsupported, unmeasurable, clinically secondary, or outside the user's scope.

**This chapter must not:**

- duplicate Chapter 22's clinical workflow, documentation, interdisciplinary integration, imaging discussion, or professional interfaces at full depth;
- become a checklist score, posture screen, hidden diagnostic system, referral algorithm, or treatment protocol;
- imply that asking more mechanical questions necessarily makes mechanics the primary explanation;
- convert posterior participation, hard/soft organization, or task modulation into a correction rule;
- present research instrumentation as routine clinical validation; or
- address the reader as a patient performing self-assessment.

## Reader entry and exit

**The reader enters** able to follow mechanical narratives but not necessarily able to reproduce the reasoning in a new situation without either skipping steps or overinterpreting observations.

**The reader exits** able to use a bounded scaffold: describe a task and history, declare a system, separate configuration from internal exposure, ask how roles and paths were divided, identify compensation and alternatives, distinguish modulation from causation, and stop when evidence does not support a stronger interpretation.

## Central question

What sequence of mechanical questions can improve clinical reasoning without becoming an unvalidated diagnostic or treatment system?

## Working answer

Begin with the actual task, environment, history, and changing state. Declare the system and phase before naming forces or paths. Treat visible configuration as one observation, then ask what internal distribution and tissue-specific exposure could follow under stated assumptions. Separate shape-preserving and form-receiving roles, posterior participation, compensation, capacity, tissue response, findings, symptoms, and whole-person alternatives. Use task or state modulation to refine a hypothesis, not prove its origin. At every step identify the evidence state and the observations that would weaken or stop the proposed route. The result is a structured context for inquiry; it is not a diagnosis, score, or prescription.

## Narrative arc

Open by returning briefly to the ordinary object-transfer task after the reader has seen four medical narratives. Ask the same seven questions of this familiar task and show that a visible movement does not reveal one internal route. Move from incoming demand to available configuration and roles, then to local exposure, compensation, modulation, and measurement. At each stage use one contrast from Part V to demonstrate that the same question can strengthen, weaken, or leave unresolved a case. End by compressing the sequence into a portable figure whose stopping points are as visible as its forward arrows.

## Argument sequence

### Opening — one familiar task, seven newly disciplined questions

Recall `BGB-X-0001` only long enough to show a person moving an ordinary object between supports and carrying it several steps. Do not analyze technique or invite imitation. Ask what can be seen, what can be measured, and what remains unavailable when the system boundary moves from person-plus-object to person, segment, joint, and tissue. Then place one row from each Part V case beside the task to establish that the same grammar can organize very different evidence without producing the same answer.

### 27.1 What demand entered the system?

**Job:** Replace generic activity or posture labels with a concrete task, environment, history, and timescale.

Ask about work, caregiving, training, injury, travel, devices, sleep position where relevant, repetition, duration, rate, direction, variability, assistance, recent change, and recovery. Identify contacts, supports, external loads, sequence, and the system whose demand is being described. Include access, job control, disability, and environmental constraints where they alter what actually occurred or what options existed.

**Exit:** The reasoning begins with an inspectable exposure history rather than a morally loaded label such as sedentary, active, weak, or poorly aligned.

### 27.2 What configuration and paths were available?

**Job:** Describe three-dimensional organization and candidate carriers without inferring a unique route from appearance.

Ask what moved or remained fixed, which contacts and supports changed, what neighboring regions contributed, and what environmental constraints limited alternatives. Name global, segment, joint, or tissue frames where orientation matters. Treat a visible path line as an inference unless carriers and quantities are measured. Include more than one viable strategy.

**Exit:** Configuration has narrowed or expanded a set of mechanical possibilities without becoming a verdict about internal distribution.

### 27.3 How were hard and soft roles divided?

**Job:** Ask what established shape, transmitted or redirected demand, actuated, restrained, yielded, conformed, glided, contained, expanded, buffered, or regulated pressure.

Apply roles to relations, directions, phases, and timescales rather than permanently coloring tissues. Ask whether a form-receiving structure was required to act as a substitute boundary, whether a shape-preserving route lacked capacity, and whether roles exchanged successfully. Include posterior participation as one anatomically important candidate observation alongside anterior, lateral, deep, active, tensile, compliant, and pressure-mediated support.

**Exit:** The division of mechanical labor is explicit enough to generate a testable distribution question without assuming that hard, posterior, or Yang are synonyms.

### 27.4 Where might local exposure have accumulated?

**Job:** Translate history and configuration into tissue-specific quantities and dose questions.

Ask about force, moment, contact, pressure, stress, strain, deformation, excursion, friction, rate, repetition, and recovery at the relevant tissue or interface. State whether each quantity is measured, calculated, modeled, inferred, or unknown. Add tissue state, development, fatigue, inflammation, prior injury, general health, and capacity. Stop if no valid bridge exists from observed configuration to local exposure.

**Exit:** A broad mechanical concern has become a challengeable exposure hypothesis—or has stopped because the needed object cannot yet be specified.

### 27.5 What compensation preserved function?

**Job:** Identify what the system protected and whether the changed strategy altered later demand.

Ask about tone, bracing, gait, balance, timing, side-to-side transfer, range, effort, breathing, external assistance, task choice, and avoidance. Distinguish immediate protection, successful adaptation, persistent substitution, and symptom-led behavior. Do not classify an observed change as costly until a relevant exposure or outcome is demonstrated.

**Exit:** Compensation is represented as capability and chronology before it becomes a candidate source of cost.

### 27.6 What changes with task, support, time, or state?

**Job:** Use modulation to compare hypotheses without mistaking it for diagnosis.

Contrast loaded and supported, early and late, rested and fatigued, one task and another, familiar and unfamiliar, or one environmental arrangement and another. Identify what changed simultaneously and what remained controlled. A change may support mechanical relevance, reveal capacity or recovery, reflect attention or expectation, or remain nonspecific. Include nonchange and paradoxical change as legitimate results.

**Exit:** Modulation updates a particular arrow and its alternatives rather than retrospectively proving the whole chain.

### 27.7 What can actually be measured, and what remains uncertain?

**Job:** Close the scaffold with evidence state, validity, alternatives, nulls, and stopping rules.

Separate ordinary observation, validated clinical assessment, research instrumentation, calculated quantity, model output, retrospective reconstruction, and hypothesis. Record reliability, validity, meaningful difference, confounds, missing intervals, alternatives, and null findings. A credible low-mechanical-priority outcome occurs when configuration does not correspond to exposure, exposure does not track function or symptoms, modulation is nonspecific, or another account explains the course better. Unknown is not support.

**Exit:** The reader can state what the lens contributes now, what requires research, and where the inquiry should stop.

### Conclusion and handoff

Present the final scaffold as a sequence with branches and stop points, not a circular score. Demonstrate it in abbreviated form across the four Part V narratives: geometry, maintained constraint, recurrent instability, and mixed chronic compensation should not converge on one answer. Chapter 28 inherits the vocabulary of demand, options, variability, capacity, and recovery and asks how it can be taught and built into environments without medicalizing daily life.

## Model obligations

### Portable reasoning scaffold

1. **Object represented:** the sequence from task/history through configuration, roles, exposure, response, compensation, modulation, alternatives, and uncertainty.
2. **System boundary and scale:** variable by question but always declared; no silent movement from whole person to tissue.
3. **Variables and states:** exact causal-chain nodes plus `OBS`, `MEAS`, `CALC`, `MOD`, `INF`, `HYP`, `EMB`, and reconstructed qualifiers.
4. **Assumptions:** professional context, population, task, phase, validity of observations, and scope of available evidence.
5. **What the model explains:** what to ask next and why a particular inference is or is not licensed.
6. **What it cannot establish:** diagnosis, score, prognosis, treatment, one correct posture, or preferred configuration.
7. **Failure or replacement condition:** poor usability, low inter-rater agreement, no incremental information, misleading inference, or emergence of a validated domain-specific tool that should replace the generic scaffold.
8. **Registry action:** to create; no stable model ID allocated here.

### Confidence-update and stop model

Use qualitative outcomes—supports this arrow, weakens this arrow, redirects to an alternative, unresolved, or stop—rather than an invented numeric score. Every update must name the proposition affected. Failure at an early arrow prevents downstream claims from inheriting confidence.

## Principal objections and responses

### “This is an unvalidated assessment disguised as a list of questions.”

The scaffold organizes inquiry and marks evidence state; it does not produce a score, threshold, diagnostic class, or treatment. Any future tool derived from it belongs in Chapter 29's validation program and Book V only after the relevant gates.

### “Clinicians already ask about activity, posture, and aggravating factors.”

The chapter does not claim invention of those practices. Its proposed addition is the explicit sequence from task and boundary through tissue-specific exposure, compensation, alternatives, and evidence state, integrated with existing reasoning rather than replacing it.

### “More mechanical detail may simply produce confirmation bias.”

Every question pairs with alternatives, disfavoring observations, and stop conditions. The four-case comparison must show the scaffold reducing confidence as well as increasing it.

### “Posterior and hard/soft observations still smuggle in the later hypothesis.”

They enter as bounded anatomy and functional-role questions established in Book I. No preferred sign, universal posterior priority, or corrective inference follows from their inclusion.
