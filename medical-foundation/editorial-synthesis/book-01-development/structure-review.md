# Book I Structural Review

**Book:** *The Back Goes Backwards*, Book I: *Medical Foundations*
**Status:** Structural review accepted at working `R2`
**Review object:** The six-Part, thirty-chapter architecture in the detailed Book I outline

## Book-level verdict

Retain the present six-Part, thirty-chapter structure. It has a coherent argument rather than merely a collection of topics:

> embodied mechanics → mechanical language → anatomy of support → adverse organization → medical interpretation → worked tests → bounded use and wider hypothesis

No chapter presently warrants deletion, merger, division, or relocation. The source-reconciliation work has given each chapter a legitimate job, and the larger sequence preserves the strongest architecture recovered from the `new` archive. The remaining structural work is mainly to sharpen boundaries between neighboring chapters, choose the exact worked examples, and ensure that recurring concepts advance rather than restart.

The [Book I brief-acceptance and control-delta docket](decisions/book-i-brief-acceptance-and-control-delta-docket.md) advances this structure and all 30 chapter briefs to working `R2`. *Accept* and *accept with refinement* below now describe the retained placement and job; a refinement is owned development work, not a structural hold. This acceptance does not mean that inherited wording or claims have survived evidence review.

The book's decisive narrative move occurs between Parts II and III. Parts I and II establish how a living body can successfully divide and route mechanical demand. Part III develops the negative account: what can happen when dose, capacity, geometry, timing, constraint, or compensation make that organization harder to sustain. Parts IV and V then earn medical relevance rather than assuming it. Part VI gives the reader a bounded present use and discloses the stronger series program.

Three devices should maintain continuity throughout:

1. The explanatory chain—task and environment → configuration and paths → local exposure → tissue response → function, findings, or symptoms—should recur in progressively richer form.
2. The neutral object-transfer task should carry Parts I and II, then yield explicitly to the more demanding cases rather than simply disappearing.
3. Hard/soft should advance through four levels: functional definition in Chapter 3, anatomical realization in Chapters 5–9, adverse role substitution in Chapters 11–16, and medical testing in Chapters 20 and 24.

## Part I — Mechanical foundations of living structure

### Structural judgment

**Why this Part exists.** The book cannot make a distinctive argument about routing until the reader understands what is being routed, through what kind of system, and at what scale. Part I establishes a shared language without reducing biology to passive engineering.

**Internal movement.** Chapter 1 establishes the unavoidable mechanical dimension of life. Chapter 2 differentiates the quantities hidden inside “load.” Chapter 3 proposes the hard/soft division of labor. Chapter 4 places quantities and roles inside changing three-dimensional configurations and load paths.

**Exit state.** The reader can describe a mechanical event without treating load as one scalar, posture as pathology, a tissue as permanently hard or soft, or a load path as a literal pipe.

**Seam risks.** Chapters 1 and 2 could become a general biomechanics preface; Chapter 3 could appear prematurely metaphysical; Chapter 4 could preview Book II too heavily. The Part should stay anchored in the neutral object-transfer task and reserve signed preferred modes for a short, clearly marked future-facing seam.

### 1. The mechanical dimension of life

- **Unit and decision:** `BGB-U-0001` — **accept**.
- **Unique job:** Establish mechanics as an inseparable dimension of embodied life while locating it inside, rather than above, biological and whole-person explanations.
- **Reader movement:** **Entry:** mechanics is associated mainly with injury, movement, or rehabilitation. **Exit:** mechanics is understood as continuously present across scales, with its explanatory priority still case-dependent.
- **Dependencies and handoff:** Opens the book without technical prerequisites; hands Chapter 2 the need for a more exact language of demand and exposure.
- **Refinement or open point:** Make the opening concrete and confident. The chapter should pose the upstream-context question without trying to answer it or previewing every later physiological branch.

### 2. A working language of load

- **Unit and decision:** `BGB-U-0002` — **accept**.
- **Unique job:** Replace an undifferentiated idea of load with system boundaries, forces, moments, pressure, stress, strain, deformation, material response, dose, energy, and inspectable load-path models.
- **Reader movement:** **Entry:** “load” functions as a loose synonym for weight or effort. **Exit:** the reader can ask which quantity, at what boundary, in what direction, over what time, and through what inferred path.
- **Dependencies and handoff:** Inherits embodied mechanics from Chapter 1; gives Chapters 3 and 4 the quantities needed to discuss role and organization.
- **Refinement or open point:** Keep the mathematics relational and visual. Choose a small canonical set of free-body diagrams that can be reused later rather than introducing examples that never return.

### 3. Hard and soft mechanical roles

- **Unit and decision:** `BGB-U-0003` — **accept with refinement**.
- **Unique job:** Introduce the project's distinctive division of mechanical labor: relatively shape-preserving and structurally resolving roles alongside relatively form-receiving and shape-conforming roles.
- **Reader movement:** **Entry:** hard and soft sound like fixed tissue categories. **Exit:** they are understood as complementary, scale-, direction-, state-, and task-dependent roles that a tissue or assembly can exchange.
- **Dependencies and handoff:** Requires Chapter 2's distinctions among load, deformation, stiffness, and capacity; Chapter 4 organizes the roles into routes, and Chapters 5–6 make them anatomical.
- **Refinement or open point:** Signpost that the relational polarity is motivated by TCM hard/soft and Yin/Yang concepts without claiming definitional identity or making later correspondence load-bearing. The brief should also distinguish the functional principle from the more specific candidate soft-to-hard tissue handoff.

### 4. Three-dimensional configuration and load paths

- **Unit and decision:** `BGB-U-0004` — **accept with refinement**.
- **Unique job:** Show how geometry, contact, active force, pressure, tissue state, and local/global frames turn external demand into different internal organizations.
- **Reader movement:** **Entry:** configuration is likely to mean visible posture or position. **Exit:** it means a richer mechanical state, and a load path is recognized as a bounded explanatory representation of transfer.
- **Dependencies and handoff:** Integrates Chapters 1–3; gives Part II the framework within which anatomical structures cooperate and Part III the basis for discussing route change.
- **Refinement or open point:** Keep local spatial frames, generalized modes, and material directions explicitly distinct. The signed-mode preview should prepare Book II in one contained section rather than diverting Book I into formalization it does not require.

### Part I conclusion

Part I is structurally complete when its four chapters feel like one tightening argument: mechanics is unavoidable; mechanical words must be specific; structures divide mechanical labor; and that labor is organized in three dimensions. It should end with a question anatomy can answer: which structures and systems perform these roles in an actual living body?

## Part II — The architecture of support

### Structural judgment

**Why this Part exists.** The hard/soft and load-path language must become anatomically credible before the book can discuss failure. Part II supplies a positive account of support and makes posterior anatomy important without making posterior preference a hidden premise.

**Internal movement.** Chapter 5 begins with skeletal geometry and contact. Chapter 6 adds active, tensile, compliant, sliding, pressure-bearing, and candidate tension-network systems. Chapter 7 maps posterior anatomy. Chapter 8 tests its contribution in tasks. Chapter 9 restores whole-body, lifespan, and population context and states the three levels of the posterior proposition.

**Exit state.** The reader can trace support as a cooperative division of labor, define posterior-chain scope, and distinguish anatomical existence, task-specific mechanical importance, and preferred-configuration hypotheses.

**Seam risks.** The five chapters could read as an anatomy catalogue, and the prominence of posterior structures could imply that Book II's conclusion has already been assumed. The neutral task should provide an active through-line, and Chapter 9's three-level ladder should govern every posterior claim in the Part.

### 5. Skeleton, joints, and shape-preserving paths

- **Unit and decision:** `BGB-U-0005` — **accept**.
- **Unique job:** Establish skeletal geometry, joint contact, and ligamentous guidance as the clearest anatomy of relatively shape-preserving routes.
- **Reader movement:** **Entry:** the skeleton is either a passive frame or an alignment ideal. **Exit:** it is a deformable, adaptive architecture whose useful routes depend on contact, restraint, active control, task, and state.
- **Dependencies and handoff:** Anatomizes Chapters 3–4; hands Chapter 6 a changing structural possibility that living tissues must actuate and support.
- **Refinement or open point:** Use one linked-segment example to move from local joint mechanics to continuity without implying that bone carries demand independently.

### 6. Active, tensile, compliant, pressure-bearing, and tension-network systems

- **Unit and decision:** `BGB-U-0006` — **accept with refinement**.
- **Unique job:** Complete the architecture of support by showing how muscle, tendon, ligament, fascia, cartilage, interfaces, fluid, pressure, and baseline tone actuate, guide, transfer, accommodate, and stabilize demand.
- **Reader movement:** **Entry:** support is principally skeletal or muscular. **Exit:** it is a coordinated exchange among several material and control systems, with tensegrity retained as one bounded model rather than a proof.
- **Dependencies and handoff:** Extends Chapter 5 and makes the hard/soft roles concrete; prepares the layered posterior anatomy of Chapter 7.
- **Refinement or open point:** Prevent the chapter from becoming an encyclopedic tissue survey. Organize every tissue discussion around its job in the same changing handoff, and keep local continuity distinct from clinically meaningful body-wide transmission.

### 7. The posterior chain as an anatomical system

- **Unit and decision:** `BGB-U-0007` — **accept**.
- **Unique job:** Define narrow, broad, and whole-support scopes of the posterior chain and establish its substantial anatomy from ground to cranium.
- **Reader movement:** **Entry:** “posterior chain” is a familiar but unstable slogan. **Exit:** the reader can state which scope is meant and separate anatomical connection, coordinated task participation, and quantitative long-range transfer.
- **Dependencies and handoff:** Applies Chapters 5–6 to one large system; Chapter 8 must now show what the mapped structures do.
- **Refinement or open point:** Preserve the large-muscle observation as an objective reason for attention, but do not let size or broad attachment substitute for functional evidence.

### 8. The physics and ordinary function of posterior support

- **Unit and decision:** `BGB-U-0008` — **accept**.
- **Unique job:** Move from posterior anatomy to task mechanics across stance, sitting, gait, lifting, deceleration, propulsion, balance, and elastic energy management.
- **Reader movement:** **Entry:** anatomical presence is easily mistaken for functional primacy. **Exit:** posterior contribution is substantial but shared, phase-dependent, and distinguishable across performance, fatigue, stability, and injury exposure.
- **Dependencies and handoff:** Requires Chapters 2, 4, and 7; completes the neutral object-transfer task and hands Chapter 9 a task-specific account that must be situated across bodies and time.
- **Refinement or open point:** Keep the evolutionary material as a bounded explanatory program. It should explain why the question is plausible, not decide the preferred-configuration hypothesis by adaptationist inference.

### 9. Posterior support across the whole body and lifespan

- **Unit and decision:** `BGB-U-0009` — **accept with refinement**.
- **Unique job:** Reintegrate posterior support with anterior, lateral, deep, pressure, developmental, environmental, disability, illness, pregnancy, aging, and reserve contexts, then state the posterior proposition ladder.
- **Reader movement:** **Entry:** posterior support may still seem like a separable subsystem or universal solution. **Exit:** it is understood as a changing participant in whole-body organization, with three distinct levels of claim.
- **Dependencies and handoff:** Synthesizes Part II; its lifespan and reserve analysis creates the transition to Part III's dose-capacity account.
- **Refinement or open point:** The three-level proposition should be a visible controlling figure or boxed synthesis. Candidate markers must remain observations until later measurement work establishes what they mean.

### Part II conclusion

Part II succeeds when the reader sees support as a living exchange rather than a rigid frame and understands why posterior anatomy deserves sustained attention without being asked to accept a universal posterior rule. The transition to Part III should be explicit: once a viable division of labor is visible, interruption, substitution, concentration, instability, and compensation become mechanically intelligible.

## Part III — Load management, failure, and compensation

### Structural judgment

**Why this Part exists.** This is Book I's principal explanatory contribution. It gives “bad load” a defensible mechanical vocabulary and develops the negative hard/soft account without jumping from position to disease.

**Internal movement.** Chapter 10 establishes dynamic viability through dose, capacity, recovery, and environment. Chapter 11 defines continuity and changed transfer. Chapters 12–15 develop six adverse-organization families: global geometric redistribution and local concentration; constraint-maintained deformation or lost excursion; instability and coordination/handoff failure; and compensatory rerouting. Chapter 16 traces possible changes of scale and keeps downstream physiological branches distinct.

**Exit state.** The reader can describe how a viable organization becomes harder to sustain, what exposure may result, how compensation changes future demand, and where the mechanical account must stop before biological and medical inference.

**Seam risks.** The taxonomy is distributed across several chapters and may be hard to retain. Chapters 11 and 15 can duplicate rerouting language; Chapters 16 and 19 can duplicate downstream biology. Part III needs an early one-page taxonomy map, stable terminology, and explicit ownership: Chapter 11 defines transfer states, Chapter 15 develops compensation through time, Chapter 16 maps cross-scale hypotheses, and Chapter 19 later evaluates tissue response.

### 10. Mechanical homeostasis, capacity, dose, time, and environment

- **Unit and decision:** `BGB-U-0010` — **accept with refinement**.
- **Unique job:** Establish the conditional baseline against which adverse organization can be judged: viable ranges depend on demand, direction, repetition, variability, recovery, current capacity, and boundary conditions.
- **Reader movement:** **Entry:** mechanical health may sound like one correct shape or a simple load threshold. **Exit:** it is understood as dynamic, task-sensitive, history-dependent viability.
- **Dependencies and handoff:** Inherits Part II's changing support system; gives Chapter 11 a reason continuity can be adequate in one state and insufficient in another.
- **Refinement or open point:** Decide whether “mechanical homeostasis” remains the principal reader-facing term after evidence review. Whatever term survives must not imply a fixed set point or make every deviation a failed regulation.

### 11. Continuity, handoff, leakage, interruption, and substitution

- **Unit and decision:** `BGB-U-0011` — **accept with refinement**.
- **Unique job:** Define the relational grammar of successful and altered transfer before the book applies the failure-family taxonomy.
- **Reader movement:** **Entry:** interruption, overload, leakage, substitution, and compensation can sound interchangeable. **Exit:** the reader can distinguish route availability, route choice, excess demand, altered carrier, and changed local exposure.
- **Dependencies and handoff:** Depends on Chapters 3–6 and 10; gives Chapters 12–15 a stable vocabulary.
- **Refinement or open point:** “Leakage” and “misrouting” should always be translated into a proposed quantity and exposure. Reserve temporal adaptation and delayed cost for Chapter 15 so this chapter remains a definition of transfer states.

### 12. Global geometry, local concentration, and strain localization

- **Unit and decision:** `BGB-U-0012` — **accept with refinement**.
- **Unique job:** Connect body- or segment-level geometry to local distribution while keeping global redistribution and local concentration as two distinct analytical families.
- **Reader movement:** **Entry:** a visible shape difference appears to be either irrelevant or directly harmful. **Exit:** geometry is understood as one input that can alter moments, contact, and boundary conditions, while exposure and damage remain separate claims.
- **Dependencies and handoff:** Uses Chapters 2, 4, 5, and 10–11; supplies the general pattern tested in Chapter 23.
- **Refinement or open point:** Because the chapter owns two taxonomy families, its brief needs a repeated two-scale diagram: what reaches the region versus how that demand is distributed within it.

### 13. Constraint-maintained deformation and loss of excursion

- **Unit and decision:** `BGB-U-0013` — **accept**.
- **Unique job:** State the project's most distinctive negative hard/soft mechanism in generic form: surrounding demand may maintain deformation or restrict the needed excursion of a form-receiving structure.
- **Reader movement:** **Entry:** narrowing or loss of space seems to be the active mechanism. **Exit:** the reader asks what load maintains the deformation, what restoring tendencies oppose it, and which downstream function is actually changed.
- **Dependencies and handoff:** Requires hard/soft, route, stress/strain, and capacity language; hands Chapter 20 a cross-system comparison and Chapter 24 a complete corridor test.
- **Refinement or open point:** Preserve the hose analogy but define its deliberate mismatches. The exact sequence—maintaining demand before geometry before downstream function—should become one of the book's canonical diagrams.

### 14. Instability, discoordination, bracing, and repeated demand

- **Unit and decision:** `BGB-U-0014` — **accept with refinement**.
- **Unique job:** Distinguish insufficient maintenance or recovery of a usable relationship from altered timing among otherwise capable participants, then show how repeated correction can become a dose.
- **Reader movement:** **Entry:** instability means looseness and greater activation means better control. **Exit:** material state, timing, sensory control, protection, and repeated correction are separate variables.
- **Dependencies and handoff:** Builds on transfer states from Chapter 11 and dose from Chapter 10; supplies the general patterns tested in Chapter 25 and the bracing route taken up by Chapter 15.
- **Refinement or open point:** Like Chapter 12, this chapter owns two families. A paired comparison should keep instability and discoordination distinct before showing how they interact.

### 15. Compensation, rerouting, and delayed consequence

- **Unit and decision:** `BGB-U-0015` — **accept**.
- **Unique job:** Make compensation an intelligent, often successful response that can nevertheless alter future dose, reserve, and the location or timing of consequence.
- **Reader movement:** **Entry:** compensation is either a defect or evidence of hidden pathology. **Exit:** it can be useful, neutral, costly, replaceable, or eventually insufficient, and its meaning depends on chronology and alternatives.
- **Dependencies and handoff:** Uses Chapters 10–14; gives Chapter 16 a route by which local changes may become regional or global and Chapter 26 its central temporal logic.
- **Refinement or open point:** Keep the full conditional sequence visible while emphasizing off-ramps—recovery, successful adaptation, and null consequence—at every stage.

### 16. Scale transition and possible regulatory consequences

- **Unit and decision:** `BGB-U-0016` — **accept with refinement**.
- **Unique job:** Preserve the wider program by mapping local-to-regional, regional-to-global, and global-to-local pathways, then separating neural/control, pressure/flow, tissue-response, and energetic/regulatory branches.
- **Reader movement:** **Entry:** a local problem and a whole-body pattern appear either unrelated or connected by one vague systemic cascade. **Exit:** multiple bidirectional scale transitions can be proposed and tested independently.
- **Dependencies and handoff:** Synthesizes Part III; hands Part IV explicit candidate branches rather than medical conclusions.
- **Refinement or open point:** End at the map of candidate transitions. Tissue-specific mechanobiology belongs to Chapter 19, and stronger breath, torus, autonomic, or whole-system dynamics belong to later books.

### Part III conclusion

Part III should feel like a single deepening argument, not six names for dysfunction. Its central sequence is: a changing demand meets a changing capacity; paths and handoffs determine distribution; adverse organization may alter local exposure; compensation may preserve function while changing later demand; and effects may cross scale. The Part hands medicine a set of precise hypotheses, not a diagnosis.

## Part IV — From mechanical organization to medical meaning

### Structural judgment

**Why this Part exists.** A mechanically coherent story is not yet a medically meaningful one. Part IV places the project inside existing medical mechanics, supplies the exposure-to-response bridge, establishes causal boundaries, and shows where routing belongs in workflow.

**Internal movement.** Chapter 17 establishes common ground. Chapter 18 states the proposed longitudinal addition. Chapter 19 builds the tissue-response bridge. Chapter 20 stress-tests the constraint model across biological systems. Chapter 21 establishes the causal contract. Chapter 22 translates the lens into clinical workflow.

**Exit state.** The reader can say what is new, what is already conventional, what intermediate links a medical claim requires, how alternatives and reverse causality enter, and what the framework can add without becoming a diagnosis or protocol.

**Seam risks.** The Part can become defensive or procedural. Chapters 17 and 18 need a crisp established/proposed boundary; Chapters 19 and 20 should use concrete tissues; Chapters 21 and 22 should demonstrate epistemic discipline through decisions rather than repeat disclaimers. The tone should be constructive: an extension of existing medicine, not an indictment of it.

### 17. Established mechanical reasoning in medicine

- **Unit and decision:** `BGB-U-0017` — **accept**.
- **Unique job:** Demonstrate that pressure, flow, patency, stability, fixation, decompression, interfaces, devices, and support are already ordinary medical concerns.
- **Reader movement:** **Entry:** the project may appear to claim that medicine has ignored mechanics. **Exit:** the reader sees extensive existing mechanical reasoning and a narrower unanswered question about route and history.
- **Dependencies and handoff:** Recasts Parts I–III in recognized medical settings; Chapter 18 must identify the incremental contribution.
- **Refinement or open point:** Use a small set of representative examples across specialties rather than a catalogue. The endpoint should be the upstream-route gap, not a claim of novelty.

### 18. The proposed addition: longitudinal load routing

- **Unit and decision:** `BGB-U-0018` — **accept with refinement**.
- **Unique job:** Define the project's added object of study: how demand entered, was partitioned, changed through compensation, reached a tissue, and altered later conditions over time.
- **Reader movement:** **Entry:** routing could sound like renamed rehabilitation or an alternative diagnosis. **Exit:** it is a longitudinal context and research object that may inform prevention, reserve, surgery, devices, and recovery without supplanting local diagnosis.
- **Dependencies and handoff:** Requires Part III's taxonomy and Chapter 17's common ground; Chapter 19 must make the biological bridge explicit.
- **Refinement or open point:** State one concrete example of information the routing history adds beyond a local endpoint. Avoid promising every intended application before the framework has been tested.

### 19. From mechanical exposure to tissue response

- **Unit and decision:** `BGB-U-0019` — **accept**.
- **Unique job:** Prevent the book from jumping from whole-body configuration to disease by tracing local mechanical exposure through dose, tissue state, mechanotransduction, response, recovery, and feedback.
- **Reader movement:** **Entry:** mechanically plausible exposure can be mistaken for injury, pain, or diagnosis. **Exit:** exposure, biological response, symptoms, diagnosis, and outcome are understood as distinct claims.
- **Dependencies and handoff:** Receives candidate exposures from Chapters 12–16; gives Chapter 20 a tissue-specific comparison and all worked narratives their biological middle.
- **Refinement or open point:** Choose representative tissue examples that demonstrate different responses rather than attempting a complete mechanobiology survey.

### 20. Comparing conduits, nerves, sliding interfaces, and pressure systems

- **Unit and decision:** `BGB-U-0020` — **accept with refinement**.
- **Unique job:** Test whether the constraint-maintained-deformation family remains useful across systems whose functions depend on patency, excursion, glide, expansion, pressure, or course.
- **Reader movement:** **Entry:** the generic hose model may appear transferable to every soft structure. **Exit:** the reader sees a common mechanical question and system-specific constitutive, regulatory, functional, and clinical differences.
- **Dependencies and handoff:** Combines Chapters 13 and 19; Chapter 24 selects one system for a complete case.
- **Refinement or open point:** Limit the main comparison to a manageable matrix and relegate the wider candidate inventory to the research asset. The chapter should discover both generalities and failed analogies.

### 21. Causal scope, alternatives, and the whole person

- **Unit and decision:** `BGB-U-0021` — **accept**.
- **Unique job:** Establish the causal contract by which mechanics can organize, contribute, modify, follow from, or add little to an explanation while nonmechanical primaries remain genuine.
- **Reader movement:** **Entry:** causal scope may be treated as a disclaimer after the mechanical story. **Exit:** alternatives, reverse causality, person-specific capacity, and context actively alter the mechanical interpretation.
- **Dependencies and handoff:** Relies on Chapters 17–20; gives Chapter 22 the rules for responsible clinical use and Part V the standards by which cases are tested.
- **Refinement or open point:** Preserve osteoarthritis/rheumatoid arthritis as a boundary exercise only if evidence review confirms that it illuminates causal priority better than it distracts into disease comparison.

### 22. Integrating the lens into clinical workflow

- **Unit and decision:** `BGB-U-0022` — **accept with refinement**.
- **Unique job:** Place routing context inside a real clinical sequence involving history, observation, imaging, diagnosis, medication, surgery, rehabilitation, referral, follow-up, and interdisciplinary care.
- **Reader movement:** **Entry:** the framework is an abstract lens with uncertain practical location. **Exit:** the reader sees what questions it adds, what it does not decide, and how observations remain separated from inference.
- **Dependencies and handoff:** Applies Chapter 21's causal contract; prepares Part V to demonstrate rather than merely describe the workflow.
- **Refinement or open point:** Keep this chapter about integration and professional interfaces. Chapter 27 later owns the compact reusable question set, so the two should not carry duplicate lists.

### Part IV conclusion

Part IV is accepted when it answers the skeptical reader in sequence: medicine already reasons mechanically; routing adds a longitudinal object; tissue response supplies the biological bridge; different soft systems test the model's limits; causal priority remains plural and case-sensitive; and the lens can enter workflow without becoming a parallel diagnostic system.

## Part V — Worked mechanical narratives

### Structural judgment

**Why this Part exists.** The framework should be tested by complete examples before the book recommends new questions, education, or research. Worked narratives force system boundaries, intermediate links, alternatives, and disfavoring observations into view.

**Internal movement.** Chapter 23 begins with the comparatively tractable relationship between geometry and localized strain. Chapter 24 tests maintained constraint in a diagnostically exposed corridor. Chapter 25 combines material state, sensory control, timing, repetition, and compensation. Chapter 26 ends with a genuinely plural chronic case in which the framework may add only partial information.

**Exit state.** The reader can construct, compare, and criticize mechanical narratives rather than treating an analogy, posture, image, or modulated symptom as proof.

**Seam risks.** The cases could become four confirmations of one thesis or repeat the generic chapters. Each must introduce a different inferential difficulty and include a credible null outcome. Exact case selection is the largest remaining authorial and evidence-dependent structural decision in Book I.

### 23. Geometry and localized strain

- **Unit and decision:** `BGB-U-0023` — **accept with refinement**.
- **Unique job:** Demonstrate the full chain from task and geometry through moments, contact, local stress/strain, material state, response, findings, symptoms, and alternatives.
- **Reader movement:** **Entry:** the reader understands the general concentration model. **Exit:** the reader can apply and challenge it in a complete anatomical case.
- **Dependencies and handoff:** Tests Chapters 12 and 19 under Chapter 21's causal contract; gives Chapter 24 a clear contrast between localized concentration and maintained constraint.
- **Refinement or open point:** Confirm the leading system. The current lumbar motion-segment proposal is coherent, but a knee or hip may offer cleaner measurement and less postural controversy. Whichever leads, the second region should test transfer rather than repeat it.

### 24. Thoracic outlet: a soft structure held under constraint

- **Unit and decision:** `BGB-U-0024` — **accept with refinement**.
- **Unique job:** Test the negative hard/soft model in one complete corridor narrative, beginning with maintaining demand and separating deformation, excursion or lumen, function, symptoms, and diagnosis.
- **Reader movement:** **Entry:** the reader understands the generic model and cross-system comparison. **Exit:** the reader can see exactly where a thoracic-outlet account is mechanically strong, medically conditional, or unresolved.
- **Dependencies and handoff:** Tests Chapters 13, 19–21; contrasts with Chapter 25's non-obstructive repeated-demand problem.
- **Refinement or open point:** Objective aTOS is selected as a bounded evidence anchor using a source-grounded composite, with repeated deformation → wall injury explicitly reconstructed. Thrombotic vTOS supplies a compact retained-state timeline; tendon–SSCT relative glide supplies the non-lumen transfer; neurogenic TOS remains an evidence-boundary contrast. These distinctions prevent evidence pooling rather than making subtype taxonomy the chapter's thesis. The controlled-salvage sheet authorizes bounded first-draft use; medical-coauthor approval, publication-anatomy review, and final figure rights remain open publication gates.

### 25. Instability, repeated demand, and changing capacity

- **Unit and decision:** `BGB-U-0025` — **accept with refinement**.
- **Unique job:** Show how one presentation can combine material injury, functional instability, sensory change, timing, behavior, repetition, and compensatory redistribution.
- **Reader movement:** **Entry:** recurrent instability appears to be the same mechanical event repeated. **Exit:** each event changes the state receiving the next demand, and structural, control, experiential, and contextual explanations remain distinguishable.
- **Dependencies and handoff:** Tests Chapters 10, 14, 15, 19, and 21; prepares Chapter 26's longer and less identifiable chronology.
- **Refinement or open point:** Recurrent lateral ankle instability is the strongest present candidate because it offers measurable and null comparison groups. Confirm that it can support the proximal/whole-body routing question without overstating a kinetic chain.

### 26. Silent compensation in a mixed chronic presentation

- **Unit and decision:** `BGB-U-0026` — **accept with refinement**.
- **Unique job:** Test whether load history and compensation add useful information in a chronic, multicausal presentation without claiming a singular hidden mechanism.
- **Reader movement:** **Entry:** chronic ambiguity invites either mechanical overstory or mechanical dismissal. **Exit:** the reader can reconstruct chronology, preserve plurality, and allow the routing model to contribute little, partly, or substantially.
- **Dependencies and handoff:** Integrates Chapters 10, 15–16, 19, and 21–22; hands Part VI a framework that has survived its hardest test.
- **Refinement or open point:** Decide whether to retain the office-worker/low-back vignette or choose a case with richer longitudinal evidence. The final case must not be engineered so that every observation confirms the framework.

### Part V conclusion

Part V should end by comparing the four narratives side by side: what was observed, what was inferred, which failure family led, what biological link mattered, what alternatives remained, and what would have disfavored the account. That comparison is the bridge to Part VI's reusable questions and research program.

## Part VI — Reasoning, prevention, development, and the wider hypothesis

### Structural judgment

**Why this Part exists.** The book needs to convert its tested framework into a bounded present contribution while preserving larger educational, research, clinical, and series ambitions at their proper maturity.

**Internal movement.** Chapter 27 compresses the method into non-diagnostic questions. Chapter 28 distinguishes literacy, environment, and prevention claims. Chapter 29 turns ambition into separable development tracks. Chapter 30 states the foundation, discloses the preferred-configuration hypothesis, and hands the series to Book II.

**Exit state.** The reader knows what can be used now, what must be studied, what is deliberately deferred, and why the stronger proposition belongs in the next book rather than inside the foundation just completed.

**Seam risks.** Part VI could feel like an appendix of checklists and programs, or Chapter 30 could overwhelm the ending with later-book detail. Keep the reader-facing movement primary: better questions → shared literacy and environments → disciplined development → open horizon.

### 27. Mechanical questions and observations in clinical reasoning

- **Unit and decision:** `BGB-U-0027` — **accept with refinement**.
- **Unique job:** Compress the full Book I method into a memorable, non-diagnostic sequence of questions about demand, configuration, role, route, exposure, compensation, capacity, change, measurement, and uncertainty.
