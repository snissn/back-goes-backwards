# Book I Pilot Source-Salvage Map

**Status:** Provisional source inventory for pilot drafting
**Scope:** `BGB-U-0002`, `BGB-U-0013`, and `BGB-U-0024`
**Source state:** The archive has been inspected for recoverable concepts and provenance. No archived passage, equation, citation, or generated case has been approved as manuscript authority or evidence.

## Purpose

This map identifies what the three Book I pilot chapters can recover from the repository without allowing an older outline, generated run, or contaminated build artifact to override the current book contract.

It is deliberately generous about intent. An old passage may preserve an important authorial observation even when its terminology, causal order, mathematical form, clinical confidence, or proposed intervention cannot survive. In that case the concept remains visible here and the unsafe expression is quarantined rather than allowed to stand in for the concept.

This map is not:

- an evidence review;
- a claim verdict;
- a prose-selection sheet;
- permission to copy generated prose into the manuscript;
- or a replacement for the claim, model, example, and figure registries.

Its immediate use is narrower: give a researcher or drafter an exact retrieval path, state what is being recovered, connect it to the pilot control records, and name the work required before the material can enter a chapter.

## Disposition vocabulary

Each salvage item receives one or more of the following dispositions.

| Disposition | Meaning in this map |
|---|---|
| **reuse with editing** | The content is aligned closely enough with the current control documents to be rewritten into the pilot, subject to its stated evidence and model gates. This does not mean verbatim reuse. |
| **conceptual source** | The source preserves a substantive idea, distinction, sequence, anatomy prompt, or organizing model. The expression must be rebuilt from current definitions and evidence. |
| **provenance lead** | The source helps establish where an idea entered or how it developed, but should not shape reader-facing wording by itself. |
| **incompatible wording** | The underlying idea remains protected, but the source fuses levels, reverses causal order, overstates clinical certainty, moralizes anatomy, invents specificity, or otherwise conflicts with current boundaries. |
| **pipeline contamination** | Editorial metadata, prompt instructions, generated extrapolation, or mechanically duplicated output was rendered as if it were book content. It may reveal intent but is not prose. |

`Incompatible wording` never means “delete the idea.” It means retrieve the kernel through the linked current claim or model and rewrite it at the appropriate epistemic level.

## Recovery findings

1. **The pilot has not lost its central authorial ideas.** The hard/soft division of labor, candidate soft-to-hard handoff, load-path interruption and substitution, maintained deformation, lost excursion, vessel and nerve constraint, and thoracic-outlet example all have traceable antecedents in the `new` outlines and legacy Markdown.
2. **The most important refinement is newer than much of the archive.** The current project-model memo correctly distinguishes a sustaining mechanical relation from “narrowing” as a complete extrinsic cause. Older corridor material should feed that refinement, not reverse it.
3. **The later Part III JSON is among the archive’s strongest editorial sources.** Its mechanism-first organization, tiered claims, exclusions, and TOS-as-context framing should be preserved. Its rendered Markdown is nevertheless pipeline contamination because editorial controls became headings.
4. **Chapter 2 recovers breadth, not trustworthy equations.** The archive wants force and moment, pressure, stress and strain, time, energy, prestress, glide, and multicarrier pathways in one grammar. The old coupled-motion and disc-spring passages do not provide a valid formal basis for that grammar.
5. **Generated prose is useful only as an image-and-friction detector.** It shows which metaphors recur—bridge, cable, container, corridor, gateway, hose—and where the generator systematically turns a hypothesis into certainty. No selected generated passage contains the claim-level citations needed for promotion.
6. **Thoracic outlet remains a good stress test, with objective aTOS selected as its evidence anchor.** The archive repeatedly chooses the region because surrounding structure and compliant neurovascular tissues meet in a dynamic corridor. The focused comparison supplies a narrow arterial anchor and source-grounded composite design while preserving evidence separation, a negative comparison, and explicit uncertainty about cumulative exposure.
7. **No concept needs to be deleted to enforce these boundaries.** Stronger posterior, systemic, breath, TCM, and preferred-configuration claims can remain in the series program or quarantine. Their presence in the archive does not require them to carry Book I’s pilot argument.

## Unresolved conflicts and decisions

The following decisions block or materially shape promotion from this map:

1. **Operational hard/soft distinction:** What observable beyond ordinary stiffness and load sharing will distinguish a hard role, a soft role, and substitution at a defined scale? This is the key re-entry question for `SAL-13-01` and `SAL-13-02`.
2. **Candidate handoff status:** Is fascia or muscle → tendon or ligament → bone or joint a recurring anatomical example, a first-order model for selected tasks, or a general proposition? It must not remain an unlabeled universal sequence.
3. **Force-line operationalization:** Does Chapter 2 introduce all three senses immediately, or reserve the embodied sense for a later book? If all remain, the figures and prose need distinct conventions.
4. **Mathematical depth:** Should Chapter 2 contain only system boundaries, dimensions, and qualitative free bodies, or one worked numerical example? The answer determines evidence and specialist-review burden.
5. **Non-lumen transfer review:** Flexor tendon–SSCT relative glide is selected for `BGB-X-0004`; anatomical, measurement, and figure review remain open, and median-nerve excursion remains the reserve.
6. **Thoracic-outlet evidence-anchor review:** Objective aTOS is selected; medical review must confirm the narrow teaching job and its definition/referral limits.
7. **Thoracic-outlet case provenance:** The focused pack identifies a source-grounded composite architecture, not a publication-ready patient case. Every fact and arrow still needs a final provenance table and clinical review.
8. **Negative comparison approval:** `BGB-X-0007` is provisionally specified as healthy provocative arterial waveform change and bony variation without aTOS; final source extraction and figure design remain open.
9. **Pressure-flow scope:** The hose analogy is useful for maintaining relations, but “narrower means less flow” is not portable without boundary conditions, compliance, regulation, network response, and the selected outcome. The biological transfer must remain downstream of system-specific evidence.
10. **Neural vocabulary:** “Neural flow,” “blocked signal,” and the wire/current analogy must be replaced with specific variables such as contact, strain, bending, excursion, intraneural physiology, conduction, function, and symptoms.
11. **Older coupled-motion model:** Its kernel should remain visible as deferred Book II work, but it should not be repaired inside the Book I pilot merely because Chapter 2 teaches three-dimensional mechanics.

## Recommended retrieval sequence for pilot drafting

For each pilot, use this map in the following order:

1. Read the current chapter brief and the linked claims, models, and examples.
2. Read the **reuse with editing** items to recover the current authorial formulation.
3. Read the **conceptual source** items to restore breadth, anatomy, metaphors, and earlier questions.
4. Read the paired **incompatible wording** and quarantine entries before drafting so the protected kernel is not confused with its superseded expression.
5. Complete the evidence pack and unresolved selection decisions.
6. Draft from the accepted claim and model records, not by editing an old or generated passage in place.
7. Run a provenance check after drafting: every distinctive formulation should identify whether it is author-direct, editorial synthesis, external evidence, a bounded analogy, or generated phrasing rewritten under control.

This sequence makes salvage additive: the archive helps the new chapters become richer and more faithful without regaining authority over their causal order, mathematical discipline, clinical scope, or publication language.
