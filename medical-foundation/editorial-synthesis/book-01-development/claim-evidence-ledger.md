# Book I Claim and Evidence Ledger

**Status:** Pilot vertical slice for structural and research review
**Scope:** `BGB-U-0002`, `BGB-U-0013`, and `BGB-U-0024`
**Evidence state:** Questions and required evidence types are mapped; no claim has yet passed a claim-level evidence review.

## Purpose

This ledger tests the claim-development method on three chapters with different burdens:

- `BGB-U-0002` establishes the ordinary mechanical language on which the book depends;
- `BGB-U-0013` develops the project's distinctive model of constraint-maintained deformation and lost excursion; and
- `BGB-U-0024` asks whether that model can illuminate a named clinical context without turning a candidate mechanism into a diagnosis or treatment claim.

The records preserve direct and inherited formulations even when their publishable wording must become narrower or more exact. Evidence review determines scope, posture, confidence, and publication language. It does not silently remove the protected authorial hypothesis.

The `BGB-C` identifiers below are stable provisional claim IDs. Confidence and exposure are editorial priors, not completed verdicts.

## Record conventions

- **Raw form** retains the source language or its closest recoverable formulation.
- **Normalized statement** is the proposition that can be researched without importing unintended implications.
- **Scope** identifies the system boundary, scale, conditions, and relevant timescale.
- **Bridge role** locates the claim within the chapter's causal or explanatory chain.
- **Evidence question** states what the review must actually decide.
- **Required evidence** names appropriate evidence classes without treating them as evidence already found.
- **Alternatives and disfavoring observations** identify what could explain the same observation or weaken the proposed interpretation.
- **Publication role** describes what the claim is allowed to do if it survives at its present level.

## `BGB-U-0002` — A working language of load

### BGB-C-0001 — Load is an umbrella term

- **Raw form:** “Load should remain an umbrella term for now, but it cannot ultimately be reduced to a single scalar.”
- **Provenance:** `project-model` from the project-model memo §7; aligned with `BGB-S-1208` and the synthesized chapter outline.
- **Normalized statement:** In Book I, *load* is a convenient umbrella for mechanically different quantities and histories. Any precise argument must identify the relevant force, moment, pressure, stress, strain, deformation, rate, or accumulated exposure rather than treating load as one universal scalar.
- **Scope:** Terminology across whole-body, segment, tissue, and pressure-bearing systems; no particular anatomy or health outcome.
- **Bridge role:** Prevents the book's foundational term from carrying conclusions that belong to a defined quantity and scale.
- **Posture / prior / exposure:** FND · E2 · M0.
- **Evidence question:** Which definitions and distinctions are standard across mechanics, continuum mechanics, and biomechanics, and where does biological usage of *load* legitimately remain broader?
- **Required evidence:** Authoritative mechanics and biomechanics textbooks or standards; terminology used in representative experimental methods; dimensional review of every later quantitative passage.
- **Alternatives and disfavoring observations:** A field may use *load* as a precisely defined local term; that does not invalidate the umbrella usage but requires the local definition to control. Any passage that compares unlike quantities as though they share units would disfavor its present formulation.
- **Gates:** DEF, FORM, EVID.
- **Publication role:** A reader-facing vocabulary rule, not a novel scientific claim.

### BGB-C-0002 — System boundary comes first

- **Raw form:** “Choose the system before describing the force.”
- **Provenance:** `editorial-synthesis` in `BGB-U-0002`, grounded in standard free-body reasoning.
- **Normalized statement:** A mechanical account must identify the body or subsystem under analysis and the interactions crossing its boundary before labeling forces as external, internal, supported, or transmitted.
- **Scope:** Whole person and object, person, body segment, joint, tissue region, vessel wall, or other explicitly bounded system; quasi-static and dynamic cases must be distinguished.
- **Bridge role:** Establishes the first step in every free-body diagram and prevents forces from changing meaning when the scale changes.
- **Posture / prior / exposure:** FND · E2 · M0.
- **Evidence question:** What minimum boundary and interaction information makes the recurring object-transfer example mechanically complete at each chosen scale?
- **Required evidence:** Standard statics, dynamics, and biomechanics methods; expert review of the pilot diagrams; worked calculations where numerical examples are used.
- **Alternatives and disfavoring observations:** Different valid boundaries can answer different questions. A model is weakened if its named force disappears, reverses category, or is counted twice when the boundary is redrawn.
- **Gates:** DEF, FORM, VIS.
- **Publication role:** Method rule used throughout the series.

### BGB-C-0003 — Mechanical quantities remain distinct

- **Raw form:** “Force, moment, torque, pressure, stress, strain, strain rate, and deformation” must not be collapsed into one meaning of load.
- **Provenance:** `project-model` and `editorial-synthesis`; source obligation `BGB-S-1208` preserves levers, moments, tension, compression, and force paths.
- **Normalized statement:** Force, moment, traction, contact pressure, fluid pressure, stress, strain, strain rate, deformation, work, power, and energy describe different mechanical objects with different units, mathematical forms, frames or reference states, and measurement methods. Moment and torque are context-sensitive names for a turning effect and require a stated point or axis.
- **Scope:** Introductory classical and continuum mechanics as applied to living structures; advanced constitutive complexity is deferred.
- **Bridge role:** Supplies the dimensional grammar needed to move from external demand to internal exposure.
- **Posture / prior / exposure:** FND · E2 · M0.
- **Evidence question:** Are all definitions, symbols, units, diagrams, and examples dimensionally consistent and appropriate for biological material?
- **Required evidence:** The [Chapter 2 definitions and notation pack](evidence/chapter-02-definitions-and-notation.md); authoritative reference definitions; dimensional audit; specialist review of traction, pressure, stress-tensor, strain, work, power, and energy examples.
- **Alternatives and disfavoring observations:** Terminology varies by discipline, especially *moment* versus *torque* and engineering versus true strain. Variants may coexist if explicitly defined. A dimensionally inconsistent equation or a pressure treated as a force without area would fail the gate.
- **Gates:** DEF, FORM, EVID, VIS.
- **Publication role:** Foundational definitions and error control.

### BGB-C-0004 — Demand and tissue exposure are different levels

- **Raw form:** “A backpack's weight, a hip-joint contact force, pressure in a vessel, tensile stress in a tendon, and deformation of a nerve are not the same quantity.”
- **Provenance:** `editorial-synthesis` in `BGB-U-0002`; consistent with the project-model memo's four-level working definition of load.
- **Normalized statement:** External task demand, segmental or joint resultants, tissue stress and strain fields, and biological response occupy related but non-equivalent levels. A defensible account must state or model the intermediate transformations rather than infer local exposure directly from an external load or visible posture.
- **Scope:** Cross-scale explanatory method; applies to the recurring object transfer and later worked narratives.
- **Bridge role:** The core scale bridge: task → configuration and contacts → internal resultants → local exposure.
- **Posture / prior / exposure:** FND/SYN · E2 · M0.
- **Evidence question:** For each pilot example, which intermediate quantities are measured, modeled, inferred, or unknown?
- **Required evidence:** Multiscale biomechanics methods; validated computational or experimental models for any numerical case; uncertainty analysis for inferred quantities.
- **Alternatives and disfavoring observations:** Some simple systems permit a close analytical relation, while active biological systems often admit multiple solutions. A one-to-one mapping from external demand or appearance to local strain without boundary conditions would disfavor the claim application.
- **Gates:** DEF, FORM, EVID, LINK, MEAS, VIS.
- **Publication role:** Governing causal-order rule for Book I.

### BGB-C-0005 — Mechanical dose is multidimensional and historical

- **Raw form:** “Dose is magnitude plus direction, rate, repetition, variability, and time,” with recovery and capacity changing what the same demand means.
- **Provenance:** `editorial-synthesis`; source obligations `BGB-S-1105`, `BGB-S-1110`, `BGB-S-1117`, and `BGB-S-1218`.
- **Normalized statement:** A useful description of mechanical exposure may require magnitude, direction, rate, duration, repetition, variability, sequence, rest, and recovery context. Their relevance and weighting depend on the tissue, task, state, and outcome under study.
- **Scope:** Mechanical exposure over time; not a universal dose equation and not yet a threshold model for injury or health.
- **Bridge role:** Connects an instantaneous mechanical description to capacity, cumulative exposure, and the later failure-family chapters.
- **Posture / prior / exposure:** FND/SYN · E2 · M1.
- **Evidence question:** Which dose dimensions predict measured response for each tissue and task, and which can be omitted without losing explanatory value?
- **Required evidence:** Tissue- and task-specific experimental loading studies; longitudinal exposure studies; fatigue and recovery models; measurement-validity reviews.
- **Alternatives and disfavoring observations:** A peak or average measure may dominate in a bounded context; variability can be beneficial, neutral, or harmful rather than inherently protective. Failure to improve prediction beyond a simpler measure would disfavor a more elaborate descriptor for that context.
- **Gates:** DEF, EVID, LINK, ALT, MEAS.
- **Publication role:** Conditional exposure framework, not a universal injury formula.

### BGB-C-0006 — A load path is an explanatory representation

- **Raw form:** “Define a load path as an explanatory representation of how demand is carried and transferred through a chosen system—not a literal pipe hidden in the body.”
- **Provenance:** `editorial-synthesis` aligned with `project-model`; source obligations `BGB-S-1104`, `BGB-S-1208`, `BGB-S-1214`, and `BGB-S-1216`.
- **Normalized statement:** A load path is a scale- and task-specific representation of how demand is distributed and resolved through contacts, compression, tension, pressure, active control, and other identified carriers within a chosen system.
- **Scope:** Conceptual and model-based routing descriptions; the degree to which a path is unique, continuous, or directly measurable must be stated case by case.
- **Bridge role:** Makes routing inspectable while preventing the metaphor from being mistaken for a single anatomical conduit.
- **Posture / prior / exposure:** SYN · E2 · M0.
- **Evidence question:** What quantities, carriers, and measurements justify drawing a path rather than a diffuse field or multiple parallel routes?
- **Required evidence:** Free-body analysis; contact and tissue-force estimation; pressure or strain measurement where relevant; sensitivity analysis across plausible routes.
- **Alternatives and disfavoring observations:** Demand may be distributed across many changing carriers with no privileged path. A drawn route unsupported by force balance, tissue capacity, or measured response should remain only a heuristic.
- **Gates:** DEF, FORM, EVID, ALT, MEAS, VIS.
- **Publication role:** Organizing representation with visible limitations.

### BGB-C-0007 — Force line has several controlled senses

- **Raw form:** “Force line” can name an external line of action, an inferred internal route, or an embodied internal connection; it can also capture a Yin relation among distant regions.
- **Provenance:** `author-direct` in `BGB-S-9005`; elaborated in the project-model memo §7; literalized formulations protected by `BGB-Q-0016`.
- **Normalized statement:** *Force line* is retained as project vocabulary for three distinguishable objects: an external line of action, an inferred internal multicarrier mechanical relation, or an embodied perception that motivates a mechanical hypothesis. Reader-facing use must state which sense is intended.
- **Scope:** Vocabulary and hypothesis generation across mechanical and embodied descriptions; no assertion of one anatomical cable or conserved whole-body resultant.
- **Bridge role:** Preserves the author's internal model while preventing three evidentiary objects from being silently fused.
- **Posture / prior / exposure:** SYN/HYP · E1 · M0.
- **Evidence question:** For inferred-internal and embodied senses, what carrier, quantity, scale, and observation could connect the experience or diagram to a mechanical model?
- **Required evidence:** Provenance review; task-specific biomechanics; measurement-design work; studies of embodied perception only where perceptual claims are made.
- **Alternatives and disfavoring observations:** The felt connection may be perceptual, attentional, or coordinative without a unique internal force route. Lack of repeatability or lack of correspondence with candidate mechanical measurements would weaken a literal mechanical interpretation without erasing the observation.
- **Gates:** INTENT, PROV, DEF, FORM, EVID, ALT, MEAS, VIS.
- **Publication role:** Controlled project term; its embodied sense is labeled as hypothesis-generating.

## `BGB-U-0003` — Hard and soft mechanical roles

### BGB-C-0009 — Hard and soft describe a relational division of labor

- **Raw form:** “Load wants to travel through the hard body so that the soft body can remain soft.” In adverse organization, load can “go into” a soft structure and hold it as a boundary.
- **Provenance:** `author-direct` and `project-model`; motivated by traditional Chinese medicine's hard/soft and Yin/Yang concepts without claiming definitional identity; protected kernel in `BGB-Q-0009`; inherited handoff and “load leak” obligations `BGB-S-1214`, `BGB-S-1217`, and `BGB-S-1219`.
- **Normalized statement:** At a stated scale, direction, task, and timescale, relatively shape-preserving structures can establish or resolve gross demand while relatively form-receiving structures remain available to conform, glide, contain, expand, and recover. A candidate failure occurs when a form-receiving structure must maintain an imposed geometry or substitute structural role at consequential stress, strain, activation, or loss of excursion.
- **Scope:** Functional roles, not fixed tissue identities; substantial loading and deformation of soft tissue can be normal and necessary.
- **Bridge role:** Defines the book's central division of mechanical labor; Chapter 13 later specializes it to the adverse substitution case without moralizing tissue, direction, or deformation.
- **Posture / prior / exposure:** SYN/HYP · E1 · M2.
- **Evidence question:** In which bounded systems does distinguishing path-establishing from form-receiving roles improve prediction of deformation, excursion, cost, or function beyond conventional tissue and load descriptions?
- **Required evidence:** Formal role definitions; comparative mechanical models; task-specific tissue-force, deformation, excursion, and outcome measurements; counterexamples where soft structures appropriately resolve gross demand.
- **Alternatives and disfavoring observations:** Soft structures routinely transmit large forces; pressure can make a compliant compartment shape-preserving; active muscle changes roles. If the polarity adds no discriminating prediction or merely renames stiffness, its publication role should narrow while its traditional and heuristic roles remain preserved.
- **Gates:** INTENT, DEF, FORM, EVID, LINK, ALT, MEAS.
- **Publication role:** Explicit organizing hypothesis, not an established universal law.

## `BGB-U-0013` — Constraint-maintained deformation and loss of excursion

### BGB-C-0008 — Maintained deformation requires a sustaining relation

- **Raw form:** “If [a compliant structure] remains narrowed, bent, tethered, or collapsed, the important mechanical question is what continues to hold it there.”
- **Provenance:** `author-direct` clarification preserved in the project-model memo §8 and normalized in `BGB-U-0013`; source obligation `BGB-S-1119`.
- **Normalized statement:** When a structure remains deformed relative to a defined reference or loses needed excursion over a defined timescale, the model must identify the sustaining boundary conditions. Those conditions may be extrinsic, intrinsic to the structure, actively maintained, history-dependent, or mixed.
- **Scope:** Generic deformable structures; reference state and timescale must be stated. Residual strain, plastic change, remodeling, adhesion, or active tone count as possible sustaining conditions rather than exceptions to be ignored.
- **Bridge role:** Opens the generic causal chain by replacing “less space” with a question about maintaining relations.
- **Posture / prior / exposure:** FND/SYN · E2 · M1.
- **Evidence question:** What constitutes persistence for the chosen structure, and which boundary conditions account for its maintained state or restricted motion?
- **Required evidence:** Constitutive and viscoelastic characterization; time-resolved deformation under controlled boundary changes; imaging or motion measures appropriate to the structure.
- **Alternatives and disfavoring observations:** Intrinsic remodeling, adhesion, wall disease, congenital geometry, active contraction, or measurement artifact may dominate. Rapid recovery after the proposed extrinsic constraint is removed supports a different account than persistence after removal.
- **Gates:** DEF, FORM, EVID, ALT, MEAS.
- **Publication role:** Generic modeling premise; not a clinical conclusion.

### BGB-C-0010 — Narrowing is not a complete extrinsic mechanism

- **Raw form:** “It is not the actual narrowing of space that's the consequential factor. It's the corresponding realignment and entrapment of strain that results from it.” The maintaining stress or strain “going into” the compliant structure is primary in the intended extrinsic case.
- **Provenance:** `author-direct` clarification normalized in the project-model memo's intrinsic/extrinsic distinction and `BGB-U-0013`.
- **Normalized statement:** In an extrinsically maintained case, reduced space, altered lumen, or changed course describes the resulting geometry but does not identify the complete mechanism. The account must first specify the surrounding contact, traction, pressure, bending, tethering, or active force that produces and maintains stress and strain in the compliant structure.
- **Scope:** Extrinsic or mixed deformation only. It does not deny that intrinsic narrowing can itself become an important boundary condition or that lumen geometry can affect flow.
- **Bridge role:** Orders the explanation: maintaining load → tissue stress and strain → changed geometry, before downstream function.
- **Posture / prior / exposure:** SYN · E2 · M2.
- **Evidence question:** Can the proposed surrounding demand and resulting tissue deformation be separately observed, manipulated, or modeled in the selected system?
- **Required evidence:** Time-resolved geometry and load measurements; contact or pressure estimates; boundary-condition manipulation; constitutive model and uncertainty analysis.
- **Alternatives and disfavoring observations:** Intrinsic stenosis, wall remodeling, mass effect, inflammation, scar, or congenital shape may explain the geometry. Unchanged deformation after credible removal of the proposed external constraint would weaken that extrinsic account.
- **Gates:** DEF, FORM, EVID, LINK, ALT, MEAS, VIS.
- **Publication role:** Mechanism-order rule for an explicitly bounded class of cases.

### BGB-C-0011 — Tube deformation can alter a pressure-flow relation

- **Raw form:** A garden hose held in a kink develops wall deformation and a smaller or altered lumen; the changed geometry then retards or otherwise changes fluid flow.
- **Provenance:** `author-direct` visual analogy, formalized in the project-model memo and `BGB-U-0013`.
- **Normalized statement:** For a deformable conduit, external contact, bending, traction, tethering, or pressure can alter wall stress, cross-sectional geometry, and course; under specified fluid and boundary conditions, those changes can alter resistance, pressure distribution, and volumetric flow.
- **Scope:** A bounded conduit model. Biological vessels are active, compliant, branching, pulsatile, regulated, and coupled to surrounding tissue; the garden hose is not a vessel model by itself.
- **Bridge role:** Provides the simplest visible demonstration of the sequence from maintained boundary load to wall deformation to altered flow relation.
- **Posture / prior / exposure:** FND/SYN · E2 · M2.
- **Evidence question:** Which conduit mechanics remain valid under the selected biological vessel's geometry, material behavior, pulsatility, regulation, and surrounding support?
- **Required evidence:** Fluid-structure mechanics; collapsible-tube and vessel studies; in vivo or validated bench measurements of geometry, pressure, and flow under controlled deformation.
- **Alternatives and disfavoring observations:** Autoregulation, collateral routes, non-Newtonian flow, active wall behavior, pressure changes, or measurement conditions may preserve or change flow in ways the hose cannot predict. A narrowing without measurable functional change limits the downstream claim.
- **Gates:** DEF, FORM, EVID, LINK, ALT, MEAS, VIS.
- **Publication role:** Bounded analogy and bridge model with explicit limits.

### BGB-C-0012 — Constraint can matter through lost excursion without a lumen

- **Raw form:** “A nerve may need to glide and accommodate limb motion; fascia and muscle need sliding relations; an airway or cavity needs to expand; an organ needs mobility within support.”
- **Provenance:** `editorial-synthesis` preserving source obligations `BGB-S-1111`, `BGB-S-1112`, `BGB-S-1119`, and `BGB-S-1223`.
- **Normalized statement:** The generic constraint model may apply to structures whose relevant freedom is excursion, glide, course change, sliding, recoil, or phase-dependent expansion rather than lumen area. The mechanically relevant variable and its functional importance must be specified for each structure.
- **Scope:** A cross-system research proposition, not a claim that all listed systems share one constitutive law or clinical consequence.
- **Bridge role:** Generalizes the maintained-deformation family beyond tubes while retaining tissue-specific branches.
- **Posture / prior / exposure:** SYN/HYP · E1 · M2.
- **Evidence question:** For each candidate structure, what excursion or deformation is normally required, how is it measured, what constrains it, and when does restriction affect function?
- **Required evidence:** The [focused non-lumen comparison](evidence/chapter-13-non-lumen-comparison.md); structure-specific anatomy and mechanics; dynamic imaging or motion measurement; experimental constraint studies; clinical association only after mechanical links are established. The selected tendon–SSCT calibration currently establishes task-dependent relative motion, not adverse maintained loss or recovery after release.
- **Alternatives and disfavoring observations:** Reduced excursion may be protective, incidental, measurement-dependent, or caused intrinsically. Normal function despite the measured restriction, or restriction that follows rather than precedes the outcome, narrows the claim.
- **Gates:** DEF, FORM, EVID, LINK, ALT, MEAS.
- **Publication role:** Named research family with examples labeled as candidates.

### BGB-C-0013 — Intrinsic, extrinsic, and mixed persistence must be separated

- **Raw form:** The project distinguishes “intrinsic or independently persistent narrowing” from “extrinsic or configuration-maintained deformation,” while allowing both to coexist.
- **Provenance:** `project-model` §8; normalized from direct authorial clarification.
- **Normalized statement:** A constrained or narrowed state may be maintained primarily by properties within the structure or corridor, primarily by surrounding configuration and demand, or by an interaction between them. These are distinct model classes that can produce similar geometry and require different observations.
- **Scope:** Generic differential model across conduits, nerves, sliding interfaces, airways, cavities, and other selected systems.
- **Bridge role:** Prevents the project's interest in extrinsic load routing from excluding intrinsic pathology or mixed causation.
- **Posture / prior / exposure:** FND/SYN · E2 · M2.
- **Evidence question:** Which manipulation or longitudinal observation can distinguish intrinsic persistence, reversible extrinsic maintenance, and interaction effects?
- **Required evidence:** Anatomy and pathology characterization; controlled positional or load changes; dynamic and resting measures; longitudinal or intervention evidence where causal direction is at issue.
- **Alternatives and disfavoring observations:** The categories may not be cleanly separable in chronic adaptation. Failure of the state to track relevant surrounding load, combined with strong intrinsic findings, weakens a primarily extrinsic account.
- **Gates:** DEF, EVID, LINK, ALT, MEAS.
- **Publication role:** Differential framework, not etiological assignment.

### BGB-C-0014 — Every downstream physiological link requires independent support

- **Raw form:** “Surrounding demand → wall or tissue stress → maintained deformation or lost excursion → changed pressure, flow, conduction, motion, or signaling → possible functional effect or symptom.”
- **Provenance:** `project-model` and `editorial-synthesis`; medical-architecture obligation `BGB-S-1301`.
- **Normalized statement:** A proposed path from configuration to health or symptoms must expose each intermediate mechanical and biological link. Evidence for one link does not establish the next, and different tissues require different constitutive, regulatory, and clinical accounts.
- **Scope:** Causal reasoning across Book I; specifically tested by Chapters 13 and 24.
- **Bridge role:** Governing rule that keeps a coherent whole-system hypothesis researchable rather than reducing it to either certainty or silence.
- **Posture / prior / exposure:** SYN · E2 · M2.
- **Evidence question:** Which links are established, conditional, plausible, unresolved, or contradicted for each selected example, and what is the weakest indispensable link?
- **Required evidence:** Claim-specific mechanical, physiological, longitudinal, and clinical evidence; triangulation across methods; explicit uncertainty and causal alternatives.
- **Alternatives and disfavoring observations:** Adaptation, autoregulation, reserve, reverse causation, common causes, asymptomatic variation, and measurement error can break or reverse links. Failure at an indispensable link prevents the full chain from carrying assertive publication language.
- **Gates:** EVID, LINK, ALT, MEAS, CLIN when a named condition is introduced.
- **Publication role:** Book-wide claim-discipline rule that preserves, rather than prejudges, higher-specificity hypotheses.

## `BGB-U-0024` — Thoracic outlet: a soft structure held under constraint

- **Alternatives and disfavoring observations:** Terminology and criteria may vary across specialties. A vignette that cannot be assigned a defensible presentation or relies only on posture and symptoms should not carry the worked causal chain.

### BGB-C-0016 — Corridor mechanics vary with anatomy, state, and task

Which factor matters must be established for the selected presentation rather than inferred from a static appearance.

- **Publication role:** Bounded anatomical-mechanical setup, not a postural diagnosis.

### BGB-C-0017 — An extrinsic thoracic-outlet account begins with imposed load

- **Raw form:** “Imposed load comes before loss of space”; the consequential mechanical event is the stress or strain maintained in the neural or vascular structure by surrounding bones and active tissues.
- **Provenance:** `author-direct` clarification applied through `BGB-U-0013` to the source thoracic-outlet candidate; protected against stronger legacy certainty by `BGB-Q-0004` and `BGB-Q-0006`.
- **Normalized statement:** For a selected extrinsic thoracic-outlet presentation, the candidate mechanism begins with identifiable contact pressure, compression, traction, bending, torsion, or tethering imposed by surrounding geometry and active demand; changed corridor or tissue geometry is an intermediate state, not the entire causal explanation.
- **Scope:** A defined presentation, task, side, structure, and timescale. It does not assert that all thoracic-outlet presentations are extrinsic or postural.
- **Bridge role:** Imports the Chapter 13 mechanism-order rule into the worked clinical narrative.
- **Posture / prior / exposure:** SYN/HYP · E1 · M3.
- **Evidence question:** Can the candidate imposed load, resulting tissue stress or deformation, and change with task or position be demonstrated in the selected presentation?
- **Required evidence:** Dynamic imaging or validated mechanical models; contact, pressure, strain, or excursion measurement; case-control and within-person task comparisons; medical-coauthor review.
- **Alternatives and disfavoring observations:** Intrinsic lesion, congenital anatomy, inflammatory or systemic neuropathy, vascular disease, distal entrapment, central mechanisms, or symptom misclassification. Lack of correspondence between proposed load, deformation, and presentation weakens this account.
- **Gates:** PROV, DEF, FORM, EVID, LINK, ALT, MEAS, CLIN, VIS.
- **Publication role:** Clearly labeled candidate mechanism for one selected presentation.

### BGB-C-0018 — A vascular account requires a tissue-specific staged chain

- **Raw form:** For vessels, distinguish “wall deformation, cross-sectional area, resistance, flow, regulation, and symptoms.”
- **Provenance:** `editorial-synthesis` preserving vascular source obligations `BGB-S-1111`, `BGB-S-1223`, and `BGB-S-1224`.
- **Normalized statement:** A vascular thoracic-outlet account must separately establish the presentation-specific sequence rather than requiring one universal wall-to-flow chain. For the arterial evidence anchor, the candidate sequence is focal anatomy and task → reconstructed repeated arterial deformation → objective wall lesion → thrombus, embolus, or occlusion where present → distal arterial or ischemic consequence → clinical presentation. A venous comparison requires its own wall, thrombotic, outflow, collateral, and temporal variables.
- **Scope:** The selected arterial or venous presentation; arterial and venous mechanics and outcomes are not interchangeable.
- **Bridge role:** Tissue-specific branch of the generic causal chain.
- **Posture / prior / exposure:** HYP/APP · E1 · M3.
- **Evidence question:** Which links are demonstrated for the selected vascular presentation, under what tasks and measurements, and which remain inferred?
- **Required evidence:** Vascular anatomy and pathophysiology; objective artery-wall and distal findings for the arterial evidence anchor; dynamic imaging, pressure, flow, or fluid-structure evidence where the claimed link requires them; diagnostic and outcome studies; clinical review.
- **Alternatives and disfavoring observations:** Intrinsic vascular disease, another embolic source, trauma, thrombosis, hypercoagulability, collateral circulation, autonomic regulation, systemic hemodynamics, incidental bony variation, and measurement artifact. The arterial chain remains incomplete where repeated deformation is rhetorically treated as observed rather than reconstructed.
- **Gates:** DEF, FORM, EVID, LINK, ALT, MEAS, CLIN, VIS.
- **Publication role:** Conditional vascular branch; no treatment recommendation.

### BGB-C-0019 — A neural account requires a staged load-to-function chain

- **Raw form:** For nerves, distinguish “strain, contact pressure, excursion, intraneural physiology, conduction, and sensation.”
- **Provenance:** `editorial-synthesis` preserving neural source obligations `BGB-S-1112`, `BGB-S-1119`, `BGB-S-1223`, and `BGB-S-1224`.
- **Normalized statement:** A neural thoracic-outlet account must separately establish imposed contact, traction, bending, or tethering; neural strain or restricted excursion; any intraneural physiological or conduction change; functional effect; and relation to symptoms or examination findings.
- **Scope:** The selected neural presentation and task; neural mechanics, neurophysiology, pain, and diagnosis remain distinct levels.
- **Bridge role:** Tissue-specific branch of the generic causal chain.
- **Posture / prior / exposure:** HYP/APP · E1 · M3.
- **Evidence question:** Which mechanical and neurophysiological links are demonstrated in the selected presentation, and which observations distinguish them from other neuropathic or pain mechanisms?
- **Required evidence:** Peripheral-nerve mechanics and physiology; dynamic excursion or strain measurement; electrodiagnostic and functional studies where appropriate; diagnostic reviews and clinical assessment.
- **Alternatives and disfavoring observations:** Distal entrapment, cervical source, generalized neuropathy, sensitization, vascular mechanisms, central or psychosocial contributors, and measurement error. Symptoms without a corresponding corridor-specific mechanical or physiological pattern limit the chain.
- **Gates:** DEF, FORM, EVID, LINK, ALT, MEAS, CLIN, VIS.
- **Publication role:** Conditional neural branch; no diagnostic shortcut or treatment recommendation.

### BGB-C-0020 — Corridor states may be dynamic, retained, or mixed

- **Raw form:** Tissue elasticity, internal or transmural pressure, fluid flow, muscle activity, and movement may resist or alter the deformation imposed by surrounding demand.
- **Provenance:** `author-direct` hose clarification and `editorial-synthesis` in `BGB-U-0024`.
- **Normalized statement:** A thoracic-outlet state may be dynamically recreated by current boundary conditions, retained through wall injury, thrombus, scar, or other material change, or mixed. Current geometry must therefore be separated from the initiating exposure history and from the state that now maintains the finding.
- **Scope:** Dynamic and path-dependent state model; specific variables differ for arterial, venous, and neural branches.
- **Bridge role:** Explains why the same anatomy can behave differently across time and tasks and why an objective retained lesion need not reveal its initiating history.
- **Posture / prior / exposure:** SYN/HYP · E2 · M3.
- **Evidence question:** Which current terms maintain or modify the observed state, which initiating exposures are observed or reconstructed, and on what timescale did the system move from reversible to retained or mixed behavior?
- **Required evidence:** Dynamic and positional measurement where relevant; material and pathological characterization; chronology; repeated-task data; retained-state imaging; validated simulations where direct measurement is unavailable.
- **Alternatives and disfavoring observations:** A fixed lesion, intrinsic obstruction, trauma, or systemic process may dominate and make the proposed dynamic exposure secondary or incidental. Present-state variation cannot by itself recover causal history.
- **Gates:** DEF, FORM, EVID, ALT, MEAS, CLIN, VIS.
- **Publication role:** Explanatory state model that keeps the analogy biologically realistic.

### BGB-C-0021 — Geometry, physiology, symptoms, and diagnosis are non-equivalent

- **Raw form:** “None follows merely from a photograph of a narrow corridor.”
- **Provenance:** `editorial-synthesis`, consistent with the Book I recurring causal chain and source guardrails `BGB-S-1301`, `BGB-S-1307`, and `BGB-S-1313`.
- **Normalized statement:** A corridor measurement, tissue deformation, physiological change, symptom report, examination finding, and clinical diagnosis are separate observations. No one of them, by itself, establishes the others or the direction of causation.
- **Scope:** Thoracic-outlet worked narrative and later clinical examples.
- **Bridge role:** Prevents the final clinical inference from being imported into an earlier mechanical observation.
- **Posture / prior / exposure:** FND/SYN · E2 · M3.
- **Evidence question:** What combination of observations and comparison groups supports each inference in the selected presentation?
- **Required evidence:** Diagnostic-accuracy and reliability studies; asymptomatic comparisons; longitudinal or manipulation evidence; clinical consensus and medical-coauthor review.
- **Alternatives and disfavoring observations:** Incidental anatomy, asymptomatic deformation, fluctuating symptoms, reverse causation, and multiple symptom generators. High prevalence of the proposed marker in asymptomatic populations weakens its diagnostic specificity without making the mechanics irrelevant.
- **Gates:** DEF, EVID, LINK, ALT, MEAS, CLIN.
- **Publication role:** Explicit clinical inference boundary.

### BGB-C-0022 — Intrinsic, extrinsic, and nonmechanical accounts may coexist

- **Raw form:** Compare surrounding configuration with “intrinsic wall disease, scar, congenital geometry, mass effect, inflammation,” and other changes within the structure or corridor; mixed cases are possible.
- **Provenance:** `editorial-synthesis` extending the project-model intrinsic/extrinsic distinction; aligns with Book I's whole-person boundary.
- **Normalized statement:** A thoracic-outlet presentation may include extrinsic mechanical constraint, intrinsic neural or vascular change, anatomical variation, other regional lesions, systemic disease, behavioral or environmental exposure, and symptom-modulating biological, psychological, and social factors in varying combinations.
- **Scope:** Differential explanation, not a complete diagnostic list and not an assertion that every factor applies to every presentation.
- **Bridge role:** Keeps the selected mechanical hypothesis inside a plural causal model and defines the comparisons it must survive.
- **Posture / prior / exposure:** SYN · E2 · M3.
- **Evidence question:** Which plausible alternatives and interactions must be assessed for the selected presentation, and what observations change their relative support?
- **Required evidence:** Current differential-diagnosis sources; condition-specific reviews; comorbidity and natural-history evidence; clinical and medical-coauthor review.
- **Alternatives and disfavoring observations:** The alternatives are the content of the claim. Stronger support for another primary process should lower the proposed mechanical account's role from primary to contributory, modifying, consequential, or incidental without forcing a universal rank scheme across cases.
- **Gates:** PROV, DEF, EVID, LINK, ALT, MEAS, CLIN.
- **Publication role:** Alternatives section and boundary on causal priority.
