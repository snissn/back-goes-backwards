# *The Back Goes Backwards*, Book I: Medical Foundations

**Status:** Provisional synthesized outline

## Editorial role

Book I establishes the body as a living biomechanical system and develops load routing as a medically useful dimension of reasoning. It introduces hard and soft as a division of mechanical labor, uses posterior support as its first major anatomical example, follows ordinary demand into adverse mechanical exposure and possible biological consequence, and ends by naming the preferred-configuration hypothesis that motivates the wider series.

The book preserves strong propositions as explicit hypotheses and research programs. Confidence, medical exposure, and readiness determine the work a proposition must survive; they do not determine whether it remains visible.

## Provisional thesis

> The human body is a living biomechanical system. Its tissues and structures continuously receive, distribute, transform, transmit, and dissipate forces and moments while managing pressure and deformation over time. Clinical reasoning can improve when load history, load distribution, and body-wide load routing are considered alongside biological, psychological, social, developmental, and environmental factors.

The book's compact mechanical intuition is:

> Load wants to travel through the hard body so that the soft body can remain soft.

### 3. Hard and soft mechanical roles

Unit BGB-U-0003 · state proposed · posture SYN/HYP · confidence E1 · medical M2 · readiness R1
Tags domain:biomechanics, model:hard-soft, mech:load-path, mech:substitution, use:organizing-model · gates INTENT, DEF, FORM, EVID, ALT, MEAS

Introduce hard and soft as a functional polarity and division of mechanical labor. A hard role is relatively shape-preserving, force-transmitting, or structurally resolving at the scale under discussion. A soft role is relatively form-receiving and shape-conforming: it yields rather than imposing geometry, enabling containment, glide, flow, pressure response, buffering, and local shape change. Preserve fascia or muscle to tendon or ligament to bone or joint as a first-order candidate handoff while allowing tissues to exchange roles with direction, state, scale, and task.

**Survival work:** Reconcile the functional-role model with the proposed tissue-pattern handoff and identify observations that distinguish capable routing from chronic substitution without assuming a universal tissue sequence.
