# Reconciliation of the `new` JSON Outline Archive

**Status:** Delta review packet 0.2

**Date:** 2026-07-10

**Scope:** `medical-foundation/new/inputs/outlines/11-section-01.json` through `19-appendix-biomechanical-diagnoses.json`

**Purpose:** Recover the substantial architecture preserved in the `new` JSON outlines without treating their generated wording, certainty, or protocols as settled book content.

**Frozen comparison baseline:** Commit `ac06691`. During this audit, the project-model memo and provisional series outline remain unchanged. This document records differences for review; it does not silently resolve them.

## 1. Editorial verdict

The `new` outlines should be promoted in status as a **structural source archive**.

They are not authoritative scientific prose, and many descriptions turn provisional ideas into confident medical or practical claims. But low claim authority does not imply low structural value. Across Parts I–VI, the outlines preserve a developed sequence of questions, taxonomies, examples, audience transitions, and future-work branches that the first editorial synthesis compressed too aggressively.

The appropriate reconciliation is therefore neither to restore the old book wholesale nor to continue reducing it to a small set of abstractions. It is to separate two judgments:

1. **Structural value:** Does this material preserve a useful order of explanation, category system, example family, research question, or pedagogical progression?
2. **Claim maturity:** Is the accompanying explanation established, defensible with revision, speculative, internally conflicting, or unsuitable for the present book?

A passage can be structurally important while its current claims require complete rewriting. That is the dominant pattern in the archive.

## 2. Disposition vocabulary

- **Promote:** Restore as a Part-, chapter-, or major-section-level obligation.
- **Integrate:** Preserve within a chapter or worked example, with new language and evidence.
- **Inventory:** Retain as a candidate-example or research bank without committing it to the manuscript.
- **Defer:** Preserve for a later book in the series.
- **Reformulate:** Keep the intended question or intuition while replacing the current ontology, terminology, or causal statement.
- **Do not import:** Exclude the existing wording or instruction from publishable prose; this does not necessarily reject the underlying research question.

### Crosswalk classifications

The granular audit below uses four mutually exclusive classifications. They describe the relationship between a source obligation and the frozen synthesis, not the truth of the source claim.

- **F — Faithfully represented:** The source obligation has a visible destination at suitable depth.
- **C — Present but over-compressed:** The central idea is present, but a useful sequence, distinction, example family, audience transition, or research obligation has become difficult to see.
- **M — Missing from the current outline:** A meaningful source obligation has no visible destination. This is a review flag, not an instruction to restore it.
- **D/R — Intentionally deferred or reformulated:** The source has been preserved in a later book, internal inventory, or revised formulation because its original placement, certainty, ontology, or practical form does not fit the current Book I architecture.

The crosswalk groups repetitive leaf headings when they perform one editorial job. Named examples remain visible as inventories even when they do not each receive a manuscript destination. Planning keys such as “Job,” “Inputs / Outputs,” and “Acceptance criteria” are treated as chapter-specification material rather than reader-facing content.

## 3. Section-by-section reconciliation

### Section 1 — Mechanics, dysfunction, and disease

#### Structure worth recovering

Section 1 contains three strong architectures that should remain visible in Book I:

1. **Integrated living structure:** skeletal geometry, joints, ligamentous restraint, active muscle, compliant tissues, pressure-bearing spaces, and connective interfaces participate in one changing mechanical system.
2. **A transition from ordinary load sharing to breakdown:** geometry and alignment affect available paths; repeated or poorly distributed demand produces local concentration; compensation can preserve function while changing future exposure.
3. **A failure-family taxonomy:**
   - axial or global geometric change;
   - loss of segmental control and instability;
   - force concentration and degenerative progression;
   - discoordination and sequencing failure;
   - impingement, tethering, and constrained space;
   - kinetic-chain distortion and compensation.

These six families are more valuable than the outline's long disease list. They should be promoted into Book I's explanation of load mismanagement and retained as one axis of a candidate-example matrix.

The section also preserves useful scale changes: local tissue stress, regional compensation, body-wide reorganization, modern exposure contexts, and medical/research implications. Book I should make those scale changes explicit rather than moving directly from local mechanics to four examples.

#### Material to reformulate

- Replace fixed hard-tissue/soft-tissue classes with the functional hard/soft roles in the project memo.
- Replace “optimal alignment” and “mechanical neutrality” with task-, anatomy-, capacity-, and scale-specific geometry.
- Treat tensegrity as one possible illustrative model, not the body's definitive architecture.
- Treat fascial continuity, baseline tone, glide, and densification as separable anatomical and biological questions rather than one proven system-wide mechanism.
- Replace inevitable cascades from small asymmetry to systemic disease with conditional pathways from configuration and demand to exposure, compensation, and possible consequence.
- Preserve circulation, lymphatic, neural, pressure, and energetic-cost questions, but trace each intermediate mechanical and biological link separately.

#### Material not to import into Book I as written

- deterministic mechanical origins for broad chronic or systemic disease;
- disease reversal through realignment or posterior loading;
- posture as a universal root cause;
- protocols, population screening programs, or claims that a complete medical paradigm is already validated.

### Section 2 — Hard/soft roles and posterior support

#### Structure worth recovering

Section 2 contains the clearest earlier explanation of why the hard/soft principle and posterior chain matter to the project. Its strongest sequence is:

1. redefine the “hard body” as structural capacity rather than visible muscular hardness;
2. identify the posterior chain anatomically;
3. explain its mechanical roles in upright support, propulsion, deceleration, elastic energy management, stabilization, and transfer;
4. examine how compliant, active, tensile, and skeletal structures hand demand among one another;
5. describe failure when a capable route is interrupted and another structure substitutes for it;
6. follow posterior participation through ordinary tasks, development, aging, and changing capacity;
7. state where the model is incomplete or secondary.

The first synthesis retained the hard/soft principle but compressed too much of this progression. The frozen Book I outline now restores posterior support as a multi-chapter Part-level subject rather than an example embedded in the opening mechanics.

#### Material to reformulate

- “Soft-to-hard” should name successful resolution of demand through a capable structural path, not a literal fascia-to-muscle-to-ligament-to-bone sequence.
- Posterior structures should be described through established anatomy and task-specific mechanics before posterior preference is introduced.
- The evolutionary argument should become a researchable account of upright locomotion and human anatomy, not proof of one universal route.
- Daily function and lifespan material should describe changing demands and capacities rather than prescribe one posterior-engagement strategy for all bodies.
- Modern sedentary and technology exposures remain useful contexts, but they should be treated as changes in task, duration, variability, recovery, and available movement—not as a culture-wide diagnosis of anterior dominance.

#### Material to defer

- posterior loading as the preferred global state: Book II;
- its full Yin/Yang and hard/soft correspondence: Book III;
- posterior-loading tests, cues, and corrective programs: Book V;
- breath as a necessary driver of posterior organization: Book IV and later testing.

### Section 3 — Medical implications specification

Section 3 is the most mature editorial architecture in the `new` archive, even though its planning keys are not prose. Its sequence should govern the medical half of Book I:

1. begin where mechanics already exists in medicine;
2. state the missing body-wide load-routing question;
3. establish scope and causal rules;
4. explain why this is medically relevant upstream of rehabilitation;
5. develop cumulative demand, compensation, delayed consequence, and degeneration without discrete injury;
6. show how the lens changes questions without becoming a diagnosis;
7. end with education, prevention, and a testable research program.

The guardrails—mechanism-first, tiered claims, no protocols, and explicit objections—belong in the Book I contract and chapter briefs. They should not appear as reader-facing headings.

The three original “failure signatures”—constraint, concentration, and instability—remain useful but are no longer sufficient by themselves. The reconciled Book I should place them within a richer pattern language that also includes interruption, substitution, discoordination, compensation, restricted excursion, and scale transition.

### Section 4 — Three-dimensional structure, meridians, and bandhas

#### Structure worth recovering

Section 4 preserves several distinct programs that should not be collapsed into one claim:

- why a three-dimensional body cannot be understood only through isolated planar levers;
- the use of basis directions and local coordinate frames to decompose complex movement and load;
- separate axial, rotational, and lateral/radial modes with anatomical examples;
- Yin/Yang as proposed signed directions rather than substances;
- the grouping of the Six Divisions and paired channels by axis;
- bandhas as candidate control points, locks, or coordination strategies;
- traditional systems as records of embodied observation that require historical as well as mechanical study;
- the emergence of a coordinated whole from several dimensional controls;
- the need for diagrams, comparative mappings, testable predictions, and explicit failure conditions.

Book I needs only the first two items in generic form: three-dimensional reasoning, local frames, forces, moments, geometry, and load paths. The signed anatomical dimensions belong in Book II. The Six Divisions, channels, and historical translation belong in Book III. Bandha mechanics and breath coupling span Books III–V.

#### Conflicts to preserve rather than silently reconcile

- The axis-based Six Division map agrees with the current authorial mapping at the level of channel groups, but the old text reverses or blurs some signed directions—most clearly the lateral Yin/Yang direction.
- The later “gross” hand-versus-foot grouping is not the same model as the paired Six Division mapping. It should remain a competing historical note, not a second settled map.
- “Rotational,” “lateral,” and “longitudinal” are sometimes treated as movement axes, sometimes as directions of translation, and sometimes as body-wide organizational modes. Later formal work must distinguish these uses.
- The proposed bandha-to-axis assignments and activation sequence are higher-specificity hypotheses, not consequences of the coordinate model.

#### Material to reformulate or defer

- “The fourth” should mean integration, phase, or an additional state variable where defined—not a fourth spatial dimension supplied by the hyoid or Maha Bandha.
- “Scientific validation” of traditional maps should become comparison, translation, hypothesis generation, and testing.
- The strung-bow analogy is valuable future explanatory material, but it should not imply that the posterior body is one continuous anatomical cable.
- Diagnostic bandha failures, treatment algorithms, and movement protocols belong only in later practice work after model, evidence, and safety review.

### Section 5 — Breath and dynamic integration

Section 5 preserves a useful dynamic sequence for Book IV:

1. revisit the three modes in a breathing body;
2. examine the hyoid, jaw, tongue, airway, diaphragm, rib cage, abdomen, pelvis, and spine as coupled structures;
3. distinguish inhalation and exhalation as phases with changing pressure, volume, force, and geometry;
4. treat one phase as preparing the next rather than assuming a complete reset;
5. investigate axis transitions, spiral continuity, and candidate mechanical “dead spots”;
6. test possible effects on flow, neural excursion, organ support, movement, and performance;
7. compare traditional phenomena such as bandha, *peng*, and *fa-jing* without presuming one mechanism;
8. define measurements, limits, and competing models.

The existing claims that inhale is universally Yang, breath necessarily loads the posterior chain in a prescribed way, the hyoid is a fourth dimension, or tensegrity directly explains systemic regulation should not be imported. Their underlying questions remain central future work.

### Section 6 — Embodiment, teaching, and practice

Section 6 supplies the source architecture now carried into Book V. It preserves four separable structures:

- **individual practice:** ordinary tasks, progression, adaptation to age and history, recognition of compensation;
- **pedagogy:** awareness → engagement → integration, accessible language, imagery, visual models, tactile feedback, and staged learning;
- **professional translation:** distinct uses and boundaries in rehabilitation, yoga, Pilates, martial arts, athletics, and education;
- **responsible development:** outcome tracking, ethics, referral, case series, collaboration, public movement literacy, and revision when results fail.

The practices themselves cannot be inherited without review. The instructional and research architecture should be preserved now so that future practical work is not rebuilt from scratch.

### Appendix — Diagnoses with biomechanical components

The empty appendix contributes one valuable structural decision: a region-first index of candidate clinical contexts. Section 1 contributes a different, mechanism-first index. These should be combined as a two-dimensional research matrix:

| Mechanism/failure family | Regional or system context |
|---|---|
| geometry and axial organization | cervical, thoracic, lumbar, craniofacial, extremity |
| concentration and localized strain | joint, disc, tendon, cartilage, enthesis, bone |
| instability and repeated demand | segmental, joint, ligamentous, motor-control contexts |
| discoordination and sequencing | gait, rib, pelvic, scapular, kinetic-chain contexts |
| constraint, tethering, and restricted excursion | nerves, vessels, airway, pressure systems, sliding interfaces |
| compensation and rerouting | local, regional, contralateral, and whole-body patterns |

This matrix should remain an internal example and research inventory. Book I can select a few worked narratives from it; a later clinician-facing reference can populate it only after claim-level evidence review.
