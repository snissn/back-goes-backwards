**Status:** Conservation-first delta review packet 0.3

## 1. Editorial verdict

The `new` outlines should be promoted in status as a **structural and hypothesis source archive**.

They are not finished scientific prose. They were written in advance of an intentionally deferred research and synthesis phase, however, so their provisional confidence should not be used as a reason to narrow or discard the ideas they carry. Across Parts I–VI, the outlines preserve a developed sequence of observations, intuitions, proposed mechanisms, hypotheses, taxonomies, examples, audience transitions, and future-work branches that the first editorial synthesis compressed too aggressively.

The appropriate reconciliation is therefore neither to restore the old book wholesale nor to continue reducing it to a small set of abstractions. It is to separate two judgments:

1. **Structural value:** Does this material preserve a useful order of explanation, category system, example family, research question, or pedagogical progression?
2. **Claim maturity:** What research, formal definition, translation, qualification, or evidence would be needed before a particular version could be published?

A passage can be structurally and conceptually important while its current wording requires complete rewriting. That is the dominant pattern in the archive. The default in this packet is therefore conservation: retain the strongest coherent version of the intended idea, then let later work support, revise, narrow, relabel, or—only after explicit adjudication—exclude it.

### Conservation-first interpretive rule

This audit reads the archive as internal research planning, not as a manuscript already making maximal public claims. In particular:

- “health,” “disease,” “benefit,” and “protection” do not automatically mean that one mechanical factor is asserted to be the sole cause or cure;
- “preferred,” “optimal,” and “natural” can name a proposed organizing tendency or reference configuration without yet claiming one rigid posture for every task and body;
- “systemic” can name a proposed chain across regions, interfaces, pressure systems, or physiology without assuming that every intermediate link is already established;
- “soft-to-hard,” posterior loading, anterior dominance, tensegrity, dimensional, topological, and traditional terms should first be reconstructed as charitably as possible in the project's own model, then formalized or tested;
- strong causal, clinical, or practical language is a marker for later research and claim review, not a deletion instruction.

Only reader-facing planning residue and unreviewed wording are excluded at this stage. Their editorial function and every substantive proposition remain recoverable.

## 2. Disposition vocabulary

- **Promote:** Restore as a Part-, chapter-, or major-section-level obligation.
- **Integrate:** Preserve within a chapter or worked example, with new language and evidence.
- **Inventory:** Retain as a candidate-example or research bank without committing it to the manuscript.
- **Defer:** Preserve for a later book in the series.
- **Formalize:** Preserve the intended observation, intuition, mechanism, or hypothesis while developing more exact terms and intermediate steps.
- **Adjudicate later:** Research the proposition before deciding its eventual evidence tier, wording, scope, or exclusion. Uncertainty is not itself a negative disposition.
- **Wording hold:** Do not copy provisional or planning language into publishable prose; preserve the substantive proposition and editorial job behind it.

### Crosswalk classifications

The granular audit below uses four mutually exclusive classifications. They describe the relationship between a source obligation and the frozen synthesis, not the truth of the source claim.

- **F — Faithfully represented:** The source obligation has a visible destination at suitable depth.
- **C — Present but over-compressed:** The central idea is present, but a useful sequence, distinction, example family, audience transition, or research obligation has become difficult to see.
- **U — Unplaced but retained:** A meaningful source obligation has no visible destination. It remains part of the project and must receive an explicit outline, research-inventory, or example-bank home.
- **P/R — Preserved for research or formalization:** The full intended proposition remains active in a later book, internal inventory, or formalization program. The current destination does not prejudge whether later research will support, revise, narrow, or exclude a publishable version.

The crosswalk groups repetitive leaf headings when they perform one editorial job. Named examples remain visible as inventories even when they do not each receive a manuscript destination. Planning keys such as “Job,” “Inputs / Outputs,” and “Acceptance criteria” are treated as chapter-specification material rather than reader-facing content. None of the four classifications means “false,” “unsafe,” or “delete.” Those judgments belong to the later research and publication audits.

## 3. Section-by-section reconciliation

### Section 1 — Mechanics, dysfunction, and disease

#### Structure worth recovering

Section 1 contains three strong architectures that should remain visible in Book I:

1. **Integrated living structure:** skeletal geometry, joints, ligamentous restraint, active muscle, compliant tissues, pressure-bearing spaces, and connective interfaces participate in one changing mechanical system.
2. **A transition from ordinary load sharing to breakdown:** geometry and alignment affect available paths; repeated or poorly distributed demand produces local concentration; compensation can preserve function while changing future exposure.
3. **A failure-family taxonomy:**
   - axial or global geometric change;
   - loss of segmental control and instability;
   - force concentration and degenerative progression;
   - discoordination and sequencing failure;
   - impingement, tethering, and constrained space;
   - kinetic-chain distortion and compensation.

The six failure families and the named clinical inventory perform complementary jobs: the families organize possible mechanisms, while the conditions test, refine, and potentially challenge those mechanisms. Preserve both. The families should be promoted into Book I's explanation of load mismanagement and retained as one axis of a candidate-example matrix; condition-specific conclusions remain for later research.

The section also preserves useful scale changes: local tissue stress, regional compensation, body-wide reorganization, modern exposure contexts, and medical/research implications. Book I should make those scale changes explicit rather than moving directly from local mechanics to four examples.

#### Intent to preserve and later formalize

- Preserve the hard/soft distinction, including its TCM motivation, while developing the functional-role model and testing where tissue classes are or are not a useful approximation.
- Preserve preferred alignment and mechanical-neutrality intuitions as candidate reference configurations while specifying how task, anatomy, capacity, scale, and individual variation modify them.
- Preserve tensegrity as a potentially important body-wide model and compare its explanatory reach with other mechanical representations.
- Preserve the integrated hypothesis connecting fascial continuity, baseline tone, glide, densification, pressure, and regulation; decompose its links so both the whole model and its components can be researched.
- Preserve the proposed path from small or persistent geometric deviation through compensation to local or systemic consequence; later work should determine its boundary conditions, typical effect sizes, and exceptions.
- Preserve circulation, lymphatic, neural, pressure, and energetic-cost pathways in their intended body-wide scope while tracing each intermediate mechanical and biological link.

#### Higher-specificity propositions held intact for research

- mechanics may be a primary or important contributor in some chronic or systemic presentations;
- changing alignment, routing, or posterior participation may improve symptoms, function, recovery, or—in defined cases—disease processes;
- posture or global configuration may act as an important upstream determinant in particular contexts;
- protocols, screening programs, and a broader biomechanical medical paradigm may eventually follow from the model.

These are active research propositions, not claims that Book I must prove or publish at maximal strength. This audit preserves them without deciding their final form.

### Section 2 — Hard/soft roles and posterior support

#### Structure worth recovering

Section 2 contains the clearest earlier explanation of why the hard/soft principle and posterior chain matter to the project. Its strongest sequence is:

1. redefine the “hard body” as structural capacity rather than visible muscular hardness;
2. identify the posterior chain anatomically;
3. explain its mechanical roles in upright support, propulsion, deceleration, elastic energy management, stabilization, and transfer;
4. examine how compliant, active, tensile, and skeletal structures hand demand among one another;
5. describe failure when a capable route is interrupted and another structure substitutes for it;
6. follow posterior participation through ordinary tasks, development, aging, and changing capacity;
7. state where the model is incomplete or secondary.

The first synthesis retained the hard/soft principle but compressed too much of this progression. The frozen Book I outline now restores posterior support as a multi-chapter Part-level subject rather than an example embedded in the opening mechanics.

#### Intent to preserve and later formalize

- Preserve “soft-to-hard” as the claim that gross demand is best resolved through a capable structural path so compliant structures can remain available for their yielding, accommodating roles. The proposed fascia/muscle → ligament/tendon → bone/joint sequence remains a first-order model to refine rather than a premise to erase.
- Preserve posterior structures as a major support and transfer system in Book I, then develop preferred posterior organization as an explicit series hypothesis.
- Preserve the evolutionary account linking posterior anatomy, vertebrate organization, bipedalism, locomotion, and endurance as a research program rather than requiring it to function as proof at outline stage.
- Preserve the proposed patterns across daily function and the lifespan; later work should distinguish general tendencies, individual variation, and appropriate applications.
- Preserve anterior-dominance and modern-exposure observations as candidate cultural and mechanical patterns while translating them into task, duration, variability, recovery, environment, and available movement where useful.

#### Programs preserved in later volumes

- posterior loading as the preferred global state: Book II;
- its full Yin/Yang and hard/soft correspondence: Book III;
- breath as a necessary driver or major organizer of posterior configuration: Book IV;
- posterior-loading observations, tests, cues, and corrective programs: Book V.

### Section 3 — Medical implications specification

Section 3 is the most mature editorial architecture in the `new` archive, even though its planning keys are not prose. Its sequence should govern the medical half of Book I:

1. begin where mechanics already exists in medicine;
2. state the missing body-wide load-routing question;
3. establish scope and causal rules;
4. explain why this is medically relevant upstream of rehabilitation;
5. develop cumulative demand, compensation, delayed consequence, and degeneration without discrete injury;
6. show how the lens changes questions without becoming a diagnosis;
7. end with education, prevention, and a testable research program.

The guardrails—mechanism-first, tiered claims, no protocols, and explicit objections—belong in the Book I contract and chapter briefs. They should not appear as reader-facing headings. They govern Book I's published medical argument; they make room for stronger later hypotheses by preventing those hypotheses from having to carry foundational weight prematurely.

The three original “failure signatures”—constraint, concentration, and instability—form the core of the medical architecture. The reconciled Book I can preserve them while adding interruption, substitution, discoordination, compensation, restricted excursion, and scale transition as related or intermediate patterns.

### Section 4 — Three-dimensional structure, meridians, and bandhas

#### Structure worth recovering

Section 4 preserves several distinct programs that should not be collapsed into one claim:

- why a three-dimensional body cannot be understood only through isolated planar levers;
- the use of basis directions and local coordinate frames to decompose complex movement and load;
- separate axial, rotational, and lateral/radial modes with anatomical examples;
- Yin/Yang as proposed signed directions rather than substances;
- the grouping of the Six Divisions and paired channels by axis;
- bandhas as candidate control points, locks, or coordination strategies;
- traditional systems as records of embodied observation that require historical as well as mechanical study;
- the emergence of a coordinated whole from several dimensional controls;
- the need for diagrams, comparative mappings, testable predictions, and explicit failure conditions.

The frozen series currently imports the first two items into Book I in generic form: three-dimensional reasoning, local frames, forces, moments, geometry, and load paths. It gives the signed anatomical dimensions a primary home in Book II; the Six Divisions, channels, and historical translation in Book III; and bandha mechanics and breath coupling across Books III–V. This placement is provisional and does not reduce the remaining programs' status as preserved project hypotheses.

#### Variants and formalization questions to preserve

- The axis-based Six Division map agrees with the current authorial mapping at the level of channel groups, while the source records signed-direction variants—most clearly in the lateral Yin/Yang direction. Preserve the variants with provenance until the mapping is reconciled.
- The later “gross” hand-versus-foot grouping is distinct from the paired Six Division mapping. Preserve it as another mapping layer or candidate relationship rather than silently merging the two.
- “Rotational,” “lateral,” and “longitudinal” are used as movement axes, directions of translation, and body-wide organizational modes. Preserve these uses while later formal work determines where they are related and where they require separate terms.
- The proposed bandha-to-axis assignments and activation sequence remain named higher-specificity hypotheses whose derivation, meaning, and relationship to the coordinate model require later work.

#### Hypotheses preserved for later formalization and research

- Preserve “the fourth dimension” as the source's name for emergent integration. Later formalization can test whether it is best represented as phase, a state variable, an order parameter, a control state, a geometric construction, or a metaphor; the hyoid and Maha Bandha remain candidate anatomical or control constructs within that inquiry.
- Retain scientific synthesis or validation of traditional maps as a research ambition. Anatomical, biomechanical, physiological, historical, and empirical work should determine which proposed correspondences are supported, revised, or not supported.
- Preserve the strung-bow analogy as an explanatory and model hypothesis. Later work should distinguish literal continuity, functional coordination, and clinically meaningful force transmission rather than decide that distinction here.
- Preserve diagnostic bandha hypotheses, treatment algorithms, and movement protocols for later practice work; their eventual form remains contingent on model, evidence, and safety review.

### Section 5 — Breath and dynamic integration

Section 5 preserves a useful dynamic sequence for Book IV:

1. revisit the three modes in a breathing body;
2. examine the hyoid, jaw, tongue, airway, diaphragm, rib cage, abdomen, pelvis, and spine as coupled structures;
3. distinguish inhalation and exhalation as phases with changing pressure, volume, force, and geometry;
4. treat one phase as preparing the next rather than assuming a complete reset;
5. investigate axis transitions, spiral continuity, and candidate mechanical “dead spots”;
6. test possible effects on flow, neural excursion, organ support, movement, and performance;
7. compare traditional phenomena such as bandha, *peng*, and *fa-jing* without presuming one mechanism;
8. define measurements, limits, and competing models.

The source's higher-specificity propositions should remain explicit: inhale as Yang, breath as a necessary driver of posterior organization, the hyoid as a fourth-dimensional or integrative node, and tensegrity as an account of systemic regulation. This pass does not decide them. It preserves them as named hypotheses and decomposes them into researchable links so later work can support, revise, narrow, reinterpret, or exclude particular formulations.

### Section 6 — Embodiment, teaching, and practice

Section 6 supplies the source architecture now carried into Book V. It preserves four separable structures:

- **individual practice:** ordinary tasks, progression, adaptation to age and history, recognition of compensation;
- **pedagogy:** awareness → engagement → integration, accessible language, imagery, visual models, tactile feedback, and staged learning;
- **professional translation:** distinct uses and boundaries in rehabilitation, yoga, Pilates, martial arts, athletics, and education;
- **responsible development:** outcome tracking, ethics, referral, case series, collaboration, public movement literacy, and revision when results fail.

The practices themselves remain active candidates rather than settled recommendations. Preserve both the practices and their instructional and research architecture now; later model, evidence, safety, and audience review will determine their eventual form.

### Appendix — Diagnoses with biomechanical components

Although its category nodes are not yet populated, the appendix is not conceptually empty. It establishes three structural commitments:

1. a clinician-facing supplemental reference distinct from the book's core causal argument;
2. conservative pattern or signature language with optional claim-strength tiering;
3. a region-first taxonomy containing twelve exact anatomical or physiological contexts.

The twelve contexts are cervical, thoracic, lumbar, craniofacial, upper extremity, lower extremity, cranial/intracranial pressure, upper airway/respiratory, rib/thoracic mechanics, diaphragmatic/abdominal pressure, pelvic-floor/urogenital, and visceral suspension/organ mobility.

Section 1 contributes a different, mechanism-first index. The future research matrix should cross these two independent axes:

| Mechanism/failure family | Regional or system context |

| geometry and axial organization | cervical, thoracic, lumbar, craniofacial, extremity |
| concentration and localized strain | joint, disc, tendon, cartilage, enthesis, bone |

This table describes the mechanism axis; it does not replace the appendix's twelve-category regional axis. The matrix can be developed now as an internal research asset. Candidate entries should preserve source provenance and receive explicit evidence, alternative-explanation, and intended-use fields before publication. Book I may select a few worked narratives from it, while the full taxonomy remains available for a later professional reference.
